## Durable thesis vs snapshot

### Durable

1. A brokerage account can be useful as more than an order-entry endpoint.
2. Information, company research, funding rails, execution tools, account management and learning content can be connected into one user journey.
3. Product design can reduce small repeated decisions and transfers; this is a friction-design question, not only a fee-ranking question.
4. `Free` information and `zero commission` should be read as specific price labels inside a wider business and cost structure.
5. Information availability does not guarantee investment quality; provider incentives and user purpose should remain separate.

### Snapshot

- 14 million cumulative general accounts (April 2026).
- 7 million NISA accounts (January 2026).
- 51% / 49% ownership snapshot (April 2026 company profile).
- Money Bridge 0.48% / 0.42% (August 3, 2026).
- SPU thresholds / multipliers.
- credit-card point-return rates.
- Kabupita spread and eligible account design.
- individual app / service names.

## Counterevidence / qualification

- An account-count headline measures accounts, not active engagement or satisfaction.
- The presence of Nikkei, Shikiho and Barron's Digest does not make the brokerage a neutral public-information utility. The content sits inside a commercial customer-acquisition and retention system.
- Barron's Digest is curated Japanese excerpts/summaries, not full-source access and not a complete proxy for `the overseas viewpoint`.
- Reward programs can reduce psychological entry friction but can also distort behavior if users transact to earn rewards rather than because the investment is appropriate.
- Money Bridge interest rates and point programs are variable conditions. They should illustrate the architecture, not define the article's long-term value.
- Zero commission is not zero total cost: spreads, FX costs, fund expenses, taxes and market risk may remain depending on product.

## Editorial model

The revised article uses the following **article-level working model**, not an industry taxonomy:

**Discover → Understand → Fund → Execute → Review**

It describes how a retail brokerage can connect information discovery, company understanding, funding, transaction execution and portfolio review. The model is used to diagnose interface friction; it is not claimed as a Rakuten Securities internal framework or validated financial-services model.
