# 「最後のお願い」が5年で復活する——松井秀喜と東スポの祝辞はなぜ面白いのか

A newspaper turns 60 and receives a congratulatory message from baseball legend Hideki Matsui. A lovely gesture—until he calls getting friendly with Tokyo Sports the “only regret” of his playing career. This is an anniversary tribute dressed as a complaint. The paper publishes it, and the reporter promises to ask again. No apology in sight.

“Harsh but affectionate” is an easy verdict, but it leaves too much unexplained. Why can an attack function as a gift? Why might a paper gain from publishing criticism of itself? And how can an outsider sense an intimate relationship? この小さな joke には、pragmatics, social psychology, ritual, and brand identity が重なっている。

*This reading examines the [Tokyo Sports article published April 3, 2020](https://www.tokyo-sports.co.jp/articles/-/187903?page=1). Quotes are brief; the original is not reproduced. Desk research checked public sources on October 24, 2026; no new interviews were conducted.*

## 守｜お祝いの形式を破りながら、最後までお祝いを続けている

A congratulatory address is a speech act: it publicly marks a milestone and sustains a relationship. A polite wish for the company's success works instantly, but it can also be written by anyone. 祝辞には、the paradox of interchangeable praise がある。The more conventional the message, the less we learn about the people involved.

Matsui first satisfies the etiquette, then moves to the request itself. In 2015, a former beat reporter invoked Matsui's jersey number 55 and called it the “last request.” In 2020 the very same person asks again. Matsui praises the reporter's audacity as characteristically Tokyo Sports. The trick isn't crude abuse; it is **impolite content delivered through the grammar of praise**. [Original](https://www.tokyo-sports.co.jp/articles/-/187903?page=1)

An anniversary normally invites corporate history. Matsui instead tells the private history of a newspaper that has inconvenienced him. The number 60 becomes evidence of a relationship lasting years. 会社を褒めるより、その会社としか共有できない history を示す。That's why the supposedly rude letter feels more specific—and perhaps more celebratory—than ceremonial praise.

## 守｜高校の部室と奇抜な一面は、相手の欠点を文化として描く

The story detours into the high-school clubhouse. Coach Yamashita read Tokyo Sports; in Ishikawa the edition arrived a day later under the Chukyo Sports name. Teenagers read things other than baseball coverage, including rather stimulating serialized fiction. We set out to inspect a grand career regret and end up studying the clubhouse's reading habits. どうしてこうなった。 [Source](https://www.tokyo-sports.co.jp/articles/-/187903?page=1)

That detour matters. A professional athlete complaining about intrusive reporters could remain a story of media versus athlete. Yet Matsui first identifies himself as a teenage reader. He was part of this odd media culture before it became professionally inconvenient. By making himself an accomplice, he prevents the criticism from becoming one-directional aggression. ここで roast が personal history に変わる。

The paper's own retrospective in 2026 recalls sensational covers such as 1989's claim about Elvis and a 2002 kappa discovery. This is not confirmation of the headlines' factual claims. Rather, the paper presents that editorial eccentricity as part of its history. [Tokyo Sports' 2026 retrospective](https://note.tokyo-sports.co.jp/n/necc31edf94bc). The roast hits harder because the target already recognizes the costume.

## 守｜笑いは「失礼」と「安全」が同時に成立したときに生まれる

Why isn't the reader offended? **Benign violation theory**, developed by psychologists A. Peter McGraw and Caleb Warren, offers a useful lens: something can be funny when it registers as a violation and simultaneously as harmless. The paper tested moral violations; applying it to Matsui's article is our interpretation, not an experiment conducted on readers of this column. [2010 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20587696/)

Here the violation is criticizing the recipient during its own celebration. The benign signals are a voluntarily accepted invitation, shared history, self-mockery by the paper, and the reporter's reply. But this is not a license to insult friends. The same sentence, placed in a different relationship, may land as a genuine attack. 笑いは sentence 単体ではなく、context と interaction の産物だ。

*Analytical illustration created for this article; not empirical proof that the theory explains every reader's response.*

Gregory Bateson argued that play carries a second-level message: **“this is play.”** He called such messages about messages *metacommunication*. A playful bite resembles a real bite while meaning something different. [Bateson's 1955 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13297882/). 松井の文面にも、内容とは別に『これは関係性の中で行う冗談だ』という signal が流れている。

Erving Goffman's **frame analysis** asks how we decide what kind of event is happening—fight, joke, performance, ceremony. A single Matsui sentence could look like a complaint. In the full editorial frame, the same words become a shared game. [Goffman's 1974 book](https://books.google.com/books/about/Frame_Analysis.html?id=7oRqAAAAMAAJ). 言葉の意味は vocabulary だけで固定されない。誰がどう受け取り、どの場に置かれるかで変わる。

## 破｜一人の毒舌ではなく、二人で作る作品だったと仮説を組み替える

The first hypothesis—Matsui is simply a gifted sarcastic writer—fails to explain the ending. Reporter Toshio Mishima's note explains why he hesitated to request the message, thanks Matsui for accepting, and then promises to make yet another “last request” for the 65th anniversary. Matsui's plea to be left alone meets an enthusiastic plan to return. [Reporter's note](https://www.tokyo-sports.co.jp/articles/-/187903?page=1)

The roles become clear. Matsui plays the exasperated victim; the reporter plays the shamelessly persistent requester. Each strengthens the other's role. In a manzai analogy, Matsui delivers an extended tsukkomi, while the reporter revives the original boke at the end. The target of the roast becomes a co-author of the punchline. 記事の主体は single author から interaction へ変わる。

Consider three counterfactual edits. Remove the criticism: politeness remains, but their history vanishes. Remove Matsui's formality: the reader may hear anger instead of play. Replace the reporter's comeback with routine gratitude: Matsui stays witty, yet the ongoing story disappears. This thought experiment is not an audience test. Still, it suggests the key variable is not the quantity of venom but the **other person's ability to return the joke**.

## 破｜55周年、60周年、65周年——冗談に時間を折り込むと関係が見える

The numbers act as a screenplay. In 2015 the paper's 55th anniversary matches jersey No. 55, giving “last request” a plausible excuse. By 2020, breaking that promise is the joke. In the 2020 article, 2025's 65th anniversary appears only as a future promise to ask again—not verified evidence that the next request happened. [Primary source](https://www.tokyo-sports.co.jp/articles/-/187903?page=1)

![The last-request running gag across 2015, 2020 and the projected 2025. The final date is only a prediction.](assets/matsui-tospo-time-loop-2026.svg)

*Timeline distinguishes the historical requests reported in 2020 from a future intention stated in the same article. A 2025 follow-up has not been independently established here.*

Comedy calls the return of an earlier joke a **callback**. Here the recurrence does more: every five years weakens the credibility of “last,” while strengthening the implied durability of the relationship. The promise becomes less believable; the bond becomes more believable. 時間によって bad promise が good story に転化する。

The 2020 note also hopes that the coronavirus crisis will have ended by the next anniversary. Reading it later reveals a trace of contemporary anxiety inside the gag. The statement was a hope and prediction, not a verified scientific forecast. A running joke may travel well through time; a forecast must still be tested against reality.

## 離｜プロレスの悪役と似ているが、傷つけないための条件が違う

Professional wrestling offers a useful analogy. A villain can provoke the crowd because spectators recognize the role and its performance frame. That resemblance helps illustrate Goffman's analysis. ただし、Matsui–reporter relationship を scripted entertainment と断定する根拠はない。This is analogy, not a claim about an arranged script.

The difference matters more than the similarity. In wrestling, audiences have a performance contract. In journalism, a person's dignity and the reporter's duty of accuracy remain real. Calling something a joke is not automatic immunity. We cannot measure Matsui's private discomfort from a published article. Read the observable exchange, not imagined inner feelings.

## 離｜祝辞は一回きりの贈り物ではなく、次回の依頼を生む贈与になった

Another unexpected lens is **gift exchange**. Anthropologist Marcel Mauss described giving, receiving and reciprocating as ways relationships persist. This is an analogy, not a proof that modern media greetings follow his account. Matsui gives the message; the newspaper prints it and expresses gratitude; the “return gift” is a booking request for five years later. You receive a present and immediately reserve the next present. [Mauss, *The Gift*](https://web.mit.edu/allanmc/www/mauss1.pdf)

What is sought may not be text alone but the continuity of access: the ability to ask again. The request becomes an anniversary ritual and a test of whether a relationship remains open. This is an interpretation, not knowledge of the reporter's hidden motive. If the request caused real distress it would be different. Still, the published acceptance makes the recurrence a public marker of continuing contact.

## 離｜ブランドが自分の欠点を語れるとき、説明より強い個性が現れる

For brand communication, perhaps the strangest actor is the newspaper. It gives space to a celebrity's prolonged teasing and lets its own reporter answer with extra shamelessness. The very trait being criticized is performed on the page. Rather than advertising “we are funny and unconventional,” the paper **demonstrates** its identity. The artifact becomes proof of the positioning, at least as an editorial impression.

That does **not** mean confession automatically builds trust. Factual accuracy remains a responsibility, even for entertainment journalism. Readers may enjoy surprise and still demand truthfulness. この distinction は重要だ。Appreciating a humorous brand identity does not certify the accuracy of every headline.

## 破から離へ｜「相手にも返答できるか」で文章の面白さを実験する

For practice, “add more sarcasm” is terrible advice. A better proposal is a small editorial experiment: compare (1) conventional praise, (2) one-way sarcasm and (3) a shared concrete episode with a reply from the recipient. Ask readers to rate amusement, warmth, trust, offensiveness and perceived relationship separately. This distinguishes funny from likable.

Two editorial safeguards follow: does the target genuinely consent, and does an unfamiliar reader have sufficient cues to recognize the joke? The aim is not more punchlines. It is relational disclosure without turning another person into a prop. If only one side gets to speak, the supposed collaboration collapses.

## 結論｜「最後のお願い」は破られた。でも関係は終わらなかった

At first, the funniest detail was a reporter who said “last time” and came back five years later. After following the evidence, the scene changes. Matsui names the newspaper's flaws; the reporter embraces and repeats the flaw; the newspaper publishes both. Irony, self-critique, ritual and role-playing coexist. The laughter looks less like the product of one brilliant writer and more like a result of mutual response.

Matsui's wish not to be asked again therefore does more than close the piece. If honored, the request ends; if comically challenged, the running story may continue. We have not established what happened in 2025. Yet in 2020 the exchange leaves readers expecting another round. A promise that loses credibility each time can paradoxically make the relationship look more durable. That's the real last-request joke.

## 調査ノート｜確認できたこと、推論したこと、次に確かめたいこと

**Verified in public sources:** the newspaper began in 1960; it published Matsui's anniversary message and Mishima's note on April 3, 2020; the 2020 article reports a 2015 request and predicts a 2025 request; a 2026 Tokyo Sports retrospective recalls its eccentric front pages.

**Interpretation, not established intent:** the greeting publicly confirms intimacy, the reporter completes a collaborative joke, and theories of play and gift exchange illuminate the scene. Benign violation research did not experimentally test this particular article.
