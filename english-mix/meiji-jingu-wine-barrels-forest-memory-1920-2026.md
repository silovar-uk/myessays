# 明治神宮にワイン樽がある。しかも、森は人工だった

*The shrine that planned its forest for a century.*

> **Reading guide:** 日本語を土台にしつつ、自然な英語の文や概念を混ぜた版。This is not a line-by-line translation. 史実、数字、参照資料、議論の順序は日本語正本と対応させている。

![Wine barrels offered at Meiji Jingu, photographed in 2025.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Wine_Barrel_Offerings_2025-01-23.jpg/960px-Wine_Barrel_Offerings_2025-01-23.jpg)

*Photo: Asanagi, 2025, [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wine_Barrel_Offerings_2025-01-23.jpg), [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).*

## 1. 守の入口――神社の参道に、なぜフランスのワイン樽がある？

Picture a Shinto shrine: a wooden torii gate, gravel paths, tall trees. Now add rows of wine barrels from Burgundy, France. Wait — wine barrels, at a shrine?

明治神宮の歴史データベースは、明治天皇が西洋文化を取り入れ、特にワインを好んだことにちなむ奉納だと説明している。The first offering of 19 barrels dates to **2006**, not the Meiji era. 奉納はその後も続く。[Source: Meiji Jingu historical database](https://www.rekishidb.meijijingu.or.jp/glossary/detail.php?id=196)

The strange detail changes the question. Instead of asking, “Why does an old shrine keep this ancient custom?”, we should ask, “Why did people in 2006 choose to remember the Meiji era this way?” 伝統は必ずしも古い物体の保存ではない。It may also involve new forms of remembering an older story.

そして、もう一つの surprise。Behind the barrels is a forest that looks ancient, but was deliberately planted in the twentieth century.

## 2. そもそも神社とは何か――何を、誰が、どうまつるのか

**Shinto（神道）**は、日本の神々や祭りに関わる信仰と実践の広いまとまりだ。A **kami（神）** can be associated with nature, ancestors, or a historical figure. 日本の宗教史には、神道と仏教が長く交わった**神仏習合**もある。The distinction between “native” and “foreign” religion is not a tidy historical line.

A shrine enshrines its **saijin（祭神）**, the kami to whom its worship and rituals are directed. 明治神宮の御祭神は、**明治天皇**と、その皇后だった**昭憲皇太后**。It was founded on **November 1, 1920**, during the Taisho era — despite “Meiji” in its name.[Source: Meiji Jingu](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/about/)

Crucially, a shrine is not necessarily a tomb. 明治天皇の陵墓は京都の**伏見桃山陵**にある。Enshrining someone's spirit and burying their remains are different practices.[Source: Imperial Household Agency](https://www.kunaicho.go.jp/visit/ryobo/122.html)

**Jingu（神宮）** is a shrine title associated with a special relationship to the imperial family, not a simple synonym for “big shrine.” **Torii（鳥居）** marks an entrance to sacred space; **sando（参道）** is an approach; **haiden（拝殿）** is a worship hall; **honden（本殿）** is the main sanctuary.[Source: Association of Shinto Shrines](https://www.jinjahoncho.or.jp/omairi/name/)

This vocabulary changes the frame: 明治神宮は「昔からある天皇の墓」ではなく、20世紀に造営された宗教・記念空間である。So why build it then?

## 3. 歴史を逆算する――明治の神社が大正にできた理由

The Meiji era, **1868–1912**, was a period of rapid state-building: modern government, schooling, industry, and the military. しかし modernization did not mean freedom for everyone. Imperial expansion, war, and colonial rule also formed part of this period.

明治天皇は**1912年**、昭憲皇太后は**1914年**に亡くなった。According to the shrine's own history, proposals for commemoration included schools, libraries, hospitals, parks, monuments, and a shrine. Eventually, a shrine was chosen.[Source: shrine Q&A](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/faq/?ca=1)

But “built by the people's heartfelt wishes” is not the whole institutional story. A **1912 memorandum** proposed that the **inner precinct (内苑)** be built using national funds while the **outer precinct (外苑)** would rely on donations. Public remembrance and state sponsorship overlapped.[Source: founding memorandum](https://www.rekishidb.meijijingu.or.jp/glossary/detail.php?id=1)

**Quick timeline:**
- **1868** — Beginning of the Meiji era.
- **1912 / 1914** — Deaths of Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken.
- **1910s** — Shrine construction and forest planting; the shrine records approximately **110,000 volunteer participants counted cumulatively**.
- **1920** — Meiji Jingu is enshrined; **chinza（鎮座）** means the establishment of a deity's presence at the site.
- **1926** — The outer precinct, Jingu Gaien, is completed.
- **1945** — Major shrine buildings are destroyed in an air raid.
- **1958** — The main shrine structures are rebuilt.
- **2006** — Offering of Burgundy wine barrels begins.
- **2020** — Centennial of the shrine's founding.

[Sources: shrine Q&A](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/faq/?ca=1), [historical archive on rebuilding](https://www.rekishidb.meijijingu.or.jp/glossary/detail.php?id=87), [Tokyo on Jingu Gaien](https://www.toshiseibi.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/machizukuri/machi_project/toshi_saisei/saisei07/qa).

**Continuity of meaning is not the same as continuity of materials.** 今の社殿を見て、すべて1920年からそのまま残った建物だと思うのは誤りだ。A place can remain culturally continuous even after physical reconstruction.

## 4. 森の正体――「自然をつくる」という、妙な百年計画

![A canopy of trees in the deliberately created forest at Meiji Jingu.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Canopy_of_trees_at_Meiji_Shrine.jpg/960px-Canopy_of_trees_at_Meiji_Shrine.jpg)

*Photo: Grendelkhan, 2018, [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Canopy_of_trees_at_Meiji_Shrine.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Unmodified.*

The forest occupies about **700,000 square metres (70 hectares)**, and roughly **100,000 trees** were donated for its creation. 明治神宮の説明によれば、御苑周辺を除けば、造営前は畑や荒れ地が多かった。The forest was not simply discovered — it was designed and planted.[Source: shrine Q&A](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/faq/?ca=1)

Experts such as **本多静六、本郷高徳、上原敬二** thought about how the forest would look and function **a century later**. That is the genuinely unusual part: they were designing for a future they would never see.[Source: University of Tokyo research](https://grene-city.csis.u-tokyo.ac.jp/project/show/id/1026)

The choice of trees mattered. According to the shrine, Prime Minister Okuma Shigenobu preferred majestic cedar trees. The designers argued for broadleaf evergreen trees such as **シイ and カシ**, suited to Tokyo's soil and urban conditions. **Shoyoju（照葉樹）** means evergreen broadleaf trees with thick, glossy leaves.[Source: shrine Q&A](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/faq/?ca=1)

One key ecological idea is **vegetation succession（植生遷移）**: the composition of a plant community changes as organisms grow, compete, die, and reproduce. The forest was intended to renew itself, not to remain a static lineup of the original saplings.

> **Diagram — from planting to self-renewal**  
> 畑・既存の植物・御苑  
> ↓ **planned planting** in the 1910s  
> 多様な若い樹木が共存  
> ↓ **growth, competition, death, regeneration**  
> 成長した都市の森  
> ↓ future climate and ecological change  
> **Goal: keep a functioning forest, not every original tree.**

This challenges a simple binary. A forest may begin as a **human-made system** and develop through natural processes. 人工と自然は、いつでも対立語とは限らない。

## 5. やりすぎ検証――木の種類が減ったのに、計画は成功なのか

Now for an awkward number. The shrine reports **365 tree species at its founding**, compared with **234** in a survey cited from **2013**. The number went down. Does that mean the project failed?[Source: shrine Q&A](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/faq/?ca=1)

Not so fast. A count of **tree species** is not the entire measure of **biodiversity（生物多様性）**. Birds, insects, fungi, soil organisms, and habitat quality matter too. Some originally planted species were poorly suited to Tokyo. さらに、234種は2013年報告書に基づく数字であって、2026年の新たな実測値ではない。

Let's test three different definitions of success. これは現地実験ではなく、評価指標を切り替える**思考実験**だ。

- **Metric A — number of tree species.** The reported count decreased. But comparisons require consistent surveying methods.
- **Metric B — capacity for self-renewal.** Look for seedlings, soil processes, and replacement of old trees. Is the forest reproducing without constant replanting?
- **Metric C — value to the city.** Include habitat, shade, landscape, quietness, and religious significance. These benefits are not independently quantified here.

The lesson is analytical rather than numerical. **A metric can be correct and still measure the wrong goal.** What you count and what you want to preserve must be aligned.

## 6. その森は誰がつくったのか――献木と国家の二つの顔

The phrase “trees donated from across Japan” sounds straightforward. Yet the shrine's own account mentions contributions from **Sakhalin, Taiwan, Korea, and Manchuria**, alongside other regions. それは、日本の帝国的拡大と植民地支配の時代の地理でもある。[Source: shrine Q&A](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/faq/?ca=1)

We should not jump from this record to a false certainty. どの献木が誰の意思で集められたのか、誰が費用を払ったのか、どのような働きかけがあったのか。The cited page alone cannot establish whether every contribution was voluntary or coerced. But the wider political geography cannot simply be erased from the story.

**State Shinto（国家神道）** refers to the modern Japanese system in which the state institutionally supported and organized shrines and rituals. ただし、各地の宗教実践のすべてを一つの制度で説明できるわけではない。Following Japan's defeat in **1945**, the Shinto Directive and subsequent legal changes altered the relationship between shrines and state power.[Background: National Diet Library material](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/download/digidepo_999337_po_1027-1126.pdf?contentNo=34)

So the question is not whether the shrine was “purely public sentiment” or “purely a government project.” It involved grief, worship, institutions, political power, specialist knowledge, and contributions from unequal parts of an empire. **The beauty of a landscape does not erase the conditions under which it was created.**

## 7. よく似た名前の別空間――内苑、外苑、代々木公園

An important geographical distinction: **Jingu Naien（神宮内苑）** and **Jingu Gaien（神宮外苑）** are different places.

> **Three places, three roles**  
> **内苑｜Yoyogi / Harajuku:** main shrine and a forest designed for natural regeneration.  
> **外苑｜Aoyama / Shinanomachi:** avenue of ginkgo trees, a memorial picture gallery, baseball and rugby facilities; completed in 1926.  
> **代々木公園｜Yoyogi Park:** a Tokyo metropolitan public park, not itself the shrine's inner precinct.

[Sources: Meiji Jingu](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/about/) and [Tokyo Metropolitan Government](https://www.toshiseibi.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/machizukuri/machi_project/toshi_saisei/saisei07/qa).

This is not wordplay. The **forest inside the sacred precinct** and the **tree-lined open spaces of the outer precinct** have different ecological and administrative contexts. 似た名前を混同すると、どの場所で何が起きているのかさえ曖昧になる。

## 8. 破――外苑の再開発は、何を「残す」計画なのか

The Jingu Gaien redevelopment combines replacement of ageing sports facilities with commercial and office development. 事業者は、観戦・競技環境の改善、歩行者動線、段差解消に加え、**外苑の施設収益が内苑・外苑の維持を支える**という説明をしている。[Developer's explanation](https://www.jingugaienmachidukuri.jp/planning/)／[Tokyo Metropolitan Government project outline](https://www.toshiseibi.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/machizukuri/shigaichi_seibi/sai-kai/saikaihatsu/saikaihatsu3_36)

On the other side, **ICOMOS（国際記念物遺跡会議）** and Japanese heritage specialists issued a **Heritage Alert（文化遺産保護の緊急警告）** in 2023, urging cancellation of the project to protect trees, public space, and the historical cultural landscape.[Source: ICOMOS Japan, September 2023](https://icomosjapan.org/media/%E3%83%98%E3%83%AA%E3%83%86%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B8%E3%82%A2%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E3%83%88%E3%83%97%E3%83%AC%E3%82%B9%E3%83%AA%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B9%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E8%A8%B32023%E5%B9%B49%E6%9C%887%E6%97%A5.pdf)

A necessary caveat: tree-removal figures quoted in 2023 are not automatically the finalized numbers for 2026. その後、保全・移植案の見直しや追加調査がある。東京都は2026年にも環境影響評価の関連資料を公表している。[Developer Q&A](https://www.jingugaienmachidukuri.jp/faq/)／[Tokyo environmental assessment](https://www.kankyo.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/assessment/information/toshokohyo/357_jingugaien_tosho)

The real debate is not “sports versus trees.” It is a problem of **trade-offs（複数の価値の両立・比較）**: who pays, who benefits, which alternatives were assessed, and which losses are irreversible. 新しい木を増やせば、育った景観がすぐ戻るとは限らない。

The century-old forest offers a useful comparison, not a verdict. **What will the area look like after fifty or a hundred years?** That question matters for ecosystems, maintenance costs, sports use, and public access — not just the architectural rendering at opening.

## 9. 離――人工の自然は、誰の未来を設計しているのか

A surprising idea emerges from the forest: **design is not the same as permanent control**. 人が最初に計画しても、その後の更新を自然に委ねる仕組みはあり得る。It was designed to become less dependent on its designers.

The analogy reaches beyond ecology. A stadium should be judged not only by opening-day attendance but by its adaptability. An organization should function beyond its founder. An education system should help learners continue learning independently. もちろん、森と組織が同じ法則で成長するわけではない。The comparison is a way to ask a better question: **What would success look like after the original creators are gone?**

Return to the wine barrels. They arrived in **2006**. The forest was planted in the **1910s**. The buildings were rebuilt after **1945**. The place that looks timeless is composed of choices made by different generations.

Perhaps the opposite of tradition is not change. **Tradition may be the continuing decision about what to carry forward while the world changes.** あのワイン樽は、そんな問いを神社の参道に置いている。

## 現地で確かめる「やりすぎ散歩」――観察する六つのこと

A proposed fieldwork checklist — **not a claim that the author has already collected these observations**.

1. Compare **sake barrels and Burgundy wine barrels**. 誰が何を奉納しているのか、説明文を読む。
2. Look at **cedars, cypresses, and glossy broadleaf leaves**. 葉の形だけで樹種を断定しない。
3. Observe **seedlings, fallen leaves, and decaying wood**, not just impressive old trees.
4. Follow the spatial sequence: **torii → approach → cleansing area → worship hall**.
5. Compare a map of the **inner precinct** and **Jingu Gaien**. 同じ境内がずっと続くと思わない。
6. Separate **documented facts, religious explanations, opinions, and future plans** in signs and websites.

**Temizu（手水）** is the cleansing of hands and mouth before worship. The ordinary prayer procedure at Meiji Jingu is **two bows, two claps, one bow（二拝二拍手一拝）**. The official site says the main shrine is about ten minutes from each entrance; opening times vary with the season.[Visitor guide](https://www.meijijingu.or.jp/sanpai/2.php)

## 基礎用語をもう一度、正確に

- **Kami / 神** — an object of reverence in Shinto, not always equivalent to a single Western concept of “god.”
- **Saijin / 御祭神** — the kami enshrined at a shrine.
- **Soken / 創建** — foundation of the shrine.
- **Chinza / 鎮座** — establishment of a deity's presence at a location.
- **Torii / 鳥居** — entrance marker to sacred space.
- **Naien and Gaien / 内苑・外苑** — inner sacred forest precinct and outer memorial / sports precinct.
- **Kenboku / 献木** — donation of trees.
- **Shoyoju / 照葉樹** — glossy evergreen broadleaf trees.
- **Natural regeneration / 自然更新** — replacement of forest generations through seeds and young trees.
- **Vegetation succession / 植生遷移** — long-term change in plant communities.
- **State Shinto / 国家神道** — the pre-1945 institutional relationship between the modern Japanese state and shrine practices.
- **Cultural landscape / 文化的景観** — landscape understood through both nature and accumulated human history.
