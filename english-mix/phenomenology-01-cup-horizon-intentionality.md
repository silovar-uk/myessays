# コップは半分しか見えていないのに、なぜ「全部」を見た気になるのか
## We see more than what is literally visible.

机の上にマグカップがある。

From the front, I cannot see the back. 底も見えない。取っ手が向こう側なら、それすらほぼ見えない。

Yet I do not experience “a white oval plus some curved patches.”

**I see a mug. 一個まるごとのマグカップとして見える。**

That is surprisingly bold. 見えていない部分のほうが多いのに、経験の中ではもう一個の対象になっている。

Phenomenology begins from strange facts like this—facts too ordinary to notice.

---

## 1. Phenomenology is not simply “everyone has their own feelings”

現象学を「主観を大切にする思想」とだけ説明すると、かなりぼやける。

Edmund Husserl wanted to describe **the structures of experience as they are experienced**.

中心概念の一つが**intentionality（志向性）**。

Here, “intentional” does not simply mean deliberate.

見る、思う、怖がる、期待する、想像する。Consciousness is normally consciousness **of something**.

- I see a mug.
- 明日の会議を心配する。
- I imagine a creature that does not exist.
- さっきの失言を思い出す。

Consciousness is not a light glowing inside a sealed box. **It is directed toward a world.**

---

## 2. The hidden side is unseen, but not meaningless

もう一度コップを見る。

Only one profile is directly visible. 少し右へ動けば見え方が変わり、持ち上げれば底が見える。

Still, each profile is experienced as a way **the same mug** appears.

さらに、見えていない裏側も完全な空白ではない。We vaguely expect that if we move around it, more of the mug will appear.

Phenomenology uses the idea of a **horizon** to think about this wider field.

Experience includes not only what is sharply in focus but also background, margins, and further possible appearances.

**What is visible opens toward what is not yet visible.**

---

## 3. Over-serious experiment: classify one mug four ways

マグカップ一個を、必要以上に分類する。

### A. 普通に正面から見る
It simply appears as “a mug.” The hidden back causes no crisis.

### B. 取っ手を向こうへ完全に隠す
The handle disappears from sight, but not from the identity of the object.

### C. 極端に近づく
「コップ」というまとまりが弱まり、surface, colour, texture が前に出る。

### D. 落として割る
Suddenly it appears as fragments, danger, and something to clean up.

The physical object may be continuous, but **its experienced meaning is not fixed**.

Phenomenology does not rush to ask, “So what is the mug really?”

Instead: **How does it appear to me as a mug in the first place?**

---

## 4. Seeing a world is not the same as taking a photograph

A camera can register light at a moment. 人間のexperienceをそれと同じだと考えると、見えていない面を含めて一個の対象として経験することが不思議になる。

Modern perception science has its own explanatory models. Phenomenology is not a substitute for neuroscience or psychology.

The question is different.

Science may ask **how perception is produced**. Phenomenology first asks **what perceptual experience is like and how it is structured**.

メカニズムを測る前に、測ろうとしている経験を雑にしない。

---

## 5. The mug appears as more than what is shown

最初の疑問へ戻る。

Why do I experience the whole mug when only one side is visible?

Phenomenology suggests that perhaps the question should change.

We do not necessarily begin with meaningless colour patches and only later attach “mug” as an interpretation.

Experience is already structured as **something appearing as something**.

A mug.
飲めるもの。
My mug.
割れたら危ないもの。
Something whose hidden side could be revealed by moving around it.

さっきまで、机の上のコップはただそこにあった。

Now it looks slightly strange.

**It appears together with sides I cannot currently see.**

Phenomenology may begin not by adding new facts to the world, but by making visible the structures that were too familiar to notice.
