# IPは「売上」の外でも育っている
## Beyond sales｜「ファン接点設計」の次に来る、熱量の測り方

Imagine two fans of the same IP.

One fan buys ¥10,000 of merchandise this month. They post nothing on social media and then disappear for a while. The other spends ¥0 this month, but recommends the work to friends, writes analysis, answers newcomers’ questions, and comes back to an event six months later.

On a sales sheet, the first fan appears to be worth ¥10,000 and the second ¥0.

But if an IP is something that grows over years, is that enough information?

This is an extreme thought experiment. The point is not to decide which person is the “better fan.” The question is what disappears when every kind of fan value is compressed into purchase alone.

---

## 1. STARTING POINT｜元のレポートは「熱量を読め」と言った。その次が空いている

The starting point is SHAPE Partners’ report 「日本のIP産業は“ブーム”で終わるのか？ IPの育て方、次の成長戦略とは」. It describes the old IP model as creating strong source content and then increasing suitable monetization exits, before identifying three major changes: SNS-born IP, East Asian IP, and the growing importance of fan touchpoint design. [SHAPE Partners PDF](https://framerusercontent.com/assets/4YVe7SQohw3V7p41jwOr4raUqZU.pdf)

The report then lists future capabilities: reading overseas markets, discovering promising IP on digital platforms, expanding worlds and stories, producing monetization across multiple exits, and balancing brand control with speed. One particularly interesting point is its claim that digital IP should be judged not only by follower or view counts but also by fan intensity, comment quality, patterns of spread, secondary creation, and purchasing potential.

That naturally creates the next question.

**What exactly are we measuring when we say fan intensity?**

The report gives the direction of travel. The sequel is to ask how to structure that intensity, what should become numbers, and what should deliberately remain outside a single score.

## 2. FACT｜マーケティング研究では、価値はすでに「購入の外」に出ている

This is not a problem unique to IP businesses. Marketing research has long argued that the value customers create for firms cannot be reduced to purchase alone.

van Doorn and colleagues define customer engagement behavior as behavior directed toward a brand or firm beyond purchase. Examples include word of mouth, recommendations, helping other customers, blogging, and reviews. [Customer Engagement Behavior](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1094670510375599)

So “how much did this person buy today?” and “what did this person cause around the brand?” are separate variables.

In IP, that is easy to imagine. Someone who pays nothing may bring three friends to a cinema. Someone who buys large amounts of merchandise may never introduce a single newcomer. The opposite can also happen.

Purchase matters. It simply shows only one route through which value can appear.

## 3. RESEARCH｜顧客価値は「買う・連れてくる・影響する・教える」に分解できる

Kumar and colleagues split customer engagement value into four components: customer lifetime value from purchasing, customer referral value from referrals, customer influencer value from affecting others through word of mouth, and customer knowledge value from feedback and knowledge supplied to the firm. [Capturing Total Customer Engagement Value](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1094670510375602)

We do not need to copy this framework directly into IP, but the logic is useful.

- **Buy**：tickets, streaming, goods, in-game purchases, membershipsなどでdirect revenueを生む。
- **Bring**：referrals, companions, invitations, word of mouthでnew peopleを入口へ運ぶ。
- **Influence**：analysis, reviews, SNS posts, fan conversationsで他者の理解や関心を動かす。
- **Teach**：surveys, feedback, inquiries, beta participationで運営側のlearningを助ける。

These routes overlap inside the same person. Instead of classifying someone only as a “high spender” or “non-buyer,” it is more informative to ask through which route value is being created.

But one thing is still missing.

IP fans often start making culture outside the company on their own.

## 4. CONNECTION｜IPでは、ファン同士の関係そのものが資産になる

Muniz and O'Guinn’s brand community research shows that admirers of a brand can form non-geographically bound social relationships. Such communities can develop shared consciousness, rituals and traditions, and a sense of responsibility toward the community. [Brand Community](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/27/4/412/1810411)

Fandom research goes even further toward participation. Henry Jenkins has described fandom as participatory culture in which audiences negotiate meaning, create works, and build communities around media. [Fandom, Negotiation, and Participatory Culture](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781119237211.ch1)

This is where measurement becomes difficult.

Sales enter the company ledger. Referral codes can be tracked. SNS post counts can be counted.

But inside jokes, annual rituals, the atmosphere that welcomes newcomers, interpretations added through fan works, and memories that keep people attached for years do not arrive as neat database columns.

And sometimes those things are exactly what changes an IP from “something selling now” into “a place people can return to.”

Value outside sales is not merely advertising-equivalent exposure. It can exist in the durability of the relationship itself.

## 5. JAPAN｜「推し活」のお金は、IPホルダーの売上だけではない

Japanese data makes this leakage of value surprisingly visible.

In 2025, Nomura Research Institute estimated the 推し活 market at roughly ¥3.8 trillion and divided it into official consumption, autonomous fan consumption, related consumption, and collaboration consumption. Official spending that flows relatively directly to content holders was estimated at about ¥1.5 trillion, while autonomous fan spending was about ¥1.0 trillion and related spending such as transport, lodging, and camera purchases about ¥1.1 trillion. [NRI「拡大する『推し活』市場のポテンシャルとビジネスの可能性」](https://www.nri.com/jp/media/column/mcs_blog/20250829.html)

The point is not that IP holders should capture all of this money.

Rather, a large part of the economic sphere generated by an IP appears outside the IP holder’s own sales account. Fans travel, gather with friends, take photographs, celebrate anniversaries, and choose products from other companies.

If we watch only direct sales, we can underestimate how deeply an IP has entered everyday life.

But a large economic sphere does not automatically mean a healthy fan relationship. That becomes important in the next step.

## 6. STRUCTURE｜「熱量」は一つの点数にせず、五つの台帳に分けた方がいい

From here, this is a proposal based on the research.

If we want to operationalize the “fan intensity” described in the SHAPE Partners report, I would avoid one master score and instead keep at least five separate ledgers.

- **Revenue**：purchase value, subscriptions, attendance, cross-category purchase。いまbusinessへ戻っている価値。
- **Continuity**：return visits, comeback after dormancy, active months, following across series。relationshipがどれだけ残っているか。
- **Diffusion**：referrals, companions, recommendations, downstream visits after sharing。別の人への入口を作ったか。
- **Participation**：posts, fan works, event participation, community support。周辺文化へ手を加えたか。
- **Learning**：reviews, surveys, requests, testing。次の改善に使えるknowledgeを返したか。

The important thing is not to turn these five axes into a “fan deviation score.”

Some people spend heavily every month. Some come to one event a year. Some never post but watch every week. If we sum all five dimensions into one number, different forms of attachment become forced onto the same ruler.

What the business needs is not a ranking of who is the greatest fan.

**We need a causal hypothesis about which touchpoint creates which kind of value, and what happens next.**

That is much more useful.

## 7. RE-RESEARCH｜ところが「反応が多い＝熱い」でもなかった

At this point it is tempting to say: fine, then just measure social reactions more precisely.

So I searched specifically for evidence that breaks that idea.

First, engagement can be negative. Research on negative customer engagement shows that strongly negative behavior and word of mouth can influence others as well. [Negative customer engagement behaviour](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0267257X.2020.1735488)

If a controversy makes comments jump tenfold, nobody should celebrate that as healthy fan intensity. Reaction volume has valence.

The second complication is the silent fan. Online-community research finds large groups of people who regularly read but rarely post. Some studies treat lurking not simply as absence but as a legitimate peripheral form of participation or a preparation stage before more active contribution. [Understanding lurkers in online communities](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563214003008) [Fan fiction communities and lurking](https://journal.transformativeworks.org/index.php/twc/article/view/2477)

Silence does not necessarily mean indifference.

That destroys the simple equation “heat = visible activity.”

## 8. ETHICS｜測れるようになるほど、ファンを“無料の労働力”にしやすくなる

There is one more uncomfortable issue.

Fan posts, translation, creation, community management, and word of mouth can generate real economic value for companies and platforms. But once we notice that, it is easy to turn the insight into “make fans post more” or “make fans promote more.”

Fandom studies has a long-running critique of this move. Suzanne Scott examines how media industries can selectively embrace fan participation while commercializing and controlling it. [Terms and Conditions: Co-Opting Fan Labor](https://academic.oup.com/nyu-press-scholarship-online/book/31055/chapter-abstract/264042614)

This is not outside the measurement problem.

What we measure shapes what we try to increase.

If “more participation is always better” becomes the design principle, an activity that was fun can become obligation. Fans do not exist as unpaid growth engines for firms.

Measurement should be used not to demand extra work from fans, but **to avoid misreading relationships that already exist**.

## 9. PRACTICE｜見るべきは「ファンの点数」ではなく、接点から価値への遷移

So what would this look like in practice?

On a dashboard, I would not place a giant individual “fan heat score” at the center. I would track transitions from one touchpoint to the next behavior instead.

- Free viewing → return → paid purchase
- SNS exposure → official-site visit → attendance
- Existing-fan referral → first contact → continued use
- Event participation → community contact → return
- Survey response → operational change → retention change

Each metric should resist the assumption that “higher is always better.” For posting volume, inspect direction and content. For participation rate, preserve the freedom not to participate. For referrals, look for actual downstream movement. For purchases, do not ignore excessive burden.

And move information that is hard to quantify into qualitative research.

How do fans describe the work? What rituals repeat every year? Can newcomers enter easily? How do fans interpret official actions? Why did people who left decide to return?

These are not weak signals simply because they are not numbers.

Sometimes we need the skill to keep information qualitative because quantifying it would destroy the meaning we wanted to understand.

## 10. DISCOVERY｜売上は“価値そのもの”ではなく、価値が通った一つの出口だった

I expected this research to end with the familiar line, “sales alone cannot measure fan value.”

The conclusion became slightly different.

Sales still matter. Revenue is necessary to sustain an IP, and purchase is a strong behavioral signal.

But sales are not value itself. They are one exit through which value created in the fan–IP relationship can pass.

Value may exit as referral. It may exit as creation. It may become community norms. It may appear years later as a return. It may become feedback to the operator. And sometimes it may remain entirely inside one person, invisible from the outside.

The SHAPE Partners report argues that the competitive logic of IP is expanding from simply “having a strong work” toward discovering promising seeds, connecting exits, and designing fan touchpoints.

If I were writing the next paragraph, I would add this.

**The next capability is not the ability to score fans. It is the ability to distinguish the routes through which value is being created.**

I am not saying a ¥0 fan is more valuable than a ¥10,000 fan.

I am saying that the number ¥0 does not tell us what that person is doing.

That is probably the first sentence of the sequel.

## APPENDIX｜IPの「熱量」を売上以外から検証するプロンプト

Below is the research method turned into a reusable prompt for sports, music, characters, games, and other IP businesses.

~~~text
You are a researcher-editor working across IP business, customer engagement, and fandom studies.

Input
【Target IP / brand】{{target}}
【Current KPI】{{KPI}}
【Business objective】{{objective}}
【Fan touchpoints】{{touchpoints}}
【Observable data】{{data}}

Goal
Do not define fan value only by sales or purchase amount.
Do not call visible SNS reaction volume “heat” without further analysis.
Identify which touchpoint creates which kind of value through which route.

Process
1. Separate what the current KPI captures from what it misses.
2. Review customer-engagement research beyond purchase.
3. Classify fan behavior provisionally into five axes: revenue, continuity, diffusion, participation, learning.
4. Separate observable metrics from qualitative information that is hard to observe.
5. Search specifically for evidence that breaks the assumption “more visible reaction = better.” Include negative engagement, controversy, and silent participation.
6. Check whether fan activity is being turned into unpaid labor or obligation for the company’s benefit.
7. Do not compress everything into one fan score. Map touchpoint → behavior → value → continuity.
8. Separate correlation from causation. Do not claim that referrals caused sales growth without supporting evidence.
9. Preserve information as interviews, free text, or observation when quantification would damage its meaning.
10. End with one sentence explaining what the measurement is protecting against being misread, not merely what should be increased.

Reject if
- High spenders are automatically labeled the highest-value fans
- Follower count, likes, or post count alone are treated as intensity
- Silent readers are assumed to be indifferent
- Negative engagement is excluded
- Everything is compressed into one fan score
- Fan creation or word of mouth is treated as naturally unpaid labor
- Correlation is written as causation
- Source URLs are missing

Output
A. What current KPIs capture
B. Missed value routes
C. Five-axis fan value map
D. Observable metrics and qualitative information
E. Re-research that challenged the hypothesis
F. Ethical and operational risks
G. Practical dashboard design
H. Causal hypotheses requiring further validation
I. References
~~~
