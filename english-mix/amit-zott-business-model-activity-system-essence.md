# Business modelは「儲け方」ではなく、活動の配線図だった

## If someone asks “What’s the business model?” why is “subscription” not enough?

「この会社のbusiness modelは？」と聞かれると、つい “monthly subscription,” “ads,” “transaction fees” と答えたくなる。

Of course, those answers matter.

But reading Raphael Amit and Christoph Zott creates a slightly odd feeling. Their business-model papers keep staring not at the price tag, but at **how transactions and activities are connected**.

In their landmark 2001 paper [“Value Creation in E-business”](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/smj.187), Amit and Zott studied 59 recently public e-business firms in the United States and Europe. Their point was that new value can come not only from what is sold, but from **the way transactions are enabled**.

すると、かなり素朴な疑問が出る。

**If the essence of a business model were only “how do we charge?”, why would we need to inspect transaction sequence and who performs each activity?**

So I followed the Amit–Zott line from 2001 through 2012, then compared it with several other influential business-model views.

先に結論を置くと、私にはこう見えた。

**For Amit and Zott, a business model is less a shape of the wallet and more a wiring diagram of value-creating activities.**

## 1. The 2001 starting point: where does an e-business actually create value?

2001年論文の面白さは、最初から「正しいbusiness modelの部品」を決め打ちしなかったところにある。

Existing strategy and entrepreneurship theories each explained part of the e-business phenomenon, but no single theory seemed sufficient. So the authors looked across the 59 firms for recurring sources of value.

The result was four value drivers that would later be summarized as NICE.

- **Efficiency（効率性）**: reduce transaction costs such as search, bargaining and information acquisition.
- **Complementarities（補完性）**: create more value by combining activities or offerings than by using them separately.
- **Lock-in（ロックイン）**: give users or partners reasons to stay in the system and transact again.
- **Novelty（新奇性）**: introduce new transaction content, structures, participants or combinations.

重要なのは、これが単なる “four rules for profitable companies” ではないことだ。

Amit and Zott treat them as **interdependent sources of value creation**. A design that improves efficiency may also strengthen lock-in; adding a complementary service may create a novel transaction structure.

つまり、最初から見ている単位が商品一個ではない。

**Products, information, participants, transactions and the relationships among them are treated as one designed system.**

That is where the business-model construct becomes useful.

## 2. By 2010, “transactions” had expanded into an activity system

In the 2001 definition, a business model was described through the content, structure and governance of transactions.

By the 2010 paper [“Business Model Design: An Activity System Perspective”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024630109000533), the idea becomes easier to see.

**A business model is a system of interdependent activities, and that system crosses the boundaries of the focal firm.**

この「境界を越える」がかなり重要である。

Listing only what employees inside the company do is not enough. Customers may enter data themselves. A supplier may hold inventory. An outside platform may process payments. A partner may install the product. Users may rate one another.

If those activities help create value, they belong on the same map.

会社組織図では、顧客は箱の外にいる。

In an activity-system map, **the customer can also be an actor who performs work**.

This makes the business model a strange but useful unit of analysis: broader than an internal organization chart, narrower and more focal than an entire industry.

## 3. What / How / Who makes the idea surprisingly touchable

The 2010 paper organizes activity-system design into three elements: Content, Structure and Governance.

- **Content = What**: which activities are performed; what is added or removed.
- **Structure = How**: how activities are linked, sequenced and connected.
- **Governance = Who**: who performs each activity — the firm, customers, suppliers, partners or other outside actors.

この三分法が強いのは、business model innovationを「新商品を作ること」から解放するところにある。

In the 2012 MIT Sloan Management Review article [“Creating Value Through Business Model Innovation”](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/store/creating-value-through-business-model-innovation), Amit and Zott explain that innovation can come from adding new activities, linking activities in new ways, or changing the parties that perform them.

つまり、新しい商品がなくてもよい。

Existing resources, existing partners and existing customer touchpoints can be **rewired into a different business model**.

That is why, after reading Amit and Zott, a boring process flowchart suddenly starts to look strategic.

## 4. Let’s overdo it: sell the exact same ¥3,000 ticket through three business models

ここで、わざと商品と表示価格を固定してみる。

Imagine a fictional sports club selling the exact same seat for ¥3,000. Same match, same seat, same posted price.

We change only Content / Structure / Governance.

1. **Direct model**: the club handles promotion, sales, payment and customer support; the spectator buys directly from the club.
2. **External distribution model**: the club manages inventory and entry, while an outside platform handles discovery, search, payment and much of the customer interface.
3. **Official resale model**: a spectator who cannot attend lists the ticket, while the club or platform connects identity checks, invalidation, reissue and settlement.

表面だけ見れば、三つとも “a ¥3,000 ticket sale” である。

But who finds the customer, who processes payment, who holds information, how cancellations are handled, and where each participant gets value are all different.

Even if total revenue somehow ended up identical, the system would still differ in what the organization learns, the customer relationship it owns, dependence on outside firms, transaction costs and ease of repeat use.

**You can hold the revenue model still while the business model moves.**

この思考実験をすると、「business model = pricing or charging method」という理解がかなり苦しくなる。

## 5. NICE is not a game where you max out four sliders

In the 2010 paper, Content / Structure / Governance are design elements, while Novelty, Lock-in, Complementarities and Efficiency are design themes that organize the activity system.

- **Novelty**: introduce new activities, new links or new governance arrangements.
- **Lock-in**: give customers and partners reasons to remain participants and return.
- **Complementarities**: make combined activities worth more together than separately.
- **Efficiency**: reorganize activities or information to reduce transaction costs.

ここで “score 10/10 on all four and win” と考えると、たぶん早すぎる。

Zott and Amit’s 2007 [Organization Science paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/orsc.1060.0232), based on 190 publicly listed entrepreneurial firms in the U.S. and Europe, found a relatively stable positive relationship between novelty-centered business-model design and firm performance. It also reported indications that trying to combine novelty- and efficiency-centered designs could create diseconomies of scope.

つまり、design themes同士にはsynergyだけでなくtensionもありうる。

A 2025 MIT Sloan Management Review article, [“Business Model Innovation: Seven Essentials”](https://shop.sloanreview.mit.edu/store/business-model-innovation-seven-essentials), makes the modern warning even more explicit: based on research on 300 companies, **novelty alone does not guarantee high performance**.

An interesting business model is not simply one with a weird trick.

**It is a system in which activities are configured so that chosen value drivers actually work together.**

## 6. Compare other famous views, and the Amit–Zott obsession becomes clearer

ビジネスモデルに唯一の定義はない。むしろ、too many definitions is part of the field’s history.

Put a few influential views side by side and their emphasis becomes clearer.

- **Joan Magretta (HBR, 2002)**: treats a business model as a story explaining how the enterprise works — who the customer is and how the economics make sense — while distinguishing competitive strategy as a separate problem. [“Why Business Models Matter”](https://hbr.org/2002/05/why-business-models-matter)
- **David Teece (Long Range Planning, 2010)**: emphasizes value delivery to customers, getting paid, and the architecture of revenues, costs and profits. [“Business Models, Business Strategy and Innovation”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002463010900051X)
- **Business Model Canvas**: uses nine building blocks covering customers, value propositions, channels, activities, resources, partners, revenue and costs. Revenue is one block among nine. [Strategyzer official explanation](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/what-is-a-business-model)

これらと比べると、Amit & Zottの癖ははっきりする。

**They do not stop at asking which boxes exist; they ask who performs which activities and how those activities connect.**

A slightly crude but useful contrast: the Business Model Canvas is good at arranging nouns, while the activity system perspective is especially good at seeing **verbs and arrows**.

もちろん、どちらか一方だけが正解という話ではない。

An activity map that forgets customers and economics becomes mere process documentation; a canvas that ignores connections among activities can become a static snapshot. Overlaying the two can be much more powerful.

## 7. Business model and strategy are neither identical nor substitutes

この話をすると、次に “Isn’t that just strategy?” という疑問が出る。

In their 2008 [Strategic Management Journal paper](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/SMJ.642), Zott and Amit studied product-market strategy and business models and concluded that the two are **complements, not substitutes**.

For example, choosing a market strategy such as “serve a broad segment at low price” does not determine one unique activity system.

You can sell direct. Outsource. Shift some work to customers. Bundle complementary services with a partner. Use digital tools to reduce search costs.

同じmarket positionを狙っても、What / How / Whoの組み合わせは複数ありうる。

So, very roughly, **strategy includes choices about where and how to compete, while the business model specifies how value creation is realized as an activity system**.

片方だけでは足りない。

## 8. But calling this “the true definition” would probably get us stopped by the researchers themselves

Zott, Amit and Lorenzo Massa’s 2011 review, [“The Business Model: Recent Developments and Future Research”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0149206311406265), explicitly notes that scholars do not agree on a single definition of the business model.

At the same time, they identify recurring themes: the business model as a new unit of analysis, a system-level view of how a firm does business, an important role for activities, and an attempt to explain value creation rather than only value capture.

つまり、彼ら自身も “Content / Structure / Governance is the one true ontology” と言っているわけではない。

Later scholarship also criticizes the field. For example, the 2021 paper [“A Business Model View of Strategy”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/joms.12580) argues that business-model research does not always provide strong theoretical justification for why particular elements are chosen.

On the other hand, the 2023 paper [“Untangling business model outcomes, impacts and value”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bse.3249) values the Amit–Zott activity-system lens precisely because it treats activities as an interdependent, boundary-spanning system rather than a simple list.

So this framework is not a CT scan that reproduces the whole company perfectly.

**It is a lens that makes it harder to miss which activities are connected, who performs them, and what value emerges from the configuration.**

## 9. In practice, don’t begin with “How do we monetize this?”

アミット＆ゾット的に事業を見るための、かなり雑で強い手順を作ってみる。

1. **Write every verb**: search, compare, apply, screen, make, deliver, use, ask, renew, cancel, resell.
2. **Draw arrows**: which activity enables the next one, and where information, money, products and rights move.
3. **Write the subject**: who performs each activity — the firm, customer, partner or platform.
4. **Check NICE**: where does the configuration create value through efficiency, complementarities, lock-in or novelty?
5. **Rewire one line**: add one activity, change one sequence, or change one responsible party; then watch both value and cost move.

この順番にすると、 “let’s make it a subscription” や “let’s add ads” より前に、商売そのものの構造を触れる。

Then, only after that, layer on who pays, where payment occurs, and who captures what share of the value.

収益モデルを捨てるのではない。

**Put the revenue model back inside the larger activity system.**

## 10. Before researching this, I saw money flows. Afterward, I see verb flows

最初の疑問に戻る。

If someone asks, “What’s the business model?” and you answer, “It’s subscription,” what is still missing?

課金方式は分かった。

But we still do not know **who does what, how those activities connect, and why that configuration creates value**.

The biggest contribution of the Amit–Zott view, to me, is that it makes the business model visible not as a list of internal functions or a revenue table, but as a boundary-spanning activity system.

その視点に立つと、business model innovationは急に身近になる。

You do not always need a new invention. Add one activity. Reconnect two existing activities. Change who performs the work. Redraw the boundary with a partner.

すると、同じ商品が別の商売になる。

Before this research, I looked at a business model and first asked, “Who pays how much?”

調べたあと、先に探すものが変わった。

**Verbs before nouns. Arrows before boxes. The wiring that carries value before the doorway where revenue enters.**

That may be the shortest portable version of Amit and Zott’s business-model thinking.
