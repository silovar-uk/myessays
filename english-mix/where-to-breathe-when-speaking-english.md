# Your mouth can stop without your lungs breathing in――英語の息継ぎはどこでするのか
## 7,065 pauses, 4,094 inhalations――meaning chunks, prosody, and speech planningから考える

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
英語を話していて困るのは、not only “I’m running out of air.” もっと手前に、**where am I allowed to breathe?**という問題がある。日本語なら何となく止まれるのに、英語では文の途中で急に空気がなくなり、`I want to / go to...`のような妙な場所で吸ってしまう。So, is there a “correct” place to breathe in English?

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In 2026, Melissa A. Redford and colleagues studied pauses and inhalations in English speech. 実験2では、若年成人のアメリカ英語話者が長短の文を繰り返し発話し、**7,065 pauses and 4,094 inhalations**が記録された。The numbers do not even match. Your mouth can stop without your lungs taking a new breath.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
さらに、inhalation was not simply a reaction to “low air.” 長い文の前では短い文の前より吸気が起こりやすく、同じ話題の途中より、新しい話題のまとまりへ入る地点で吸気が起こりやすかった。It almost looks as if the lungs know how long the next sentence will be. Of course they do not read grammar. **Speech planning in the brain and breathing are coordinated.**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
ここで問いを少し変えたい。“Where should I breathe?” is not merely a punctuation question or a lung-capacity question. 英語の息継ぎを理解するには、**pause, grammar, meaning chunks, intonation, speech planning, and physiology**を一枚の地図に重ねる必要がある。

## 1. Speech breathing is not ordinary breathing with words added

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
息継ぎの場所を考える前に、we need to separate ordinary breathing from speech breathing. 安静時の呼吸は、吸って、吐いて、また吸うという生命維持の周期。一方、発話呼吸（speech breathing）は、airをvoiceへ変えるために、その周期をかなり作り替える。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Classic physiology studies show that during speech, **inspiration becomes shorter and faster, while expiration becomes longer and slower** than at rest. 声は基本的に吐く空気で作るため、話者は短時間で空気を取り込み、その空気を長い発話へ配分する。Other work on phonation also reports shorter inspiratory time and longer expiratory time during voice production.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
So speaking is not a fifty-fifty cycle of inhale and exhale. **You compress the inhale and stretch the exhale for language.** 息継ぎが一瞬に見えるのは、話者が雑に呼吸しているからではない。The respiratory cycle itself is reorganized around speech.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
That already weakens the advice “just take a deeper breath.” 必要な空気量はもちろん要る。しかし実際の発話では、where to take a quick breath, how far to carry one exhalation, and where to reset the cycleが重要になる。This is not only a capacity problem; it is an allocation problem shaped by linguistic structure.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
だから最初の練習目標は“make one breath longer”ではない。**Put one meaningful stretch on one exhalation, then replenish before the next stretch.** 肺活量の問題に見えたものが、ここでsentence segmentationの問題へ変わる。

## 2. A pause, a breath, and a meaning chunk are related――but not identical

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
英語学習では“breathe at thought groups”のように説明されることがある。方向は悪くない。But at least three different things are being stacked together: **stopping the voice, actually inhaling, and grouping language into meaningful units.**

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
A pause is a temporary stop in speech. そのpauseの中でinhalationが起こることもあれば、起こらないこともある。Then there is the呼吸群（breath group）, whose definition has shifted across traditions. Some work treats it physiologically as speech between breaths; historically, Philip Lieberman also used “breath group” as a prosodic-syntactic unit rather than simply “the stretch between two literal breaths.”

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Phonetics and phonology also use terms such asイントネーション句（intonational phrase）やトーン単位（tone unit）, defined through pitch, rhythm, duration, and boundary patterns. 教育でいう「意味のまとまり（thought group）」は、それら研究上の単位と完全に同じ専門概念ではない。It is safer to treat it as a teaching label for chunks that listeners can process as meaningful units.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This distinction matters in practice. If every slash on a script means “inhale here,” you collapse pausing and breathing into one event. 実際には、**a small boundary may be marked without breathing, while a strong boundary may carry a new inhalation.** The 7,065-pause / 4,094-inhalation result makes that difference hard to ignore.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
だから学習用の記号もone symbol is not enough. 本稿では、`/`を“small semantic/prosodic boundary”、`//`を“strong boundary and good inhalation candidate”として使う。**Once you separate “chunking” from “breathing,” practice becomes much closer to real speech.**

> **Symbols used here**
>
> `/` = small boundary. You may phrase here, but you do not have to inhale.  
> `//` = strong boundary. A good candidate for replenishing air before a new chunk.  
> `×` = a place that splits tightly connected words and often becomes an emergency breath.

**Key sources**: [Włodarczak & Heldner, “Respiratory Constraints in Verbal and Non-verbal Communication”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5434352/) ／ [Peter Roach, discussion of breath-groups and tone-units in *English Phonetics and Phonology*](https://mlsu.ac.in/econtents/3427_English%20Phonetics%20and%20Phonology%20A%20Practical%20Course%20by%20Peter%20J.%20Roach.pdf) ／ [MIT Press, *Intonation, Perception, and Language*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262120241/intonation-perception-and-language/)

## 3. Grammar gives you a strong breathing map――but a map is not the terrain

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
So where do people actually inhale? The most stable answer is: **stronger grammatical and semantic boundaries are more likely breathing points.** 文末、節境界、段落やtopicの切り替わりは、呼吸を更新してもlistenerの理解を壊しにくい。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In 1994, Alison L. Winkworth and colleagues repeatedly measured lung volumes while six healthy young women read passages aloud. 吸気量にはindividual variationがあった一方、**inspirations occurred almost exclusively at grammatically appropriate locations** such as paragraph, sentence, clause, and phrase boundaries. A 2017 review likewise summarizes very high alignment between inhalations and syntactic boundaries in read speech.
