# Your mouth can stop without your lungs breathing in――英語の息継ぎはどこでするのか
## 7,065 pauses, 4,094 inhalations――meaning chunks, prosody, and speech planningから考える

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
英語を話していて困るのは、not only “I’m running out of air.” もっと手前に、**where am I allowed to breathe?**という問題がある。日本語なら何となく止まれるのに、英語では文の途中で急に空気がなくなり、`I want to / go to...`のような妙な場所で吸ってしまう。So, is there a “correct” place to breathe in English?

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In 2026, Melissa A. Redford and colleagues studied pauses and inhalations in English speech. 実験2では、若年成人のアメリカ英語話者が長短の文を繰り返し発話し、**7,065 pauses and 4,094 inhalations**が記録された。The numbers do not even match. Your mouth can stop without your lungs taking a new breath.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
さらに、inhalation was not simply a reaction to “low air.” 長い文の前では短い文の前より吸気が起こりやすく、同じ話題の途中より、新しい話題のまとまりへ入る地点で吸気が起こりやすかった。It almost looks as if the lungs know how long the next sentence will be. Of course they do not read grammar. **Speech planning in the brain and breathing are coordinated.**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
ここで問いを少し変えたい。“Where should I breathe?” is not merely a punctuation question or a lung-capacity question. 英語の息継ぎを理解するには、**pause, grammar, meaning chunks, intonation, speech planning, and physiology**を一枚の地図に重ねる必要がある。

## 1. Speech breathing is not ordinary breathing with words added

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
息継ぎの場所を考える前に、we need to separate ordinary breathing from speech breathing. 安静時の呼吸は、吸って、吐いて、また吸うという生命維持の周期。一方、発話呼吸（speech breathing）は、airをvoiceへ変えるために、その周期をかなり作り替える。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Classic physiology studies show that during speech, **inspiration becomes shorter and faster, while expiration becomes longer and slower** than at rest. 声は基本的に吐く空気で作るため、話者は短時間で空気を取り込み、その空気を長い発話へ配分する。Other work on phonation also reports shorter inspiratory time and longer expiratory time during voice production.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
So speaking is not a fifty-fifty cycle of inhale and exhale. **You compress the inhale and stretch the exhale for language.** 息継ぎが一瞬に見えるのは、話者が雑に呼吸しているからではない。The respiratory cycle itself is reorganized around speech.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
That already weakens the advice “just take a deeper breath.” 必要な空気量はもちろん要る。しかし実際の発話では、where to take a quick breath, how far to carry one exhalation, and where to reset the cycleが重要になる。This is not only a capacity problem; it is an allocation problem shaped by linguistic structure.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
だから最初の練習目標は“make one breath longer”ではない。**Put one meaningful stretch on one exhalation, then replenish before the next stretch.** 肺活量の問題に見えたものが、ここでsentence segmentationの問題へ変わる。

## 2. A pause, a breath, and a meaning chunk are related――but not identical

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
英語学習では“breathe at thought groups”のように説明されることがある。方向は悪くない。But at least three different things are being stacked together: **stopping the voice, actually inhaling, and grouping language into meaningful units.**

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
A pause is a temporary stop in speech. そのpauseの中でinhalationが起こることもあれば、起こらないこともある。Then there is the呼吸群（breath group）, whose definition has shifted across traditions. Some work treats it physiologically as speech between breaths; historically, Philip Lieberman also used “breath group” as a prosodic-syntactic unit rather than simply “the stretch between two literal breaths.”

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Phonetics and phonology also use terms such asイントネーション句（intonational phrase）やトーン単位（tone unit）, defined through pitch, rhythm, duration, and boundary patterns. 教育でいう「意味のまとまり（thought group）」は、それら研究上の単位と完全に同じ専門概念ではない。It is safer to treat it as a teaching label for chunks that listeners can process as meaningful units.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This distinction matters in practice. If every slash on a script means “inhale here,” you collapse pausing and breathing into one event. 実際には、**a small boundary may be marked without breathing, while a strong boundary may carry a new inhalation.** The 7,065-pause / 4,094-inhalation result makes that difference hard to ignore.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
だから学習用の記号もone symbol is not enough. 本稿では、`/`を“small semantic/prosodic boundary”、`//`を“strong boundary and good inhalation candidate”として使う。**Once you separate “chunking” from “breathing,” practice becomes much closer to real speech.**

> **Symbols used here**
>
> `/` = small boundary. You may phrase here, but you do not have to inhale.  
> `//` = strong boundary. A good candidate for replenishing air before a new chunk.  
> `×` = a place that splits tightly connected words and often becomes an emergency breath.

**Key sources**: [Włodarczak & Heldner, “Respiratory Constraints in Verbal and Non-verbal Communication”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5434352/) ／ [Peter Roach, discussion of breath-groups and tone-units in *English Phonetics and Phonology*](https://mlsu.ac.in/econtents/3427_English%20Phonetics%20and%20Phonology%20A%20Practical%20Course%20by%20Peter%20J.%20Roach.pdf) ／ [MIT Press, *Intonation, Perception, and Language*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262120241/intonation-perception-and-language/)

## 3. Grammar gives you a strong breathing map――but a map is not the terrain

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
So where do people actually inhale? The most stable answer is: **stronger grammatical and semantic boundaries are more likely breathing points.** 文末、節境界、段落やtopicの切り替わりは、呼吸を更新してもlistenerの理解を壊しにくい。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In 1994, Alison L. Winkworth and colleagues repeatedly measured lung volumes while six healthy young women read passages aloud. 吸気量にはindividual variationがあった一方、**inspirations occurred almost exclusively at grammatically appropriate locations** such as paragraph, sentence, clause, and phrase boundaries. A 2017 review likewise summarizes very high alignment between inhalations and syntactic boundaries in read speech.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
So for a learner, sentence ends and independent clause boundaries are excellent first targets. たとえば`After the meeting, // I’ll call Sarah / and send the revised file // before I go home.`なら、`After the meeting`の後は前置詞句が完結し、`before I go home`の前も意味のまとまりが切り替わる。Breathing there is less likely to make the listener rebuild the grammar.

<!-- level:3 role:qualification -->
But do not turn this into “breathe at every comma.” Prosodic structure and syntactic structure overlap, but they are not perfectly isomorphic. 何を強調するか、どの語を一続きに聞かせたいか、speech rate、残っている空気、次に言う内容で境界は動く。And spontaneous conversation shows more within-constituent inhalations than careful reading does.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Grammar is not a fence saying “only breathe here.” It is a **topographic map of places where you can breathe with less damage to comprehension.** まずstrong syntactic boundariesを使い、慣れてから文脈に合わせて動かす。You do not need to wait for mysterious “native intuition” to make a reasonable first decision.

**Key sources**: [Winkworth et al., “Variability and consistency in speech breathing during reading”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8084185/) ／ [Winkworth et al., “Breathing patterns during spontaneous speech”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7731204/) ／ [Włodarczak & Heldner (2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5434352/)

## 4. Breathing is pulled not only by what you just said, but by what you are about to say

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
This is the strangest part. An inhalation is not merely the consequence of using up air; it can also be **preparation for the utterance that comes next.** 息継ぎは文のaftercareだけでなく、次の文のsetupでもある。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In Redford et al.’s 2026 Experiment 2, speakers produced a repeated sequence of long and short sentences organized into thematic groups. The probability of inhalation was **higher before long target sentences than before short ones**, and **higher at the beginning of a thematic group than inside the group**. 研究者は、直前のprosodificationだけでは説明できない形でこの差を確認した。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A 1995 spontaneous-speech study also found, in all six speakers, that longer upcoming breath groups were preceded by greater inspired lung volumes. 朗読研究でも、longer utterances and stronger sentence/paragraph boundaries were associated with higher starting lung volumes. Not every study finds equally strong planning effects, but anticipatory coordination is not a one-off observation.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
For a learner, this means the point where you run out of air may be too late to fix. `the revised / file`で苦しくなったとしても、the real repair may be several words upstream. 長いchunkへ入る前のstrong boundaryで空気を補給する。**Move the refueling point, not just the crash point.**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Now breathing changes from a passive physiological event into part of speech design. Good breath management is not “hold on until you cannot.” It is **inhale early enough that the next meaning unit can arrive intact.**

**Key sources**: [Redford et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2026.104763) ／ [Winkworth et al., “Breathing patterns during spontaneous speech”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7731204/) ／ [Winkworth et al. (1994)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8084185/)

## 5. English boundaries are not made of silence alone

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
At this point it is tempting to think, “Fine, I’ll pause at every boundary.” But English phrasing is not built out of silence alone. The listener can often **hear a boundary approaching before the silence arrives.**

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Prosody research describes several cues around larger phrase boundaries:休止（pause）、句末長化（phrase-final lengthening）、そしてpitch changeやpitch resetなどである。English research also shows that lengthening near phrase ends and boundary tones can guide syntactic interpretation. A boundary is not created only at the instant the waveform goes quiet.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
David R. Scott’s experiments with syntactically ambiguous English sentences showed that changing pause duration and phrase-final timing could shift where listeners perceived a phrase boundary. A separate 2014 experiment manipulated pause, final lengthening, and pitch reset in Chinese sentences and found that listeners used all three as boundary cues. 後者はEnglish dataではないのでcue weightingをそのまま英語へ移せないが、boundary perception being multi-cue rather than “silence only” is useful context.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
In practice, when you say `After the meeting / I’ll call Sarah`, `meeting` can lengthen slightly, the intonation can complete a contour, and a tiny gap can appear. 必ずしもそこで大きく吸わなくても、“one chunk ended here”と聞かせられる。If you create a full silent stop at every slash, by contrast, speech can sound mechanically segmented.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So breath training cannot look only at the lungs. **The better you can signal a boundary acoustically, the more places you can chunk without actually inhaling.** 「どこで吸うか」を学ぶには、「where can I *not* breathe and still make the boundary clear?」も学ぶ必要がある。

**Key sources**: [Carlson, “How Prosody Influences Sentence Comprehension”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-818X.2009.00150.x) ／ [Scott, “Duration as a cue to the perception of a phrase boundary”](https://pubs.aip.org/jasa/article/71/4/996/683037/Duration-as-a-cue-to-the-perception-of-a-phrase) ／ [Yang et al., “How Listeners Weight Acoustic Cues to Intonational Phrase Boundaries”](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0102166)

## 6. For Japanese learners, “not enough lung capacity” is probably the wrong diagnosis

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
日本語話者が英語で途中停止しやすいと、“English needs longer breath, so you need more lung capacity”と言いたくなる。But second-language research points toward a more linguistic problem: **the cognitive load of building what comes next shows up in pause location and duration.**

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A 2023 study compared ten L1 English speakers with ten Mandarin-L1 speakers using English. 第二言語話者では、lower prosodic boundaries, syntactically complex phrases, and phrases containing less frequent words were associated with longer pauses. The authors interpret the pattern as involving L2 syntax, lexicon, and prosodic knowledge and processing.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Then a 2026 study using spontaneous English from Japanese learners and native speakers found that **syntactic distance between adjacent words predicted pause location and duration** more effectively than simpler boundary counts. 著者らは、not every pause harms fluency: strategically placed pauses can structure discourse and improve comprehensibility.

<!-- level:2 role:qualification -->
A 2025 study also reports that Japanese and American-English listeners do not necessarily weight prosodic-boundary cues in the same way. ただし、this does not license “Japanese people speak like X.” Proficiency, individual differences, task type, exposure, and small samples matter. Being an L1 Japanese speaker is not a respiratory destiny.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The practical target should therefore not be “zero pauses.” Reduce stops **inside tightly connected words and phrases**, and move them toward stronger structural boundaries. 流暢さはno-pause speechではない。It is closer to putting pauses where the listener can use them to predict structure.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So breath practice for Japanese learners shifts from “store more air” to “pre-build the English phrase and preselect a refueling point.” **What looked like respiratory training is also speech-planning training.**

**Key sources**: [“Pausing behavior of late bilingual and monolingual English speakers”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e21322) ／ [Coulange & de Jong, “Fluency assessment: Incorporating syntactic distance in a new measure of pause location”](https://doi.org/10.1075/jslp.25042.cou) ／ [Mizuguchi & Tateishi, “Prosodic boundary processing is language-dependent”](https://doi.org/10.3765/plsa.v10i1.5884)

## 7. Cut the same sentence four ways, and bad breathing becomes visible

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Theory is easier to feel when one sentence is cut four different ways. 例文は`After the meeting, I’ll call Sarah and send the revised file before I go home.`。“会議の後、サラに電話して、家に帰る前に修正版のファイルを送る”という一文である。

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
First, the emergency-breath version: `After the × meeting, I’ll call × Sarah and send the revised × file before I go × home.` 前置詞と名詞、動詞と目的語、修飾語と名詞などのstrong connectionsが途中で裂ける。The grammar may remain recoverable, but the listener receives segmentation based on the speaker’s oxygen crisis, not the sentence’s structure.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Second, the no-break version: `After the meeting, I’ll call Sarah and send the revised file before I go home.` If you can deliver it comfortably, it is not wrong. ただし学習段階では、air supply and speech planning must both stay stable to the end, and speakers may speed up or weaken the final words. **Not stopping is not proof of fluency.**

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Third, chunk by hierarchy: `After the meeting, // I’ll call Sarah / and send the revised file // before I go home.` At `/`, signal a light boundary; at `//`, inhale if needed. これなら一文をmanageable meaning unitsへ分けつつ、verb-objectのような密接な結び付きを保てる。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Fourth, move the boundary with information structure. If you want to package “call Sarah + send the file” as one task block, `After the meeting, / I’ll call Sarah and send the revised file // before I go home.` is also possible. 正解は一本ではない。**Prosodic boundaries are constrained by grammar but shaped by information structure and speaker intention.**
