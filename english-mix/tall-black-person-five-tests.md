# Is 「あの背の高い黒人ね」 a Problem?
## 日常会話を5つのテストにかける

「ああ、あの背の高い黒人ね」

This is where the whole series started.

2本調べて、ようやくこの一文へ戻る。

First, the direct answer.

**This sentence alone is not enough to prove racism or to define the speaker as a racist person.**

「黒人」という語自体は、常に避けるべきslurではない。

People also use visible features to identify someone in everyday conversation.

But there is another mistake on the opposite side.

**“The word itself is not a slur, so there is no problem at all.”**

それも早い。

The useful question is not only “Was the word used?”

It is:

**What is race doing in this sentence?**

そこで5つのtestを使う。

---

## Test 1: Relevance――その情報は必要か

Ask the simplest question first.

**Would the conversation still work without mentioning race?**

20人くらいの会場で、名前を知らない一人を急いで探している。

If that person is the only Black person and there are few other shared clues, race may genuinely help identification.

その場合、「背の高い黒人の方」はreferential informationとして機能する。

But if this is enough:

「ああ、入口で受付をしていた背の高い人ね」

then race may be unnecessary.

AP’s race guidance encourages careful, precise use of racial labels and warns against broad labeling.

だから実践的には、

**“Can I say race?”より“Do I need race here?”**

と聞くほうが役に立つ。

---

## Test 2: Accuracy――そのlabelは本当に識別を正確にするか

Race can feel like a fast visual shortcut.

でも、fast does not always mean accurate.

Racial categories are not objective skin-color measurements. They combine appearance, ancestry, social classification, and sometimes self-identification.

A 2009 study on televised suspect descriptions found that, under some conditions, specific skin-tone descriptions helped viewers identify the correct person better than broad racial labels. Racial labels also sometimes pulled attention away from other details such as age.

これはcrime-reportingの研究なので、everyday conversationへそのまま一般化はできない。

Still, it challenges one assumption:

**“Race is always the clearest visual description.”**

Sometimes these details are more useful:

- tall
- red jacket
- near the entrance
- working at reception

「黒人」という一語より、具体的な複数情報のほうが正確なことがある。

---

## Test 3: Symmetry――別の人にも同じ基準を使うか

This test is very simple and surprisingly useful.

Imagine a Japanese-majority workplace.

黒人の同僚だけ「あの黒人の人」と呼ぶ。

Japanese coworkers are never called「あの日本人の人」.

Then race may be doing more than identification.

**One person becomes “racially marked,” while others become the unmarked default.**

ただし、perfect symmetryを求めるテストではない。

If 19 of 20 people are Japanese, saying “the Japanese person” is useless while “the Black person” may identify someone quickly.

So context changes informational value.

それでも自分へ問い返せる。

**Who gets described as having a race, and who gets treated as simply “a person”?**

この問いは、自分のdefaultを見つけるために使える。

---

## Test 4: Identity & context――本人の自己認識と場面に合うか

How someone looks and how someone identifies are not always the same thing.

APA recommends using people’s own identity terms when possible and warns that Black and African American are not universal synonyms.

名前が分かるなら、name is usually the most specific identifier.

If nationality or region is relevant and known, that may be more precise in some contexts.

一方、本人がBlackとして受けたracial discriminationについて話しているなら、race is directly relevant.

The point is simple:

**Do not turn an externally assigned visual label into the person’s entire identity.**

見た目から分かると思ったカテゴリーと、本人がその場で名乗るidentityを分ける。

---

## Test 5: Inference――その属性から何を勝手に足したか

Now the most important test.

「あの背の高い黒人ね」

may sometimes be explained as identification.

But then imagine:

「黒人だから運動神経がよさそう」

“Black, so probably likes this kind of music.”

「黒人だから怖そう」

Now the sentence is doing something different.

It moves from description to **stereotypical inference**.

AABA rejects the idea that socially defined racial groups map neatly onto biological traits or fixed human capacities.

だから、人物を見つけるためのdescriptorと、能力・性格を決めつけるstereotypeは分ける。

The key question is:

**What did you add after noticing race?**

---

## Put the sentence through all five tests――実際に判定する

### Case A: 名前を知らない人を会場で探す

There are 20 people, and this is genuinely the fastest shared clue.

→ **Not automatically discriminatory.**

ただし「赤い服で入口にいた背の高い人」など、よりspecificな手掛かりがあればそちらも使える。

### Case B: 名前を知っている同僚を、いつも「あの黒人」と呼ぶ

→ **More concerning.**

Race is no longer needed for identification, but it remains the person’s default label.

### Case C: 黒人差別について、その人の経験を説明している

→ **Race is highly relevant.**

Removing the category could make the explanation less accurate.

### Case D: 「あの黒人、足速そうだよね」

→ **The problem is the inference.**

人種カテゴリーから能力を推論している。

Same word, different function.

That is why a simple banned-word list cannot solve the problem.

---

## Better alternatives――人種が不要なら、情報を具体化する

If race is not needed, drop it.

- 「ああ、あの背の高い人ね」
- 「入口にいた背の高い人ね」
- 「赤いジャケットを着ていた人ね」
- If you know the name, use the name.

「黒人」を「黒人の方」に変えれば全部解決、でもない。

The honorific wording makes the sentence more polite.

But it does not answer the deeper question:

**Why was race selected as the identifier?**

丁寧語だけでは、category choiceは変わらない。

---

## Final answer――safe / unsafeより5つの問い

Before this research, I wanted a verdict.

「黒人」はセーフか。

Is 「あの背の高い黒人ね」アウトか。

The research produced something more useful than a yes/no rule.

1. **Relevance**――なぜ今、人種が必要か
2. **Accuracy**――そのlabelは本当に正確か
3. **Symmetry**――一部の人だけを“racially marked”にしていないか
4. **Identity & context**――本人と場面に合うか
5. **Inference**――raceから性格・能力を推論していないか

If the sentence survives these checks, mentioning race does not need to be treated as automatically forbidden.

逆に、辞書上は中立なwordでも、この5つで引っかかるなら言い方を考え直せる。

And one final point:

**Do not condemn a whole person from one sentence. Do not stop thinking just because there was no bad intent.**

一文を人格判定に使わず、でも「なぜその特徴を目印にしたのか」は考える。

I started by researching one Japanese word.

最後に残ったのは、もっと自分側の問いだった。

**When I look at another person, what do I choose to notice first?**
