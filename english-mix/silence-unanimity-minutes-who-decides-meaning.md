# 「異論なし」は、誰の賛成なのか
### When Silence Becomes Agreement

Five minutes before a meeting ends, someone asks, **“Can we move forward with this plan?”** Nobody answers. 一人は次の予定を見ていて、別の人は飲みかけのお茶を片付ける。The chair says, “All right, let's proceed.” The minutes later read: **“Everyone agreed.”**

This is an imagined meeting, not an observed case. だが、そこには小さな論理の飛躍がある。**No one voiced an objection** is something we could observe. **Everyone genuinely supported the proposal** is a claim about people's minds. The first does not automatically establish the second.

We began with the philosophical question, 「意味とは何か」. But perhaps the more urgent question is: **Who gets to decide what an event meant?** そして、その解釈によって他人の行動まで決まるとき、何を根拠とすべきなのだろう。

## 1. 「意味」の三つの問いを、いったん分ける

The word “meaning” bundles together several questions. What does a sentence mean? What did its speaker intend? And what social consequences does it have? 「異論なし」という文の意味、進行役の意図、会議として成立した決定は、重なっても一致しない。

Philosopher **ハーバート・ポール・グライス（Herbert Paul Grice）** distinguished conventional sentence meaning from **speaker meaning（話し手が伝えようとする意味）**. “It's getting late” can state a fact about time and also suggest that the meeting should end. That is a problem of **pragmatics（語用論）**—how meaning works in context. Grice's account has been revised and debated; it does not settle all questions about meaning ([Grice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/grice/), [Pragmatics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatics/)).

Even actual words can mean more than they literally say. 沈黙ならなおさら、誰も発していない「賛成」という言葉を勝手に補うことはできない。Yet silence can have a procedural effect when a group has agreed beforehand: **“If there are no objections, the motion passes.”** The crucial distinction is **procedural approval versus personal conviction**. 手続に従って決まったことと、各人が心から賛成したことは別だ。

## 2. 人が黙る理由は、賛成だけではない

Silence may look like the absence of information. In organization studies, the absence itself has become a research subject. **エリザベス・ウォルフ・モリソン（Elizabeth Wolfe Morrison）** and **フランシス・ミリケン（Frances J. Milliken）** developed a model of **organizational silence（組織的沈黙）** in 2000: circumstances can make employees withhold concerns because speaking up seems unwise. This is a theoretical account, **not evidence that every quiet meeting hides opposition** ([original paper](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amr.2000.3707697)).

In 1999, **エイミー・エドモンドソン（Amy Edmondson）** studied 51 teams at a manufacturing company. Her research connected **psychological safety（心理的安全性）**—a shared sense that interpersonal risk-taking is safe—with learning behavior. これも「沈黙した人は反対だった」という証明ではない。It shows why the conditions for speaking up deserve attention when interpreting silence ([study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2307/2666999)).

The same thirty seconds can have different explanations: agreement, confusion, too little time, no desire to argue, or fear that speaking up will change nothing. 見た目が似ているからこそ、一つの解釈で片付けると重要な違いが消えてしまう。**Silence is observable. The reason for silence usually is not.**

## 3. 議事録は出来事を保存し、同時に削っている

Meeting minutes are not a perfect copy of a meeting. They are a selective record designed to support action after the meeting. **Useful summaries compress; compression leaves things out.** 省略があるから実用的になるが、その省略が重要な区別を消す場合もある。

Consider three versions of the same imaginary meeting. これは実験参加者を集めた調査ではなく、記録が何を主張するかを比較する思考実験である。

**Record A: “Everyone unanimously supported the plan.”** 沈黙しか確認していないなら、これは強すぎる記述だ。It asserts a state of mind that was never checked.

**Record B: “No objections were voiced. The chair declared that the plan would proceed.”** This separates observation from a decision. ただし、その宣言に正式な効力があるかは会議のルール次第である。

**Record C: “No objections were voiced. Individual positions were not confirmed. The chair declared that the plan would proceed.”** 少し長いが、**what remains unknown（未確認事項）**を残している。

The difference is not merely stylistic. **It changes what future readers can verify.** あとから経緯を調べる人は、記録された結論だけでなく、その根拠の限界まで知る必要がある。

## 4. 説明が滑らかなほど、分かっていないことは隠れやすい

A smooth explanation is easy to repeat. It is not always easy to test. **レオニード・ローゼンブリット（Leonid Rozenblit）** and **フランク・カイル（Frank Keil）** studied the **illusion of explanatory depth（説明の深さについての錯覚）** in 2002. People can feel that they understand a mechanism better than they can actually explain it. Their experiments focused on causal explanations, including how ordinary devices work. They did **not** directly test the meaning of meeting minutes ([original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3062901/)).

Still, the comparison is useful. “Everyone agreed” feels like a complete account. But ask one follow-up: **“Did you check each person's position?”** If the answer is no, part of the account turns out to be an inference, not a recorded fact.

Philosophers use **underdetermination（証拠による決定の不十分さ）** for situations in which available evidence does not uniquely settle a theory. これは「どの解釈も同じように正しい」という主張ではない。It means that we may need more evidence to choose between competing explanations ([overview](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-underdetermination/)).

A responsible summary should tell us not only **what we concluded**, but also **what would make us reconsider**. そうして初めて、次に読む人も判断に参加できる。

## 5. 反論：それでも、いつかは決めなければならない

A strong objection is obvious: **organizations cannot wait until every private conviction is known**. 手続に従って議決したり、一定時間異議がなければ進めたりすることには合理性がある。We can respect a procedural decision without claiming to know everyone's inner thoughts.

There is another limit to this essay. Some meanings are personal. ある詩に救われたという経験は、会議の承認記録と同じ方法で検証する必要はない。**Personal significance is not the same question as institutional consent.** The point is narrower: when an interpretation affects other people's duties, resources, or choices, its evidential basis deserves scrutiny.

So the question shifts from **“Does this event have meaning?”** to **“What are we entitled to do on the basis of that meaning?”** 自分だけの感慨と、他人の行動を拘束する決定とでは、求められる説明の厳密さが違う。

## 6. 「分かったこと」だけでなく「まだ分からないこと」を残す

Try distinguishing four fields when a decision matters: **Observation（観察）**, **Interpretation（解釈）**, **Decision（決定）**, and **Not yet verified（未確認）**. これは本稿で提案する整理法であり、その効果が実証された標準的な記録方式ではない。

An example: **Observation:** no objections were voiced. **Interpretation:** the chair took the silence as absence of opposition. **Decision:** the chair announced adoption of the proposal. **Not yet verified:** each member's personal position. こう記せば、後から読む人は、議事録に何が書かれ、何はまだ確認できていないかを区別できる。

Not every meeting needs this much detail. 何を記録するかは、決定の重大さと、後で修正できる余地によって変えるべきだ。The point is not to create endless paperwork. It is to preserve the distinctions that later decisions may depend on.

## 7. 意味を与える力と、意味を取り消せる余地

The lights go off in the meeting room. Everyone leaves with different thoughts. One sentence remains in the minutes, and that sentence may guide work for months. 会議にいた人の胸中は複雑だったとしても、後任者の手元には短い文章しか残らないことがある。

Perhaps **understanding meaning is not just finding a convincing interpretation**. At least when our interpretation affects other people, it also requires seeing where the evidence ends. 言葉が持つ力は、相手を動かすことだけではない。その意味を記録として残し、後から来た人にも作用させることにある。

**A good record does not only preserve a conclusion. It preserves a way to question the conclusion.** 私たちが「意味を確定した」と思うとき、最後に確かめたいのは、その解釈を別の誰かが点検し、必要なら訂正できるかどうかなのである。

## 調査の足跡――守・破・離

**守 — Rebuild the concepts.** 言葉の通常の意味、話し手の意図、会議の決定としての効力を切り分け、グライスの研究を確認した。

**破 — Challenge the easy stories.** “Silence always means agreement” is too strong. “Silence never matters” is also too strong. 手続の効力と内心の賛否を区別し、組織的沈黙と心理的安全性の研究によって反例を検討した。

**再調査 — Test the limits.** Organizational silence is a theoretical model; psychological safety research does not prove the motives of any individual silent participant. 研究が示したことと、架空の会議を用いたこちらの推論を混同しない。

**離 — Change the question.** We moved from “What is meaning?” to “Who can establish, audit, and revise an interpretation that affects others?” 後半の問いは、本稿の議論から導いた新しい検討課題であり、先行研究がそのまま実証した結論ではない。

Next, the proposal should be tested in actual work: compare ordinary minutes with minutes separating observations, interpretations, decisions, and unknowns. 後任者が経緯を正確に再構成できるか、再確認に要する手間がどう変わるかを調べる必要がある。
