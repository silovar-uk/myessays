# Duck Hunt vs Villager: do not stop using projectiles

## Pocket is not a ban on zoning. It is a timing commitment you can make Villager spend

Villager can put Duck Hunt's Can in a pocket.

This is, annoyingly, what one is normally supposed to do with litter.

But for Duck Hunt, that Can is not rubbish.

It is a coworker.

And suddenly your coworker has changed companies.

Worse, Pocketed projectiles are returned with a **1.9x damage multiplier** according to Ultimate Frame Data.

[Ultimate Frame Data: Stats](https://ultimateframedata.com/stats)

So the first instinct is obvious:

**Maybe I should stop using projectiles.**

That instinct is understandable.

It is also how Duck Hunt voluntarily becomes a much worse character.

対むらびとのテーマは、飛び道具を消すことではない。

It is learning that Pocket is not an always-on forcefield.

**Pocket is a move. Villager has to choose a time to use it.**

That choice is the opening.

---

## 1. Pocket is powerful because of its window — and limited for the same reason

Ultimate Frame Data lists Pocket as:

- invulnerable on frames 5–23
- able to capture projectiles on frames 8–23
- a 50-frame commitment when used to store
- projectile release beginning on frame 9

[Ultimate Frame Data: Villager](https://ultimateframedata.com/villager)

SmashWiki also notes that a stored projectile can be held for the rest of the stock.

[SmashWiki: Villager neutral special](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Villager_(SSBU)/Neutral_special)

That sounds terrifying.

But the same data gives us the counter-idea.

Pocket is not automatic.

Villager is not firing slingshot, moving forward, planting a tree or grabbing while the move is committed.

つまり、Pocketを使わせること自体に価値がある。

The projectile may disappear.

But Villager also spent time answering it.

The practical question becomes:

**What are you doing while Villager is answering the Can?**

If the answer is “watching the Can get pocketed,” you lost twice.

If the answer is “walking forward, changing angle, preparing grab or placing the next delayed threat,” the interaction is not over.

---

## 2. The worst Can is the one that arrives at the same time every time

Can is not bad in this matchup.

Predictable Can is bad.

Spawn Can.

Ping.

Ping.

Ping.

Straight line.

Repeat.

Now Villager's problem has become beautifully simple:

“Press Pocket when Can arrives.”

Duck Hunt is supposed to manage several timelines.

Do not compress them into one metronome for the opponent.

Instead:

spawn Can and leave it.

Ping once, then wait.

Move to the opposite side.

Hit it with a normal.

Change the trajectory.

Walk toward Villager while Can remains in midfield.

Trick Shot can be manipulated by Duck Hunt's attacks, and hitting it from the opposite side can reverse the direction of its remaining shots.

[SmashWiki: Trick Shot](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Trick_Shot)

The goal is not “make Can unpocketable.”

The goal is:

**make the correct Pocket timing unclear.**

A Can that is not moving can still be doing work if Villager is staring at it.

---

## 3. Whiffed Pocket does not mean “dash in and mash”

Pocket missed.

Great.

Now do not immediately become stupid.

Villager's neutral air is frame 3.

From shield, it is a 6-frame option.

[Ultimate Frame Data: Villager](https://ultimateframedata.com/villager)

So a careless rush after Pocket can simply run into Nair.

The value of a whiffed Pocket is not that it gives permission to attack blindly.

It is that one defensive option has just been spent.

Now ask:

Will Villager Nair?

Shield?

Retreat?

Pocket again?

その次まで見る。

Then you can choose grab, Dtilt, spaced Bair, delayed Can or simply more stage.

Pocket bait is not the combo.

**Pocket bait is information.**

---

## 4. Clay is a useful way to test how Villager uses Pocket

Clay Shooting begins on frame 17 and can lead to strong confirms on hit.

[Ultimate Frame Data: Duck Hunt](https://ultimateframedata.com/duck_hunt)

Against Villager, it also has another job.

Use Clay to learn **how** the opponent Pockets.

Do they Pocket on reaction?

At a fixed distance?

Before the projectile is even dangerous?

Do they stop Pocketing when you walk forward?

Try:

Clay.

Watch.

Next time Clay, then step in.

Next time no Clay, same movement.

Next time delay.

The question is not “Can I trick them once?”

It is:

**Is their Pocket reactive, habitual or predictive?**

Reactive Pocket loses to timing variation.

Habitual Pocket can be whiff-punished.

Predictive Pocket can be made to protect against a projectile that never comes.

Clay becomes less a bullet and more a diagnostic tool.

---

## 5. Wild Gunman matters even when he never fires

Wild Gunman has 5 HP and can be interrupted by attacks before firing.

[SmashWiki: Wild Gunman](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Wild_Gunman)

That sounds like a weakness.

Against Villager, it can become useful structure.

Villager's Fair and Bair slingshots are projectiles; Fair begins frame 10, Bair frame 13.

[Ultimate Frame Data: Villager](https://ultimateframedata.com/villager)

Put a Gunman between you and Villager.

A pellet hits him.

He loses his career.

Sad.

But the pellet did not reach you.

So even a dead Gunman may have functioned as:

**one extra object Villager had to process before reaching Duck Hunt.**

This is important because Villager wants the screen to become simple:

slingshot lane,

Lloid lane,

Pocket response.

Duck Hunt should make it messy again.

Gunman is not only a delayed bullet.

Sometimes he is just a small pixelated piece of furniture blocking the hallway.

---

## 6. Do not try to “win the projectile war.” Break its shape

Villager is built for long-distance control.

Slingshots.

Lloid Rocket.

Tree.

Pocket.

Lloid itself does not become active until frame 52.

[Ultimate Frame Data: Villager](https://ultimateframedata.com/villager)

That means Villager is spending current time to create a future obstacle.

If every Lloid makes you retreat, the match can quietly become:

Lloid.

Back up.

Slingshot.

Back up.

Tree.

Back up.

Suddenly you are at ledge wondering how Animal Crossing became a military occupation.

Instead, when Lloid begins:

check whether you can take centre.

Place Can at a different height.

Summon Gunman.

Move before the rocket owns the lane.

**Projectile advantage is not projectile count. It is stage ownership.**

---

## 7. The tree is not a mandatory boss fight

Timber creates an area where Villager becomes much more dangerous.

So Duck Hunt players may feel compelled to destroy it.

Can.

Clay.

Gunman.

Dog versus forestry.

But you do not get points for defeating the tree.

If the tree controls one area, move the problem elsewhere.

Put Can on the other side.

Pull Villager away from it.

Restart neutral outside its strongest range.

設置キャラ同士の大事な原則：

**You do not need to solve every object the opponent places.**

Sometimes “do not stand there” is the correct counterplay.

---

## 8. The real nightmare is often offstage, not Pocket

Villager's forward smash drops a bowling ball below the ledge and is specifically strong against low recoveries.

[SmashWiki: Villager (SSBU)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Villager_(SSBU))

Villager also has slingshots, Dair and a long recovery that lets them chase deeply.

Duck Hunt's recovery has no attacking hitbox during its initial rise.

So the worst pattern is predictable low recovery.

Do not give Villager one clean script.

Save jump.

Change height.

Use Can while recovering.

Duck Jump can be cancelled into attack or air dodge from frame 51 onward, and Can can still be controlled during the move.

[Ultimate Frame Data: Duck Hunt](https://ultimateframedata.com/duck_hunt)

対むらびとでは、Canはneutral toolだけではない。

It is part of your emergency exit plan.

---

## 9. When Villager recovers, trap the destination instead of chasing forever

Balloon Trip travels far.

That means “I sent Villager offstage” does not automatically equal a kill.

Duck Hunt can lose more by overchasing than by simply rebuilding a ledge trap.

So rather than racing Villager toward the blast zone:

place Can near ledge.

Put Gunman on a future line.

Watch high return with Bair.

Cover the final destination.

**The goal is not to hit Villager at maximum distance.**

The goal is to decide where Villager is allowed to come back.

Long recovery gives Villager distance.

It also gives Duck Hunt time to prepare the landing zone.

---

## 10. Shield is useful, but Villager's Nair changes how close you should stand

Villager's grab is slow: standing grab frame 14, shield grab 18.

But Nair is frame 3.

[Ultimate Frame Data: Villager](https://ultimateframedata.com/villager)

So when projectile pressure finally gets you close, do not treat Villager's shield like a free invitation.

Use grab.

Dtilt tip.

Bair spacing.

Empty movement.

The matchup is not:

“Projectiles failed, now use A moves.”

It is:

**use projectiles to create the right distance for the normals you actually want.**

## 12. A 10-minute Villager drill

### 2 min — stop auto-pinging Can

Spawn.

Ping once.

Wait.

Walk.

Hit it with a normal.

Break the rhythm.

### 2 min — Clay, then no follow-up

Throw Clay.

Instead of forcing a confirm, move forward one step.

Train the idea that projectile and body can have separate jobs.

### 2 min — Gunman as cover

Stand behind Gunman.

Move while imagining a slingshot line.

### 2 min — recovery variation

Practice Can during Duck Jump.

Use high and low timings.

Add the frame-51 cancel options.

### 2 min — ledge reset

Pretend Villager survives offstage.

Build the next ledge trap instead of chasing forever.

The skill is not “never get Pocketed.”

It is:

**still having a plan after Pocket happens.**

---

## 13. Before the research, Pocket looked like a projectile ban. After it, it looks like a timing tax

Pocket is strong.

It steals the projectile.

It can return it harder.

But if you stop throwing anything because Pocket exists, Villager has won an interaction without pressing the button.

That is the wrong trade.

Pocket requires timing.

Commitment.

Position.

So the matchup is not really projectile versus anti-projectile.

It is:

**who decides when the projectile becomes important?**

If Can arrives exactly when Villager wants to Pocket it, Villager owns the timing.

If you show Can when you want Villager to spend Pocket, then move the dog during that commitment, Duck Hunt owns more of the sequence.

Villager may temporarily own the Can.

Do not also hand over ownership of the match.

---

## Match checklist

- Do not delete your own B moves because Pocket exists
- Do not send Can on the same rhythm
- After whiffed Pocket, respect frame-3 Nair
- Gunman can be useful before he fires
- When Lloid starts, ask whether you can take centre
- Do not make recovery a single low route
- Against Balloon Trip, trap the destination instead of overchasing
