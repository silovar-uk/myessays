# 球場が休みの日、who gets paid?

*104 keywords for sports-led urban development：球場・不動産・公共政策の見取り図*

## 野球をしない日の方が多い球場で、家賃を数えてみた

An odd discovery: in a baseball company's financial statement, the property business reports a larger number than baseball itself. 野球で勝つ会社が、rentでも戦っている。Is the ballpark secretly a landlord business?

Atlanta Braves Holdingsの2025年決算では、baseball segmentのAdjusted OIBDAが$51.104 million、mixed-use developmentが$68.527 million。不動産事業の売上は$97.432 millionで、primarily rental income。This is not a ranking of business quality; it is evidence that **one sports organization can operate two different engines of value**。[決算原文](https://www.bravesholdings.com/news/press-releases/detail/215/atlanta-braves-holdings-reports-fourth-quarter-and-year-end)

But stop before declaring “real estate beats baseball.” Adjusted OIBDA is a company-defined non-GAAP measure, not return on investment. The group also reported a GAAP operating loss in 2025. Bigger segment earnings do **not** prove better capital efficiency. Three separate questions: revenue, operating performance, and investment return. [Financial notes](https://www.bravesholdings.com/news/press-releases/detail/215/atlanta-braves-holdings-reports-fourth-quarter-and-year-end)

## ① 何を開発するのか――施設と地区を混ぜない

First distinguish the **unit of analysis**. Stadium and arena are venues; a stadium district includes neighbouring homes, shops and offices. 「球場が好調」と「地区が好調」はnot the same observation. The boundary defines what must be measured.

Japan's Sports Agency defines スポーツコンプレックス more broadly than a shopping mall beside a stadium: it can connect venues for both spectators and participants, public spaces, and transport. By contrast, the real-estate term stadium district focuses more on a sports-anchored mixed-use ecosystem. Related ideas, different scopes. [Sports Agency](https://www.mext.go.jp/sports/b_menu/sports/mcatetop02/list/jsa_00138.html) / [JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/the-stadium-district-boom)

An **anchor facility** attracts visitors. It does not, by itself, confer ownership of the nearby café. That gap matters: the power to create foot traffic is different from the legal right to collect the money it generates.

## ② どんな街にするのか――図面には時間が描かれている

Next comes **urban planning**. Mixed-use development combines residential, retail, work and hospitality. A master plan coordinates land use, pedestrian routes, transit and development phases. 建物一棟のdesign briefではなく、街全体のoperating hypothesisでもある。

At Hong Kong's 啓徳体育園（Kai Tak Sports Park）, a 28-hectare former airport site links a 50,000-seat stadium, a 10,000-seat arena, community facilities and public space. Populous highlights the pedestrian spine from Station Square to the harbourfront. It is not merely a venue cluster; **movement itself is designed**. [Populous](https://populous.com/showcases/kai-tak-sports-park)

Phased development means building in stages as demand and funding become clearer. All-at-once construction offers immediate scale but concentrates risk; phased delivery preserves options while creating temporary gaps. A master plan is therefore also a **map of time and uncertainty**.

## ③ なぜ価値が生まれるのか――「年間365日」は呪文ではない

Does adding a hotel magically create activity for 365 days? No. **Event demand** spikes around games and concerts; **everyday demand** comes from residents, workers and repeat local use. Year-round activity is the desired outcome, not a property of the blueprint.

Footfall is not spending. **Circulation** describes how people move; dwell time measures how long they stay; visitor spending measures transactions. Someone can enjoy a public square for two hours and buy nothing. That can be socially valuable without being retail revenue.

JLL's research treats stadium districts as a long-term mixed-use investment theme, with potential rental premiums. Yet a market observation is not a guarantee for every proposed site. **Value creation** asks whether people prefer the place; **value capture** asks who receives the extra rent. [JLL 2025](https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/the-stadium-district-boom) / [JLL 2026](https://www.jll.com/en-ca/guides/why-stadium-districts-are-winning-at-mixed-use)

## ④ お金はどこから入るのか――七つの財布を開ける

Seven possible wallets: tickets, sponsorship/naming rights, food and merchandise, rent, hotels, venue hire, and property disposals. But beware **double counting**: a tenant's total retail sales are not the landlord's rental revenue. 同じ100万円でも、お金の受取人が違う。

Finance vocabulary serves different jobs. Capex is upfront expenditure; operating expenses recur. NOI describes property operating income under stated definitions. EBITDA and Adjusted OIBDA track operating performance, while NPV and IRR evaluate future cash flows and the time value of money. Never compare their numbers as if they were one metric.

A capitalization rate relates a property's operating income to value; project finance relies heavily on the project's future cash flows. This reveals what our Braves comparison cannot tell us: invested capital, debt, maintenance capex, land basis, and contract duration. Good analysis starts by naming the **missing denominator**.

## ⑤ 誰が決めて誰が受け取るのか――「人を集めた人」がもらえるとは限らない

A developer, sports club, facility operator, landowner, public authority and architect can all be different parties. In October 2026 Populous was appointed to explore London Stadium's wider campus opportunities. **Designing the plan does not imply owning the land or collecting its rent.** [Announcement](https://populous.com/article/populous-to-create-development-vision-for-london-stadium-campus)

Governance terms need precision. PPP is an umbrella for public–private cooperation; PFI and concession involve more specific legal and contractual structures. Japan's designated-manager system is yet another mechanism for managing public facilities. **Similar purpose does not mean identical rights.**

Seoul's March 2026 Jamsil announcement proposed privately financed sports-and-MICE development and a mechanism to reinvest part of future proceeds in citywide development. Yet the city also listed further approvals before final implementation. Agreement, construction, and realized redistribution are three separate stages. [Seoul city release](https://english.seoul.go.kr/public-private-partnership-agreement-reached-for-jamsil-sports-mice-park-project-targeted-for-completion-in-2032/)

The critical documents are the **revenue-sharing agreement** and the **risk-allocation schedule**. Who receives rent growth? Who pays when construction overruns? Who absorbs a cancelled-event loss? Emotional attachment to a team does not answer contractual questions.

## ⑥ つくった後に何をするのか――運営の主役は水曜日かもしれない

Once construction ends, the question becomes operational. Venue multi-use adds different events to the same building; **area management** coordinates streets, squares, programming, cleaning and tenants. The overlooked hero may be an ordinary rainy Wednesday at 10 a.m.

Placemaking is about enabling meaningful use, not merely installing benches. Tenant mix decides which needs can coexist. If every shop targets only ticket holders, the district has invented a very expensive off-season.

Quintain reported 6,000 completed homes at Wembley Park by September 2025. That is not “6,000 more football fans.” It means the district now contains ordinary daily lives alongside extraordinary event crowds. [Quintain](https://www.quintain.co.uk/news-and-media/press-releases/2025/solarlaunch)

## ⑦ 街にとって良いのか――賑わいと公共の得は別会計

More activity does not automatically mean more public welfare. **Opportunity cost** is the value of the best alternative use of scarce land or money. A public stadium project competes, conceptually, with transit, schools, housing and parks for resources.

Bradbury, Coates and Humphreys reviewed more than 130 studies and found generally limited metropolitan economic impacts from professional teams and venues. That does not mean every project fails locally. It means **local sales growth is not necessarily net citywide growth**. [Academic review](https://doi.org/10.1111/joes.12533)

If a fan eats at the new district instead of an existing local restaurant, spending has shifted, not necessarily increased. Add possible noise, congestion and housing pressure. Social evaluation must include **who benefits, who pays, and who is displaced**.

## ⑧ どう測るのか――「来場者100万人」の正体を調べる

Metrics need definitions. One million visits may count repeat visitors. Event days are not identical to operating days. A high non-event-day share may signal daily use, but says little about spending per visit. Occupancy is not proof of healthy rents or tenants.

The two strongest evaluation words are **additionality** and **counterfactual**. Additionality asks what happened because of the project. The counterfactual estimates what would have happened without it. Before/after comparisons cannot automatically distinguish a project effect from wider tourism or population growth.

Cost–benefit analysis and economic-impact modelling answer different questions: net social welfare versus modelled spending flows. Private underwriting must stress-test cost overruns, interest rates, refinancing and demand. Never let “success” remain an undefined label.

## やりすぎ検証｜試合がゼロの日だけで商店街を成立させられるか

A deliberately excessive thought experiment—not a forecast for any actual district. Assume 300 non-game days, 1,000 visitors per day, and ¥1,500 spending per visitor: **¥450 million in annual gross transactions**. The arithmetic is correct. The business case is not yet.

Four missing bridges: gross spending is not the developer's rent; displaced local spending is not net new citywide demand; passersby are not necessarily caused by the project; and earnings without capital costs say nothing about ROI. The ¥450 million is useful precisely because it reveals the **four wrong inferences** one could make.

For a real study, collect time-of-day footfall, event calendars, visitor origins, sales by tenant, lease terms, operating costs and displaced spending. Build **four scorecards**: venue operator, district owner, sports club, and public/community. A single “economic impact” total obscures their divergent interests.

## 104語の索引――8つの引き出しに戻す

Now the compact taxonomy: **104 terms in eight roles**. The index keeps the Japanese concept as the reading base, while the English version shows transferable technical vocabulary. Each term has one primary home, even if it connects to several domains.

**01｜施設・地区**：スポーツ施設（スポーツベニュー）／スタジアム／アリーナ／スポーツコンプレックス／スタジアム地区／スポーツ集積地区／スポーツキャンパス／娯楽集積地区／公共空間／応援・交流区画（ファンゾーン）／集客の核となる施設。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

**02｜都市計画・開発**：複合用途開発／地区基本計画（マスタープラン）／土地利用計画／段階開発／都市再生／既利用地の再開発／未利用地の新規開発／公共交通を中心とする開発／歩きやすさ／交通アクセス性／地区内外の接続性／人工地盤。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

**03｜需要・価値創出**：イベント需要／日常需要／通年型需要／集客拠点効果／スポーツの知的財産／来訪目的地化／滞在時間／回遊性／滞在消費／地価上昇／賃料の上乗せ／価値創出／外部性。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

**04｜収益・投資・金融**：入場券収入／協賛収入／施設名称使用権（命名権）／賃料収入／宿泊施設の運営収益／入居店舗の売上／価値回収／開発利益の還元／土地価値の回収制度／初期投資額／運営費／不動産営業純利益／減価償却前利益／調整後営業利益指標／正味現在価値／内部収益率／不動産還元利回り／事業収入に依拠した融資。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

**05｜主体・所有・契約**：開発事業者／スポーツクラブ／施設運営者／土地所有者（地権者）／行政／設計者／特別目的会社／官民連携／民間資金等活用事業／公共施設運営権制度／指定管理者制度／土地賃貸借・借地権／収益分配契約／リスク分担。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

**06｜運営・利用**：施設多目的化／施設稼働日／催事誘致／会議・報奨旅行・国際会議・展示会（MICE）／上質な観戦・接遇サービス（ホスピタリティ）／地区運営／場所づくり／入居業種の構成／イベント時輸送計画／ファン体験。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

**07｜公共政策・社会価値**：スポーツ振興／地域活性化／都市競争力／スポーツ観光／大会開催後の遺産（レガシー）／社会的包摂／公共便益／公共負担／機会費用。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

**08｜成果評価・リスク**：イベント日数／非イベント日来訪比率／床面積当たり店舗売上／賃貸稼働率／総来訪者数／投資回収期間／費用便益分析／経済波及効果／追加性／反実仮想／需要リスク／建設費超過／金利・借換えリスク／域内消費の置換／騒音・混雑の外部不経済／住居費の負担可能性／地域への利益帰属。Read these as a vocabulary map, not as a claim that all concepts mean the same thing.

## 結局、球場が働くのは試合の日だけではなかった

We began with a baseball company earning significant property income. The answer is not “every club should become a landlord.” It is to examine four boundaries—venue, district, contracts, and public welfare—and ask where newly created value actually goes.

Next time you see a café next to a stadium, ask: whose success is its popularity? The tenant, landlord, team, or resident enjoying the nearby square? Sports-led urban development is not just the layout of buildings. It is the allocation of value, rights, responsibilities, and everyday life.
