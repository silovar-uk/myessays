# Everybody knows Eine kleine Nachtmusik — but one movement is missing
## From its name and KV 525 to serenade, sonata form, and the history hidden behind a famous tune

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
《アイネ・クライネ・ナハトムジーク（Eine kleine Nachtmusik）》の冒頭を聞けば、“Oh, I know this”となる人は多い。But the first thing worth knowing is not about the melody. **The four-movement version we usually hear is not the whole work Mozart wrote. One movement is missing.**

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
And that is only the beginning. 「アイネ・クライネ・ナハトムジーク」という言葉はMozart's own handwritten catalogueにあるが、modern publicationのようなformal titleだったとは言い切れない。“Serenade No. 13”という呼び方も広く定着している一方、the 2024 Köchel catalogue classifies it as a “Divertimento in G for strings (fragment).” ものすごく有名なのに、its name, genre, and surviving shape are less stable than they look.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This is not pedantic musicology. むしろ、この曲を理解する入口になる。The more famous a piece becomes, the easier it is to imagine that it has always existed in the same finished form we know from school, commercials, concerts, and recordings. しかし実物の歴史をたどると、composition, loss, publication, classification, performance, and recordingという複数の段階を経て、いま私たちが知る作品像ができている。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So this essay assumes no music-theory background. 「クラシックとは何か」「KV525のKVとは何か」「ト長調とは何か」から始め、最後にhow to hear an overfamiliar piece againまで進む。The goal is not to dumb the subject down, but to **build enough footing that the technical terms become usable.**

## 1. “Classical music” and the “Classical era” are not the same thing

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
まず、《Eine kleine Nachtmusik》を“a classical masterpiece”と呼ぶとき、the word “classical” is doing two different jobs.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
日常語の“classical music”は、Bach, Mozart, Chopin and much moreを含む、西洋の芸術音楽をかなり広くまとめた呼び方である。一方、音楽史の“Classical era”はもっと狭い。It usually points to the later eighteenth-century style associated with composers such as Joseph Haydn, Mozart, and the early Ludwig van Beethoven. 《Eine kleine Nachtmusik》was written in 1787, right in that historical world.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The key point is not to hear “Classical” as “old and stiff.” 当時の器楽曲には、symphonies and concertos for formal venuesだけでなく、宴席、祝宴、屋外、社交の場で使われるentertainment musicも多かった。If we only meet these works later inside silent concert halls, we lose the sense that much of this music once belonged to social life.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So to understand KV525, “a masterpiece by a genius” is only one entrance. もう一つ必要なのは、**multi-movement instrumental music that eighteenth-century listeners could encounter as evening or social entertainment**という入口である。That is where the words “serenade” and “divertimento” begin to matter.

## 2. “Eine kleine Nachtmusik” is Mozart's wording, but not quite a modern formal title

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
ドイツ語のEine kleine Nachtmusikをかなり直訳に近く分けると、eine is “a/one,” kleine is “little,” Nacht is “night,” and Musik is “music.” つまり、“a little night music”ほどの意味になる。

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
What matters is that Mozart really did write those words. 彼は1784年から亡くなる1791年まで、自分の新作をa handwritten thematic catalogueへ記録した。The British Library manuscript records dates and descriptions on one side and musical openings on the facing page. 1787年8月10日の項目に《Eine kleine Nacht Musick》がある。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
But the surviving autograph score itself has no title, according to the New Mozart Edition. 現在のKöchel catalogueも、“Serenade”というgenre designationはMozartではなくthe first printed editionから来たものだと説明する。The Morgan Library likewise treats “A little night music” as descriptive wording from Mozart's catalogue rather than a modern-style formal title.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So it is slightly misleading to imagine Mozart writing “EINE KLEINE NACHTMUSIK” across a title page. 本人の言葉ではある。But it is closer to “a little night music, consisting of...” in his own record of works. 後世がそのdescriptive phraseを固有名詞として固定し、いまでは誰もが知る作品名になった。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Famous titles often look inevitable in hindsight. ここでは、**words used for record-keeping grew into the name that now represents the work itself.** 作品史では、what the composer wrote and what later publishers and cataloguers stabilized as a title should be separated.

## 3. The “525” in KV 525 does not simply mean “Mozart's 525th finished work”

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
作品名の後ろに付く“KV 525”も、初心者には突然現れるcodeである。KV stands for Köchel-Verzeichnis, the Köchel catalogue used to identify Mozart's works.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
19世紀の研究者ルートヴィヒ・フォン・ケッヘル（Ludwig von Köchel）が、Mozart's worksを年代順に整理したcatalogueを1862年に刊行した。The first edition numbered 626 works. その後1905年、1937年、1964年などに研究成果を取り込みながら改訂され、2024年には60年ぶりのmajor revisionが公表された。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So KV525 is not the same thing as an “opus number” and it is not a year. 「モーツァルト研究上、この作品を一意に指すaddress」に近い。Because changing date estimates had made older catalogue numbering increasingly complicated, the 2024 edition also returned authentic works, in principle, to the earliest main-catalogue number they had received.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
When a number appears after a classical work, ask first: who assigned it, and why? **KV525 is a research and identification key, not part of the music itself.** この区別だけでも、title, catalogue number, and composition dateを混同しにくくなる。

## 4. The four movements we hear are the surviving shape of an original five-movement work

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
この曲でもっとも意外な事実は、the familiar four-movement sequence is not how Mozart listed the whole work.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In his 1787 catalogue, Mozart described five movements: Allegro; Minuet and Trio; Romance; Minuet and Trio; Finale. ところが現存する自筆譜と今日の演奏で確認できるのは、第1楽章Allegro、第3楽章Romance、第4楽章Menuetto、第5楽章Rondoのfour movements。The first Minuet and Trio, originally movement two, is lost.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The current Köchel catalogue even reconstructs a likely physical reason from the manuscript paper. モーツァルトはslow movementの初稿だったハ長調Larghetto KV525aのleafを自筆譜から破り取った。That action also loosened the other half of the folded sheet carrying the first Minuet and Trio, which was later lost. It may have disappeared during Mozart's lifetime.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
“Unfinished” can mean two very different things. 作曲家が最後まで書かなかった場合もあれば、the complete historical object no longer survivesの場合もある。Here the secure point is that **the transmitted materials no longer preserve all five movements.** The current Köchel catalogue classifies the work as a fragment, but that should not be simplified into “Mozart never finished writing all five movements.” Incomplete composition and incomplete transmission need to be kept separate.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
And that creates a strange reversal. 世界中の人がfour movementsを“the work”として覚えたことで、the incomplete surviving form became culturally complete. If an unquestionably authentic missing movement appeared tomorrow, the work would be “restored,” yet to our ears it might feel as if someone had added a new piece.

## 5. “Serenade No. 13” is a useful label, but the genre is less settled than the label suggests

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
《Eine kleine Nachtmusik》is commonly introduced as “Serenade No. 13.” しかし、ここでのserenadeは、単に“quiet night piece”という意味ではない。

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
In the eighteenth century, serenades could be multi-movement instrumental works connected with evening performance, celebrations, private occasions, or university events. モーツァルトの故郷Salzburgでは、outdoor orchestral serenades and cassationsが盛んで、four movementsより多く、複数のminuetsを含む作品も珍しくなかった。一方、divertimento was a label often associated with lighter entertainment music on a smaller scale, and these historical genre boundaries were not rigid.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The 2024 Köchel catalogue calls KV525 a “Divertimento in G for strings (fragment).” 「Serenade」というdesignation appears only in the first print, not from Mozart himself. The catalogue also notes that this distinction matters for performance practice: serenades tend toward orchestral thinking, while notturni and divertimenti can point toward chamber-music scale.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So genre is not just a CD-store label. 「どこで、何人で、何のために演奏する音楽だったか」というsocial useまで含む。A large string ensemble and a near-one-player-per-part performance can make the same notes sound strikingly different.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Instead of forcing a one-word verdict — serenade or divertimento — it is more useful to notice that **the unstable label places this work near the border between public entertainment and chamber music.** ジャンルは本質を封印する箱ではなく、historical useを考える手がかりである。

## 6. The written parts and the number of players are different questions; the piece is not fixed to five performers

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
楽器編成も、the abbreviations become simple once unpacked. 現行Köchel catalogueはfirst violin, second violin, viola, cello plus bassを示し、the New Mozart Edition presents two violins, viola, cello, and double bass.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
“First violin” and “second violin” mean different musical parts, not automatically one player each. オーケストラならeach violin part can be played by several musicians, and a smaller ensemble can use far fewer. だから「複数の弦楽パートが書かれている」ことと、“there must be exactly five players”は同じではない。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The New Mozart Edition preface discusses a small chamber-orchestra conception and the possibility of very limited forces in each string part. 一方、the current Köchel catalogue stresses the chamber-music implications of classifying the work as a divertimento. So the evidence supports a range of plausible ensemble sizes more comfortably than one absolute headcount.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
With a large ensemble, the famous opening can sound thick, public, and celebratory. 小編成では、the handoffs between voices and inner-part motion become more exposed. 同じnotesでも、the impression can shift from “monumental famous Mozart” to “a few musicians talking with remarkable agility.”

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
In classical music, not only “which instruments?” but also **how many players share each part?** can change the character. KV525 is a convenient piece for hearing that difference directly.

## 7. G major does not mean “a happy piece”; it means the music has G as a home base

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
この作品はト長調、in English G major,で書かれている。日本語の音名ではGを「ト」と呼ぶため、G major becomes ト長調.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
A beginner-friendly way to think about key or tonality is “where does the music feel at home?” ト長調ならGの音とGを根に持つ和音がlarge-scale point of restになる。“Major is happy, minor is sad” can be a rough first clue, but it is not a rule that assigns one emotion to a whole piece.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The first movement is interesting not just because it has memorable melodies. It establishes a home, leaves it, moves through other tonal territory, becomes less stable, and eventually returns. 音楽理論では、このlarge-scale journeyをsonata formとして説明できる。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
If tonality becomes **a system of musical gravity** rather than merely “bright versus dark,” the next concept becomes easier. Form is not just a melody template; it can be a way of organizing expectation, distance, and return through time.

## 8. Sonata form starts to make sense as “present → wander → return”

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The phrase “sonata form” is confusing because a sonata as a genre and sonata form as a movement design are different things. KV525全体はpiano sonataではないが、its first movement can be analyzed in sonata form.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
In a basic textbook model, sonata form has three large functions: exposition, development, and recapitulation. 提示部（exposition）ではmain material and tonal contrastを出す。展開部（development）ではその素材をbreak apart, transform, and move through unstable territory. 再現部（recapitulation）ではopening material returns and material that had moved away is brought back toward the home key.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
With a little messiness left in the explanation: exposition says, “Here is today's world.” Development takes that world apart and travels. Recapitulation brings familiar things back, but after the journey they no longer mean exactly the same thing. 重要なのはsimple A-B-Aではなく、**return becomes an event because the music first left home.**

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Open Music Theory likewise describes sonata form not as one rigid recipe but as a network of norms in which tonal and thematic questions are set up, destabilized, and answered through return. KV525の第1楽章も、the opening unison is only the front door; hearing the large “away and back” motion makes the whole movement easier to follow.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
And we do not need to imagine Mozart filling out a modern worksheet labelled “exposition / development / recapitulation.” これらのterms are also later analytical tools extracted from many eighteenth-century practices. **A term is most useful as a map that gives you more things to hear, not as an answer that closes the music.**

## 9. The other three surviving movements use different ways of organizing musical time

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The first movement gets most of the recognition, but the remaining three are not repetitions of the same idea. 楽章ごとに、time is organized differently.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
What we now perform as movement two, 《Romanze: Andante》, is a slow movement whose calm main material returns. “Romanze” here does not literally mean a love story; it is better understood as a movement label suggesting a lyrical, song-like character. “Andante” likewise is not one fixed BPM number but a traditional tempo-and-character indication associated with a moderate, walking flow.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Movement three, 《Menuetto: Allegretto》, is a dance-related movement in triple meter. A contrasting “Trio” appears in the middle, and the Minuet returns afterward. ここでTrioは“three people perform”という意味ではなく、historical name for the contrasting middle section. The same string forces play it.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The finale is labelled 《Rondo: Allegro》. The basic rondo idea is simple: a recognizable main section keeps returning between contrasting sections. Open Music Theory gives patterns such as A-B-A-C-A as a basic model. KV525のfinale can be analyzed in more detail, but for a first hearing, simply catching “the idea that keeps coming home” already reveals a lot.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Across the four surviving movements, the first makes a large tonal journey, the Romanze changes the breathing, the Minuet establishes bodily pulse, and the Rondo intensifies the pleasure of return. A multi-movement work is not just one long piece cut into four. **It is also an edit of different kinds of musical time placed in sequence.**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
And originally there was another Minuet between the opening Allegro and the Romanze. それを知ると、the clean modern sequence “fast → slow → dance → fast” is no longer simply an untouched original plan. It is also the result of what history failed to preserve.

## 10. We know the date, August 10, 1787; we do not know who it was for

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Famous works attract tidy origin stories. しかしKV525について、the securely documented background is surprisingly limited.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Mozart entered the work in his catalogue in Vienna on August 10, 1787. 同じ年、he was working with Lorenzo Da Ponte on the opera 《Don Giovanni》KV527, and the New Mozart Edition places KV525 during work on Act II. But we do not know who commissioned KV525, where it was first performed, or what exact occasion it served.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Night music and serenade-like works were often written for name days, celebrations, or commissions, so those are historically plausible contexts. ただし、“often” is not evidence that this particular work had one specific occasion. Without documentation, a later writer cannot simply install an imaginary host, garden, or birthday into the story.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This is also a useful warning against over-biographical listening. Mozart's father Leopold died in 1787, but turning that fact into “Mozart wrote this bright work to overcome grief” would create a causal story the sources do not give us. 《Don Giovanni》was being written at the same time, but that does not prove KV525 was a bright reaction against a dark opera.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Leaving something as unknown is not a failure of explanation. It is historical information. **August 10, 1787 is strong evidence; commissioner and occasion are unknown; beyond that we enter hypothesis.** この線引きができると、famous-work anecdotes become much easier to evaluate.

## 11. Its present fame is not an unbroken line from 1787; publication and twentieth-century media helped build it

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Given how famous the piece is now, it is easy to imagine that it was already a signature Mozart work right after composition. The Köchel catalogue explicitly says otherwise.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The work was not published during Mozart's lifetime. It first appeared in print later through Johann André in Offenbach. ここにa small bibliographic trapがある。The 2024 Köchel source record dates the first print to “1825-1827,” while the André archive and one Morgan exhibition listing give 1827. Yet the prose on the Köchel page and another Morgan page say 1837.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
So even the apparently simple question “What year was the first edition?” does not produce one perfectly consistent institutional answer. 本稿では無理に一つへ丸めず、**the secure point is that the work was first printed decades after Mozart's death.** The first-edition title page also uses the French genre label “Sérénade.”

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The current Köchel catalogue goes further: the work's worldwide rise came especially in the twentieth century through radio and recordings. ここで“masterpiece history” becomes media history too. A compact piece with an instantly recognizable opening, flexible string scoring, and strong repeatability can circulate extremely well through broadcast and records.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The notes did not change after 1787, but **the number and location of encounters with those notes changed, and that changed the cultural size of the work.** 《Eine kleine Nachtmusik》is an eighteenth-century composition whose modern monumentality was also amplified by twentieth-century media.

## 12. The more interesting criticism may be not “this piece is too simple,” but “it is too familiar to hear”

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Extremely famous works have a peculiar problem. 最初の数秒で“Oh, that piece”と認識した瞬間、we can stop listening while the music keeps going.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Conductor and early-music specialist Reinhard Goebel argued in a 2025 SWR feature that layers of later performance habits had accumulated on the work, to the point that it had become an area some musicians preferred to avoid. He also reads repetition, the forceful opening, and instantly graspable material as deliberate tools for making music that reaches a broad audience.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
“Simple” does not have to mean “shallow.” 短い動機をrepeatし、ensembleをそろえて鳴らし、return pointsを明確にすることは、listenersがstructureをその場でつかむための技術でもある。Being memorable after one hearing is not the same thing as requiring little compositional skill.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The larger problem may be that the work succeeded so completely at being legible that later listeners stop noticing how it works. “Elegant Mozart” or “bright classical music”というlabelだけで流すと、the Romanze's shadows, inner voices, dynamic shocks, repetitions, and the missing movement all become harder to hear.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Criticism does not need to make a famous work artificially difficult. **Its job can be to restore differences that familiarity has erased.** 名曲を疑うとは“実は大したことがない”と暴くことではなく、why it enters the ear so easily and why it still rewards analysisを問い直すことだ。

## 13. To hear it again, carry five observations instead of trying to memorize theory

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
After learning the terms, the useful move is to return to actual sound. 次に聞くときは、five simple observations are enough to change the picture.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
- **At the opening, notice who moves together.** 有名なmelodyというより、hear it as a call that points the whole ensemble in one direction at once.
- **In movement one, notice where “away” and “back” happen.** 細かなkey namesが分からなくても、sonata formの“present → destabilize → return”は追える。
- **In the Romanze, ask whether calmness stays calm.** “Night music”という先入観を外し、listen for darker or tenser moments in the middle.
- **In the Minuet and Trio, notice how density and character change.** 三拍子を数えるだけでなく、hear the contrast between dance section and middle section.
- **In the finale, count how often you feel “that idea came back.”** ロンドというformal termを、your own hearing of returnへ接続する。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
You do not even need to repeat the same recording. A large string-orchestra performance beside a small-ensemble performance can make the scoring discussion audible immediately. 古楽器やhistorically informed playingを意識した録音、thick modern strings, and very fast readings can reveal surprisingly different outlines in the same score.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Music history and form are not there to force a “correct way to listen.” Every useful concept gives the ear one more object to notice. In that sense, 《Eine kleine Nachtmusik》is not valuable for beginners because it is “easy”; **it is valuable because it builds a bridge from an untrained ear to real technical language.**

## 14. A small glossary turns this one piece into reusable classical-music knowledge

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
最後に、本稿で使ったwordsを“facts only for this piece”ではなく、ほかの作品にも持っていける形でまとめる。

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
- **楽章（movement）**：a large unit inside a multi-movement work. 章ごとにtempo, key, form, and characterが変わる。
- **調／調性（key / tonality）**：音楽がどの音・和音をhome baseとして組み立てるかという仕組み。
- **ト長調（G major）**：Gを中心とするmajor key。日本語の音名ではGを「ト」と呼ぶ。
- **セレナーデ（serenade）**：historically linked with evening, celebration, and social performance; its meaning shifts by period and region.
- **ディヴェルティメント（divertimento）**：a label often used for instrumental entertainment music; boundaries with serenade and related genres are not fixed.
- **ソナタ形式（sonata form）**：typically organizes thematic and tonal motion through exposition, development, and recapitulation — roughly “present, travel, return.”
- **メヌエットとトリオ（minuet and trio）**：a triple-meter dance section, a contrasting middle section, and the return of the minuet. “Trio” does not require three performers.
- **ロンド（rondo）**：a form in which a main section repeatedly returns between contrasting material.
- **KV（Köchel-Verzeichnis）**：the Köchel catalogue number, assigned by later scholarship to identify Mozart's works, not a number Mozart gave the piece.
- **自筆譜（autograph manuscript）**：a score written by the composer himself, distinguished as a historical source from printed editions and later copies.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Technical vocabulary can look like a gatekeeping code until each term is defined. But once translated into actions, it becomes practical: not “sonata form means prestige,” but “listen for how return is made”; not “G major means happy,” but “listen for what functions as home.” 言葉をactionへ変えると、knowledge becomes a listening tool.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
《Eine kleine Nachtmusik》appears constantly in classical introductions, and not only because it is famous. One piece can open doors to title history, cataloguing, genre, ensemble size, tonality, form, source criticism, and reception history. ほとんど全部の入口がここにある。

## 15. Before the research it looked like Mozart's business card; afterward it looks like a work completed by surviving incomplete

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
調べる前、《Eine kleine Nachtmusik》was basically Mozart's musical business card: bright, elegant, familiar. That was enough to end the explanation.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
But the sources show five movements in Mozart's own catalogue, with one now lost. “Serenade” appears later in the first print, while the current Köchel catalogue leans toward “divertimento.” The commission is unknown. It was not a lifetime blockbuster that simply stayed famous; twentieth-century broadcasting and recording greatly expanded its reach.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the work we know is not only “what Mozart wrote on August 10, 1787.” It has also been shaped by missing paper, posthumous publishers, cataloguers, performers choosing ensemble size, radio stations, record companies, music education, and our own memory of hearing the opening again and again.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That returns us to the opening question. Why does a piece missing one movement not feel “broken”? Perhaps because **a work can acquire another kind of completeness through being performed, heard, and named for a very long time.** The lost movement is still absent. But once you know it was there, a new kind of silence appears between the four movements you thought you already knew.
