# 当事者感×領域解像度で、Willは「見つかる」より「育つ」

### 現場と本場を往復し、the question you want to ownをつくる

「What is your Will?」と聞かれると、急に答えに詰まる。What do you want to change? 自分の内側を深掘りすれば、本心というhidden treasureが見つかるような気がする。

But how can you strongly want to change a world you barely know? 逆に、domain expertiseが高くても、自分が引き受ける理由がなければ動けない。ここでのworking hypothesisは、**Will＝ownership × domain resolution**。

この掛け算を育てるphysical practiceが、**現場に行くことと、本場に行くこと**。ただし、this is a heuristic, not a validated psychological equation。心理尺度として確立された公式ではない。

> 本稿の問い：Willを内省だけでdiscoverするのではなく、誰の何を変えるかをfieldで確かめたほうが、明確になるのではないか。

![概念図：当事者感と領域解像度が掛け合わさり、具体的な意思になる構造。](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/will-ownership-domain-model-2026.svg)

*図1：A conceptual model, not a measured causal formula。「現場」と「本場」を行き来し、Willを育てる仮説。*

## 1. Willを「好きなこと」ではなく、「引き受けたい変化」として捉える

Here, Will is not simply a preference or a corporate target. It means **a change I am willing to take responsibility for**。例えば「I love sports」は入口。「子どもが競技に参加できない障壁を減らす」は、誰の何を変えたいかが見える。

**Ownership**とは、自分との関係で問題を捉え、放置しない理由があること。Direct lived experienceは必須ではない。他者の課題をcareすることでも育つが、「本人の声を聞かずに代弁する」こととは別だ。

**Domain resolution**とは、その領域のusers, constraints, incentives, workflows, and trade-offsを細かく区別できること。「競技人口を増やす」を、施設枠・指導者・送迎・費用・調整へ分解できるようなseeing powerだ。

Why multiply? Because passion without understanding can misfire, and expertise without ownership can remain inert。ただしreal motivationには生活条件、支援、制度も関わる。これは説明のlensであって、人の評価式ではない。

## 2. 掛け算が生む四つの状態を見れば、次の行動が変わる

Low ownership × low resolution is not necessarily apathy. It may mean no meaningful encounter yet。ここで使命を無理に決めず、small exposureから始める。

High ownership / low resolution means strong emotion but a blurry map: investigate causes。High resolution / low ownership means knowing a lot but standing far away: meet the people affected and ask what role you could own。

High × high may support sustained action, but it does not guarantee success。Resources, authority, timing, and team capability still matter。Will is not the whole business model。

**The matrix is a map for the next experience, not a ranking of people.**

## 3. 現場は「困りごとの真相」、本場は「基準の高さ」を教える

Two places teach different things. **The field（現場）** is where real problems happen: users' rooms, factory floors, operations booths, local facilities. **The epicenter（本場）** is where advanced practice, standards, talent, and competition come together。

A top-league stadium may be both field and epicenter。同時に、local amateur eventsには違う制約がある。One flagship venue does not represent the whole market。

At the field, ask: Why does this manual workaround survive? Who pays the hidden cost? At the epicenter, ask: How do excellent teams handle it? What is already assumed? **The field reveals the problem; the epicenter expands what you believe possible.**

![往復の図：現場で課題を知り、本場で可能性を見て、小さく試し、また現場へ戻る。](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/will-field-epicenter-loop-2026.svg)

*図2：Field → Epicenter → Hypothesis → Small test → Field。Seeing is not the same as learning; return to the users and test。*

This connects two distinct ideas. IDEO.org's **Guided Tour** observes routines in people's real settings。Porter's **cluster theory** explains why geographic concentration can matter for knowledge and relationships。ただしcluster theory does not directly prove how an individual's Will grows。参照：[IDEO.org](https://www.designkit.org/methods/guided-tour.html)／[Porter](https://hbr.org/1998/11/clusters-and-the-new-economics-of-competition)。

## 4. 知識は資料だけでなく、参加を通じて身につく

Why visit? One reason is **tacit knowledge**。Michael Polanyi argued that knowing exceeds what we can fully articulate。現場の気配、判断の間合い、微かな異常は、spreadsheet cellsに簡単には落ちない。

> “We can know more than we can tell.” —Michael Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension*。言語化以前のknowingがある。[University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/T/bo6035368.html)

Jean Lave and Etienne Wenger's **situated learning** sees learning as social participation。**Legitimate peripheral participation** means that newcomers gradually move from the edge of a community of practice toward fuller participation。

A single visit is only an entry point。Participate, help, return with a changed proposal, and keep learning。As your participation deepens, your sense of **who you are in this practice** may also change。Source: [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/situated-learning/6915ABD21C8E4619F750A4D4ACA616CD/legitimate-peripheral-participation/28CD74BD15EBFABE881F24826917EC4C)。

## 5. Airbnbは、画面を見ていた創業者が利用者の部屋へ行った

Consider Airbnb in 2009。Its founders traveled to New York, met hosts, and personally improved listing photos。Paul Graham recalls that meeting hosts revealed not only product problems but also their financial circumstances。

The deeper insight: a listing-quality problem also became a human livelihood problem。Graham reports weekly fees rising from $460 in early February to $1,428 by the third week of that month。ただし、these figures alone do not isolate a causal effect of field visits or photography。

> “We are not going to slow down.” —Brian Chesky's reply, as recounted by Paul Graham。 [Primary account](https://www.paulgraham.com/airbnbs.html)

It is tempting to read a perfect founder-mission narrative backward。We cannot prove that from the story。What we can verify is a sequence of user contact, learning, and changed operations。Did Will drive action, or did action strengthen Will? Both directions remain plausible。

## 6. トヨタの現地現物は、「分かったつもり」を許さない

Toyota's **genchi genbutsu** means going to see for yourself。A Toyota quality executive described why walking the production line matters instead of relying only on office meetings。This does not abolish reports; it checks them against practice。

A single downtime KPI can hide different causes: equipment, material layout, communication, or hesitation。Higher resolution means distinguishing mechanisms and choosing different remedies。Source: [Toyota USA](https://pressroom.toyota.com/our-point-of-view-walk-the-line/)。

Genchi genbutsu is not a management photo opportunity。Its value depends on learning from practitioners rather than turning them into research objects。

## 7. 本場へ行く意味は、流行を真似ることではなく、自分の物差しを更新すること

An epicenter visit should update your benchmark, not your status。Porter's clusters remind us that knowledge, relationships, and competition can be locally concentrated。At an epicenter, observe not only equipment but **what questions expert practitioners no longer need to ask**。

Eric von Hippel's **lead users** add another twist: some of the most valuable epicenters are not glamorous headquarters but users at the leading edge of a need。They may already have built solutions。Source: [MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/books/book/2821/Democratizing-Innovation)。

For a sports-video project, visit both world-class production operations and a small community event where one person handles filming, streaming, and permissions。The first shows technical possibility; the second shows economic and operational reality。This is an illustrative scenario, not a documented case study。

## 8. 連想を広げると、Willは「学習」と「責任」の交差点にある

This connects to **self-determination theory**, associated with Edward Deci and Richard Ryan。Its three basic psychological needs are **autonomy, competence, and relatedness**。Choice, efficacy, and human connection can support the quality of motivation。

The equation overlaps with, but is **not derived from**, self-determination theory。Meeting people may support relatedness; gaining skill may support competence; choosing a problem may support autonomy。But SDT does not claim ownership × resolution = Will。Source: [SDT official site](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/topics/application-basic-psychological-needs/)。

Across design research, ethnography, lead-user innovation, clusters, and situated learning, a pattern emerges: **Will may be a revisable hypothesis about what I will commit to—not a finished object hidden inside my mind.**

## 9. ただし、現場礼賛と本場信仰は、どちらも危険である

**Risk 1: sampling bias.** One powerful field encounter is not a representative dataset。Check observations against logs, other roles, actual behavior, and different contexts。

**Risk 2: prestige bias.** What works at an elite institution may fail elsewhere because of budget, staff, culture, or regulation。Ask not only what to copy but what cannot transfer。

**Risk 3: moralizing ownership.** People need freedom to choose, rest, support, and real authority。Burnout or coerced unpaid labor should not be romanticized as Will。

After every visit, capture not how inspired you felt, but **what belief changed**。Separate prior assumptions, observations, interpretations, counterevidence, and next actions。

## 10. 二週間で、Willの輪郭を変える小さな実験

Try a **two-week Will experiment**。Day 1: write one problem statement, three stakeholders, three workarounds, three constraints。Choose one field and one epicenter。Remote interviews or expert sessions are fallback options, not perfect substitutes for physical visits。

At the field, observe and ask who bears time and cost。At the epicenter, compare what experts measure, assume, and treat as failure。Get consent before photography or recording; respect confidentiality。

In week 2, rewrite your problem statement and ask the affected person whether it is accurate。Try one small intervention when appropriate。Review two separate questions: **What did I understand better?** and **What change am I now willing to own?**

Do not optimize a made-up Will score。Keep a learning ledger: disconfirmed assumptions, newly discovered stakeholders, and the next action you voluntarily put on your calendar。Behavior is observable, but it is not a complete measure of commitment。

## 11. 最後に残る問いは、「何が好きか」ではなく「何を引き受けるか」

The field makes abstract problems concrete。The epicenter makes your current benchmark unstable。Moving between them can change both understanding and responsibility。

**Will is not reserved for people who can declare a life mission on day one.** It can grow as we observe, participate, and choose a responsibility worth sustaining。The next step may be less introspection—and one real visit on the calendar。
