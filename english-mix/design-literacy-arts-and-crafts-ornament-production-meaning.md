# Arts and Crafts――装飾は「余計」なのか。それとも仕事をしているのか
## Design Literacy #20｜Reading Ornament Through Role and Production

**Don’t ask whether something is decorative. Ask what work the decoration is doing.**

直前のDesign Literacy #19では、Progressive Disclosureを扱った。

We asked **what should be visible now, and what should appear later.** つまりInformation Timingの話だった。

But after information is organized, another question remains. 模様、質感、illustration、motion、typographic characterみたいな「なくても操作はできる」表現は、どう評価すればいいのか。

今回は19世紀後半のArts and Crafts Movementを手掛かりに、**ornamentを“ある／ない”ではなく、“what job is it doing?”で読む**。

## 1. まず一言：Evaluate decoration by its work, not its existence

イベントカードを二つ想像する。

```text
A
┌─────────────────┐
│      SALE       │
│                 │
│    9.19 SAT     │
│                 │
│   BUY TICKET    │
└─────────────────┘
```

```text
B
╔═════════════════╗
║  /// 炎日 ///   ║
║                 ║
║    9.19 SAT     ║
║   pattern / heat║
║                 ║
║   BUY TICKET    ║
╚═════════════════╝
```

B has more decoration. でも、そこからすぐに、

```text
A = good
B = noisy
```

とは決められない。

Instead ask:

```text
Does the pattern strengthen the event identity?
Does it damage hierarchy?
Does it interrupt scanning?
Does it create memory or expectation?
Is the production cost worth the effect?
```

**More decoration does not automatically mean worse design.**

装飾の量とdesign qualityは、同じ軸ではない。

## 2. 仕組み：Ornament Function Audit

When deciding whether to reduce decoration, split its work into four categories.

### 1. Meaning / Information

**Does it help communicate meaning?**

区切り線、強調背景、directional shape、状態を補助するpatternなど。

Something may look decorative while still carrying information structure.

### 2. Expression / Identity

**Does it create the character of the service, event, or brand?**

```text
texture
illustration
pattern
motion
typographic character
```

These may not add a function directly, but they can shape identity and expectation.

### 3. Structure / Rhythm

**Does it organize attention or reading rhythm?**

反復、余白、rule、color fieldなどがsection changeやattentionの強弱を作ることがある。

### 4. Production / Material

**How is it made, and what kind of work does that production create?**

ここがArts and Craftsから強く借りられる視点。

```text
Who makes it?
With what tools?
How often is it reused?
How editable is it?
What burden does maintenance create?
```

見た目だけでなく、production relationshipまで見る。

So the decision is no longer:

```text
Decorative?
→ Yes / No
```

It becomes:

```text
What does it do?
↓
Keep / Reduce / Redesign / Remove
```

## 3. Before → After：From “too busy” to diagnosis

### BEFORE

レビューで、

> 「ちょっとゴチャついているので、装飾を減らしてください」

と伝える。

The problem is that the instruction contains no diagnosis.

```text
Remove the pattern?
Shrink the illustration?
Delete the border?
Reduce the colors?
```

担当者は推測するしかない。

### AFTER

First classify the role.

```text
background pattern
→ event identity

heavy section rule
→ information grouping

flame illustration
→ attention / emotional tone

18 tiny sparkles
→ role unclear
```

The last one is a removal candidate.

The first three should be tuned according to their job and side effects—not removed just because they are “decorative.”

**Reduction should follow diagnosis.**

削る前に診断する。

## 4. そのまま使える制作・修正指示

制作指示なら、こう言える。

> **装飾を一律に減らすのではなく、各要素がMeaning / Expression / Structure / Productionのどの役割を担っているか整理してください。役割がない、または他要素と重複している装飾は削減し、役割があるものは可読性・操作性・制作コストを損なわない強度へ調整してください。**

For review, start with:

> **“If we remove this, what is lost besides information?”**

Then add:

> **“Is that value worth the cost of creating and maintaining it?”**

ここまで聞くと、visual tasteだけでなくproductionまでレビュー対象になる。

## 5. 歴史との接続：Arts and Crafts was not one single style

V&A describes the Arts and Crafts Movement as emerging in late-19th-century Britain in reaction to the damaging effects of industrialisation and the relatively low status of decorative arts.

But it was **not one unified style or one organization.**

V&A notes that the movement included multiple societies such as the Arts and Crafts Exhibition Society and Arts Workers Guild, individual craftspeople, small workshops, and even large manufacturing companies.

[Victoria and Albert Museum — Arts and Crafts: an introduction](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/arts-and-crafts-an-introduction)

William Morris was enormously influential. ただし、

```text
Arts and Crafts = Morris alone
```

とすると、movementの幅が消える。

The Metropolitan Museum likewise describes Arts and Crafts not as the promotion of one fixed style, but as a reform movement concerned with decorative design and industrial labour.

[The Metropolitan Museum of Art — The Arts and Crafts Movement in America](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-arts-and-crafts-movement-in-america)

So the lesson is not just “what Arts and Crafts looked like.”

**How things are made can be a design question.**

何を作るかだけでなく、どう作るかまでdesign problemになる。

## 6. Morris did not simply say “machines are bad”

This is one of the easiest simplifications to make.

```text
handmade = good
machine = bad
```

It is memorable, but historically incomplete.

V&A explicitly notes that Morris was not entirely against machines. His criticism focused strongly on the division of labour and the weakened relationship between makers and the results of their work.

In **Useful Work versus Useless Toil** (1884), Morris argues that in a different social order, machines could be used to reduce unattractive labour rather than intensify it.

[William Morris — Useful Work versus Useless Toil, 1884](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/1884/useful.htm)

In another lecture from the same period, he frames the goal as making machines our “servants,” not our “masters.”

[William Morris — A Socialist's View of Art and Labour, 1884](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/1884/prestonart.htm)

So it is more accurate to read his criticism as:

```text
technology
×
labour conditions
×
purpose of production
×
relationship to the maker
```

rather than simply “technology itself.”

This can be useful when looking at today’s tools:

```text
template
component library
generative AI
automation
no-code
```

But this is a **STRUCTURAL ANALOGY**. Morris did not write about AI or Design Systems.

We are borrowing his way of asking what production methods do to people, work, and outcomes.

## 7. 誤解しやすい点：Craft does not automatically mean “for everyone”

Arts and Crafts carried a strong social ideal: beautiful, well-made objects should belong to everyday life.

But there was a practical tension.

The Metropolitan Museum notes that craft-centered production could be time-consuming and prohibitively expensive, making it difficult to make well-designed objects available to a broad public.

[The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Design, 1900–1925](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/design-1900-25)

Another Met essay points out the irony that high manufacturing costs made many Morris-associated objects too expensive for the wider public.

[The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Design Reform](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/design-reform)

So this trade-off can appear:

```text
careful craft
→ quality ↑
→ labour / time ↑
→ cost ↑
→ access ↓
```

**Craft quality and social accessibility can conflict.**

丁寧に作ることと、多くの人に届けることは常に同じ方向へ進まない。

That matters today too.

```text
bespoke
vs
reusable component

handmade asset
vs
template

custom motion
vs
system motion
```

Don’t decide only by “which feels more premium.” Compare quality, labour, speed, maintainability, price, and reach.

## 8. Morrisの「Useful / Beautiful」をfunctionalist sloganにしない

Morris’s famous 1880 advice about the home says, in effect, not to keep things you do not know to be useful or believe to be beautiful.

V&A attributes this statement to Morris in 1880.

[Victoria and Albert Museum — Furnishing the aesthetic home](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/furnishing-the-aesthetic-home)

The interesting part is:

```text
Useful OR Beautiful
```

not:

```text
Useful ONLY
```

So reading it as “Morris rejected decoration without utility” would be too strong.

His wallpapers and textiles were richly patterned, and V&A describes how his naturalistic pattern work expanded the market for decorative interiors, including wallpapers, fabrics, and rugs.

[Victoria and Albert Museum — Pattern design after William Morris](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/pattern-design-after-william-morris)

One useful modern interpretation is:

**Beauty itself can be part of what an object is for.**

ただし、これは今回の記事上のINTERPRETATION。Morrisの一文からmodern UX theoryを直接導いたわけではない。

## 9. 以前の学びとの接続：Information Density ≠ Expressive Density

In #19 Progressive Disclosure, we asked:

```text
What should be visible now?
What should appear later?
```

That mainly concerns **Information Density**.

Today we add another axis:

```text
Information Density
= how many decisions, explanations, and options are visible

Expressive Density
= how strongly pattern, image, texture, type, and motion shape the experience
```

These are not the same thing.

```text
less information
≠
less expression
```

これが今日の**前より解像度が上がる接続**。

You can hide advanced settings while keeping illustration, texture, or typographic personality. Conversely, even a screen with very little information can still become hard to read if expression destroys hierarchy.

**Simplicity is not a single slider.**

「シンプルさ」は一つのつまみではない。

## 10. 30秒でできる観察：Audit one decorative element

Open a website, app, or poster you like.

Pick just one decorative-looking element.

Ask four questions:

```text
MEANING
What does it help me understand?

EXPRESSION
What does it make me feel or expect?

STRUCTURE
Where does it make me look?

PRODUCTION
What does it cost to create and maintain?
```

Then ask:

**“What disappears if I remove it?”**

If nothing disappears, it may be removable.

If something meaningful disappears, it is doing a job. Then the design question becomes whether the strength and cost of that job are appropriate.

## 11. 次につながる概念：Form follows function

Next comes one of modern design’s most famous phrases:

**Form follows function.**

But don’t jump immediately to:

```text
function matters
→ ornament should disappear
```

First ask:

**What counts as function?**

```text
physical function
communication
recognition
emotion
identity
social function
maintenance
```

Once function becomes this broad, the simple opposition between:

```text
function vs ornament
```

starts to wobble.

そこからModernismとOrnamentの議論へ進める。

---

**Today’s claim:**

**Don’t ask, “Is this decorative?” Ask, “What work is this decoration doing, and what does that work cost?”**

日本語なら、

**「これ、飾りだから消す？」ではなく、「この飾りは何の仕事をしていて、その仕事にどれだけコストがかかっている？」と聞く。**

Arts and Craftsから受け取れるのは、昔の植物柄だけではない。

It gives us a way to see **appearance, production, labour, and access as one design problem.**
