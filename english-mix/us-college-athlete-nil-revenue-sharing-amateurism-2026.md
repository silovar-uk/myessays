# Who owns the college athlete's value?――米国大学スポーツのrevenue revolution

*From NIL deals to direct university payments: rights, competition, education, and the unsettled boundary.*

A student wears a university jersey, appears in a commercial, and gets paid in their own name. What once conflicted with the amateur ideal has become ordinary business in the United States. ただし、2026年の変化は**NIL deals aloneでは説明できない**。Universities can now make direct payments too. The deeper question is who creates collegiate sports value and who receives it. [NCAA statement](https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-a-letter-from-ncaa-president-charlie-baker/)

This essay starts from a Nikkei headline dated October 11, 2026: student athletes earning money and the fading amateur–professional boundary. The full original text was not accessible. したがって、this is **an independently researched essay**, not a reconstruction of Nikkei's individual examples or wording. Its evidence comes from official rules, regulators, legal scholarship, and Japanese sources. [Original headline](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQODH1147N0R10C26A9000000/)

## 1. 2021 unlocked endorsements; 2025 changed the payment system

**NIL** stands for Name, Image and Likeness: the commercial use of an athlete's identity, including endorsements and advertising. 日本語の「肖像権」は便利な訳語だが、氏名や広告上の本人の価値まで正確に言い尽くさない。NIL also does not automatically transfer copyright in game footage, university logos, or league rights to the athlete. [NCAA NIL guide](https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/name-image-likeness/)

On June 30, 2021, the NCAA adopted an interim NIL policy, effective July 1. Student athletes could take endorsement deals and other third-party opportunities. たとえば製品紹介、店舗イベント、広告への出演。But this was not a blanket approval for universities to pay athletes simply for winning games. **Endorsement freedom and salary-like payments were different questions.** [NCAA history](https://www.ncaa.org/about-us/history/)

A second turning point arrived with the **House v. NCAA settlement**, approved in June 2025. From July 2025, participating institutions could pay athletes directly within a cap: about **$20.5 million per school** in 2025–26. ただし上限は義務額ではない。For the first time, athlete compensation became an explicit university budget line rather than only an outside endorsement opportunity. [NCAA statement](https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-a-letter-from-ncaa-president-charlie-baker/) / [adopted rules](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=108870)

## 2. Two wallets pay athletes—and they measure different things

There are **two wallets**. One is a third-party NIL agreement, typically payment for real commercial use of an athlete's identity. The other is university-funded direct compensation under the settlement framework. 同じ選手が両方を受け取れる。Yet a reported NIL contract value and an actual university distribution are not the same accounting measure. [College Sports Commission](https://www.collegesportscommission.org/)

![Two payment routes for college athletes: original diagram](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/us-college-nil-two-wallets-2026.svg)

*Figure 1: Original conceptual diagram. Actual contracts may have additional parties and restrictions. Sources: [NCAA](https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/name-image-likeness/) and [CSC](https://www.collegesportscommission.org/).*

NIL has a compliance layer. Third-party deals at or above the general **$600 reporting threshold** must be disclosed. Contracts involving school-associated organizations receive scrutiny over genuine commercial purpose and reasonable market value. 大学への勧誘金を広告契約と呼び替えれば許される、という仕組みではない。The distinction makes market access possible while attempting to limit disguised recruiting payments. [NCAA guide](https://www.ncaa.org/student-athletes/name-image-likeness/) / [rules](https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=108989)

## 3. $1.77B paid by schools and $582M in cleared NIL deals are not comparable totals

In its first-year report published in October 2026, the **College Sports Commission (CSC)** recorded about **$1.770B** in direct payments by 307 schools to 34,915 athletes, across 33 conferences and 45 sports, for July 2025–June 2026. 追加奨学金などを含む記録総額は約$1.976B。That is why “$1.8B paid” and “nearly $2B in total benefits” refer to different definitions. [CSC release](https://www.nilnewsstand.com/updates/college-sports-commission-revenue-sharing-report-2025-26) / [breakdown](https://businessofcollegesports.com/name-image-likeness/division-i-athletes-took-home-1-8-billion-in-revenue-sharing-in-2025-26/)

The commission's NIL Go data show **46,478 cleared third-party deals worth $582.49M**, cumulatively from June 2025 to August 2026. July–August 2026 alone accounted for 12,283 deals worth $227.25M. だが、これは**cleared contract value**, not verified cash paid during the same period as university revenue sharing. Different windows, definitions, and populations mean we must not simply add the two totals. [CSC September report](https://www.nilnewsstand.com/updates/nil-go-data-reports-september)

Fast growth needs interpretation. More approved deals may reflect more business, but also a faster review process, more staff, and clearance of pending applications. These operational changes were reported during summer 2026. 取引額グラフだけでは選手個人の受取額や履行率は分からない。A rising contract total is evidence of a growing recorded pipeline, not a complete income survey. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/6c31dd63e16fcb613156c404c28df914)

## 4. Amateurism is being renegotiated, not simply switched off

Calling this **professionalization** captures a real shift but hides unresolved boundaries. Athlete eligibility, academic progress, rosters, transfers, and contracts still matter. 何より**being paid** is not identical to **being legally classified as an employee**. The NCAA called the settlement a new beginning, not the end of every legal dispute. [NCAA president's letter](https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-a-letter-from-ncaa-president-charlie-baker/)

Rules changed beyond pay. Participating schools moved away from sport-specific scholarship limits toward **roster limits**, opening possibilities for more scholarships. その一方、大学は有限の予算を競技間で配分する。The freedom to compensate athletes therefore expands a university's responsibility for deciding which programs receive opportunities and support. [NCAA roster changes](https://www.ncaa.org/news/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits/)

## 5. Market value does not settle fairness inside a university

Paying athletes in revenue-generating sports can make commercial sense. Broadcast fees, tickets, and sponsorship demand are often concentrated. しかし大学は、単なる興行会社ではない。It also has educational obligations. Who receives money is therefore a decision about **what a university values**, not merely an exercise in pricing athletes.

A major dispute concerns **Title IX**, the US law against sex discrimination in education. A June 2026 legal analysis argues that allocating university payments entirely by market value may conflict with gender-equity duties. ただしこれは法的な**analysis and argument**であり、すべての大学の支払いが違法と確定した、という事実ではない。The debate concerns how revenue logic and equal educational opportunity coexist. [Harvard Journal of Law & Gender](https://journals.law.harvard.edu/jlg/2026/06/playing-with-house-money-title-ixs-application-to-college-athlete-compensation/)

The star athlete is visible; the typical athlete is less so. Big brand deals are easy to photograph, but ordinary concerns remain: training time, classes, access to teams, contracts, and advisers. **スターの契約額を制度全体の幸福度と取り違えない**。The system must be judged both by exceptional opportunity and the conditions faced by everyone else.

## 6. Japan should borrow rights clarity before it borrows American payment scale

Copying American dollar amounts into Japanese university sports would be misleading. Media income, audiences, team structures, and governing bodies are different. まず比較すべきは**rights architecture**だ。Who controls the athlete's identity, the school name, the uniform mark, the match footage, and the streaming rights? Revenue can only be negotiated clearly when the underlying permissions are clear.

Japan already confronts these questions. A UNIVAS match-streaming project required universities to clear rights concerning the images and personal information of people appearing in footage. ここから分かるのは、**permission to stream a match does not automatically mean permission to reuse a student's face in a sponsor advertisement**。A clear license needs a defined purpose. [UNIVAS requirements](https://univas.jp/news/article/202405311107262/)

## 7. The practical opportunity is usable rights, not just athlete commercialization

**The following is a proposal, not a reported fact.** Japanese universities and sponsors could start with a **rights inventory** rather than a generic athlete sponsorship package. Separate personal appearances, team endorsements, school trademarks, match-video reuse, and social posting. For each, record the contracting party, permission holder, media, term, price, and post-graduation treatment. 権利を混ぜずに定義することが、新しい広告メニューを作る基礎になる。

The first experiments should be modest and legally clear. A local business might ask an athlete to describe a product, without assuming permission to use university branding or game footage. その際、本人の広告出演を大学公式の推薦にすり替えない。Smaller, well-defined deals may create more repeatable opportunities than one ambitious agreement with unclear rights.

Do not measure success only by sponsorship sales. Also track **days to obtain consent, rights inquiries, athlete compensation actually paid, delivered campaigns, and complaints or amendments**. ここで重要なのは、選手が納得して参加し、必要なら断れる運用。The opportunity is not merely monetization; it is lowering the cost of responsible participation.

## 8. College athletes are becoming negotiating parties, not merely subjects of the show

The amateur–professional boundary is not the only line moving. Schools build teams, brands buy attention, media companies distribute games, and fans support athletes. The athlete at the center of this system is gaining a stronger position as a **party to commercial agreements**. その結果、契約の自由と同時に、情報格差・競技間格差・男女の機会・学業との両立が新しい論点になる。

The novelty of “students getting paid” will fade. The deeper test is whether people can explain **whose rights are used, why money is distributed as it is, and whether the athlete can make an informed choice**. 金額の大きさより、分配の理由を説明できる制度かどうか。That may be the better measure of a mature college sports economy.
