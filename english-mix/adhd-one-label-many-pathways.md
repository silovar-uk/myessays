# ADHD is not one cause: multiple pathways can meet under one diagnosis
## I asked whether ADHD was a bundle of phenomena, and the word factor split into three meanings

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Is ADHD basically a name for several phenomena? それとも、one hidden factorがあって、inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivityが全部そこから出ているのか。I started with this either-or question, but the question itself turned out to be slightly wrong.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder（ADHD）は、a neurodevelopmental disorderとして扱われる。Diagnosis is based on persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity, when symptoms started, whether they appear across settings, and whether they cause meaningful problems at school, work, home, or in relationships. There is no single blood value that says, "above this line = ADHD."

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The U.S. National Institute of Mental Health（NIMH）says researchers do not know one single cause of ADHD and that a combination of factors, including genes, is likely involved. The U.K. National Institute for Health and Care Excellence（NICE）also says diagnosis should not rest on a rating scale alone and requires symptoms plus impairment across important settings. A 2024 Nature Reviews Disease Primers review describes ADHD as a heterogeneous neurodevelopmental condition.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So ADHD is not a random bag of complaints. It is a structured clinical category with researched validity. でも、そのcategoryが「everyone has the same cause」を意味するわけではない。The confusion begins when we treat a diagnostic label as if it were already a causal explanation.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The word "factor" makes this even trickier. A statistical symptom factor? Yes, we can find one. Risk factors for developing ADHD? Many. One biological factor shared by every person with ADHD? Current science does not give us that. 同じ「factor」がthree different levelsを行き来している。

[NIMH, Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: What You Need to Know](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know) ／ [NICE, Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: diagnosis and management](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations) ／ [Faraone et al., Nature Reviews Disease Primers, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41572-024-00495-0)

> This essay整理するのはADHD as a diagnostic concept and research factors. It does not diagnose an individual. Group-level differences in genetics, brain measures, or cognition cannot simply be copied onto one person.

## 1. ADHD is defined by symptoms and impairment, not by one known cause

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
First point: the name ADHD itself does not explain the cause.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Diagnosis looks mainly at inattention and hyperactivity-impulsivity. たとえばdifficulty sustaining attention, losing things, interrupting, or difficulty waiting matter when they persist, are developmentally inappropriate, appear across settings, and impair daily functioning. One symptom alone is not the diagnosis.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
NIMH describes three presentations: predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, and combined. NICE asks clinicians to consider impairment across social, family, education, and work settings, plus coexisting conditions, physical health, and the person's circumstances.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This means ADHD is not "people who all have Cause A." It is closer to "people whose pattern of traits reaches a clinically meaningful level of persistence, spread, and impairment." 診断カテゴリーはuseful。でも、そのboundaryとbiological causeのboundaryは同じではない。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So is ADHD a "general name for phenomena"? Half yes. It is defined from observable symptom patterns, but not casually. It also includes developmental timing, cross-situational presence, and functional impairment. **It groups phenomena, but it does not group them carelessly.**

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[NIMH, Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: What You Need to Know](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-what-you-need-to-know) ／ [NICE, Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations)

## 2. Symptoms can share a common factor, but that factor is not the cause itself

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Then, is there a shared "ADHD factor" behind inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity? In statistical language, the answer can be close to yes.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
In psychology, when many symptoms rise and fall together, researchers can model a latent factor that explains some of their covariance. ADHD research has tested correlated dimensions such as inattention and hyperactivity-impulsivity, and also bifactor models with one general ADHD factor plus more specific dimensions.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
One study of adolescent symptoms found support for a model with a general component plus separable inattention and hyperactivity-impulsivity dimensions. A later review of bifactor studies reported that a general ADHD factor often explained much of the shared variance, while some specific dimensions could still retain meaningful information.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
But statistical modeling cannot teleport straight into biology. Factor analysis tells us how symptoms move together. It does not show that a physical substance called "ADHD factor" exists in the brain. 身長と体重にcommon factorを置けても、そのfactorが体内の一個の部品ではないのと同じ。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
ADHD symptoms do have structure. That matters: they are not simply unrelated events. But **a factor that summarizes symptoms is different from a factor that causes them.** This distinction stops a big logical jump.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Martel et al., The unity and diversity of inattention and hyperactivity/impulsivity in ADHD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19562477/) ／ [Arias et al., Bifactor Models of ADHD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27872349/)

## 3. On the causal side, many small influences stack rather than one ADHD factor

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Move from symptom structure to causes, and the picture spreads out quickly. Modern research is not converging on one ADHD gene or one environmental cause.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Genetic influence is substantial. Twin studies have often estimated ADHD heritability around 70–80%. ただし、this does not mean "80% of one person's ADHD is genetic." Heritability describes variation within a population and environment, not a percentage recipe inside an individual.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A 2023 genome-wide association study analyzed 38,691 people with ADHD and 186,843 controls and identified 27 genome-wide significant loci. It also found evidence involving both common genetic variants and rarer variants. This does not mean there are "27 ADHD genes." It points to a complex, highly polygenic architecture.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Environmental associations are also studied. NIMH mentions areas such as brain injury, nutrition, and social environment, while the 2024 Nature review notes that environmental correlates have been identified but causal inference is difficult. 遺伝と環境をseparate boxesとして足すより、developmentの中でmany influences interactすると考えるほうが近い。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Here, "factor" becomes plural. Causal research looks less like finding one culprit and more like understanding how many small contributions shape developmental pathways. **High heritability and no single cause can both be true.**

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Demontis et al., Nature Genetics, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-022-01285-8) ／ [Franke et al., The genetics of ADHD in adults](https://www.nature.com/articles/mp2011138) ／ [Faraone et al., Nature Reviews Disease Primers, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41572-024-00495-0)

## 4. "ADHD = low dopamine" is clear, but too compressed

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
When we want one cause, "ADHD is dopamine deficiency" is a very tempting explanation. It is not pure fiction, but taken literally it compresses the evidence too far.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Dopamine is involved in reward learning, motivation, movement, attention, and many other functions. It has long been important in ADHD research. Stimulant medication also affects catecholamine signaling, including dopamine, which makes the story feel intuitive.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
However, a 2024 review of the dopamine hypothesis concluded that dopamine signaling is involved in ADHD, while evidence is limited for a simple hypodopaminergic state as the core feature of ADHD. Results vary across brain regions, development, methods, and people.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Treatment effect cannot be reverse-engineered into a complete cause. 頭痛にpainkillerが効くからといって、the cause of headache is not "painkiller deficiency." A treatment pathway can overlap with a causal pathway, but they are not automatically identical.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
"Dopamine" gives us one neat knob for a complex system. That simplicity is useful and dangerous at the same time. We need to separate **a mechanism that is involved** from **the one mechanism that explains everything**.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[MacDonald et al., The dopamine hypothesis for ADHD, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39619336/)

## 5. Executive function is important too, but multiple developmental pathways remain possible

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If dopamine is not the single key, maybe executive function is. Again: important, yes; universal master key, no.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Executive functions include maintaining goals, inhibiting responses, working memory, planning, and switching. On average, ADHD groups often perform worse on tasks involving inhibition, working memory, vigilance, and planning.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A meta-analysis of 83 studies found medium-sized weaknesses across several executive-function tasks in ADHD groups. But the authors concluded that these weaknesses were not universal and were neither necessary nor sufficient for every ADHD case. Another theoretical review proposed multiple developmental pathways, including executive-control problems and differences related to delayed reward.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This suggests different routes can arrive at a similar diagnostic destination. ある人ではworking memoryやinhibitionが大きく、別の人ではreward timingやstate regulationが目立つ可能性がある。Research is not yet at a point where every individual can be cleanly assigned to one mechanism.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
ADHD heterogeneity may not be mere noise waiting to disappear. It may be part of the condition itself. **People can meet the same diagnostic criteria without sharing exactly the same pathway to those symptoms.**

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Willcutt et al., Validity of the executive function theory of ADHD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15950006/) ／ [Sonuga-Barke, Causal models of ADHD: from common simple deficits to multiple developmental pathways](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15949993/)

## 6. ADHD traits look more like a continuum than a sealed box

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Another strange point: ADHD-like traits do not suddenly appear only inside people with an ADHD diagnosis.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Everyone can become inattentive, impatient, or restless to some degree. Research increasingly supports a dimensional view in which ADHD traits are distributed continuously in the population, rather than forming two perfectly separate natural kinds.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Taxometric analyses have found evidence for a dimensional latent structure across inattention, hyperactivity-impulsivity, and ADHD overall. A separate review also concluded that accumulated evidence fits a predominantly dimensional view better than a sharply discrete category.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
That does not make diagnosis meaningless. Continuous measures can still need clinical thresholds when impairment becomes serious. 血圧がcontinuousでもhypertensionというclinical cutoffを使うのと同じで、continuumとcategoryは両立する。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
ADHD diagnosis starts to look less like a thick border that nature drew in advance, and more like **a clinical boundary placed on a continuum to identify meaningful impairment and need for support.** Categories can be tools for handling continuous reality.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Marcus & Barry, Does ADHD have a dimensional latent structure?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20973595/) ／ [Coghill & Sonuga-Barke, Understanding ADHD as a continuum](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27965331/)

## 7. A diagnosis is not the final mechanism; it is a place to start looking more closely

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
This section is interpretation based on the research above. If we use ADHD as the final explanation for every behavior, the diagnosis becomes less informative.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
People with the same diagnosis can differ in inattentive versus hyperactive-impulsive features, executive functioning, sleep, anxiety or depression, learning difficulties, autism-related features, age, and environment. The visible difficulty can also change across contexts.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The 2024 Nature review describes ADHD heterogeneity across symptom presentation, impairment, co-occurring mental and physical conditions, neurocognitive domains, and small structural and functional brain differences. NICE also asks diagnostic assessment to include coexisting conditions, social and family circumstances, education or work context, and physical health.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So after the label, the useful question becomes more specific: in this person, under which conditions, which functions are connected to which difficulties? 診断名はa strong shared language。でも、それだけでindividual mechanismは自動入力されない。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**ADHD is closer to a legend on a map than a full explanation of your location.** The legend is essential. But staring at the legend alone does not tell you where you are. The value of diagnosis and the need to understand individual differences can coexist.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Faraone et al., Nature Reviews Disease Primers, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41572-024-00495-0) ／ [NICE, Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations)

## 8. After researching it, I want to use the word ADHD more narrowly and more carefully

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
I started with a binary question: is ADHD a bundle of phenomena, or is there one factor underneath it?

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
After researching, the answer split. There are statistical factors that organize symptoms. There are many genetic, developmental, and environmental risk factors. But there is no known single biological cause shared by everyone with ADHD, and the pathways leading to similar symptoms may differ.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So one word, ADHD, contains several levels: diagnostic criteria, symptom structure, genetics and development, and the individual's real-life difficulties. If we collapse them, we get neat stories such as "dopamine deficiency," "executive dysfunction," or "it's genetic." 分かりやすい。でもtoo narrow.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Before this research, ADHD looked either like a big bag or like one disease with one hidden cause. Now it looks different: **a clinically useful entrance that groups similar patterns of symptoms and impairment, with multiple roads continuing behind that entrance.**

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
So when someone asks, "What is the ADHD factor?", the best first move is to ask which meaning of factor we are talking about.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
There are factors that summarize symptoms. There are many factors that influence risk. There is no single known factor that explains every case. Once those three are separated, the label ADHD becomes not fuzzier, but more precise.
