# Count Something, Feel a Little Calmer
## 小さなルールは「次」を決めてくれる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
1, 2, 3, 4. The number goes up. The world barely improves.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
皿を一枚しまって “one,” 次で “two.” 階段、洗い物、机の上の物。The count itself has almost no meaning.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Still, a tiny rhythm sometimes appears. 少なくとも、次に何をするかで止まりにくい。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Maybe the useful part is not the number. Maybe it is **a short period in which the next move is already decided**.

So I looked at research on repetition, ritual, breath counting, attention, and action planning.

The first correction is important.

**There is no solid basis here for saying “count anything and anxiety will go down.”**

What we do have are several smaller findings that can be connected carefully.

> **情報基準日：2026年9月23日**
>
> This essay separates direct evidence from interpretation. 「儀式をすれば不安が下がる」という有名な研究の一つは2024年に撤回されているため、evidenceとして使わない。

## 1. Counting may matter because it decides the next move / 数えると「次」が決まる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
“Clean the room” and “put away ten things” point in the same direction, but they create different mental landscapes.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
「部屋を片づける」には、where to start, what matters first, when to stop, and what to do with every new problem が入っている。「10個戻す」なら、at least the finish line is already visible.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
One object becomes “1.” The next becomes “2.” The task is cut into discrete units.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
That does not guarantee efficiency. でも、曖昧な未来を “this move + a few more moves” に縮める。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Counting can be more than measurement. **It can divide the future into one-move pieces.**

以前、rhythmを「次の拍を予測できる時間構造」として考えた。

This time, there may be rhythm without a beat.

If the procedure is fixed, “next” still exists.

Call it **手順のリズム — procedural rhythm**.

## 2. Anxiety can make movement more repetitive and rigid / 不安なとき、動きは型に寄る

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
There is experimental evidence for one direction: anxiety can push behavior toward repetition.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In 2015, マーティン・ラング（Martin Lang）ら induced anxiety with a public-speaking paradigm and then measured hand movements while participants cleaned a decorative object. Higher induced anxiety increased **repetitiveness** and **rigidity** of movement. Redundancy itself did not show the same clear effect.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So anxiety did not simply produce “more useless actions.” Instead, movement became less variable and more stereotyped.
<!-- level:4 role:qualification -->
But the direction matters: this study mainly shows **anxiety → ritualized movement**. It does not prove **repetition → lower anxiety**.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Still, it is striking that under uncertainty, behavior can spontaneously narrow toward **more predictable patterns**.

[Lang et al., “Effects of Anxiety on Spontaneous Ritualized Behavior”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26096971/)

## 3. The convenient “rituals reduce anxiety” story was retracted / 都合のいい研究ほど疑う

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
During the search, I found a paper that fit this essay almost too perfectly. Then I found the retraction.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
A 2016 paper, “Don't Stop Believing: Rituals Improve Performance by Decreasing Anxiety,” reported that ritualized actions could reduce anxiety and improve performance.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The paper was retracted in 2024. Its ScienceDirect page now clearly marks it as retracted.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the neat story — “even arbitrary rituals calm us down” — cannot be used here as reliable evidence.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
This makes the argument less tidy, but better: **when a claim feels exactly right, separate the causal arrows before believing it.**

[Retracted paper page on ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074959781630437X)

A 2018 review by ニコラス・ホブソン（Nicholas M. Hobson）ら organizes ritual research around repetition, rigidity, formality, emotion, performance states, and social connection.

But it predates the 2024 retraction. It is useful as a conceptual map, not as proof that any arbitrary ritual reliably reduces anxiety.

[Hobson et al., “The Psychology of Rituals”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29130838/)

## 4. In breath counting, numbers become a return path for attention / 数字が注意の帰り道になる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Breath counting is much closer to the literal act of counting.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In 2014, ダニエル・レビンソン（Daniel B. Levinson）ら tested breath counting across four studies with more than 400 participants. In a representative task, participants repeatedly counted breaths from 1 to 9, used a different button for breath nine, and restarted at one after catching a counting error.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The design is interesting because the number never needs to grow forever. Nine returns to one. 注意がそれても、return point がある。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Breath-counting accuracy was associated with more meta-awareness and less mind wandering. In a four-week randomized online training study, the breath-counting group also showed reduced mind wandering relative to control conditions.
<!-- level:3 role:qualification -->
But again, “numbers caused calm” is too strong. 呼吸への注意、mind wanderingへの気づき、attentionを戻す反復が一つのtaskに入っている。The authors explicitly note that counting is not necessary for mindfulness.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
What counting clearly offers is a structure: **when attention wanders, the route back is already marked.**

[Levinson et al., “A Mind You Can Count On”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4208398/)

## 5. A rule can make a future decision in advance / 未来の判断を先に済ませる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Another research tradition studies what happens when we decide the cue and response before the moment arrives.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
ピーター・ゴルヴィッツァー（Peter M. Gollwitzer）の**実行意図（implementation intentions）** takes the form: “If situation X occurs, then I will perform behavior Y.”
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Not “I will exercise,” but “when I get home and take off my shoes, I will prepare for a five-minute walk.” Not “I will clean,” but “when I sit at my desk, I will sort three sheets of paper first.”
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Implementation-intention research shows that pre-linking a cue and response can facilitate action initiation. Gollwitzer describes this as delegating part of the control of goal-directed action to the anticipated situational cue.
<!-- level:3 role:qualification -->
This is research on goal pursuit, not a direct test of calmness. 「判断が減るから不安が下がる」とまでは言えない。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
But the design principle is useful: **a good rule stops future-you from reopening the same negotiation every time.**

[Gollwitzer, “Implementation Intentions: Strong Effects of Simple Plans”](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-strong-effects-simple-plans)

## 6. Good micro-rules let freedom rest locally / 自由を局所的に休ませる

From here, this is **my proposal**, not a validated psychological intervention.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If the goal is a calmer rhythm, the best rule may not be the strictest rule. It may be the rule that makes “what next?” temporarily unnecessary.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
I would design a short-lived **micro-rule** with five conditions.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
1. **One clear next move** — 「目についた物を1個戻す」.  
2. **One count or sequence** — “10 times” or “A → B → C.”  
3. **A finish line** — ten items, three rounds, five minutes.  
4. **No reset penalty** — 数え間違えても最初からやり直さない。  
5. **Always stoppable** — breaking the rule has no punishment or symbolic meaning.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Then the rule is not a cage. It only handles a few minutes of “what should I do next?”
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Freedom is not only having every option open. Sometimes freedom is choosing a small zone where you do not need to choose.**

片づけなら、  
“until the room is clean” ではなく “put away ten visible things.”

読書なら、  
“focus harder” ではなく “one page → one-line note, five times.”

仕事なら、  
“move the project forward” ではなく “read one page → write one thing I learned, ten times.”

The output may not be larger.

But “next” becomes smaller.

## 7. Test count vs. sequence in nine minutes / 9分だけ比べてみる

This is a self-experiment proposal, not a validated scale.

Use the same light task for three three-minute conditions.

**A: Free condition**  
「机を片づける」。No sequence, no count.

**B: Count condition**  
Put away one visible item and count 1 to 10. After 10, return to 1.

**C: Cycle condition**  
“Put one thing away → wipe one small area → pause once,” repeat.

After each three minutes, rate only three things from 0 to 10:

- 迷いの少なさ / less hesitation
- 続けやすさ / ease of continuing
- 心の静かさ / subjective calm

Do not conclude from one trial. Try it on a few days and look for a repeated pattern.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The question is not “which rule is most productive?”
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Some people may like counting. Others may find the count distracting. For some, a simple A→B→C loop may work better.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
You are trying to identify what your uncertainty is made of: invisible progress, no finish line, no return point, or too many choices.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Rule design can become **a tiny diagnostic experiment for where hesitation appears**, rather than another system you must obey.

## 8. If counting becomes compulsory, it is a different problem / やめられないルールは別物

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
There is an important boundary between a voluntary micro-rule and compulsive counting.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The U.S. National Institute of Mental Health lists **compulsive counting** among examples of compulsions in obsessive-compulsive disorder（強迫症、OCD）. OCD can involve repetitive behaviors that are difficult to control, time-consuming, distressing, or disruptive to daily life.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the rule in this essay is not “I must reach the correct number or something bad will happen.” It is the opposite: you can stop, miscount, skip, and continue with no penalty.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**A good rule gives you a place to return to, but never forces you to return.**

[NIMH, “Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder: When Unwanted Thoughts or Repetitive Behaviors Take Over”](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-when-unwanted-thoughts-or-repetitive-behaviors-take-over)

## 9. Numbers solve nothing, but they can solve “what next?” / 数は世界を直さない

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
I started with a small thought: maybe counting something is strangely calming.
<!-- level:2 role:analysis -->
The research did not reveal magic in numbers. Anxiety can make movement more repetitive and rigid, but that does not prove repetition reduces anxiety. Breath counting gives attention a return structure. Implementation intentions can pre-link future cues and actions.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So “counting feels calming” may bundle several different things: repetition, attention, predictability, finish lines, and pre-decided action.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
What I liked may not have been counting itself. It may have been this: **inside a tiny rule I chose, the future becomes readable one move ahead.**

1, 2, 3, 4.

The number grows.

The dishes remain.

The work remains.

But for one moment, “what do I do next?” is already answered.

**A rule does not control the future. It makes the future one move smaller.**

That changes how I see the rhythm of simple tasks.

Maybe we do not always calm down first and then move.

Sometimes, **because the next move is already decided, we can move — and calm down a little while moving.**

## AI prompt: Design a five-minute micro-rule / 5分間のマイクロルール設計

This is a practical prompt derived from the essay, not a clinical intervention.

> 今から取り組む曖昧な作業を、5〜15分だけ続けやすい「マイクロルール」に変換してください。  
> Goal is not maximum efficiency or strict self-control. Goal is to create a short period where I do not have to decide “what next?” every time.  
>
> 条件：  
> - next move を一文で言える  
> - use only one count or one sequence, such as “10 times,” “3 rounds,” or “A→B→C”  
> - 明確な終了条件をつける  
> - mistakes, miscounts, and interruptions never require restarting  
> - いつでも無罰でやめられる  
> - do not attach symbolic meaning such as “something bad happens if I fail”  
> - 複雑な最適化や点数化をしない  
>
> First, identify up to three decisions hidden inside the original task.  
> 次に、  
> 1. カウント型 / count rule  
> 2. 循環型 / cycle rule  
> 3. 「もしXならY」型 / if–then rule  
> の3案を出してください。  
> Each should fit in 5–15 minutes and explain in one sentence which uncertainty it removes.  
> 最後に、一番単純な案を一つだけ選び、成果量ではなく “Is the next move clear?” を基準にしてください。
