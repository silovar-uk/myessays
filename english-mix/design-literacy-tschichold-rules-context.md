# 「余白を増やして」は、なぜ危ない指示なのか――Why design rules need context
## Design Literacy #29｜Jan Tschichold, rules, reasons, and conditions

デザイン修正で、かなり普通に言う。

> もう少し余白を増やしてください。

Sounds reasonable. むしろ、ちょっとdesignを分かっている人っぽい。

But wait.

**Is more whitespace always better?**

もしそうなら、究極のgood designは白紙になる。

That seems inconvenient.

「左揃えの方が読みやすい」「グリッドは守る」「CTAは目立たせる」「情報量は減らす」。The more design rules we learn, the easier it becomes to speak like this.

ところがdesign historyを調べると、rulesを猛烈に体系化した人物が、後に自分のpositionを大きく更新している。

Jan Tschicholdである。

今回は1920s New Typographyと1940s Penguin Booksを同じ机に並べる。

すると、“correct design rule”という言葉が少し怪しくなる。

---

## 1. First, Tschichold in 1928 was very serious about rules

1920〜30年代のCentral Europeでは、いわゆるNew Typographyが展開した。MoMAは、traditional symmetrical columnsから離れ、typeやphotographyをasymmetrical compositionの中で組織し、visual and verbal communicationを明快にする運動として整理している。

Tschicholdは1928年の『Die Neue Typographie』で、その潮流をaccessible guidelinesとして体系化した。

He did not merely say, “This style looks modern.”

**He codified it.**

MoMA所蔵の1930年《Plakate der Avantgarde》では、black and red、horizontal / vertical type、asymmetry、simple elementsがrational compositionとして組み合わされている。MoMAも、この作品を1928年の原則を示す典型例として説明する。

New Typographyの背景にはindustrial printing、photomechanical reproduction、advertising、magazinesなどの変化があった。

つまり、they were not simply trying to make things look minimal.

**A new information environment seemed to require a new way of organizing information.**

### FACT

- Tschichold published *Die Neue Typographie* in 1928.
- New Typography emphasized asymmetrical composition, photography, standardized letterforms, and clarity of communication.
- His 1930 poster visibly demonstrates those principles.

### INTERPRETATION

現代に引き寄せるなら、left alignmentやsans serifそのものが本体ではない。The deeper question is **why those rules made sense for that information environment.**

---

## 2. Then the same man goes to Penguin—and makes more rules

1946年、TschicholdはPenguin Booksに招かれた。

This is where it gets strange.

Penguin already had a recognizable visual system: horizontal grids, genre colors, simple typography. ただしDesign Museumによれば、individual booksの細部にはeditorやprinterによるvariationがあった。

Tschicholdはfront, spine, backを統一し、titleとauthorのpositionを定め、Penguin symbolをredesignし、さらに**Composition Rules**を作った。

He made rules again.

この人、really liked rules.

でも1928年と1946年ではproblemが違う。

1928: reform an existing visual culture.

1946: stabilize quality across many books, editors, typesetters, and printers.

Same word—rule. Different job.

前者は**reform**。

後者は**system**。

Suddenly, a design rule looks less like an eternal aesthetic truth and more like a tool for solving a specific problem.

---

## 3. Did Tschichold simply abandon Modernism?

Not quite.

後年のTschicholdは、若い頃のNew Typographyから距離を取り、historical booksやclassical typographyを再評価した。

Letterform Archiveは、彼のlater positionについて、totalizing design systemsがauthoritarian logicを反復する危険への懸念と結び付け、historical referenceとaudience specificityを重視する方向へ進んだと整理している。

ただ、

“Young Tschichold = modern, old Tschichold = conservative”

では雑すぎる。

He did not stop caring about order. Penguin proves almost the opposite: he created a disciplined system.

### FACT

Later Tschichold showed strong interest in classical book typography and historical references. At Penguin, he also created systematic Composition Rules.

### INTERPRETATION

So his shift is more useful to us if we read it not as “rules → freedom,” but as **rule → context of application**.

---

## 4. Let’s abuse the whitespace rule

冒頭へ戻る。

> もう少し余白を増やしてください。

Let’s take it literally.

### A｜Whitespace = 0

```text
[企画名][日付][場所][説明][CTA][注意事項]
```

Hard to parse. Relationships collapse.

### B｜Moderate whitespace

```text
[企画名]
[日付・場所]

[説明]

[CTA]

[注意事項]
```

Groups become visible.

### C｜Whitespace × 100

```text
[企画名]







[日付・場所]







[説明]
```

Now the relationships disappear again.

So “increase whitespace” was never the real principle.

What we actually wanted was probably:

**Use distance to express relationships between elements.**

Spacing is not the goal. Grouping is the goal.

余白はgoalではなくvariableだった。

---

## 5. Store design rules as four-part models

ここまで来ると、rulesの覚え方を変えたくなる。

Do not store only this:

```text
RULE
余白を広げる
```

Store this instead:

```text
RULE
Increase spacing

WHY
To separate different information groups

CONDITION
When relationships between elements are ambiguous

EXCEPTION
In dense comparison views, too much spacing may reduce scanability
```

同じようにCTAなら、

```text
RULE
Make the CTA stronger

WHY
To place the primary action higher in the visual hierarchy

CONDITION
When the main action is visually buried

EXCEPTION
When several actions have equal priority or excessive emphasis interrupts reading
```

A little annoying? Yes.

But probably stronger than memorizing “100 rules every designer must know.”

**Rule + Why + Condition + Exception.**

ルールを単語ではなく、small causal modelとして保存する。

---

## 6. Before → After: even feedback changes

### Before

> もう少し余白を増やしてください。

### After

> 商品ごとの境界が分かりにくいため、カード間の余白をカード内部の要素間隔より明確に大きくしてください。The goal is not more whitespace itself, but a visible hierarchy between “between cards” and “inside a card.”

後者にはreasonがある。

More importantly, it leaves room for another solution.

Maybe a divider.

Maybe background contrast.

Maybe stronger headings.

If “more spacing” is the goal, the solution is already fixed. If “separate the cards” is the goal, the designer can still design.

This turns feedback from **solution instruction** into **problem sharing**.

---

## 7. Put Tschichold next to yesterday’s Weingart

前回のWolfgang Weingartから得たmodelは、

```text
Rule
↓
Variable
↓
Experiment
```

だった。

To break a rule meaningfully, you need to know what rule you are breaking.

Tschichold adds another layer above it:

```text
Problem
↓
Choose Rule
↓
Apply
↓
Observe
↓
Keep / Change Rule
```

Weingart asks: **How do you deviate?**

Tschichold makes us ask: **Why did you choose this rule in the first place?**

これはかなり嫌な進化だ。

「左揃えにした方がいいですよ」と気軽に言えなくなる。

Instead:

Why left-align?

What problem are we solving?

Does the rule still apply here?

面倒。でもresolutionは上がる。

---

## 8. “Learn the rules, then break them” is only half the lesson

Design advice often says: learn the rules before you break them.

Useful advice.

But there is one question before that:

**Why does this rule exist?**

Breaking a rule without knowing it can become chaos.

Following a rule without knowing why can become dogma.

Opposite-looking failures, same underlying problem: **neither is looking at context.**

制作指示ならこう変えられる。

> 「○○してください」だけで終わらせず、その変更で改善したい認知・情報・操作上のproblemを一つ明示する。そのうえで、そのruleが今回のcontextでも成立する理由を確認する。

Or even shorter:

> **What problem is this rule supposed to solve?**

---

## 9. After researching this, whitespace feels slightly more dangerous

最初はTschicholdの「思想の変化」について書くつもりだった。

1928 New Typography → later classical book typography.

A story about changing your mind.

But Penguin’s Composition Rules make the story stranger.

He did not stop making rules.

He made more.

So perhaps the problem is not having rules.

**The problem begins when we mistake a rule for the answer itself.**

Increase spacing.

Use a grid.

Align left.

Make the CTA prominent.

All useful.

But from tomorrow, add one small word after each rule:

**Why?**

余白を見るたびに、前より少し面倒になった。

That is probably progress.

---

## 中心命題 / Core proposition

**Do not collect design rules. Collect the reasons behind them.**

デザインルールだけでなく、そのruleが何をsolveし、どんなconditionで効き、どこでbreaks downするかまで覚える。

A good designer may not be the person who knows the most rules.

**A good designer knows when a rule applies.**
