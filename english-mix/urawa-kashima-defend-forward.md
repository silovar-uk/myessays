# Don't drop. Defend forward. — 浦和は「引かずに守る」ために3バックを使った

Kashima 0-1 Urawa.

For the first time this league season, Urawa kept a clean sheet.

しかも相手は、開幕5試合で13得点していた鹿島。Leo Ceara and Yuma Suzuki were waiting up front. 鹿島はホームで公式戦21試合 unbeaten だった。

The obvious headline is simple.

**Urawa changed to a back three.**

曺貴裁監督は3-4-2-1を採用し、試合後にはこの変更を**当日の朝に決めた**と明かした。

But “four defenders conceded, three centre-backs did not” is too easy.

I think the interesting part is different.

**The back three did not make Urawa passive. It helped Urawa defend forward.**

## Shape: three defenders against two forwards

Urawa started roughly like this.

```text
                Komori

          Kaneko      Watanabe

Hayashi     Ueki       Seko       Inagaki

       Nemoto    Boza    Miyamoto

               Nishikawa
```

Kashima used a 4-4-2 with Suzuki and Leo Ceara as the front two.

Three centre-backs against two forwards creates a simple advantage: **3v2 at the back**.

一人余れる。

That spare defender matters because another centre-back can step forward without leaving the entire line exposed. Wing-backs can also start higher instead of immediately becoming a flat back five.

So the system was not only a way to add one defender.

**It was a way to make aggressive defending safer.**

## The real first-half story was the high press

Urawa did not begin by sitting in a low 5-4-1.

They went forward.

Early in the game, Urawa pressed Kashima's build-up high and repeatedly pushed them toward longer passes. Match reports noted that Kashima increasingly used long balls to escape the pressure.

前から行く。

Make Kashima play long.

Win the first duel with three centre-backs.

Then bring the midfield back for the second ball.

That sequence was the mechanism.

The risk did not disappear. Danilo Boza was booked in the 17th minute after stopping Leo Ceara. If the first pressure is beaten, a high line means a centre-back may have to defend a strong forward in open space.

So this was not risk-free football.

**Urawa did not remove risk. They organised where to take it.**

## The goal was an own goal. The pressure around it was not an accident

The winner came in the 20th minute.

Urawa took a short corner, Ryoma Watanabe sent a dangerous ball across goal, and Kashima conceded an own goal.

Officially: own goal.

But an own goal does not mean the whole situation was random.

浦和は序盤から鹿島陣内へ出ていき、相手のミスから速く前進する場面も作っていた。その流れでCKを獲得し、ショートで角度を変えて、ゴール前へ速いボールを入れた。

**You cannot plan the exact mistake. You can create the place where mistakes become expensive.**

That was enough for 1-0.

## Defence continued after the ball was won

曺監督が試合後に強調したのは、プレスの成功だけではなかった。

He also pointed to what Urawa did **after winning the ball**. The movement of the ball in the first half often became a threat to Kashima.

This matters.

If a team adds defenders only to survive, the striker becomes isolated. Clear the ball, lose it, defend again.

Urawa tried not to fall into that loop.

Kaneko and Watanabe stayed close enough to the forward line. The wide players had starting positions from which they could move forward.

So even when Kashima had possession, Urawa had an exit.

**A counter-attacking threat is also part of defending.**

相手に「失ったら後ろを使われる」と思わせれば、鹿島も全員で自由に押し上がれない。

## Then Kashima found an answer inside

This was not a 90-minute tactical shutdown.

That distinction matters.

Kashima replaced Haruki Hayashi with Yuta Matsumura at half-time, then brought on Gaku Shibasaki and Kyosuke Tagawa in the 63rd minute.

曺監督の振り返りでは、前半と後半の立ち上がり15〜20分、鹿島はinsideへボールを集め、そこから危険な位置でクロスまで運ぶ場面を増やした。

That creates a different problem.

If Urawa simply forces the ball outside, three centre-backs can attack crosses with numbers.

But if Kashima first enter the half-space or central lane, Urawa's midfield has to narrow. Then the ball goes wide. The back line has already moved.

Now the cross arrives against a defence that is shifting, not waiting.

That is much harder.

Matsumura eventually had a major late chance and missed.

**Urawa kept a clean sheet. They did not completely erase Kashima.**

## The important part: Urawa adjusted again

This is where the win becomes more interesting.

曺監督は、鹿島にinsideからクロスまで持っていかれる時間があった一方、最後の15分には対応できるようになったと振り返った。

The opponent changed the question.

Urawa changed the answer again.

Watanabe went off for Matheus Savio at 60 minutes. Seko was replaced by Eiichi Katayama at 68. Kaneko made way for Harumi Minamino at 80.

The team refreshed both the defensive work and the attacking exit instead of simply dropping everyone closer to Shusaku Nishikawa.

And the score stayed 0-1.

Five league matches, 15 goals conceded before this night.

Then a clean sheet away to Kashima.

That is a meaningful change.

## Should Urawa now keep the back three forever?

Not so fast.

曺監督自身が、システム変更そのものを勝因として強く押し出していない。

He described a system as a simple way to show where players stand. The important thing is what players do from those positions. He also explained that this particular shape came from the opponent's weak points and the condition of his own players.

So the lesson is probably not:

**Back three = correct answer.**

The better lesson is:

**Urawa changed the shape without losing the principle.**

The principle was still to go forward.

Press forward.

Win the ball and look forward.

Keep an attacking exit even while protecting the lead.

## Not a back five story. A defend-forward story.

Before this match, Urawa had scored 11 and conceded 15 in five league games.

Attack was alive. Defence was leaking.

試合前、私は「攻撃の火を消さずに、最後の安い失点だけを削れるか」が鍵だと書いた。

That is roughly what happened.

But the method was bolder than I expected.

Change the back line.

Do not automatically drop deeper.

Press Kashima high.

Attack after winning the ball.

Suffer when Kashima find the inside.

Then adjust again.

**Urawa used three centre-backs, but they did not become a defensive team.**

Maybe the order is the other way around.

**They used three centre-backs so they could defend without becoming passive.**

That is the tactical sentence I want to keep from Kashima 0-1 Urawa.
