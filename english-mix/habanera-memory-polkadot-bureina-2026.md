# 曲名を忘れても、the sensuality remains――「無礼な！」からCarmenへ
知らない曲名なのに、I know this melody. ポルカドットスティングレイの「無礼な！」、2分09秒付近で強い既聴感が生まれた。浮かぶのは「ちゃっちゃちゃららら」という擬音だけ。Classic music? Circus? Sports day? しかもチョコレートのCMっぽい。検索するほど候補が増え、melody huntは迷子になった。

The turning point was not a genre or an instrument. It was the phrase「どこか色気がある」—a seductive feeling. その一言で、ビゼー『カルメン』の「ハバネラ」が浮かんだ。Why did an emotional cue work better than「運動会」? しかも、この有名曲 itself borrowed an earlier melody. たった一つの鼻歌が19世紀まで連れていく。Quite a journey for「ちゃっちゃちゃららら」。

An important boundary first: as of 9 November 2026, **official confirmation that「無礼な！」intentionally quotes the Habanera has not been found**. The starting point is a listener's recognition, not a production credit. 本稿ではdocumented facts、academic findings、personal interpretationを分ける。Mixing ideas is fine; mixing evidence levels is not.

## 1｜Dropping「運動会」as a category finally revealed the name

The clues changed their form. First came onomatopoeia—the contour of sound. Then situations: circus, sports day. Next came mood: playful but slightly sad, like a chocolate commercial. Finally, a sense of sensuality. 「剣闘士の入場」や「こんぺいとうの踊り」はpartial matchesだったが、決定打ではなかった。

![Memory search: onomatopoeia to situations, mood and sensuality](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/habanera-memory-search-2026.svg)

Figure 1: The sequence of clues in this conversation. This is a single search case, not experimental evidence.

「運動会」はconcrete-sounding but ambiguous. Was it an actual memory of a sports-day soundtrack, or just an imagined scene prompted by the rhythm? 「色気」はabstract, yet highly discriminating. The value of a clue depends not merely on how concrete it sounds, but on how effectively it separates possible answers.

## 2｜Habanera is not just a song title; it is a musical form that crossed the sea

Habanera refers both to the famous aria from『カルメン』and to a dance/song form associated with Havana, Cuba. In the nineteenth century, Caribbean musical cultures and European dance traditions interacted; the rhythm later travelled to Spain and France. A sound perceived as「Spanish」therefore carries a Cuban history too. Musical categories move more easily than national borders.

One audible clue is rhythm. A common habanera pattern uses a dotted eighth, sixteenth, eighth and eighth note: unequal durations with a characteristic catch. It does not march straight ahead. In『カルメン』the opening chromatic descent—notes stepping down by semitones—adds tension. Yet neither syncopation nor chromaticism automatically means「seductive」. Tempo, harmony, performance and cultural familiarity matter.

「色気」はnot a formal music-theory term, yet it may have bundled multiple musical features into one perceptual cue. Theory should not replace the listener's description; it helps unpack what the description might contain.

## 3｜Even Carmen's famous melody was adapted from an earlier song

In the 1860s, Spanish composer Sebastián Iradier wrote『El Arreglito』. Bizet adapted its melody for the Habanera in『Carmen』, premiered in 1875. Music theorist Andrew Pau notes that the borrowing was acknowledged in the score and that Bizet wrote much of the new text himself. The material was familiar; the dramatic context was new. [Research paper](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.15.21.3/mto.15.21.3.pau.html).

![A musical journey from Cuban dance form through Iradier and Bizet to a 2026 listening experience](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/habanera-melody-journey-2026.svg)

Figure 2: The first three nodes describe historically documented links. The final node is a listener's association, not proof of quotation by「無礼な！」.

If originality meant「borrow nothing」, this history would be awkward. But one melody can acquire a different function when assigned to a new character, text and audience. Pau reads Carmen's number as an onstage performance addressed to people watching her, rather than a private monologue. The creative change is not only in the notes: it is in what those notes do in a scene.

This does not mean any existing song is free to use. Historical borrowing and today's copyright rules are separate questions. We will return to that distinction.

## 4｜I cannot name it, but I know it: a puzzle in cognitive psychology

In psychology, **Recognition Without Identification (RWI)** means feeling that something is familiar without being able to identify it. Kostic and Cleary's 2009 paper tested the phenomenon with music across four experiments. Participants could discriminate familiar material even when song identification was very low. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19203174/).

The experiments included song fragments, notes separated from their original rhythms, and tapped rhythms. The effect shows that failure to name a tune is not the same as absence of musical memory. But this single conversation cannot establish which exact feature triggered recognition.

A title is a search label, not the feeling of familiarity itself. We also recognize faces while failing to retrieve names. Perhaps musical memory is less like a neat catalogue and more like a network of partial cues. That is an interpretation, not the direct conclusion of the experiments.

## 5｜Carmen and「無礼な！」may share an asymmetry of freedom

「無礼な！」was released on 30 October 2026 for Sanrio's rhythm game『Sanrio Kawaii Me Live!』. 雫 wrote the lyrics and music; the band arranged it. She describes a cute girl standing next to someone almost unbearably cute, and calls the fast arrangement a jazz/metal-like technical piece. [Artist comment](https://news.joysound.com/article/695851).

One short lyric says「可愛いはただただ無礼」. The other's charm is not literally an insult. Yet the admirer feels their emotions have been hijacked. I chose to be attracted—but it feels as if someone made me. ほぼ言いがかりなのに、妙に分かる。 [Lyrics source](https://www.uta-net.com/song/401408/).

By contrast, Carmen's Habanera asserts the unpredictable freedom of love. Another person's attraction does not create an automatic obligation to reciprocate. Music theory also treats her aria as a staged act of attention and seduction. Together, the works suggest an asymmetry: freedom for the person who attracts, felt constraint for the person attracted.

This comparison is **an interpretation, not an established authorial intention**. There is no verified statement that 雫 wrote the lyrics with Carmen's plot in mind. The opera also carries contested representations of gender and ethnicity. Feeling unable to control one's attraction is different from having a right to control another person. That ethical distinction matters.

## 6｜Finding a resemblance is not proof of a musical quotation

The reaction「That's the Habanera!」is a genuine listening experience. But proving quotation takes another method: compare pitch sequence, rhythm and phrase length against the original score or recording, then check production statements and credits. Recognition is a clue, not a verdict.

![Evidence ladder for distinguishing musical resemblance from intentional quotation](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/habanera-evidence-ladder-2026.svg)

Figure 3: A conceptual evidence ladder. Even strong evidence needs context; no single rung automatically proves quotation.

Three possibilities remain: (1) intentional quotation, (2) stylistic homage, or (3) resemblance arising from shared musical vocabulary or the listener's association. The supported wording is「the passage reminded me of the Habanera」. Saying「the band plays the Habanera」would overstate the evidence.

Historical borrowing and modern audio reuse are also legally distinct. In Japan, copyright generally lasts until 70 years after the creator's death, but modern recordings carry separate neighbouring rights held by performers and producers. [Agency for Cultural Affairs](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/chosakuken/taisetsu/point/) explains why a public-domain composition does not make every recording free to copy.

## 7｜The chocolate-commercial association exposes a marketing measurement problem

「Sounds like a Meiji chocolate commercial」was an association, **not evidence that the Habanera appeared in that campaign**. Yet it raises a useful question: when music carries a mood, what exactly is remembered—the tune, the ad, the category, or the brand?

A 2024 ad experiment compared happy and sad background music with 134 female participants. Sad music produced higher brand recognition; happy music produced higher purchase intention, while product recall was not affected by musical valence. In those conditions, **remembering a brand and wanting to buy did not move in the same direction**. [Study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691824002105).

Treating「it was memorable」as one metric mixes tune recognition, brand recognition, ad recall and purchase intention. A possible future test would separate audio-only brand identification from visual product recall. This is a proposed experiment, not one conducted here. If people love the song but forget the sponsor, the music may have gained a fan rather than the brand.

## 8｜In a rhythm game, music becomes part of the product itself

Sanrio plans a global launch of『Sanrio Kawaii Me Live!』in January 2027. Announced features include an accessible Performance Mode and a more demanding Technical Mode. The fast, technical「無礼な！」could fit that experience, but actual chart difficulty and player response remain to be observed after release. [Official announcement](https://www.sanrio-games.jp/news/00011.html).

In a conventional advertisement, music helps communicate another product. In a rhythm game, listening, playing and improving are parts of the product experience. The relationship reverses: instead of putting music in an ad, the service becomes a place people visit for music. Continued listening may create touchpoints beyond the game, though the size of that effect is not yet known.

The quotation question returns here. If an older melody is evoked within a new song, different generations may reach the same work through different memories. But since quotation in「無礼な！」remains unconfirmed, this is an exploratory hypothesis—not a quantified marketing forecast.

## 9｜The wrong guesses were data for reframing the question

At one point「剣闘士の入場」looked like the answer. Listening again overturned it. Had we defended the first hypothesis by cherry-picking similarities, the search would have stalled. Replacing a category—march music—with a richer set of cues made a new candidate possible.

The same issue appears in consulting. When a client says「Excel is hard to use」, buying a new tool may miss an approval-process or data-quality problem. Treat the statement as a clue to experience, not an automatic diagnosis. In music search, follow-ups such as「Does the phrase rise or fall?」or「Is the sensuality in the rhythm or the melody?」can be more diagnostic.

- Observe: record the passage, tempo and melodic movement actually heard.
- Classify: separate imagined scenes from musical features.
- Falsify: check where the leading candidate does **not** match.
- Qualify: without production evidence, say「resembles」rather than「quotes」.

Real progress may be noticing that the current question cannot produce the answer. A wrong guess is useful when we can specify why it failed. Yet not every detour deserves a heroic story: direct audio comparison could well have been faster. Good research learns from its mistakes without romanticizing inefficiency.

## 10｜After finding the name, the first few notes sound different

What we wanted was not merely the word「ハバネラ」. It was an explanation for the playful, melancholic, slightly provocative sound that had stayed in memory. Once named, that sound acquires a history: Bizet, Iradier, and a dance form with Cuban roots. It also acquires a limit: we still cannot call the「無礼な！」passage a confirmed quotation.

After recovering the name, the nonsense syllables sound different. 「ちゃっちゃちゃららら」was not an empty imitation; it was a surviving piece of musical memory without its label. Sometimes what we have forgotten is not the music, but how to explain what we remember.

## Research note｜What is verified and what remains open

**Verified:**「無礼な！」was released for a Sanrio game on 30 October 2026; 雫 describes affection entangled with frustration. Habanera is a form with Cuban roots, and Bizet adapted Iradier's『El Arreglito』. Recognition Without Identification has been studied experimentally in music.

**Interpretation:** the two works may share an asymmetry between attraction and freedom;「sensuality」proved useful in one search experience. **Unverified:** note-for-note matching at 2:09, intentional borrowing by the band, or any specific chocolate-ad usage. These are not presented as facts.
