# What Game Do I Want to Play?
## ダックハントでカンと一緒に動く

When I play Duck Hunt, I sometimes watch the tin can more than the dog.

ちょっとおかしい。

This is supposed to be a fighting game.

なのに画面の端で転がっている空き缶の位置をずっと気にしている。

At first, I thought the Can was simply a projectile to hit the opponent with.

最近、それだけではない気がしている。

**The Can changes the shape of the game.**

だから、Canを出したあとに止まらない。

Keep moving.

**Move with the Can.**

And before all of that, there is a bigger question.

> **What game do I want to play in this match?**

この問いが決まると、Duck Huntの行動が少し一本につながる。

---

## 1. If you think “shoot the Can,” the game becomes too small

Duck Hunt's Neutral Special, Trick Shot, kicks out a tin can and lets you keep moving it with later special inputs.

ゲーム内Tipsでも、Canはshotや通常攻撃で動かしながらadvantageous positionへ置くものとして説明されている。

SmashWiki describes Duck Hunt as a character built around zoning, setups and stage control with several projectiles.

So the Can is not just one shot.

**It is a movable object that stays in the game for a while.**

「相手へ当てる」だけで考えると、

Can goes right.

相手が避ける。

Shoot again.

また避けられる。

Then the Can becomes a slightly complicated fireball.

でも問いを変える。

**What does the opponent have to think about because the Can exists here?**

Canの向こうへ行きたくない。

They have to watch the Can and the body at the same time.

ジャンプしたくなる。

They may shield more.

相手のchoicesが少しずつ偏る。

The value of the Can is not only its hit rate.

**It adds another problem to the screen.**

---

## 2. After spawning the Can, move yourself too

自分の中で、まず試したいruleがある。

> **Spawn the Can, then move.**

Canと同じ場所に固まらない。

If possible, move around to the other side.

すると本体とCanが、same lineから相手を追うだけではなくなる。

Body from one side.

Can from another position.

Now the opponent has to read two spatial relationships.

ただし、ここは調べ直して一つ大事なcorrectionがあった。

**Simply moving Duck Hunt to the other side does not automatically reverse the Can's shot direction.**

Trick Shot can reverse the direction of later shots when the Can is hit from the opposite side.

なので正確には、

```text
spawn Can
→ reposition yourself
→ create access to the opposite side
→ touch / hit the Can from there when needed
→ reverse its future direction
```

という流れになる。

This lets you create situations where the Can can come back toward your side.

「反対側へ行く」こと自体がmagicなのではない。

**Moving there creates a board position where you can choose the Can's next direction.**

The important thing is spatial relationship, not a memorized input sequence.

---

## 3. Do not separate “Can time” and “body time”

When I first learned Duck Hunt, I mentally separated the match.

Set up the Can.

Move the Can.

Okay, setup done.

Now fight with the body.

たぶん、この分け方が弱い。

A stronger state is:

**while controlling the Can, the body is already doing another job.**

Press B and close distance.

Canへattentionを向けさせながらjumpを見る。

Move into the place that punishes how they try to deal with the Can.

The Can changes what the body can do.

本体が動くことでCanの意味も変わる。

Do not treat them as two separate phases.

**Think of the dog and the Can as one system.**

犬にはアヒルも乗っているので、already a crowded systemではある。

---

## 4. Keep more than one believable intention alive

ここからDuck Huntの外へ少し広がる。

Suppose I want the opponent to jump.

そこで露骨にanti-airだけ待つ。

They will probably notice quickly.

Instead, make one movement support several meanings.

Walk forward.

Maybe it is an approach.

Maybe I am going to touch the Can.

Maybe I am watching a roll.

Maybe I am preparing to retreat.

**Do not let the opponent reduce your movement to one obvious intention.**

Research on deception in interactive sports often separates two ideas.

One is hiding cues that reveal your real intention.

もう一つは、misleading cueを出して別の意図に見せること。

Basketball head fakes and hiding whether a volleyball attack will become a smash or a lob are classic examples.

This maps surprisingly well to Smash.

The goal is not merely “do one fake.”

**Delay the moment when the opponent can confidently identify your intention.**

Canへ触りに行くように見せてapproachする。

Approachに見せてCanを返す。

Looks like a Can shot, then do nothing.

理想は、**one movement, several valid futures**である。

---

## 5. Be unreadable, not random

ここで罠がある。

If hiding intention means “do random things,” you lose your own plan.

それはdeceptionではなく迷子である。

Game theory uses the idea of a mixed strategy: several possible actions remain in play rather than one perfectly predictable pure action.

An especially useful interpretation is that mixed strategy does not have to mean literally rolling dice in your own head.

It can describe **the opponent's uncertainty about what you will choose next.**

この感覚がかなり近い。

I know what I ultimately want.

But the opponent cannot fully know it yet.

That is the state I want.

So the rule is not:

> Have no plan.

It is almost the opposite.

> **Have a clear plan. Hide which route leads to it.**

狙いは強く持つ。

But do not expose the whole route too early.

---

## 6. So, what game do I want to play?

ここが一番大事。

Not “What do I want the opponent to do?”

その一歩前にある。

> **What kind of game do I want this match to become?**

For Duck Hunt, maybe it is one of these.

### A game where the body and Can are not in the same place

相手がone directionだけ見れば済む時間を減らす。

Body here.

Can elsewhere.

Deal with one and the other still exists.

### A game where walking on the ground has a small tax

Can and Gunman interfere with grounded movement.

If the opponent starts jumping more, the body can prepare for that.

The projectile does not need to hit.

**It only needs to change how the opponent travels.**

### A game where shield is useful, but not free

Projectiles create shield pressure.

するとbody movement、grab、empty movementに価値が出る。

You are not merely hitting shield.

You are changing which defensive answer feels natural.

### A game where solving one problem creates the next one

They look at the Can.

You approach.

They look at the body.

You move the Can.

The answer to one problem becomes the entry point to another.

ここまで来ると、技選びの順番が変わる。

Not:

> What move should I use?

But:

> **What game am I trying to build?**

Then choose the move that builds it.

---

## 7. Build a small game inside the big game

The formal rules of Smash do not change.

Take stocks. Survive. Win.

でも、その中で何を何度も起こすかは変えられる。

A close-range specialist wants repeated close-range situations.

A ledge specialist wants repeated ledge states.

For Duck Hunt, I probably want to maximize time where:

**the Can exists, the body is moving, and the opponent has to process more than one threat.**

大きなgameの中に、自分が得意なsmall gameを作る。

Then replay that small game again and again.

Maybe learning a character is not only learning combos.

**It is learning which situations you want to reproduce.**

---

## 8. Review the match by asking whether you got your game

試合後の見方も少し変わる。

Did I miss a move?

Did I fail to kill?

大事。

But first, ask bigger questions.

- After spawning the Can, did I stop moving too?
- Did the body and Can chase from the same direction all the time?
- Did I move myself to change what the Can could do next?
- Did one movement preserve several options?
- Did my real intention become obvious too early?
- Most importantly: **How much time did I spend playing my game?**

Sometimes you win while spending the whole match inside the opponent's preferred game.

Sometimes you lose, but successfully create your own game for long stretches.

The second loss may contain more useful information.

---

## 9. One rule for the next match

全部やろうとすると無理なので、一つだけ。

> **When I spawn the Can, I move too.**

それだけ。

Then add:

> **Do not keep the Can and body in the same place.**

Then:

> **How many intentions can my movement plausibly represent?**

And before the match begins, ask once:

> **What game do I want to play today?**

If that answer is clear, Can placement, movement and timing start to connect.

Duck Hunt may not simply be a character who throws a tin can.

**The Can is a way to build the game you want to play onto the stage.**

少なくとも今は、そう考えて使ってみたい。
