# CAPEX vs. OPEX: Who Pays for the Machine, and Who Owns the Risk?

*English Mix edition, 29 October 2026. Based on publicly available sources. Factual accounting rules, hypothetical examples, and forecasts are distinguished.*

“Should we buy the video system for ¥12 million, or pay ¥250,000 a month for a service?” At first glance, this sounds like a question about payment schedules. しかし、二つの選択肢は、単なる一括払いと分割払いではない。

When you buy equipment, you generally take responsibility for ownership, maintenance, and replacement. With a service, the real question becomes who guarantees uptime and continuous improvement. **This is about the allocation of capital, risk, and control.** 何を費用と呼ぶかだけでは決まらない。

We will follow three steps: **Shu（守）** for accounting fundamentals, **Ha（破）** for business-model implications, and **Ri（離）** for broader connections to AI investment and strategic flexibility. フォトロンのような映像技術企業がスポーツ市場で「何を売るか」を考えるための論考でもある。

## 守｜CAPEX and OPEX are not synonyms for upfront and monthly

CAPEX stands for **capital expenditure**—spending on assets intended to generate value over several periods. Think cameras, broadcast equipment, servers, or venue upgrades. Under [IAS 16](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-16-property-plant-and-equipment/), recognition depends on matters such as probable future economic benefits and reliable measurement of cost. 日本語では「資本的支出」と説明される。

OPEX means **operating expenditure**, money spent to run the business: cloud services, routine maintenance, consumables, and personnel. ただし、経営上のOPEXと財務諸表上の「営業費用（operating expenses）」は常に同義ではない。Depending on the business, a cost can also belong to cost of sales.

Here is the critical distinction: **expenditure is not the same as expense**. 支払った日と費用を認識する日は一致しない。Buying a machine with cash does not necessarily create an equal expense in the same year; conversely, an expense can be recognized before the cash is paid.

## 守｜The same ¥12 million looks different in profit and cash flow

Take a hypothetical ¥12 million video system. Assume a five-year useful life, zero residual value, and straight-line depreciation. **Cash leaves immediately, but depreciation is ¥2.4 million a year for five years.** これは制度上の耐用年数を示したものではなく、説明用の仮定だ。

Now imagine a service delivering the same function at ¥250,000 per month for five years. Under a simplified service-expense assumption, that is ¥3 million a year and **¥15 million in total**—¥3 million more than the purchase price. だから「OPEX化＝低コスト」は成り立たない。ただし保守、更新、障害対応、陳腐化リスクへの対価が含まれているなら、差額の評価は変わる。

Look at three statements, not one. The **P/L（損益計算書）** shows depreciation or service cost. The **B/S（貸借対照表）** shows assets and, in some cases, lease liabilities. The **cash flow statement（C/F）** shows real cash movements. Under [IAS 7](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows.html/), cash paid to acquire fixed assets is generally an investing cash flow, while ordinary service payments are generally operating cash flows.

EBITDA—earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization—adds depreciation back, while ordinary service expenses still reduce earnings. **A choice that looks better on EBITDA may be worse for cash today.** 税効果、金利、保守費、割引率、残存価値は、この例では省略している。A real investment decision should compare discounted cash flows rather than merely the payment labels.

## 守｜SaaS is often a service, but a monthly fee is not automatically OPEX

SaaS means **Software as a Service**. Users usually access software over a network instead of owning and installing the underlying application. In a [2019 IFRS Interpretations Committee decision](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2019/ifric-update-march-2019/), a contract granting only future access to the supplier's cloud-hosted software was treated as a service, not as an acquired software asset. ただし、ソフトウェアの支配や特定の使用権が顧客へ移る場合、検討は変わる。

What about implementation fees? A [2021 IFRS agenda decision](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2021/ifric-update-march-2021/) explains that configuration and customization costs depend on whether a separate intangible asset is controlled and whether the services are distinct. **Even a prepaid service can initially create a prepayment asset.** 「初期導入費」だから必ず資産、あるいは必ず費用とは言い切れない。

The words “subscription,” “monthly fee,” and “setup fee” are not accounting conclusions. **Ask what the customer controls, when services are received, and what remains as an asset.** なお、ここでの原則は主に国際会計基準を参照しており、日本基準・税法・個別企業の会計方針とは区別する必要がある。

## 守｜The boundaries: repair, improvement, and leases

A repair is not always an operating expense. Japan's National Tax Agency explains that ordinary maintenance and restoration are generally repair costs, but spending that extends useful life or increases asset value can be capital expenditure. **Substance matters more than the invoice label.** [国税庁・公式解説](https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/hojin/5402.htm)

Leasing adds another complication. Under [IFRS 16](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-16-leases/), a lessee generally recognizes a **right-of-use asset** and a **lease liability** when a qualifying lease provides the right to use an identified asset. There are short-term and low-value exceptions. 月々の支払いがある、という事実だけでは会計上の結論にならない。

Consider a dedicated camera installed at one stadium, where the club substantially controls its use, versus access to a shared analytics platform operated for many venues. Their economic substance may differ. **Who directs the use of an identified asset?** ここでは判断軸を示す架空の例であり、具体的な契約の会計処理を確定するものではない。

## 守｜A budget is also an organizational decision, not just an amount

Some companies allocate capital investment and operating budgets to different departments and approval processes. A major system purchase might need board-level approval; a smaller recurring service might be approved locally. これは会社によって異なる**組織設計上の仮説**であり、OPEXとCAPEXの会計定義から自動的に決まるわけではない。

Subscriptions may lower the entry barrier, but an uncancellable five-year contract still creates a major economic commitment. **Annual affordability is not the same as total affordability.** 解約条件、値上げ、データ移行、撤去費用まで含めて比較すべきだ。

Do not ask only, “Do you have a capital budget?” Ask **who signs the contract, what term can be approved, who handles failure, and what happens at the end**. 単価より先に意思決定の仕組みを知る。It is a procurement question as much as an accounting question.

## 守｜History: selling usage instead of machines is not a new invention

The ownership-to-usage shift predates cloud computing. Xerox launched its 914 copier in 1959, using leasing and charges tied to copy volume to make an expensive machine commercially accessible. **The customer needed copies, not necessarily ownership of the copier.** [Strategyzer's Xerox business-model history](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/xerox-business-model)

Rolls-Royce introduced **Power-by-the-Hour** in 1962. Its original idea was engine replacement and maintenance under a fixed cost per flying hour. The incentives shifted toward reliable engine performance. 「機械の売上」と「機械が働き続ける成果」の距離を縮めた歴史的な例だ。[Rolls-Royce公式説明](https://www.rolls-royce.com/media/press-releases-archive/yr-2012/121030-the-hour.aspx)

In 1988, Sandra Vandermerwe and Juan Rada described **servitization**—adding services to products and transforming customer relationships. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1016/0263-2373(88)90033-3). つまり、「物が突然消えた」のではない。**The business question evolved from selling an asset to recovering its value through use and outcomes.**

## 破｜Moving CAPEX away from customers does not make hardware disappear

A football club may avoid purchasing cameras by subscribing to a video service. Yet someone must still fund cameras, storage, networks, and processing capacity. **Customer-side CAPEX can become provider-side CAPEX and working-capital needs.** 設備が不要になったのではなく、負担する主体が変わる。

An equipment sale may generate a large payment upfront. In a subscription model, the provider may pay for equipment today and recover the cost over several years. **Revenue growth can coexist with cash shortages.** 売上高や会計上の利益とは別に、投下資本の回収期間（payback period）を測る必要がある。

The upside is important, too. A provider responsible for ownership and maintenance has reasons to improve reliability, extend equipment life, support remote upgrades, and share assets across customers. **Reliability becomes an economic incentive**, not merely an after-sales promise. 故障から修理売上を得る商売とは、動機づけが変わり得る。

The slogan “Turn customer CAPEX into OPEX and create recurring revenue” is only half a business case. **Recurring revenue is not the same as healthy capital returns.** 保守、資金調達、解約、未回収設備、技術更新のリスクを価格へ反映しなければならない。

## 破｜Three business models, compared on the same dimensions

Model one is **equipment sales**. The club buys cameras, recording systems, and software, with maintenance contracted separately. 初期投資は大きいが、機器の使用・更新の主導権を持ちやすい。一方で技術が古くなるリスクも購入者に残る。

Model two is **a managed monthly service**. The provider installs and operates the system, while the club pays for video access, analytics, and support. 導入の初期負担を減らせる可能性がある半面、提供側の資金負担、解約、設備撤去、サービス水準保証（SLA）が重要になる。Lease accounting must be assessed separately.

Model three is **shared infrastructure or usage-based pricing**. A league, venue, or group of clubs shares systems and pays per match, camera-hour, analysis job, or stored video volume. **Utilization drives the economics.** 稼働率を高められる反面、試合が同じ週末に集中すれば設備不足が起きる。

A fair comparison needs total cost of ownership（TCO）, customer cash outlay, provider working capital, equipment utilization, upgrade responsibility, cancellation flexibility, margins, data governance, and exit arrangements. 月額単価だけで優劣を決めるのは、映画館を座面の価格だけで評価するようなものだ。

## 破｜For a sports-video company, the unit of sale itself can change

Hardware-led proposals often begin with “How many cameras and recorders per venue?” But the customer's job may be to capture reliable referee-review footage, create a clip quickly, deliver it to analysts, or make video usable for broadcasting and advertising. **Customers may buy an outcome, not an object.** この見方から、商品を再設計できる。

Consider three pricing units: **per installation, per match, or per defined outcome**. The first is easy to specify. The second follows utilization. The third could promise a review clip within an agreed time or delivery at an agreed quality—while raising difficult questions about measurement and liability. これらは事業構想上の仮説であり、フォトロンの実際の現行契約を示していない。

Sports demand has overlapping layers: permanent stadium systems and temporary event systems, league-wide procurement and club-level purchases, broadcast uses and competition operations. A TAM model based on **number of clubs × monthly price** may double-count the same purchasing opportunity. 誰が契約し、誰が予算を持ち、どの用途が一つの契約に束ねられているかを確かめる必要がある。

Start with interviews about current equipment acquisition, replacement, and maintenance. Then estimate required operating hours by use case. Finally, compare five-year profitability and cash flow under sale and service models. **First discover a unit that a real customer will buy.** 大きな市場規模の数字より、ここが事業仮説を強くする。

## 破｜Forecast: the competitor is not always another camera manufacturer

First hypothesis: a video-technology company may increasingly compete and partner with software suppliers, production companies, venue operators, and cloud platforms. **As the product shifts from camera hardware to reliably usable video, the capability stack expands.** これは契約単位の変化から導く予測であり、確定した市場動向ではない。

Second hypothesis: providers operating equipment across venues will compete on utilization, replacement cycles, and maintenance intelligence. スポーツでは大会日程に需要が集中する。Moving equipment between sites may create value, but simultaneous events impose a hard capacity limit. Shared infrastructure is not a free lunch.

Third hypothesis: **access to financing becomes part of technology competitiveness**. Two companies with similar cameras can offer very different five-year contracts if one can fund the hardware upfront. ただし利用率が低ければ損失も大きくなる。The hypothesis can be tested using initial fees, churn, utilization, and capital payback months.

## 離｜The AI paradox: more cloud can mean more capital expenditure

Cloud marketing often highlights that the customer no longer needs to own servers. But cloud providers invest heavily in chips, power, cooling, networking, and data centers. **Capital spending has not vanished; it has become concentrated elsewhere.** 個別企業の設備投資が減ることと、社会全体の設備投資が減ることは同じではない。

Microsoft's [FY2025 annual report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/index.html) describes continuing capital investment in data centers and computing systems to support cloud and AI growth. An October 19, 2026 [J.P. Morgan research outlook](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/artificial-intelligence/ai-capex) estimated global AI CAPEX at around **$900 billion in 2026**, and cumulative spending of roughly **$5.5 trillion through 2030**. いずれもJ.P. Morganの**予測・推計**であって、確定した実績ではない。

The customer's service may look light because the provider's infrastructure is heavy. スポーツ映像クラウドでも同じ構図があり得る。The strategic question becomes **where to pool investment to gain efficiency**, not how to eliminate every camera at every venue.

## 離｜The true product may be flexibility, not the absence of ownership

Owning equipment can reduce the marginal cost of each use. But new standards emerge, demand changes, and an event strategy can shift. A service may offer more than financing and maintenance: **the ability to change course later**. その価値は単純な月額比較には現れにくい。

This relates to **real options**—the value of delaying, expanding, reducing, or abandoning an investment under uncertainty. But a non-cancellable five-year subscription may offer very little flexibility. **OPEX does not create flexibility by magic; contract design does.** 月額という支払形態と、撤退できる権利を混同してはいけない。

Servitization also keeps the supplier in contact with the customer after installation. Usage and failure data can improve the product, while the customer may become more dependent on that supplier. 売上の継続性だけでなく、**who learns, who gains bargaining power, and who can leave**という問いが残る。That is the deeper economics of ownership versus service.

## 実務｜Six questions for the next strategy meeting

1. **Who invests?** 顧客、販売会社、リーグ、会場運営者、金融会社の誰が資産を所有し、資金を出すか。
2. **What is the pricing unit?** 設備、使用時間、試合、解析件数、稼働保証、業務成果のどれに支払うか。
3. **Where does the cost appear?** P/L、B/S、C/Fと、実際の社内予算・承認者を分けて調べたか。
4. **What is the five-year TCO?** 購入、保守、クラウド、更新、撤去、移行、金利、残存価値、税効果を比較したか。
5. **Who bears risk?** 故障、陳腐化、需要変動、情報漏えい、解約、引き継ぎの責任分界は明確か。
6. **What could disprove the thesis?** 稼働率、導入率、獲得費、解約率、回収月数、障害時間、再契約率を測定するか。

“From CAPEX to OPEX” makes a convenient sales headline, but it does not prove that a business model is better. **Who puts money at risk first? Who manages uncertainty? What outcome does the customer receive?** この三つに答えて初めて、equipment saleとservice contractの比較ができる。
