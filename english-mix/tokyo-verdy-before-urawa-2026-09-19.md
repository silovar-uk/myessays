# 東京ヴェルディは、アウェイでまだ一度もスコアを動かしていない

## Seven league matches, three goals, all by defenders｜浦和戦前に見る defensive stability と attacking rebuild

Tomorrow, September 19, Urawa Reds face Tokyo Verdy at Saitama Stadium. Verdy arrive with a very strange number.

**In league away matches this season, they have not scored once. But they have not conceded once either.**

0-0 at Okayama. 0-0 at Cerezo Osaka. Tokyo Verdy's first 180 away minutes are literally zero goals for, zero goals against.

さらに妙なのは、their three league goals. By the J.League's registered positions, Naoki Hayashi has two and Shuhei Mizoguchi has one. **All three were scored by registered defenders.** No registered forward has scored yet.

[J.League official: Tokyo Verdy](https://www.jleague.jp/club/tokyov/)

If you read only "19th place, 0 wins, 4 draws, 3 losses," the story looks simple. They cannot win.

But split the numbers and the shape changes: three goals and nine conceded at home, zero and zero away, with scoring concentrated in defenders. Tokyo Verdy do not look uniformly bad. They look like a team whose problems are **highly uneven depending on location and phase of play.**

---

## 1. FACT｜Split seven matches into home and away, and it looks like two teams

At home: 1-1 Kawasaki Frontale, 1-3 Kashiwa Reysol, 0-2 Kashima Antlers, 0-2 Vissel Kobe, 1-1 JEF United Chiba. Five games: 0 wins, 2 draws, 3 losses, three scored, nine conceded.

Away: 0-0 Fagiano Okayama and 0-0 Cerezo Osaka. Two games: 0 wins, 2 draws, 0 losses, zero scored, zero conceded.

[Tokyo Verdy official: 2026/27 match schedule](https://www.verdy.co.jp/match/schedule/)

Of course, two away matches are too small a sample to conclude that Verdy are "strong away from home." Opponents and game states differ.

Still, it matters before Urawa. What Verdy have achieved away so far is not explosive attack. It is **180 minutes of refusing to let the match break open.**

A 19th-place team coming to Saitama can easily be imagined as an opponent Urawa should simply push back and crack. But in league away games this season, nobody has moved Verdy's scoreboard yet. Before the table, that is the first thing worth seeing.

---

## 2. FACT｜All three goals came from defenders; the front line works, but does not score

Verdy's three league goals are Mizoguchi against Kawasaki, and Hayashi against Kashiwa and Chiba.

Mizoguchi is registered as a defender by the J.League. Hayashi is also a defender. So in league play, **the attacking unit has not yet produced a goal in the official scoring column.**

[J.League official: Shuhei Mizoguchi](https://www.jleague.jp/player/1636235/)
[J.League official: Naoki Hayashi](https://www.jleague.jp/player/1632226/)

Itsuki Someno is the clearest example. He has played all seven matches, with 97 total sprints and 33 aerial duels won. But he has zero goals. He presses, competes for long balls and acts as a post player, yet the finish has not arrived.

[J.League official: Itsuki Someno](https://www.jleague.jp/player/1629408/)

In August, coach Hiroshi Jofuku explained that overloading Someno with every aerial contest could leave him unavailable when the team really needs him. He discussed balancing runs in behind, long balls toward Someno and ground combinations, while adding other players who can compete physically.

[Tokyo Verdy official: Jofuku pre-match interview before Kashiwa](https://verdy.co.jp/news/15157)

So "the forwards do not score" is not the same as "the forwards do nothing." The more interesting issue is that a front line asked to defend, connect and contest has not yet received a clean final role: **who actually finishes the move?**

---

## 3. OVERKILL RESEARCH｜Add every shot, and the Chiba match alone becomes almost one third

Now for slightly unnecessary arithmetic.

Verdy's league shot totals: 14 vs Kawasaki, 11 vs Kashiwa, 5 vs Okayama, 4 vs Kashima, 6 vs Kobe, 5 vs Cerezo, 21 vs Chiba. Total: 66.

[J.League official: Tokyo Verdy fixtures and results](https://www.jleague.jp/club/tokyov/)

The most recent Chiba match alone produced 21. **That is about 32% of all Verdy league shots across seven matches.**

This does not prove the attack is fixed. Shot quality, location and opponent behaviour all matter. Volume alone cannot certify improvement.

But the jump matters after 4 shots against Kashima, 6 against Kobe and 5 against Cerezo. Even with 21 attempts, the only goal came from Hayashi in the 90+2nd minute. The number of attacks changed; the identity of the finisher did not.

[J.League official: Tokyo Verdy vs JEF United Chiba](https://www.jleague.jp/match/j1/2026/091302/preview/)

So Verdy may be moving from "a team that cannot generate enough" toward "a team generating more, but still unsure who ends the action." That is only a one-match signal, not a settled trend. But the location of the problem may have moved forward.

---

## 4. TACTICS｜Rather than destroy the 3-4-2-1, they are looking for more bodies near goal

Verdy's base shape is 3-4-2-1. Against Chiba, Someno played centrally with Hayato Hirao and Yuya Fukuda around him.

[Tokyo Verdy official: Tokyo Verdy vs JEF United Chiba](https://www.verdy.co.jp/match/info/2026091302/result)

After Kazunari Ichimi joined, Jofuku said a two-striker shape is always in consideration. But the point is not to throw away previous work. He wants more than one striker-type player involved, with two or three players entering the box while keeping the accumulated base.

[Tokyo Verdy official: Jofuku pre-match interview before JEF United Chiba](https://www.verdy.co.jp/news/15221)

That distinction matters.

Tokyo Verdy are not trying to become a completely different football team overnight. They are keeping front-foot defending, a back-three structure, running and intensity, while trying to **add one more finishing presence near goal.**

When results are poor, outside analysis often jumps to "change the system." Jofuku's public comments suggest a narrower internal question: how do you add the last runner without deleting the structure that still works?

---

## 5. ROSTER｜Injuries arrive, then Ichimi and Fujimoto arrive almost immediately

This attacking redesign is happening while the squad itself is moving.

On September 10, Verdy announced Kazuya Miyahara had suffered an ACL and meniscus injury, Koki Morita a tibial stress fracture, and Shimon Teranuma a lateral meniscus injury. Morita and Teranuma were listed at 8-10 weeks, Miyahara at 8-10 months. Hiroto Yamami had already been announced in May with an ACL injury and an expected absence of about eight months.

[Tokyo Verdy official: injuries to Miyahara, Morita and Teranuma](https://www.verdy.co.jp/news/15208)
[Tokyo Verdy official: injuries to Yamami and Yoshida](https://www.verdy.co.jp/news/14892)

Also on September 10, forward Kazunari Ichimi joined permanently from Fagiano Okayama. He was already on the bench against Chiba. Ichimi said he understands goals are expected from him, while also describing defending, post play and selfless work as strengths.

[Tokyo Verdy official: Kazunari Ichimi joins](https://www.verdy.co.jp/news/15209)
[Tokyo Verdy official: first interview with Kazunari Ichimi](https://www.verdy.co.jp/news/15220)

Then on September 17, midfielder Kanya Fujimoto returned on loan from Birmingham City. A Verdy academy graduate, he comes back after playing in Portugal and England.

[Tokyo Verdy official: Kanya Fujimoto joins on loan](https://www.verdy.co.jp/news/15235)

Public information is not enough to state exactly who can or will play against Urawa. But one thing is clear: this is not a settled team seven matches into a finished plan. It is **a squad being rebuilt while injuries and recruitment happen at the same time.**

---

## 6. WATCH｜Against Urawa, watch for the first moment Verdy break their away 0-0 pattern

On September 19, Urawa enter 12th at 3 wins and 4 losses. Tokyo Verdy enter 19th at 0 wins, 4 draws and 3 losses. The table shows separation.

[J.League official: Urawa vs Tokyo Verdy](https://www.jleague.jp/match/j1/2026/091904/)

But from Verdy's side, there are simpler things to watch.

First: do they concede away for the first time, or make it three straight away clean sheets?

Second: do they score their first away goal? And if they do, is it another defender, or does Someno, Ichimi or another attacker finally become the first forward scorer?

Third: can the 21-shot volume from Chiba survive at Saitama Stadium? If it does, the change begins to look less like one strange match and more like a developing attacking pattern.

Instead of watching "how a 19th-place team plays," the sharper question is this: **can Verdy turn their away 0-0 machine into a 1-0 machine without breaking the defensive part first?**

---

## 7. DISCOVERY｜They look less like "a team that cannot win" and more like "a team with the final function still disconnected"

Before researching them, Tokyo Verdy looked like a 19th-place team still waiting for its first win.

After looking closer, the picture changes.

They have gone 180 away minutes without conceding. Someno runs, contests and connects. Against Chiba they reached 21 shots. Yet all three league goals still belong to defenders and the forwards remain scoreless. At the same time, important players are injured, while Ichimi and Fujimoto have arrived within days.

The parts are not all missing.

There is a defending function. There is a running function. There is a progression function. Recently, there may even be a shot-generation function.

But **the last function that converts those parts into three points is still not cleanly connected.**

That makes tomorrow's match more than a simple "can Verdy get their first win?"

Can they keep the away defence that has not broken once, and finally move only their own side of the scoreboard?

That may be the shortest description of Tokyo Verdy right now.
