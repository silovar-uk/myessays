# What Is Missing When We Do Not Understand?｜分からないのは、何が分かっていないからなのか

## BRIDGE: A diagnostic framework for knowledge gaps

### 要旨 / Abstract

When we struggle to understand something, the difficulty may lie in missing prerequisites rather than the topic itself. 本稿は、理解を妨げる前提知識を六つの観点から診断するブリッジ（BRIDGE）を提案する。The aim is not to memorize an answer, but to reconstruct the reasoning that makes it meaningful.

## 1｜A difficult answer may rest on a missing foundation

「胆汁は脂質を乳化する」。Bile helps emulsify fats. The sentence is short, yet it assumes that the reader knows what digestion is, where it happens, and what emulsification means.

消化の流れを説明できないまま、途中にある胆汁の働きを覚えようとしていた。Repeating the same sentence may not resolve the problem. What would I need to understand before the sentence starts to make sense?

This is the starting point of BRIDGE. 「分からない」を、知識同士の接続を点検する手掛かりとして扱う。

## 2｜Six questions help locate the missing prerequisites

BRIDGE was developed in this dialogue as a proposed framework, not adopted from an existing validated taxonomy. The metaphor is simple: build a bridge between what we already know and what we want to understand.

![Figure 1: Six dimensions connecting prior knowledge to new knowledge](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/bridge-framework-2026.svg)

- **B — Background（背景・目的）**: What is it for? なぜ消化が必要なのか。
- **R — Relationship（関係性）**: How are the elements connected? 肝臓と胆嚢はどう関係するのか。
- **I — Inventory（全体像・構成要素）**: What are the parts of the whole? 消化器系は何から構成されるのか。
- **D — Definition（定義）**: What does the term mean? 乳化とは何か。
- **G — Genesis（因果・発生原理）**: Why does it happen? 胆汁はなぜ脂質の消化を助けるのか。
- **E — Evolution（順序・過程）**: How does the process unfold? 食べ物はどの順序で処理されるのか。

Inventory concerns what exists; Relationship concerns how those parts are connected. Genesis asks why, while Evolution asks how a process unfolds over time. Evolution here is not biological evolution. 六つの観点は順番に通る学習段階ではない。They are diagnostic questions, and they may overlap.

## 3｜Understanding bile requires connecting the organs and the process

Digestion and absorption are different. Digestion breaks food down so that nutrients can be absorbed; absorption brings nutrients into the body.

食べ物は口、食道、胃、小腸、大腸を通る。Most nutrients are absorbed in the small intestine. The large intestine absorbs water and performs other functions.

![Figure 2: The digestive process and the role of bile](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/bridge-digestion-2026.svg)

*Figure 2. An original schematic based on the [NIDDK overview of digestion](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/digestive-system-how-it-works), not an anatomical illustration.*

The liver produces bile. The gallbladder stores it, and bile is released into the small intestine. 食べ物そのものが肝臓や胆嚢を通るわけではない。

Emulsification means dispersing one liquid into small droplets within another. Bile acids help disperse fat into smaller droplets, making it easier for digestive enzymes to act. 胆汁そのものが消化酵素として脂質を分解するわけではない。

The original sentence now has a place in a larger process. 孤立した知識が、消化という仕組みにつながる。

## 4｜Established learning theories explain why prior knowledge matters

BRIDGE is a proposed diagnostic framework. The broader idea that new knowledge connects to existing knowledge, however, has a substantial research history.

### Meaningful learning: start with what the learner already knows

In 1960, David Ausubel studied advance organizers（先行オーガナイザー）: relevant conceptual material introduced before unfamiliar learning material. 先行オーガナイザーは単なる目次ではなく、新しい内容と既有知識を結び付ける足場を提供する。[Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0046669)

### Schemas and concept maps: knowledge has structure

A schema（知識の枠組み）organizes related information. Joseph Novak's concept maps（概念地図）, developed from a research program beginning in 1972, represent relationships between concepts. [Theory and method](https://cmap.ihmc.us/docs/theory-of-concept-maps.php)

BRIDGE shares this concern with the connections between knowledge, not merely the number of facts we know.

### Cognitive load: missing prerequisites may increase processing demands

John Sweller's 1988 paper examined cognitive load during problem solving and discussed domain-specific schemas in the difference between experts and novices. [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4)

複数の未知の前提を一度に処理するより、必要な前提を分けて理解する方がよいのではないか。This is a hypothesis consistent with the general idea, not a proven effect of BRIDGE.

### Self-explanation: try to explain the reasoning yourself

Michelene Chi and colleagues studied learners' self-generated explanations of worked examples and their later problem solving in 1989. [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1)

Reading an explanation is not the same as being able to reconstruct it. 自分の言葉で説明し、詰まった場所を点検する。

## 5｜BRIDGE is designed to diagnose, not replace existing methods

Concept maps visualize relationships. Advance organizers provide conceptual preparation. Self-explanation generates explanations from the learner.

BRIDGE asks a different practical question: what kind of prerequisite should I examine when I cannot understand? 既存手法を置き換えるのではなく、何を補うべきかを考えるための入口である。

## 6｜Work backward, fill the gap, and reconstruct the answer

A suggested learning routine:

1. State the target claim in one sentence.
2. Use the six questions to identify what you cannot explain.
3. Check whether each missing prerequisite depends on other knowledge.
4. Study only the prerequisites needed for the original question.
5. Close the material and explain the claim in your own words. Return to the gaps if necessary.

The goal is not infinite preparation. 本題を説明できるかどうかを、基礎へ戻る範囲の判断基準にする。

### Suggested AI prompt

> Use the six BRIDGE dimensions: Background, Relationship, Inventory, Definition, Genesis, and Evolution. Identify the missing prerequisites I need to understand the target topic. Check those prerequisites recursively, but stay within the scope of the original question. Connect the foundations to the conclusion, then give me a question that tests whether I can explain it myself.

守破離と組み合わせるなら、「守」で前提を診断し、「破」で予測や反証を検討し、「離」で他領域への応用を考える。

## 7｜The framework is a hypothesis, not a validated theory

Prior knowledge is not the only source of difficulty. Fatigue, attention, incorrect prior beliefs, and flawed explanations may also matter.

The completeness, overlap, and effectiveness of the six dimensions have not been established. Nor has the worldwide originality of the name or taxonomy been verified. 既存研究との接点は、BRIDGEそのものが実証されたことを意味しない。

A possible future test would compare ordinary explanations with explanations preceded by a BRIDGE diagnosis. Immediate self-explanation, delayed recall, and transfer to new problems could be examined.

## 8｜Turn confusion into a question about your knowledge structure

Don't just learn the answer. Learn what makes the answer make sense.

分からないとき、同じ説明を何度も求めるのではなく、どの前提まで戻れば理解できるのかを問う。Confusion may be a clue to what we need to learn next.
