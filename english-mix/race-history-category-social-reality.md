# How Did Skin Color Become a Category?
## 「黒人」に歴史が乗るまで

Skin color can be visible.

それは単純な事実に見える。

But here is a different question:

**Why did skin color become a major way to divide people?**

身長も違う。髪質も違う。耳の形も違う。

Yet societies do not usually build laws, neighborhoods, schools, and political rights around earlobe shape.

肌の色は、ただ見える特徴だっただけではない。

It was made socially meaningful.

この過程を考えるキーワードが**racialization――人種化**である。

---

## 1. Race is not a clean biological box――人種は生物学の箱ではない

The American Association of Biological Anthropologists is clear on this point.

Socially recognized racial groups do not accurately map human biological variation.

人間には遺伝的・身体的な違いがある。

But those differences do not create neat natural boxes called Black, White, Asian, and so on.

Skin color, hair texture, facial features, and genetic variants show variation within and across populations.

だから、

**physical difference is real.**

しかし、

**fixed racial borders are not simply read from biology.**

この二つを同時に理解する必要がある。

---

## 2. Race can still be socially real――作られた概念なのに、現実に効く

“Race is socially constructed.”

この言葉は、ときどき「ならraceは存在しない」という意味に誤解される。

That is not the point.

AABA explains that the Western race concept developed together with European colonialism, oppression, and discrimination.

その分類が何世紀も制度に組み込まれると、結果は現実になる。

Who can own land?

誰が市民として扱われるか。

Who gets access to schools, housing, jobs, or protection?

どの集団が危険だと見られやすいか。

If society repeatedly organizes decisions around race, race changes people’s lives even if it is not a precise biological taxonomy.

つまり、

**race is not a good biological map, but it can be a powerful social map.**

ここが一見矛盾していて、一番重要なところである。

---

## 3. “Dark skin” and “Black” are not identical information

「肌の色が濃い」は見た目の記述に近い。

“Black” or 「黒人」 is a social category.

そこにはskin toneだけではなく、

- ancestry
- history
- culture
- how others classify a person
- self-identification

が重なり得る。

APA defines race as a social construction based partly on perceived physical traits and linked to sociopolitical hierarchy.

So calling someone Black is not the same as reading a color value from a screen.

同じようなskin toneでも、国籍、地域、家族史、本人のidentityは違う。

The label contains more social meaning than the color itself.

---

## 4. Japan is not outside this history――ただし米国と同じでもない

It is easy to say, “That is an American issue.”

半分は正しい。

Japan does not share the exact same history as U.S. slavery and segregation.

日本語の「黒人」の語史を、英語のBlackの歴史と完全に重ねるのは間違いである。

But Japan was never completely outside modern racial classification.

Historian Rotem Kowner studied Western discourse about the Japanese “race” from 1854 to 1904.

当時、西洋側は日本人を自分たちのracial systemの中で分類し、その評価は政治関係やpower relationsによって変化した。

Japanese people themselves were racialized by others.

さらにYasuko Takezawa’s study of early Meiji textbooks shows that concepts of race introduced from Europe and the United States were translated into Japanese and transformed as Japanese writers repositioned Japan in relation to other groups.

So 「黒人」 and English Black are not identical.

でも、they are also not isolated from the global history of race.

---

## 5. Color-evasion is not always the answer――人種を言わないことが反差別とは限らない

Learning this history can make race words feel dangerous.

「もう人種を言わなければいい」と思いたくなる。

But then another problem appears.

Japanese geography researcher Chihiro Ito argues that some realities cannot be described adequately without terms such as race and Black people.

たとえば、

- slavery
- segregation
- racial discrimination in housing or work
- Black cultural movements
- different social experiences produced by racism

をraceなしで説明しようとすると、肝心のstructureが消えることがある。

Avoiding a category can sometimes hide the inequality organized around that category.

だから必要なのは、

**Do not treat race as natural biology.**

そして同時に、

**Do not pretend race has no social effects.**

この二つである。

---

## 6. Racialization is not exactly the same as racism

ここも分けたい。

**Racialization** means the process by which race becomes meaningful in how people or groups are understood and classified.

It does not automatically mean open hostility.

たとえばracial health disparitiesを調べる研究も、人種カテゴリーを使う。

But racialization becomes more dangerous when a category is connected to fixed assumptions about ability, personality, intelligence, danger, or value.

また、一部の人だけを常に「人種のある人」として見ると、majority側が「無色の標準」に見えやすくなる。

Now the original sentence returns.

「ああ、あの背の高い黒人ね」

That sentence alone cannot prove that the speaker is racist.

でも、we can still ask:

**Why did race become the useful marker in that moment?**

必要な識別情報だったのか。

Or did the speaker unconsciously treat one group as “normal” and another as “racially marked”?

歴史の話が、日常会話へつながる。

---

## 7. What changed after the research――肌の色は見える。でも「分類」は見たままではない

Before the research, race could look like a simple visual fact.

肌の色が違う。だから分類名がある。

Now the sequence looks different.

People have visible differences.

Societies choose which differences matter.

History gives those differences institutions, stereotypes, identities, and power.

その結果、categoryが現実の経験を作る。

So the goal is not to become silent about race.

**The goal is to understand what the category is doing, and use it only when it helps us describe reality accurately.**
