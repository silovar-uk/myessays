# 浦和レッズのファンは178万8,454人いる。もちろん違う
## 113.1億円 revenue, 70.9万 visits, REX CLUB 22.9万, SNS 82.9万｜一つの「熱量」にしない

Urawa Reds has 1,788,454 fans.

Of course, it does not.

Take 709,655 J1 home attendances in 2025, roughly 20,000 season ticket holders, 229,446 REX CLUB members as of the end of June 2026, and 829,353 official social-media followers as of February 2026. Add them together and you get 1,788,454. [浦和レッズ「新規パートナーシップに関するお問い合わせ」](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/business/partnership_inquiry/)

The sum is wrong for obvious reasons. Annual attendance is not unique people but cumulative visits. Season ticket holders are also REX CLUB LOYALTY members. The same person may follow multiple social platforms. REX CLUB also includes free WHITE members. [REX CLUB 2026 お客様窓口](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/about_rex/2026/information.html)

Still, this wrong number is useful. The problem is not that we cannot produce one perfectly correct fan count. **The deeper problem is that collapsing different relationships into one count destroys what those relationships mean.**

---

## 1. FACT｜浦和にはすでに巨大な数字がある。しかし、全部違うものを測っている

Start with public numbers.

Urawa Reds reported record business revenue of ¥11.310 billion for FY2025. Ticket revenue was ¥2.214 billion, advertising revenue ¥4.195 billion, merchandise ¥1.682 billion, and other revenue ¥3.219 billion. By simple calculation, the shares are 19.6%, 37.1%, 14.9%, and 28.5%. [浦和レッズ「2025年度経営情報の開示について」](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/clubinfo/240501/)

In the same 2025 season, average J1 home attendance was 37,350 and annual home attendance was 709,655. The club reports about 20,000 season ticket holders. REX CLUB had 229,446 members at the end of June 2026, while official social accounts totaled 829,353 followers in February 2026: X 431,753, Instagram 217,000, YouTube 98,600, and Facebook 82,000. [浦和レッズ「新規パートナーシップに関するお問い合わせ」](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/business/partnership_inquiry/)

The important point is not just scale but unit. Revenue is yen. Attendance is cumulative visits. Season tickets are contracts. REX CLUB is membership IDs. Social followers are account follows.

So if we compress “fan count,” “heat,” and “engagement” into a single KPI, calculation remains possible but meaning disappears. Before Urawa has a data problem, it has a naming problem: **different relationships should not be called the same thing.**

## 2. REVENUE｜113.1億円のうち、ファン価値は「直接支払われた金額」だけではない

Revenue makes fan value look straightforward. Ticket revenue of ¥2.214 billion plus merchandise revenue of ¥1.682 billion equals ¥3.896 billion, around 34.4% of total business revenue. This is only a simple sum of two categories close to direct fan spending, not a strict definition of B2C revenue, but it shows scale.

Yet the largest revenue line is advertising at ¥4.195 billion, including partner sponsorships and RBC fees. Fans therefore create value not only when they buy tickets or shirts. A large audience, recognition, support, and local presence make a partnership with Urawa valuable to companies. [浦和レッズ「2025年度経営情報の開示について」](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/clubinfo/240501/)

The club also cites a Nielsen Sports survey from October 2024 showing that when companies were recognized as Urawa Reds partners, average corporate favorability rose by 29 points, purchase/use intention by 22 points, recommendation intention by 31 points, and search behavior by 22 points. [浦和レッズ「パートナー効果」](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/business/partnership_inquiry/)

That changes the model. Fan value is not simply “money fans paid to the club.” We need to see **a relationship system in which fan connections are converted differently into tickets, merchandise, sponsor value, and local value.**

## 3. IDENTITY｜22万9,446人のREX CLUB会員を、そのまま「濃いファン」と呼んではいけない

The figure of 229,446 REX CLUB members is powerful. But interpreting it directly as 229,446 highly engaged fans is risky.

REX CLUB includes LOYALTY for season ticket holders, paid REGULAR members, and free WHITE members. WHITE can include people who registered for REX TICKET, the online shop, or newsletters, as well as former LOYALTY or REGULAR members who did not renew. [REX CLUB 2026 お客様窓口](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/about_rex/2026/information.html)

So 229,446 is better understood not as an intensity score but as a large population of IDs that have established an identifiable relationship with the club at least once.

This changes the practical question. Instead of asking only “how many members can we add?”, ask “how many of these 229,446 had any meaningful touchpoint in the last 365 days?”

I would introduce an **Active Relationship Base**, defined as unique IDs with at least one meaningful action in the last 365 days. Candidate actions include attendance, ticket purchase, merchandise purchase, point earning or redemption, and event application or participation. Public information does not tell us which datasets can actually be integrated on the club side, so this is a proposal, not a factual claim.

Before increasing the count, count how many relationships are still moving. The 229,446 figure becomes not the finish line but the outer edge of a map that includes dormant relationships too.

## 4. DATA｜浦和で必要なのは「ファン台帳」ではなく、「接点→行動→次の接点」の履歴である

J.League’s data environment states that J.League ID and One Touch Pass can involve ticket and merchandise purchase histories, stadium attendance, app usage, and campaign entry histories. One Touch Pass has recorded stadium attendance across home and away matches since 2009. [Jリーグ プライバシーポリシー](https://auth.jleague.jp/contents/privacypolicy.html) [Jリーグ用語集「ワンタッチパス」](https://aboutj.jleague.jp/corporate/terminology/)

That means the useful analytical unit is closer to event history than to a static fan profile. An ID learns about a match, buys a ticket, passes the gate, buys merchandise, returns next month, and maybe brings a friend. The sequence matters.

A proposed minimum data model can stay simple: person_id, timestamp, touchpoint, action, match or campaign ID, amount, source, referral source, and outcome. The goal is not to build an enormous psychological dossier; it is to preserve sequence.

A database designed to decide “who is a strong fan?” classifies people. A database designed to ask “which touchpoint led to the next action?” improves interventions. For Urawa, the second is more valuable.

## 5. SIX LEDGERS｜浦和版では、ファン価値を6冊の台帳に分ける

In the previous article, fan value was split into five dimensions: revenue, continuity, diffusion, participation, and learning. Applied to Urawa Reds, one dimension was missing: community and social value.

- **Revenue**：ticketing, merchandise, membership, food and beverageなど、クラブへdirectly戻るeconomic value。
- **Continuity**：repeat attendance, multi-year attendance, comeback after dormancy, season-ticket useなど、relationshipが残る価値。
- **Diffusion**：referrals, companions, sharing, word of mouthなど、別の人への入口を作る価値。
- **Participation**：events, support, posting, community activityなど、自分から場へ働きかける価値。
- **Learning**：surveys, inquiries, reviews, requestsなど、クラブが改善する材料になる価値。
- **Community / Social**：hometown activities, contact with children, co-creation around local issuesなど、クラブの外側へ残る価値。

Do not add these six ledgers into one score. There should be no “80 points revenue + 20 points community = 100 fan score.”

The reason is that Urawa Reds is not merely a content-selling business. A sports club sells matches while becoming part of the local landscape, creating corporate relationships, and entering children’s experiences. **Rather than ranking individual fans, the more important task is to avoid losing sight of the different relationships the club holds.**

## 6. NUMBER TEST｜22.9万人の「1％」が一度だけ動いても、約2,294人になる

Now place some numbers into the model. These are scenario calculations using public figures, not actual outcomes.

One percent of 229,446 REX CLUB members is about 2,294 people, 3% is about 6,883, and 5% about 11,472. If a program caused 1% of members with no attendance in the past year to make one additional paid visit, that would mean roughly 2,294 additional visits.

If FY2025 ticket revenue of ¥2.214 billion is divided by the 2025 J1 home attendance of 709,655, the result is about ¥3,120 per visit. But the numerator is annual ticket revenue while the denominator covers only J1 home attendance, so this is not an average ticket price. It is only a rough “ticket-revenue density” proxy.

Using that proxy, a 1% additional-visit scenario corresponds to roughly ¥7.16 million in ticket-revenue scale, 3% to about ¥21.48 million, and 5% to about ¥35.79 million. Actual incremental revenue would change with seat type, complimentary tickets, competition mix, and substitution from purchases that would have happened anyway, so these figures should not be used as budget forecasts.

Still, they reveal scale. With a base of 229,446 IDs, not everyone needs to change dramatically. **Precisely capturing small behavioral shifts of a few percent may be more actionable than a huge objective such as “gain 100,000 more fans.”**

## 7. SEASON TICKET｜約2万人の「来る・来ない」は、売上よりスタジアム密度に効く

There were about 20,000 season ticket holders in 2025. If all 20,000 attended all 19 J1 home matches, that would represent 380,000 seat uses, or around 53.5% of the actual 709,655 annual home attendance.

Of course, not everyone attends every match. Football research on season ticket holders shows no-show behavior exists and can be associated with distance, temperature, and previous non-attendance habits. [Schreyer et al., Predicting season ticket holder no-show behaviour](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00036846.2021.1925081)

As a proposal, I would track a separate **Seat Utilization Rate** alongside season ticket sales. Using roughly 20,000 holders across 19 matches, a year-on-year improvement of 1 percentage point would mean about 3,800 additional occupied seat-visits, +3 points about 11,400, and +5 points about 19,000. A +3 point improvement is roughly 600 more occupied seats per match.

This KPI does not directly increase ticket revenue because those seats are already sold. But it may reduce visible empty seats and create more opportunities around atmosphere, food and beverage, experience, and sponsor exposure. Again, revenue alone does not tell us whether the relationship was actually used.

## 8. REPEAT｜新規獲得より「2回目」を独立KPIにすると、集客施策の意味が変わる

In 2025, J.League reported acquiring about 146,000 new J.League IDs through large invitation and acquisition programs, and said subsequent CRM efforts led to repeat attendance rates above 30% among new visitors. [J.LEAGUE SEASON REVIEW 2025](https://aboutj.jleague.jp/seasonreview2025/topics/attendances/)

This is not Urawa Reds’ own result. But it can serve as an external reference point.

For Urawa, I would treat second-visit rate within 180 days of first attendance as a standalone KPI rather than ending evaluation at first-time attendance volume. The first year should establish Urawa’s own baseline. If that baseline turns out to be around the league example, 35% could be tested as a provisional stretch target rather than assumed as truth.

For illustration, if there were 10,000 first-time attendees in a year, a 30% repeat rate would produce 3,000 repeat visitors and 35% would produce 3,500—a difference of 500. With 20,000 first-time attendees, the difference would be 1,000.

The point is not that 35% is inherently correct. The point is that **separating “how many came once” from “whether that experience created the second visit” changes how acquisition campaigns are evaluated.**

## 9. SOCIAL｜82.9万フォロワーをファネルの分母にすると、かなり危ない

Urawa Reds reports a combined 829,353 official social-media followers as of February 2026. But that is not a unique-person count. One person following X, Instagram, and YouTube appears three times.

Sport research also warns against equating silence with weak engagement. In a minor-league baseball study, social-media lurkers who rarely posted still identified strongly with the team and, in the sample, attended games more frequently than posters. [Williams, Heiser & Chinn, Social Media Posters and Lurkers](https://digitalcommons.usm.maine.edu/business-faculty/20/)

A 2024 comparative study of football-league social accounts similarly found that content quality mattered more to engagement than posting frequency itself. [The more we post, the better?](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1464666824000067)

So goals like “grow followers to 850,000” or “raise engagement rate” are not enough to represent Urawa’s relationship value. The better question is how social contact moves into the official site, REX registration, ticket browsing, purchase, and attendance. Social is more useful as an entry point into the next relationship than as the final score.

## 10. SIXTH AXIS｜浦和では「地域」を収益への前段階として扱わない

The sixth ledger appeared because of the 2025 Heart-full Club numbers. Across Saitama City and Saitama Prefecture, including schools, kindergartens, and childcare facilities, the club conducted 544 activities and interacted with 39,718 children through football. [浦和レッズ「2025年度経営情報の開示について」](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/clubinfo/240501/)

We could read those 39,718 participants merely as 39,718 future ticket prospects. But that would narrow the meaning of the activity.

Hometown programs may eventually contribute to awareness and attendance. But value can also exist in access to football, connection to the community, and children’s experiences even when no purchase follows.

So the sixth ledger should not be reduced to revenue conversion. Track participation volume, repeated participation, geographic reach, awareness and evaluation among participants and parents, and connection to local issues, choosing measures according to purpose.

If Urawa Reds is viewed only as a large sports IP, five ledgers might have been enough. **Viewed as a hometown club, a sixth ledger is needed to preserve value that does not need to be recovered as sales.**

## 11. DASHBOARD｜浦和版の最上段に置く数字は、「ファン総数」ではなく6つの状態である

Now turn the argument into operations.

At the top of the dashboard, do not display only ¥11.31 billion in revenue and 37,350 average attendance. For each of the six ledgers, show current value, year-on-year change, intervention effect, and data quality. Revenue can contain ticketing, merchandise, and membership-related value. Continuity can contain second-visit rate, annual visit frequency, and dormant return. Diffusion can contain referral-attributed attendance. Participation can contain events and UGC. Learning can contain feedback and whether it changed operations. Community can contain participation and local outcomes.

Below that, connect the dashboard to J.League’s to-C journey of awareness → interest → attendance → repeat → retention, organized around J.League ID. [J.LEAGUE SEASON REVIEW 2024](https://aboutj.jleague.jp/seasonreview2024/fanengagement/fa_1/)

If provisional numerical goals are needed, I would keep only three. First, establish Urawa’s own 180-day second-visit baseline, then set improvement against the league example above 30%. Second, test a +3 percentage-point season-ticket Seat Utilization hypothesis; with about 20,000 holders across 19 matches, that equals roughly 11,400 additional occupied seat-visits, or about 600 per match. Third, test reactivating 1% of dormant REX members into one additional paid visit; that equals about 2,294 visits and, under the rough ticket-revenue-density proxy above, around ¥7.16 million in scale.

The key is not to treat these as “correct targets.” The last two are explicitly scenarios and should be updated after real baseline data is available. **The dashboard exists not to freeze numbers, but to make hypotheses falsifiable.**

## 12. RE-RESEARCH｜測定を精密にするほど、「熱いファンを作る」という発想から離れた方がいい

At first, the question seemed to be how to quantify Urawa Reds’ enormous fan base more precisely.

The more I re-researched it, the more the conclusion moved in the opposite direction. People who do not post can still attend often. Season ticket holders do not necessarily attend every match. Free members may remain interested for years. A child reached through a community program does not need to become future revenue for that interaction to have value.

If raw behavioral volume is simply renamed “heat,” different forms of relationship become a ranking.

Urawa does not need to search for the single highest-value fan. It needs to see how people who attend, buy, refer, watch silently, or encounter the club in the community continue their relationship through different touchpoints.

Do not measure heat. **Measure where relationships begin, where they continue, and where they break.** The numbers become less flashy, but the interventions become much more concrete.

## 13. DISCOVERY｜浦和レッズが持っているのは「178万人のファン」ではなく、巨大な関係網である

Return to the wrong sum.

709,655 + 20,000 + 229,446 + 829,353 = 1,788,454.

This is not a count of anything. Yet it reveals Urawa Reds’ strength in another form.

There are 709,655 stadium visits, about 20,000 annual contracts, 229,446 membership IDs, and 829,353 social follows. People connect to the club through different entrances.

The record ¥11.31 billion revenue in FY2025 is one output of that network. Advertising revenue of ¥4.195 billion, ticket revenue of ¥2.214 billion, and merchandise revenue of ¥1.682 billion show different routes through which relationships become economic value. And 544 Heart-full activities involving 39,718 children show that some relationships do not need to become sales at all.

So Urawa Reds’ fan engagement does not need to be compressed into one “fan count” or “heat score.”

**What the club possesses is not a number of fans, but a network of relationships.**

The next dashboard should not turn that network into one league table. It should show which touchpoints create which kinds of value and where they create the next touchpoint.

There are not 1,788,454 fans.

But there are already enough different relationships that adding them together is tempting.

## APPENDIX｜浦和レッズのファン価値を6軸で設計するプロンプト

~~~text
You are a researcher and business designer working across sports-club CRM, fan engagement, ticketing, merchandise, partnerships, and community activity.

Input
【Target club】{{club}}
【Public baseline figures】{{revenue, attendance, membership, social, community activity}}
【Available internal data】{{ticketing, attendance, purchase, web, membership, survey}}
【Priority issue】{{acquisition, repeat, reactivation, merchandise, membership, partnership}}

Goal
Do not rank fans with one heat score.
Use six ledgers—revenue, continuity, diffusion, participation, learning, community/social—
to understand the transition touchpoint → behavior → value → next touchpoint.

Process
1. Separate public data from internal data and state units and time periods.
2. Do not mix cumulative attendance, unique people, member IDs, social accounts, and contracts.
3. Split revenue into ticketing, merchandise, advertising, and other, and distinguish direct and indirect value.
4. Define an Active Relationship Base separately from total membership: unique IDs with at least one meaningful action in the last 365 days.
5. Build six ledgers: revenue, continuity, diffusion, participation, learning, community/social.
6. Put current value, year-on-year change, intervention, and data quality in each ledger.
7. Measure transitions such as first visit → second visit, social → owned ID, dormant → return.
8. When only public data exists, clearly separate fact from scenario calculation.
9. Do not infer fan strength only from posting or purchase volume. Check lurkers, no-shows, free members, and other counterexamples.
10. Do not evaluate community activity only as future sales conversion; preserve social outcomes as independent value.
11. Use causal hypotheses by intervention rather than one personal composite score.
12. Update targets after collecting baselines; make the dashboard able to falsify its own assumptions.

Always calculate
- people represented by 1%, 3%, and 5% of the member base taking one additional action
- occupied seat-visits created by +1pt, +3pt, and +5pt season-ticket utilization
- repeat visits created by a +5pt change in first-visit repeat rate
- sponsor and community value that does not appear as direct fan revenue

Reject if
- combined social followers are treated as unique fans
- annual attendance is treated as unique attendees
- total membership is treated as active fans
- everything is compressed into one fan score
- correlation is written as causation
- community activity is valued only through revenue conversion
- assumed figures are written as actual results
- source URLs are missing

Output
A. Current factual baseline
B. Unit and overlap risks
C. Six-ledger dashboard
D. Behavioral transition model
E. 1%, 3%, 5% scenarios
F. Candidate targets and rationale
G. Re-research that challenged the first hypothesis
H. Data capture and integration requirements
I. Ethics and privacy notes
J. References
~~~
