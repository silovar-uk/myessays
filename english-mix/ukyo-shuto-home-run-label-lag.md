# 周東佑京の2026年目標は「ホームラン」だった
## When a speedster says “watch my home runs”

Open Ukyo Shuto's official 2026 Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks profile.

アンケート欄に、こうある。

**2026年の目標（野球について）**

> ホームラン

Then one line below:

**自分のプレーのここを見て欲しい**

> ホームラン

Same answer twice.

Not stolen bases.

Not baserunning.

Not defence.

**Home runs.**

Now put his 2026 numbers next to that answer. As of September 7:

```text
Stolen bases   27   1st in Pacific League
Home runs       2
Triples          5   tied for 2nd
Runs            70   3rd
Hits           124   5th
```

通算では、盗塁257、本塁打19。

That is roughly **13.5 stolen bases for every career home run**.

And this player writes “home run” twice.

At first, the question seems obvious.

**Why would Shuto, one of Japan's most famous runners, choose home runs?**

But that question slowly became less interesting.

The better question was this:

**Why are we still so quick to describe him only as “the fast guy”?**

---

## 1. First, yes: the speed is absurd

The label did not come from nowhere.

2020年、周東は50盗塁で盗塁王になった。

He also stole a base in **13 consecutive games** from October 16 to October 30.

The previous NPB record was 11 games, held by Yutaka Fukumoto.

NPB officially recognised Shuto's 13-game streak as a new professional baseball record and gave him a Commissioner's Special Award.

Thirteen straight games with a stolen base sounds simple when written as one sentence.

It is not simple.

First, you need to reach base.

次の塁が空いていないといけない。

The game situation has to allow a steal.

The pitcher and catcher know what is coming.

Then you still have to succeed.

And repeat the whole setup in the next game.

**A stolen-base streak depends on both speed and opportunity geometry.**

だから「周東＝足」は、雑なイメージではなく、かなり強い事実からできている。

---

## 2. In the WBC, 10.28 seconds completed the image

Then came the 2023 World Baseball Classic semifinal against Mexico.

Japan trailed 5-4 in the bottom of the ninth.

Shohei Ohtani doubled.

Masataka Yoshida walked.

周東は吉田の代走として一塁に入った。

Munetaka Murakami drove a ball off the left-center-field wall.

Ohtani scored from second.

Shuto scored from first.

Japan won 6-5 and advanced to the final.

MLB later included the play among the defining moments of the tournament.

And one number became attached to Shuto's run:

**10.28 seconds from first base to home.**

Reported peak speed: **30.4 feet per second**, about 9.27 m/s.

野球の塁間は90フィートなので、一塁から二塁、三塁、本塁までを単純に足すと270フィート、約82.3m。

```text
82.3 m ÷ 10.28 sec ≈ 8.0 m/s
```

This is only a rough illustration. A runner rounds the bases, accelerates, decelerates and does not follow three perfect straight lines.

Still, the basic fact is wild enough.

**A man standing on first base was scoring the winning run about ten seconds later.**

That play made Shuto globally easy to explain.

Fast.

Very fast.

説明として正しい。

But a perfectly memorable explanation can become a trap.

---

## 3. A small experiment: ban the words “fast,” “steal” and “speed”

Let's try something silly.

周東を説明するとき、「速い」「盗塁」「足」を使ってはいけないことにする。

What remains?

In 2024, he played 123 games, collected 115 hits and batted .269.

He won his first **Best Nine Award as an outfielder** and his first **Mitsui Golden Glove Award**.

2025年は96試合で110安打、打率.286。

He won both awards again — back-to-back Best Nine and back-to-back Gold Glove.

We have still not used the word “stolen base.”

And the résumé already looks serious.

Then October 26, 2025: Game 2 of the Japan Series.

Shuto went **5-for-5**.

```text
single to right
triple to left-center
single to center
single to center
double down the left-field line
```

**Five hits in one game became a new Japan Series record.**

A player who became internationally famous by entering a game as a pinch-runner later set a Japan Series record with his bat.

その事実だけで、少し認識が揺れる。

Maybe the player changed.

Maybe our shorthand failed to change with him.

---

## 4. The 2026 version of Shuto exists before he reaches first base

As of September 7, 2026, Shuto had played 118 games.

His batting line included 124 hits, 70 runs, a .276 average and 27 stolen bases.

The stolen bases led the Pacific League.

But look at the sequence.

**You cannot steal second until you first reach first.**

周東の速さは「出塁したあと」の武器。

A full-time player has to keep creating the moment before the famous moment.

In 2026, Shuto had already taken 514 plate appearances and produced 124 hits.

He was fifth in the league in hits and third in runs.

Now defence.

NPB's official outfield fielding table through September 6 listed Shuto with 115 games in the outfield, 287 putouts, four assists and **zero errors**, for a 1.000 fielding percentage.

Fielding percentage is not a complete measure of outfield defence. It does not tell us range, route quality or difficulty of chances.

でも、115試合外野を守って失策0という事実は、「代走要員」の説明からはかなり遠い。

The current version of Shuto does several jobs:

- gets plate appearances
- gets hits
- scores runs
- steals bases
- plays the outfield every day

His speed is still there.

**The problem is that the speed is so vivid that it can hide everything around it.**

---

## 5. A useless ratio makes “home run” look even stranger

Back to the original answer.

Through September 7, 2026, Shuto's career totals were 806 games, 620 hits, 257 stolen bases and 19 home runs.

Here is a metric nobody needs:

```text
257 SB ÷ 19 HR ≈ 13.5
```

So if we compress an entire career into one ridiculous sentence:

**Shuto steals about 13.5 bases for every home run he hits.**

そして公式プロフィールには、

```text
2026 goal           home run
play to watch       home run
```

と並ぶ。

It is still funny.

But now the joke lands differently.

At first:

**“The stolen-base guy says home run.”**

After the research:

**“I still expected the stolen-base guy to talk only about stolen bases.”**

That second sentence is more revealing.

---

## 6. A player's strongest trait is not the same size as the player

A defining skill is useful.

It helps fans remember players.

It helps broadcasters explain them quickly.

It helps stories move.

周東の場合、その特徴はあまりにも強い。

50 stolen bases.

A 13-game stolen-base streak.

10.28 seconds in a World Baseball Classic semifinal.

“Speedster” is not wrong.

**But a correct label can still be incomplete.**

In 2024 and 2025, Shuto was a Best Nine outfielder and Gold Glove winner.

In the 2025 Japan Series, he set a five-hit game record.

In 2026, he was among the league leaders in hits and runs while again leading in stolen bases.

だから今の説明は、

**「走れる選手」より、「打って、守って、そのうえで異常に走れる選手」**

のほうが近い。

Same ingredients.

Different order.

And order changes perception.

---

## 7. Maybe the label is the slow thing

Before researching this, I thought the funny part was the profile.

A famous base stealer writing “home run” twice.

調べ終わっても、その字面は面白い。

But my point of confusion moved.

Shuto did not suddenly become more complicated in 2026.

He had already been changing in plain sight.

2020: 50 stolen bases.

2023: the 10.28-second WBC run.

2024: Best Nine and Gold Glove.

2025: those awards again, plus a Japan Series record of five hits in one game.

2026: still leading the league in steals, while carrying a regular outfielder's workload.

And in his age-30 player profile, the play he wants people to watch is:

**home run.**

We cannot know his exact intention from one questionnaire answer, so there is no need to invent a dramatic story about it.

But the answer works as a useful test for the viewer.

Why did “home run” feel surprising?

Because I had made one of his skills into his whole identity.

次に周東が一塁へ出たら、たぶん「走るか」と見る。

That is reasonable.

But it is worth remembering who got him to first base.

Very often, it was Shuto himself with the bat.

**Players keep moving. Labels are often slower.**

その意味では、周東より足が遅かったのは、こちらの認識だったのかもしれない。

## Useful English chunks

- **defining trait** — その人を代表する特徴
- **player shorthand** — 選手を短く説明するための定型的な見方
- **back-to-back awards** — 2年連続の受賞
- **reach base** — 出塁する
- **score from first** — 一塁から生還する
- **plate appearance** — 打席
- **fielding percentage** — 守備率
- **incomplete label** — 間違いではないが不十分なラベル
- **update our image of someone** — その人についての認識を更新する
- **the label is slower than the player** — ラベルの更新が本人の変化に追いつかない

- 2026 batting and career totals use NPB official data through September 7, 2026.
- 2026 outfield fielding data use NPB official data through September 6, 2026.
