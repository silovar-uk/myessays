# 自己表現は、自分だけではできない
## self-expression is made of borrowed codes

「self-expression」と聞くと、insideにある本当の自分をoutsideへ出す感じがする。

But look at the tools. 日本語は自分で発明していない。Clothes, hairstyles, music genres, emojis, photo conventions, profile fields—almost everything already existed before us. 黒い服を選ぶことは自分のchoiceだが、その黒が何を意味するかは自分一人では決められない。

では、completely originalな記号だけで自分を表したらどうなるか。

Nobody can read it.

**Pure originality turns self-expression into encryption.**

ここがこのテーマの変な入口になる。Self-expression seems personal, but it often needs shared codes to become legible. Yet if we follow shared codes completely, the personal difference disappears.

So this essay uses a different model. 自己表現を「完成済みのinner selfを外へ出す矢印」と考えず、**inner state, shared code, medium, audience, and feedbackのあいだでselfを翻訳するloop**として見る。

> “Self-expression” is operationalized differently across psychology, cultural psychology, consumer research, and media studies. 本稿は一つの研究をuniversal lawとして扱わない。「五つの軸」はvalidated scaleではなく、researchを比較するためのeditorial scaffoldである。

## 1. 自己表現・自己呈示・自己開示 are overlapping, not identical

Everyday Japanese uses 「自己表現」very broadly. You speak, dress, draw, decorate, choose a team, stay silent. 感情、価値観、所属、好み、self-understandingの一部がexternal formへ変われば、広い意味ではself-expressionと呼べる。

**自己呈示（self-presentation）** and **印象管理（impression management）** are narrower. Mark R. Leary and Robin M. Kowalski's 1990 review defines impression management around controlling the impressions others form of us, and separates two processes: impression motivation and impression construction.

**自己開示（self-disclosure）** is about revealing personal information. 「この音楽が好き」はpreference expressionでも、personal informationのdisclosureでも、場合によっては「こう見られたい」というpresentationでもある。

The categories overlap, but they ask different questions.

ここで大事なのは、performanceをfakeと同一視しないこと。アーヴィング・ゴフマン（Erving Goffman）は『日常生活における自己呈示』（The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life）でsocial interactionをtheatrical performanceになぞらえた。People present themselves, guide impressions, and adapt to situations.

But adapting to an audience does not automatically make the presented self false. 職場と友人の前でvoiceや語彙が変わる。One version can be formal and another playful without either being counterfeit.

Even “no filter” is a choice of filter.

参照：[Leary & Kowalski, “Impression Management”](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.107.1.34) ／ [Penguin Random House, The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/61106/the-presentation-of-self-in-everyday-life-by-erving-goffman/)

## 2. 「自分らしさ」needs a shared code before it can look personal

Clothing makes the paradox easy to see.

Grant McCracken and Victor J. Roth tested whether clothing communication has a code. Their 1989 study supported both the existence of a clothing code and the fact that knowledge of it is unevenly distributed. 同じ服でも、everyone does not read the same message.

A band T-shirt can signal music taste to one audience and look like a black shirt to another. A suit can signal reliability in one context and stiffness in another. Meaning is not locked inside the object.

Jonah Berger and Chip Heath found another piece of the puzzle. In identity-relevant domains such as music or hairstyles, people were more likely to diverge from majority choices or groups they did not want to resemble. 選択は「好き」の反映だけでなく、“I am not that kind of person”というsignalにもなる。

Russell W. Belk's **拡張自己（extended self）** widens the field further. Possessions can contribute to and reflect identity. Clothes, rooms, cars, photos, collections—self can be scaffolded by things outside the skin.

The paradox can now be stated cleanly.

**To be understood, expression needs shared symbols. To feel singular, it needs a difference in how those symbols are used.**

<figure>
  <img src="https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/self-expression/originality-legibility.svg" alt="完全な共有コードでは個性が薄く、完全な私的コードでは読めず、その中間に自己表現が成立することを示す概念図" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Conceptual map, not an empirical curve: self-expression often sits between perfect conformity and unreadable private code.</figcaption>

Self-expression is less “invent your own language” and more “borrow vocabulary, then make a recognizable deviation.”

参照：[McCracken & Roth, “Does clothing have a code?”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-8116(89)90044-X) ／ [Berger & Heath, “Where Consumers Diverge from Others”](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/where-consumers-diverge-others-identity-signaling-product-domains) ／ [Belk, “Possessions and the Extended Self”](https://doi.org/10.1086/209154)

## 3. Audience does not necessarily contaminate expression; it helps shape its form

「人目を気にしたらauthenticityが消える」と考えるとsimpleで気持ちいい。

But communication is already audience-shaped.

Presentation slides, first-date clothing, profile photos, email endings—form changes because another person has to decode it. Audience adaptation can be transmission, not betrayal.

Leary and Kowalski's model also makes impression construction relational. It can depend on self-concept, desired and undesired identities, role constraints, the target's values, and one's current social image.

So even “ありのまま” has a camera angle.

The stronger conclusion is not that everything is fake. It is that **self-expression is co-produced by self and situation**. 親にはweaknessを見せ、仕事ではjudgmentを見せ、友人にはhumorを見せる。同じ人のdifferent layers can all be real.

Self-expression is not one passport photo. It is a lens with changing focal length.

## 4. On social media, the stage becomes an exhibition—and audiences collapse

Bernie Hogan argued in 2010 that Goffman's performance model needs an additional distinction online: **performances** happen in situations, while **exhibitions** consist of persistent artifacts such as profiles, status histories, and photo sets.

That difference is huge. 投稿者がその場にいなくても、old posts keep presenting them.

Alice E. Marwick and danah boyd describe **文脈崩壊（context collapse）**: audiences that would normally be separate—friends, family, coworkers, strangers—can converge on the same post. Users therefore address an **imagined audience**, not a neatly bounded room.

A 2024 Instagram study found that imagined audiences differ between ordinary posts and Stories in dimensions such as size, diversity, specificity, and closeness. Platform features do not merely carry expression; they alter who we feel we are speaking to.

There is a stranger loop too.

Sukyoung Choi and colleagues tested whether public self-presentation changes self-concept under ephemeral versus permanent conditions. In their experiment, participants who publicly presented themselves on ephemeral social media internalized aspects of the personality they had portrayed under particular conditions.

つまり、posting is not always a copy of a pre-existing self. Sometimes the copy feeds back into the original.

Social media may be less like a mirror and more like an editing room where the preview returns slightly late.

参照：[Hogan, “The Presentation of Self in the Age of Social Media”](https://doi.org/10.1177/0270467610385893) ／ [Marwick & boyd, “I tweet honestly, I tweet passionately”](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444810365313) ／ [Yao et al., “Who’s Viewing My Post?”](https://doi.org/10.1177/20563051231224271) ／ [Choi et al., “A snap of your true self”](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444820977199)

## 5. “Express yourself” is not a culturally neutral command

The phrase sounds universal, but its psychological meaning is culturally situated.

Heejung S. Kim and David K. Sherman compared European American and East Asian/East Asian American participants across four studies. European Americans placed greater value on self-expression, and expressing choice increased investment in the chosen option for European Americans but not in the same way for East Asian American participants.

これは「East Asians do not express themselves」というclaimではない。It means the social value attached to expression, choice, autonomy, and relational adjustment can differ.

Hirofumi Hashimoto's 2021 study sampled 1,000 Japanese adults aged 21–69. Self-expression scores tended to rise with age, while rejection-avoidance scores declined. More interestingly, the theoretical framework the paper draws on treats self-expression as a strategy for **making oneself predictable to others**.

That changes the picture.

Self-expression is not only “look how unique I am.” It can also mean: **here is how you can understand what I will do next.**

「これは苦手」「こう進めたい」「ここは譲れない」。These statements display individuality, but they also reduce uncertainty in relationships.

Expression can be coordination.

参照：[Kim & Sherman, “Express Yourself”](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3jq8c82v) ／ [Hashimoto, “Cross-Generational Differences in Independence and Interdependence”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8239137/)

## 6. Buying, wearing, arranging, deleting: much expression happens without speaking

If we equate expression with speaking, we miss half the room.

A bookshelf. Desktop icons. Team merchandise. A carefully empty desk. A photo taken but never posted. These can participate in identity, especially when they are chosen, remembered, displayed, or repeatedly used.

Belk's extended-self perspective is useful because it does not stop the self at the skin.

But possession is not automatically expression. A company uniform, a cheap umbrella bought in a storm, a laptop issued by IT—reading identity straight from these objects can become projection by the observer.

Silence is similar.

Not replying, leaving a bio blank, dressing down in a flashy setting: these can communicate. But only if there is a shared expectation that makes the absence legible.

**Nothing becomes a sign only against a background where something was expected.**

Blank space is not automatically a message. It becomes one when the frame says something should have been there.

## 7. “Just be your true self” is stronger than the evidence

Erica R. Bailey and colleagues analyzed data from 10,560 Facebook users in 2020. They compared self-reported personality with personality inferred from Likes and status language. Greater alignment—what they called quantified authenticity—was associated with greater life satisfaction.

They also ran a preregistered longitudinal experiment. When participants were prompted to post more authentically than usual, within-person mood and positive affect improved.

Interesting result. Dangerous slogan.

The study does not show that everyone should disclose everything, post every impulse, or remove all audience awareness. Their authenticity measure concerns **alignment between self-view and observable expression**, not total disclosure.

Sample, platform, and operationalization matter too.

So a narrower lesson is better: **editing does not automatically destroy authenticity; a problem may arise when the edited persona drifts far from the person's own self-view.**

本音と配慮 can coexist. Role and authenticity can coexist. Consistency across every room is not the same thing as authenticity.

参照：[Bailey et al., “Authentic self-expression on social media is associated with greater subjective well-being”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18539-w)

## 8. Overdoing the analysis: five axes for comparing self-expression

To make the research usable, this essay adds a heuristic—not a validated psychological scale.

<figure>
  <img src="https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/self-expression/five-axes.svg" alt="自己表現を観客、持続性、可読性、コスト、制約の五軸で見る概念図" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>Five-axis scaffold: audience, persistence, legibility, cost, constraint. It compares conditions, not “how expressive” a person is.</figcaption>
</figure>

The five axes are **audience, persistence, legibility, cost, and constraint**.

A tattoo is usually persistent and costly to reverse. A public playlist is easier to edit and uses relatively shared codes. A business suit may be highly legible but strongly constrained by role. A private diary has almost no social audience yet can be read by a future self and can function as self-clarification. A blank profile can look meaningful only on a platform where filling the profile is expected.

The point is not to rank them.

High cost does not equal sincerity. Publicness does not equal courage. Permanence does not equal depth.

What the axes reveal is that expression includes the **conditions chosen for the expression**, not only the content.

## 9. The hidden risk of self-expression is over-explaining yourself

Expression can be misunderstood by others. It can also become too convincing to the person who expressed it.

「自分は内向的」「自分はこういうキャラ」「このジャンルしか聴かない」。A sentence that begins as description can become a constraint if repeated long enough.

Online, old self-presentations remain visible. Hogan's exhibition keeps yesterday's self in today's room. Choi's experiment also suggests that self-presentation can feed into later self-perception under some conditions.

Consistency helps other people predict us. Too much consistency can make us obey our own prediction.

That is why identity should not be managed like a rigid brand manual.

People need permission to revise, retract, try something ugly, change tastes, and pass through periods that do not fit a clean label.

**Expression needs reversibility as well as consistency.**

## 10. Self-expression is not leaking the inside; it is placing a legible difference between self and others

Back to the opening puzzle.

Most materials of self-expression are borrowed: language, colors, genre, clothing conventions, emoji, profile fields, photo formats.

And still, expression can be intensely personal.

Because identity does not reside in owning the raw material. It appears in **selection, omission, combination, emphasis, audience, and deviation**.

A perfectly private language becomes encryption. Perfect conformity erases difference. Between them, we borrow shared codes, load them with our difference, receive a response, and revise our self-understanding.

Before researching this, self-expression looked like a simple arrow: inside → outside.

Afterward, it looks more like a translation loop.

**Self-expression is not a window. It is translation.**

And translation changes the translator. 読まれた表現がfeedbackとして戻り、原文だと思っていたselfまで少し書き換える。

So self-expression is not shipping a finished “true self” into the world.

**It is prototyping an unfinished self in a form other people—and sometimes your future self—can read.**
