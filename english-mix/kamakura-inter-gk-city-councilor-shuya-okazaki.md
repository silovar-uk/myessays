# A city councilor is also a goalkeeper—and he is still playing official matches
## Looking at Kamakura Inter's Shuya Okazaki makes the distance between a local football club and local government suddenly concrete

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Open the official Kamakura City Council page and you find「岡崎修也（1期）」—Shuya Okazaki, first-term council member. Open Kamakura International FC's 2026 player profile and you find「背番号31 岡崎修也」, goalkeeper. Same name, same person. His business card is the kind that needs an unusually tall「役職」column.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Okazaki ran as an independent in the April 27, 2025 Kamakura City Council election and won 1,327 votes. Forty-one candidates competed for 26 seats, and he finished 26th in the official vote count. The city's current member list shows him serving his first term from May 15, 2025 to May 14, 2029, and lists his council group as「鎌倉前進の会」.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The club's May 2025 announcement adds the stranger part: after entering the council, he would continue both his「地域パートナー担当」work and his role as a goalkeeper. A March 2026 player interview still presents him as number 31, and official match reports for June 28 against Shinagawa CC Second and July 12 against Tokyu S Reyes FC both list him among the players who took part.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So this is not「a politician who used to play football」, nor someone keeping a ceremonial registration with a club. At least in the 2026 season, the same name appears both on the city council roster and in official match records. A council seat and shirt number 31 are simultaneously attached to one person.

## 1. It really is the same person—and「goalkeeper」is not past tense

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
My first suspicion was a search-result mix-up. Then official sources started confirming the same identity from every direction. There was nowhere left for the mistake to hide.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
According to the club profile, Okazaki was born on August 3, 1997 in Okayama City. His football history includes Okayama Gakugeikan High School and Niigata University of Management before Kamakura Inter. When the club announced his election in 2025, it described him as both a top-team goalkeeper and a club staff member.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
More importantly, 2026 did not stop at the label「player」. The June 28 Kanagawa Prefecture Adult Football League Division 1 match against Shinagawa CC Second lists「31 岡崎修也」among the players, in a 2–0 win. The July 12 match against Tokyu S Reyes FC again records him among the players, in a 1–1 draw.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Registration alone would make this a funny title. Match records change the meaning. He is doing council work while still training as a competitive player and entering official matches. This is not the abstract phrase「using football experience in politics」. It is the physical fact that balls are still being kicked toward him.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
「City councilor and goalkeeper」was not a metaphor. In city politics he deals with proposals and administrative issues; in football he literally stands in front of a goal. Research somehow made the title contain even more information than it did at the start.

## 2. A city council seat and an active playing career can legally coexist

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The most basic question arrives immediately:「Can a city council member have another job?」The short answer is that Japan's Local Autonomy Act does not impose a blanket ban on private-sector work by local assembly members.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Article 92 of the 地方自治法（Local Autonomy Act）bars certain combinations, including serving simultaneously as a member of the National Diet, another local assembly, or certain employees of the relevant local government. It does not, however, list ordinary private employment, business activity, or being a sports player as universally prohibited occupations.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
That does not mean there are no restrictions. Article 92-2 regulates certain contracting relationships with the local government itself. A legal amendment that took effect in 2023 also changed the treatment of individual contracting below a specified annual amount. Separate municipalities may also have political-ethics rules, so any concrete conflict of interest has to be checked on its own facts.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the relationship is not「football player means you cannot be a councilor」or「once elected, you must retire from football」. At the national-law level, the more relevant questions concern the kind of concurrent office and the person's contractual or financial relationship with the municipality, not the job title「goalkeeper」itself.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
I searched the statute and found no special clause banning concurrent employment as a goalkeeper. Naturally. But confirming the obvious matters here: the two titles look bizarre together, yet the legal structure itself does not make the combination especially mysterious.

## 3. The interesting part is that politics did not turn football into a past-tense biography

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Legal possibility is one thing; actually continuing both roles is another. What stands out in Okazaki's case is that winning a council seat did not automatically turn his playing career into「former goalkeeper」.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
When announcing his election, the club said he would continue as a city council member while remaining both a「地域パートナー担当」and goalkeeper. In his 2026 player interview, Okazaki said council activity had become the main part of his schedule, while the time he went to training became completely「football time」.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In that same interview, he explained that before entering the council he had extensive contact with Kamakura Inter because he also worked for the club. After the change, some weeks contained only training and matches as his「Inter time」. He said this helped him understand how difficult it can be for many working adult players to know everything happening around a club when their time with it is limited.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
That is a useful wrinkle. Adding a new role actually increased his distance from the club in some respects. And according to his own account, that distance created a different player's-eye view.「Concurrent work」is therefore not only an arithmetic problem of doing two jobs; reducing time in one place can also change what becomes visible from that place.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Instead of reading this simply as「footballer becomes politician」, it may be more accurate to see one person looking at the same city from two different locations: inside a community club and inside a local assembly. Whether that produces better political work or better football cannot be inferred from the titles alone.

## 4. Kamakura Inter itself operates beyond the boundaries of the pitch

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Explaining the double career only as Okazaki's unusual biography misses some context. Kamakura Inter is a club whose activity already extends beyond playing matches into stadium operation and community events.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Together with the Kamakura Sports Commission, the club operates「ゴールドクレストスタジアム鎌倉」. In 2026 it continued「草っ原タイム」, a program opening the grass pitch to the community for free, and the club reported 1,643 visitors to its August「クレスタ夏祭り」.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The team plays in 神奈川県社会人サッカーリーグ1部 and uses the vision「クラブ・ウィズアウト・ボーダーズ（CLUB WITHOUT BORDERS）」. It is a competitive football club, but it also uses its stadium as a place for local activity. Before entering the council, Okazaki already occupied two club-side roles: player and staff member working in partner sales and community activity.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
None of this proves that club activity caused his decision to run for office. That causal leap would be too large. What the sources do establish is an environment in which「community-based」meant more than sponsors and match promotion; the club was also involved in operating a physical place and organizing local events.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Seen in that context,「a local-club player also sits in the local assembly」starts to look less random. A club can have contact points far outside the touchline, and one person's field of activity can also extend outside football. There is at least a visible continuity between those facts.

## 5. After going too far and reading council publications, I found that his questions were not only about sports

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Since I had already gone this far, I checked what he actually discusses as a council member. This was partly to eliminate the lazy punchline「he is a GK, so maybe everything in council is about defense」before it could happen.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Issue 272 of「かまくら議会だより」summarizes one of Okazaki's general-question topics as「伝わる広報・発信について」. The record says he asked how the city understands the need for clearer public communication and whether generative AI could be used to make administrative information easier to understand.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The same entry lists other questions on「スポーツ政策の推進について」and citizen participation in「深沢・村岡のまちづくり」. The city's June 2026 committee roster also lists him as a member of the 教育こども常任委員会.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
What this establishes is limited but useful: his recorded council topics are not confined to football. Evaluating the quality of those questions or the merits of any policy would require a different analysis. Here it is enough to note that the public record does not fit the simple category「athlete who only talks about sports」.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
At the beginning,「active GK is a city councilor」was already a complete curiosity. Once the council newsletter enters the research pile, the center of interest shifts slightly—from an unusual pair of titles to the practical design of a life spread across several working environments.

## 6. The story changes when「city councilor + GK」becomes「one person with two positions inside the same community」

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
When I first heard the story, the comedy was entirely in the title combination. City councilor. Goalkeeper. Those two words do not usually sit next to each other in a profile.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
After checking the details, there is legal room to continue competitive sport after election; Okazaki had already worked in community-facing club roles; and the club itself had built contacts outside football through stadium and community activity. The two labels were not suddenly glued together from unrelated worlds.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
In his 2026 interview, Okazaki said that becoming involved in city government gave him more chances to see sport from the outside, while the inside view from the pitch remained something he wanted to preserve. That is his own description of the dual role, not proof that it produces any particular policy or sporting result.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Still, the simple fact that「active player for a community club」and「member of that community's city council」are happening inside the same person expands the outline of what local sport can look like. A club plays matches; a council debates public administration. Those institutions remain distinct. The people around them do not always fit into only one box.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
In council, he votes on matters. On weekends, he stops shots. I could end by saying「both jobs are about protecting something」, but that would make the whole investigation suddenly much worse, so I will not.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Before researching it, this was a「wait, someone actually does that?」story. After researching it, what remains is the sense that **local assemblies and community clubs are both less sealed-off as career spaces than they first appear**. The city council roster and the number 31 team sheet exist at the same time. That fact alone makes「working in a community」look a little broader.
