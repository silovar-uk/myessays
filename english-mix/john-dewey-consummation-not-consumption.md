# 消費 consumption の隣に、成就 consummation がある
## Finishing a work is not the same as completing an experience｜John Deweyから考える鑑賞

前の記事「[作品は減らない。でも、こちらの時間は減る。なら何を持ち帰るか](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/entertainment-from-consumption-to-nourishment?lang=ja)」では、how to turn entertainment from consumption into nourishmentを考えた。

One name kept bothering me: **John Dewey**.

調べ直すと、まずwords themselvesが妙だった。

**consumption** means using, eating, or using something up.
Dewey repeatedly uses **consummation** for something closer to fulfillment or a completed experience.

They look almost like the same word if your eyes move too fast.

But their roots differ. Consumption comes from Latin consumere, to use up. Consummation comes from consummare, to sum up or bring to completion.[Etymonline: consumption](https://www.etymonline.com/word/consumption) / [Etymonline: consummation](https://www.etymonline.com/word/consummation)

**Using something up and bringing something to fulfillment sit next to each other in English with almost the same face.**

That accidental resemblance turns out to be a useful doorway into Dewey.

Finishing a title is not the same as completing an experience.

---

## 1. AUTOPLAY｜The credits can end before the experience does

Streaming service. Movie ends. Credits roll. A box appears: Play next.

The file has finished.
But has the experience?

Dewey gives us a sharper distinction: mere **cessation** versus **consummation**.

In *Art as Experience* (1934), he describes experiences that have a felt beginning, development, and fulfillment. A problem is solved. A game is played through. A conversation rounds itself out. A meal becomes a whole episode. He calls this not experience in general but **“an experience.”**[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Dewey's Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)

Consummation does not mean a happy ending. A quarrel or a storm can also become an experience if its parts belong to one developing whole.

So a video reaching 100 percent is not enough.

**Content has an ending time. Experience also needs an ending on the side of the experiencer.**

履歴に「視聴済み」とつくことと、one experience reaching fulfillmentは別の出来事になる。

## 2. ART PRODUCT｜A finished object can still be an unfinished experience

Dewey does not begin aesthetics by worshipping precious objects.

*Art as Experience* grew from his 1932 William James Lectures at Harvard and was published in 1934.[Open Library: Art as Experience](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL6309819M/Art_as_experience)

He distinguishes the physical **art product**—book, painting, building, object—from the work of art as something that happens in experience.

The book remains on the shelf. The film file remains intact. Yet the aesthetic event requires an interaction between the object and a perceiver.[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: John Dewey](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)

Meaning is not delivered like a parcel.

We receive color, sound, language, rhythm, scenes, movements, relations. Then we connect them, test them against memory, follow tension, revise expectations.

**The object may be finished. The experience is still unfinished.**

これは「観客も作者だから好きに意味づけていい」という話ではない。

The work constrains us. Its form pushes back.
But the audience still has work to do.

## 3. DOING / UNDERGOING｜To receive deeply, we have to send attention outward

Dewey joins two movements that look opposite: **doing** and **undergoing**.

We act on the environment, and the environment acts back on us.[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Dewey's Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)

Watching a film looks passive. We did not shoot the scene.

But while watching, we connect one expression to an earlier scene, expect a turn, get surprised, reorganize the story, notice a sound, bring in a memory, then let the new scene modify that memory.

Dewey says the perceiver in full appreciation must in some sense **re-create** the work in experience. This does not mean reproducing the artist's process. It means selecting, relating, and gathering parts into a whole.[J-STAGE: Creation and Reception of Work as a Human Activity in John Dewey’s Theory of Art](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/uaesj/49/1/49_369/_article/-char/en)

So the paradox is useful:

**receptivity is not passivity.**

作品に委ねるためにも、attentionをこちらから差し出す必要がある。

That turns “ながら見は悪い”というmoral argument into a better question:

How much attention am I making available for the work to act on me?

## 4. RECOGNITION｜“Ah, foreshadowing.” And perception stops

Dewey distinguishes **recognition** from **perception**.

Recognition identifies something through an existing scheme.
Perception lets the present object reorganize the scheme.

You see someone on the street and think, “That’s him.” Done. Recognition.

But maybe you notice a tired expression you have never seen, a pause in the voice, a new way of carrying the body. The familiar person becomes newly visible.[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: archived explanation](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2007/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)

Entertainment gives us easy recognition shortcuts.

“Coming-of-age story.”
“Tsundere.”
“Foreshadowing paid off.”
“Sad ending.”
“Prestige drama.”

Classification is useful. But it can also stop the experience early.

Knowing more does not automatically mean perceiving more.

知識が全部「これはあれ」と処理するspeed-up toolになれば、expertise can become a stencil.

The Deweyan question is not only “What do I know about this?” but:

**What in this work resisted what I already knew?**

## 5. RESISTANCE｜A good experience is not always frictionless

Digital products are excellent at removing friction.

Next video starts automatically.
Recommendations arrive before we search.
Music never stops.
Payment disappears into one tap.

Convenient is good. Dewey is not a UX critic from 1934.

But his theory gives resistance a positive role.

Aesthetic experience includes direction, rhythm, accumulation, and **resistance**. Expectations fail. Materials push back. Something is difficult, strange, unresolved. Our response to that resistance helps give the experience form.[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Dewey's Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)

The point is not “make everything inconvenient.”

The point is that **zero friction can sometimes mean zero encounter**.

If autoplay removes the tiny space in which an unnamed feeling might develop, then convenience has not only saved effort. It may have removed part of the conditions under which an experience could become a whole.

That is my application of Dewey, not something Dewey said about streaming.

A 2026 paper in *Frontiers in Psychology* explicitly rereads *Art as Experience* in relation to fragmented perception today.[Frontiers in Psychology: “Art as Experience” revisited](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1805282/full)

Dewey survives the smartphone era surprisingly well because he was not diagnosing one medium. He was asking what makes experience cohere.

## 6. MUSEUM｜Dewey is not telling us to become solemn art people

At this point Dewey can sound like a philosopher asking us to stare at one painting for thirty minutes.

Actually, he attacks the separation of art from ordinary life.

His critique of the **museum conception of art** targets the idea that aesthetic value belongs mainly to rare objects inside special institutions.[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Against “The Museum Conception of Art”](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)

Dewey wants continuity between art and meals, games, work, ritual, conversation, tools, ordinary perception.

That matters for entertainment.

A two-hour film is not deep because it is long.
A book is not superior because it is a book.
A short clip is not shallow merely because it is short.

The Deweyan axis is different:

**Did the event accumulate, develop, meet resistance, and become a meaningful whole in interaction with the perceiver?**

高尚さではなく、experience qualityの問題になる。

## 7. CONTINUITY｜Nourishment means becoming able to receive more next time

Now move from aesthetics to education.

In *Experience and Education* (1938), Dewey makes a crucial distinction:

Not every experience is equally educative.

Some experiences can narrow sensitivity, harden habits, or reduce the possibility of richer later experience. He calls such cases **mis-educative**.[Experience and Education excerpt, Exploratorium](https://www.exploratorium.edu/sites/default/files/pdfuploads/john_dewey_on_experience_and_education.pdf)

Another key principle is **continuity**: every experience modifies the person who undergoes it, and that modification affects the quality of later experiences.[Google Books: Experience and Education](https://books.google.com/books/about/Experience_And_Education.html?id=JhjPK4FKpCcC)

This gives 「糧」a more precise meaning.

Nourishment is not:
- extracting a lesson,
- remembering every detail,
- writing a long review,
- turning leisure into productivity.

It is this:

**The range of what you can notice next becomes slightly wider.**

After a film, you wait longer before interrupting silence.
After a football match, you start watching players away from the ball.
After one song, bass becomes audible in the next.
After a novel, contradiction in a person becomes easier to tolerate without instantly solving it.

The experience lives on because perception itself has changed.

So instead of asking “What did I get from this work?” we can ask:

**What can the world now give me that I could not receive before?**

## 8. THOUGHT EXPERIMENT｜Three people “watch” the same film

Let’s make the conditions extreme.

A knows the entire plot, ending, themes, and foreshadowing. Afterward, A can explain the film almost perfectly.

B watches the whole thing cold, enjoys it, then immediately starts the next title. The next day, most details are gone.

C watches the whole film and cannot explain everything. But one silence remains. In a meeting the next day, C leaves a three-second pause unfilled for the first time.

Who watched “best”?

We cannot measure that from this thought experiment. It is conceptual, not empirical.

But Dewey gives us several dimensions.

A has rich **recognition**.
B had immediate **enjoyment**.
C shows **continuity** into a later experience.

For this essay, I can reorganize Dewey into four layers:

- **Contact**：作品に触れた。
- **Perception**：既知の型だけで処理せず、relationsを組み直した。
- **Consummation**：parts became one developing experience.
- **Continuity**：その経験がlater perceptionの可能性を変えた。

This is not Dewey's official four-stage model. It is my reconstruction using his concepts.

でも、この並びにすると「消費から糧へ」がはっきりする。

The opposite of consumption is not storage.

**It is carrying contact far enough that the next experience becomes different.**

## 9. COUNTERPOINT｜If everything becomes a lesson, we are consuming again

There is a trap.

Once we decide every work must “feed” us, we start mining movies for lessons, books for quotable lines, concerts for work ideas.

That is another form of extraction.

Dewey's consummation is not only a future investment. The present experience has intrinsic value as a fulfilled whole.

Even criticism, for Dewey, should not hand us the final meaning of a work. Its role is to help us see and hear more, to widen perception.[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: John Dewey, Notes](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/notes.html)

So nourishment does not require an output.

I cried.
I laughed.
I hated it.
I felt disturbed.
I still do not understand it.
One image remains.

That can be enough.

Sometimes the most useful residue is not an answer but **an unresolved sensitivity**.

## 10. PRACTICE｜Give the experience 45 seconds before the next title

I do not want to turn Dewey into a productivity ritual.

So: 45 seconds.

Before the next title, ask three questions.

1. **What happened to me?**  
   何を受けたか。Scene, sound, phrase, discomfort, body feeling.

2. **What did I do with it?**  
   何を予測し、結びつけ、外し、考え直したか。

3. **What can I notice next?**  
   次の作品や生活で、what might become visible now?

Nothing comes up? Stop. Do not fabricate a lesson.

The goal is not more notes.

It is to give cessation a chance to become consummation.

Those 45 seconds are not necessarily “after” the artwork.
In Deweyan terms, they may still be part of the experience.

## 11. DISCOVERY｜Completion does not close experience. It changes the next beginning

At first, I thought “turning entertainment into nourishment” meant carrying something home.

Dewey shifts the idea.

Maybe there is no object to carry.

An experience can alter the relationship between self and environment, making new things receivable later.

Back to the weird pair:

**consumption** uses up.
**consummation** brings together and fulfills.

作品は減らない。
こちらの時間は減る。

But if experience reaches consummation and then enters continuity, the time has not simply disappeared.

**To complete an experience is not to seal it shut. It is to begin the next one as a slightly different perceiver.**

So the opposite of consumption is not preservation.
Not restraint.
Not converting everything into a lesson.

One experience becomes a whole.
Then that ending changes how the next one can begin.

That is what I did not have before reading Dewey:

**A good ending is not the opposite of continuation. A good ending creates a different continuation.**

エンタメを糧にするとは、人生をefficientにすることではない。

It is becoming able to receive a little more of the world.

## APPENDIX｜Dewey式 “carry experience forward” prompt

~~~text
You are a reflection partner using John Dewey's theory of experience.

Goal:
Do not summarize, rate, or extract a moral from the work.
Help me notice where my perception moved,
how the experience became or failed to become a whole,
and what later experience may now become possible.

Use these lenses:

1. undergoing
What acted on me?
Ask about scenes, sounds, words, emotions, discomfort, and bodily reactions.

2. doing
What did I predict, connect, judge, revise, or actively attend to?

3. recognition / perception
Where did I simply label something using an existing category?
Where did the work disturb or update that category?

4. consummation
Where, if anywhere, did this become “an experience” with a felt whole?
Do not force closure if it remained fragmented.

5. continuity
After this experience,
what might I be able to notice in another work, person, job, or ordinary situation
that I could not notice in the same way before?

Rules:
- Ask one question at a time.
- Start concrete.
- Do not add meanings I did not state.
- Treat “I don't know” and “nothing remains” as valid answers.
- Do not turn leisure into homework.
- End with one sentence describing an opened possibility,
  not a lesson extracted from the work.

Work:
{{作品名}}

Raw reaction:
{{感想}}
~~~
