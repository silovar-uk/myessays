# 男子の名ランナーたちの出身校を探したら、女子校だった

## 1. Looking for “Ngaru High” led to a gate marked GIRLS

箱根駅伝には、長いあいだ妙な名門校が存在していた。Nihon University’s Gitau Daniel, Takushoku University’s John Maina, and Nihon University’s Benjamin Gando. When you follow broadcasts and race records, the same school name keeps appearing in their profiles: **Ngaru High**.

実際、日本テレビの過去大会ページには、ギタウ・ダニエルやジョン・マイナ、ガンドゥ・ベンジャミンの出身校として「ガル高」と記録されている。Sendai Ikuei’s official page also lists “Ngaru Secondary School” as the previous school of Kenyan track exchange students including Samuel Wanjiru. If this had existed only as an anonymous-board rumor, the mystery would be easy to dismiss. But **Ngaru High exists inside official Japanese records**. [日本テレビ・第85回箱根駅伝](https://www.ntv.co.jp/hakone/backnumber/85/team_kukan/2.html)／[仙台育英・Sports Exchange Students](https://www.sendaiikuei.ed.jp/i_html/ie_04spo.html)

ところが泉秀一『アフリカから来たランナーたち』の現地取材は、ものすごく嫌な方向に話を進める。When he visits the Ngaru High School found through search, the gate says **Ngaru Girls High School**. The students are girls, and there is not even the kind of running track one would imagine at a legendary athletics school. [文春オンライン](https://bunshun.jp/articles/-/88883)

The supposed producer of several elite male runners was a girls’ school.

At this point, we can still escape with “maybe there is another school with the same name.” But one by one, the escape routes close.

## 2. The school history removes even the possibility that those men studied there

Ngaru Girls High School was not always girls-only. According to a school history obtained locally by Izumi, it began in 1982 as a coeducational school with 16 boys and seven girls. But it became a girls’ school in 1989. [文春オンライン](https://bunshun.jp/articles/-/88884?page=4)

これなら「昔は男子もいた」で説明できそうに見える。But the timeline does not work. Samuel Wanjiru, born in 1986, was two years old in 1988, the final year before the school became girls-only. Gitau Daniel was one, and Benjamin Gando had not even been born.

Current Kenyan school information also lists Ngaru in Kirinyaga County as Girls Only. School directories in 2026 still identify Ngaru Girls Secondary School as a girls’ school. [Kenya Schools Directory](https://kenyaschoolsdirectory.co.ke/school-types/county-high-schools/ngaru-girls-secondary-school/?view=about)／[Kenyan Schools](https://kenyanschools.org/schools/ngaru-girls-secondary)

つまり、「現地にNgaruという学校がない」のではない。**Ngaru exists. What fails to line up is the physical school and the histories of the male runners recorded in Japan.**

That difference matters. A story about a completely fictional school is a ghost tale; a real school name attached to incompatible biographies is a systems problem, and somehow much stranger.

## 3. And this mystery was not suddenly invented in 2025

ここで意地悪く、「本を面白くするために最近つくられたミステリーなのでは」と疑ってみた。

Older traces immediately appear. In 2011, a blogger with Kenya experience researched Ngaru High and noted both the extreme lack of information and the possibility that it functioned as a waypoint for students going to Japan. In 2019, another traveler went to the area and found only Ngaru Girls, while local people denied that Wanjiru had attended it. [2011年の記録](https://ameblo.jp/kogetaku/entry-10755820830.html)／[2019年の現地探索](https://africa-middle-east-faraway.com/kenya-ngaru-school/)

面白いのは、彼らが泉氏とは別ルートで、ほとんど同じ壁にぶつかっていることだ。The school name is familiar on Japanese television and in Japanese records, yet the corresponding institution becomes hard to locate once you arrive in Kenya.

Meanwhile, the Japanese record has not disappeared. Sendai Ikuei still lists “Ngaru Secondary School” among its Kenyan sister schools today. [仙台育英・Sister Schools](https://www.sendaiikuei.ed.jp/hs/en/education/sister-school.html)

ガル高は、現地ではつかめないのに、日本の記録世界ではかなり頑丈に存在している。

## 4. Three hypotheses make “it was just a typo” increasingly difficult

The first hypothesis is **another school with the same name**. Somewhere in Kenya there may have been a Ngaru Secondary School for boys or coed students that researchers have not yet located. It is the least dramatic explanation, but neither Izumi’s fieldwork, current school directories nor earlier independent searches have produced public evidence for such a second school.

第二の仮説は、**単純な記載ミス**である。One athlete’s history could have been entered incorrectly and then copied. Yet Ngaru appears repeatedly for several runners, across both school exchange records and university-ekiden records. A shared input route starts to explain the pattern better than a one-off typo.

The third hypothesis is that it worked as **a routing label for passage to Japan**. In a 2026 interview, Izumi said that before traveling he had suspected Ngaru might have functioned as part of a system connecting Japanese schools seeking fast runners with local athletes who lacked sufficient educational records. [NEWSポストセブン](https://news.livedoor.com/article/detail/31803603/)

ただし、ここは線を引く必要がある。Public sources do not establish who produced which document, or exactly how any administrative procedure was handled. It would therefore be too strong to declare that “Ngaru High was a fabricated school used for falsification.”

What we can say is narrower: **the school name likely carried a function beyond the ordinary meaning of “the school this person attended.”**

## 5. Stop looking for a school and follow the string instead

発想を変える。Instead of searching for a building called Ngaru High, follow the string “Ngaru Secondary School” through the system.

A runner is identified in Kenya. A Japanese high school receives the athlete as an exchange student. A previous-school field appears on an international exchange page. The athlete later enters university, and that school name becomes part of Hakone Ekiden athlete data. Fans see the broadcast or web profile and infer, quite reasonably, that Ngaru is a Kenyan running powerhouse.

この流れでは、途中の一つの項目が正しいかどうかを、下流の利用者はほとんど再検証しない。Instead, **the more often the same string appears on multiple official-looking pages, the more trustworthy it seems.**

In databases, the path showing where data came from, how it was transformed and where it traveled is called data lineage. The Ngaru puzzle is an ekiden story, but it also looks almost like a pure data-lineage failure.

記録は現実を保存する。But once records begin validating one another, they do more than preserve reality — they **amplify the appearance of reality itself**.

## 6. The phrase “five runners from Ngaru” automatically generates a story

ここで小さな思考実験をする。Suppose one fast runner arrives from an unfamiliar foreign high school. We simply conclude: “that school happened to have a great athlete.”

But what if the same school name appears for two, three, then five runners? We begin imagining a system behind it: perhaps an elite coach, a secret altitude program, or a special pipeline into Japan.

泉氏の調査では、日本の高校を経ずケニアから直接大学へ進み箱根を走ったランナーのうち、同じ高校名が複数回出てくる例はガル高の5人だけだったという。 [文春オンライン](https://bunshun.jp/articles/-/88884)

So the aura of Ngaru as a “powerhouse” did not originate in a famous campus or documented Kenyan championships. **It emerged because the same label repeated in Japanese data.**

学校が名門だから有名選手が多かったのか。Or did a prestigious school materialize in our minds because famous athletes kept carrying the same school label? The direction of causality starts to flip.

## 7. Still, this is not simply a story about exposing a lie

The worst move here would be to treat the existence of a mystery as proof of misconduct by someone.

2000年代の国際的な高校留学では、現地の教育制度、在籍証明、日本側の入学要件、競技スカウトの実務を接続する必要がある。When schools, intermediaries, athletes and families across multiple countries handled those processes in a less digitally connected era, inconsistent spellings, provisional affiliations and administrative shortcuts were entirely possible.

At the same time, the Ngaru label persisted across multiple official or quasi-official Japanese records for years, so dismissing it as a trivial detail is unsatisfying. Why was this particular name needed? Who first entered it, and why did it recur for multiple athletes? Public evidence still does not fully explain that.

That is exactly why it stays interesting.

If the answer were simply “there was a villain,” the mystery would close. Instead Ngaru leaves a larger question: **what kinds of intermediate realities are created when institutions have to connect paperwork across national borders?**

## 8. Before researching, it was “a school that did not exist.” Afterward, it became “a record that did exist”

最初、ガル高の面白さは「有名な学校なのに現地にない」という都市伝説にあると思っていた。

After researching it, the picture reverses. Ngaru Girls exists in Kenya. The string Ngaru Secondary School exists on official Japanese pages. It exists in Hakone records too. **What is missing is not the string, but a coherent explanation that connects all those records into one reality.**

学校名は普通、「その人がどこで学んだか」を示す欄だと思う。But in the Ngaru case, that field may have behaved more like an interface connecting the Japan–Kenya recruitment route, exchange-student administration, scouting, and television-profile data.

Seen this way, Ngaru High is not a ghost. It is almost the opposite.

**It may be a school name that survived longer than its explainable reality because it was preserved in too many records.**
