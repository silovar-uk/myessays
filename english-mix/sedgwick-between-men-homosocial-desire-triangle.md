# In a love triangle, the two men may be watching each other the most
## Reading competition, friendship, and recognition through Sedgwick's『男同士の絆』

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Man A and man B are competing for woman C. In the simplest diagram, the arrows are A→C and B→C. でも物語を少し長く眺めると、a strange thing happens.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
A watches B's clothes, job, reputation, confidence, and way of treating C. B watches A too. They are supposed to be focused on C, yet their eyes keep turning sideways. 恋愛ドラマの顔をしているのに、途中から互いの戦闘力を測るranking matchみたいになる。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The scholar who turned that sideways gaze into a serious theory was Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick. In a 2000 lecture, she described the germ of『男同士の絆（Between Men）』in strikingly ordinary terms: when two men were in love with the same woman, they often seemed to care much more about each other as rivals than about the woman on whom their desire was supposedly fixed.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That changes the geometry. A love triangle is no longer only「two men wanting one woman」. It can also become a story in which **two lines aimed at a woman strengthen the third line between the men**.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, “Thinking through Queer Theory,” 2001](https://www2.igs.ocha.ac.jp/en/wp-content/uploads/2002/04/04-Sedgwick.pdf) ／ [名古屋大学出版会『男同士の絆』](https://www.unp.or.jp/ISBN/ISBN4-8158-0400-1.html)

> **Information cutoff: September 23, 2026**
>
> This essay does not apply Sedgwick's concept unchanged to every contemporary relationship among men. 『男同士の絆』is primarily a historical literary study of early modern through nineteenth-century English literature, tracing relations among male bonds, women, class, heterosexuality, and homosexuality. 以下では、そのhistorical scopeと、現在の関係を見るための応用を分けて扱う。

## 1. The starting question was not only「I want her」but also「why do I care so much about beating him?」

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
What makes Sedgwick's question interesting is that she did not try to diagnose the private feelings inside each person. She watched how desire moved through a relationship.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The original『Between Men: English Literature and Male Homosocial Desire』was published in 1985; the Japanese translation appeared in 2001 as『男同士の絆――イギリス文学とホモソーシャルな欲望』. Reading texts from Shakespeare to Dickens, Sedgwick examined how male friendship, mentorship, entitlement, rivalry, heterosexuality, and homosexuality were related to women, class, and the wider gender system.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Later, Sedgwick said the project began from the observation that when two men loved the same woman, they seemed intensely preoccupied with each other as rivals. 重要なのは、she did not reduce this to「the men were secretly in love with each other」.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Her question was structural: the object of desire and the person who intensifies or organizes desire do not have to be the same. For A, B may be more than an obstacle. B's desire for C, A's wish to defeat B, and A's wish to be recognized as the more valuable man can all reshape what C means to A.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The love triangle then becomes more than a diagram around an object. It becomes a diagram of **recognition, imitation, and rivalry circulating through three positions**. If we look only at who loves whom, we may miss whose gaze makes that love feel valuable.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Columbia University Press,『Between Men』](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/between-men/9780231082730/) ／ [Sedgwick, “Thinking through Queer Theory”](https://www2.igs.ocha.ac.jp/en/wp-content/uploads/2002/04/04-Sedgwick.pdf)

## 2. 「Male homosocial desire」is not another name for romantic or sexual desire between men

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The easiest phrase to misread is「male homosocial desire」. On the surface, it looks like an awkward splice between social bonds among men and erotic desire. Sedgwick herself described the phrase as something like an oxymoron.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Homosocial was already used in history and social science for same-sex social bonds before Sedgwick's book. She deliberately pulled it back into the orbit of「desire」. ただし、ここでのdesireは特定の恋愛感情やsexual urgeだけを指さない。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In a 1984 essay, Sedgwick placed friendship, mentorship, rivalry, institutional subordination, homosexual genitality, and economic exchange within the spectrum of male homosocial desire. In her 2000 lecture, she explained that she chose「desire」to name a systemic social or affective force—something like glue—that shapes an important relation, even when it appears as hostility or hatred.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So「A and B are fierce enemies, therefore they are not bonded」does not necessarily follow. If they constantly observe one another, if each person's status rises or falls through the other's judgment, and if defeating the other organizes their behavior, then hostility itself can be a very strong social attachment.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Sedgwick's「desire」is therefore less a word for asking「do they like each other?」and more a word for asking **who has become indispensable to whose sense of value**. Once we see that, friendship and rivalry stop sitting at opposite ends of a simple scale.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Sedgwick, “Sexualism and the Citizen of the World,”『Critical Inquiry』1984](https://criticalinquiry.uchicago.edu/past_issues/issue/december_1984_v11_n2/) ／ [Sedgwick, “Thinking through Queer Theory”](https://www2.igs.ocha.ac.jp/en/wp-content/uploads/2002/04/04-Sedgwick.pdf)

## 3. In the triangle, a woman can be both「the love object」and「a mediator of relations between men」

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Sedgwick drew on René Girard's triangular desire and on the「traffic in women」framework associated with Gayle Rubin and others because she wanted to make visible how relationships between men can be built through women.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Take A and B competing for C. On the surface, C is the center. Yet A competes because B is there, while B may read C's value partly through A's desire. Add institutions such as marriage, property, inheritance, or status, and「who gets the woman」can also help determine rank or alliance among men.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Sedgwick did not treat this as a symmetrical triangle. The positions of the two men and the woman were historically unequal. In the structures she analyzes, a woman is a person with her own desires, but she can also be placed in a position through which male bonds, status, and power are transmitted.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The unsettling point is not a reveal that「the men secretly loved each other」. It is that a story sincerely organized around heterosexual love can simultaneously organize male recognition, rivalry, property, and status. 恋愛感情がfakeなのではない。Feeling simply does not float outside social structure.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The triangle matters because it shows that **one relationship can become infrastructure for another relationship**. If we look only at A→C, we may miss the recognition and power moving through A↔B.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Sedgwick, “Sexualism and the Citizen of the World”](https://criticalinquiry.uchicago.edu/past_issues/issue/december_1984_v11_n2/) ／ [De Gruyter / Columbia University Press,『Between Men』overview](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.7312/sedg90478/html)

## 4. Male intimacy and homosexuality are not the same, but the line declaring them「completely unrelated」is also socially produced

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Another major move in Sedgwick's argument was refusing to place homosocial and homosexual into two perfectly sealed boxes: social friendship on one side, sexual homosexuality on the other.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Compared with relations among women, Sedgwick argued that modern American culture made the continuity between male homosocial and homosexual bonds far less visible. She also noted that forms of「male bonding」could be accompanied by intense homophobia.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Looking back in 2000, she described a potentially unbroken continuum between homosocial and homosexual relations while emphasizing that its visibility had been radically disrupted for men. ここでのcontinuumは、all male friendship is secretly sexual、という主張ではない。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The stranger point is that strongly bonded groups of men may sometimes police the declaration「this is not homosexual」with unusual force. Closeness is permitted, but its meaning is pre-limited. In that sense, homophobia can function not only as hostility outside male bonding, but as a boundary line that regulates what counts as an acceptable bond between men.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So the useful question is not「is this friendship actually sexual?」. It is **how close are men allowed to become, where is the line drawn, who draws it, and what social order does that line protect?** Sedgwick turns the border around a relationship into part of the relationship itself.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Sedgwick, “Thinking through Queer Theory”](https://www2.igs.ocha.ac.jp/en/wp-content/uploads/2002/04/04-Sedgwick.pdf) ／ [Nils Hammarén & Thomas Johansson, “Homosociality,” 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244013518057)

## 5. If we reduce homosociality to「bad male-group vibes」, we lose the concept's weird strength

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
In contemporary Japanese usage,「ホモソーシャル」often appears as a critical term for male in-groups, exclusion of women, or misogynistic norms. That use can be meaningful, but it is narrower than Sedgwick's full analytical field.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Sociologist Noritaka Moriyama argued in 2022 that the term is widely used to criticize the internal rules and atmosphere of male-centered society, while those usages can differ from Sedgwick's. Rather than eliminate the ambiguity, he argues for controlling and reshaping it according to the context of description.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Sedgwick's own list already included friendship, mentorship, rivalry, institutional subordination, sexual relations, and economic exchange. つまり、good relationsだけでもbad relationsだけでもない。Misogyny and homophobia are crucial axes of analysis, but simply「men being friends」or「a group containing only men」does not automatically identify the same structure.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
When the word becomes a label for a bad group, judgment gets easy:「this is homosocial」and the analysis can stop. Sedgwick becomes more interesting after that point. Who evaluates whom? Whose recognition creates value? Is a woman participating, mediating, or being excluded? Where do rivalry and intimacy connect?

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**A concept is more useful when it adds invisible lines than when it merely sticks a name onto people.** Instead of painting「male society」as one dark block, we can draw friendship, hostility, imitation, recognition, and hierarchy as separate but connected relations.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[Noritaka Moriyama,「ホモソーシャル概念の多義性を使い尽くす」2022](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsr/73/1/73_2/_article/-char/ja/) ／ [名古屋大学出版会『男同士の絆』](https://www.unp.or.jp/ISBN/ISBN4-8158-0400-1.html)

## 6. Push it too far: audit a love triangle with verbs instead of a character chart

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The following is not Sedgwick's method reproduced exactly. It is a small paper test built from the concept so that「homosocial」does not turn into a vague label. When you find a triangle, write verbs before writing conclusions.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Use fictional A, B, and C. A and B are men, C is a woman. Both men desire C and treat each other as rivals. A normal character chart stops at「A→C←B」. Add four more checks.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
- **Watch**：What does A observe about B besides C? What does B keep watching about A?
- **Measure**：By what standards do A and B compare value—appearance, money, work, courage, reputation, something else?
- **Recognize**：Beyond being chosen by C, does A also want B to acknowledge defeat?
- **Delete**：If C disappears from the scene, how much of the A–B rivalry remains? If B disappears, how much does the shape or intensity of A's desire for C change?

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The「delete」question is especially useful. It is not a scientific test and it cannot prove hidden motives. でも、it can show where the narrative load is sitting. If A and B keep comparing themselves after C is removed, the story's energy is not coming only from the love object. If A's desire collapses when B disappears, the rival may have been amplifying that desire.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Once these verbs are added, the triangle stops being a map of「who likes whom」. It becomes **a circuit diagram of who watches, measures, recognizes, defeats, and gives value to whom**. The fun of Sedgwick is not hunting for a secret romance behind the romance. It is noticing the line the first diagram forgot to draw.

## 7. After『Between Men』, friendship and hostility look less like opposites and more like different voltages on the same circuit

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Return to the opening triangle. A and B compete for C. Before reading Sedgwick, I would have explained it simply: both men like C, therefore they fight.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Sedgwick adds more questions. Who does A want to defeat? Does B's desire intensify A's desire? Has the relationship with C become a sign of rank between the men? Are A and B sharing the same standards of masculine value even while hating each other? And what positions are assigned to women and homosexuality so that this male bond can hold together?

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This does not make every male friendship suspicious. Tender friendships and supportive mentorships obviously exist. But「they are friends, so power is irrelevant」and「they are enemies, so they are not bonded」are both too simple. People can make one another important through affection, recognition, imitation, competition, fear, or material interest.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That is why the triangle looks slightly different after『Between Men』. Drawing two arrows from men toward a woman is not enough. We also need one thick line along the bottom. **What flows through that line is not necessarily romance. It can still be a form of desire powerful enough to organize lives.**

Then even the Japanese title『男同士の絆』stops sounding purely gentle.

A bond is not simply another name for friendship.

**You can also be bonded to the person you cannot stop measuring yourself against.**
