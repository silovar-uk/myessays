# 身だしなみ is the work of reducing information that never needed to be read
## Finding the floor of「最低限」through visual noise, not fashion points

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
「身だしなみは最低限でいい」と言われても、I never knew where that minimum actually ended.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Fix your hair. Remove wrinkles. Check your shoes. Wear something that fits the situation. Fine. But then what? 眉毛はどこまで触るのか。How expensive should shoes be? Do I need to follow trends? 「清潔感を出す」と言われても、cleanliness is a condition while the impression of cleanliness exists in another person's perception.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The list keeps expanding because I was asking an additive question: **What else should I add to look better?**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
A different question suddenly gave the minimum a floor: **What visual information does the other person not need to process?** 身だしなみ then stops being mainly about decoration. It becomes information design for an interaction.

> **Research cutoff: September 22, 2026**
>
> 「Visual noise」in this essay is not an established psychological theory of grooming. It is my synthesis using research on first impressions, person perception, visual clutter, and processing fluency. Visual-clutter research mainly studies displays and scenes, not human appearance, so I use it as an analogy rather than direct proof.

## 1. If the axis is「おしゃれ」, the minimum never ends

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The floor is hard to see because style is an open-ended scoring game.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Clothes have price, fabric, color, shape, trend, brand, season, fit. Hair has length, texture, color, styling. 「もっと良く」を目標にすれば、there is always another upgrade. Better shoes reveal the bag. A better bag reveals the watch. A haircut reveals the eyebrows.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
On this axis, 「最低限」is merely the arbitrary point where you stop collecting bonus points.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So minimum grooming needs another axis. Not “How attractive can I become?” but **“At what point does unintended information begin to compete with the actual purpose of this encounter?”** Once the floor is defined as interference rather than excellence, the task becomes finite.

## 2. People start reading appearance before we start explaining ourselves

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Why reduce visual noise at all? Because observers do not treat appearance as blank background.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In work by Janine Willis and Alexander Todorov, 100 milliseconds of exposure to unfamiliar faces was already enough for judgments of attractiveness, likeability, trustworthiness, competence, and aggressiveness to correlate highly with judgments made without time pressure. これは「100ミリ秒で人を正しく見抜ける」という意味ではない。It means impression formation starts extremely quickly.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Neil Hester and Eric Hehman's 2023 review goes wider: clothing, hairstyle, makeup, and accessories are integrated into person perception. Observers infer social identities, mental states, status, and aesthetic tastes from dress. しかも、その読み取りにはculture, stereotypes, attitudes, and preferences on the observer's sideも入る。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
That makes grooming less magical and more defensive. 外見は、こちらが意図していない意味まで読まれる。The goal is not to force a favorable judgment. It is to avoid supplying unnecessary accidental material for judgment when we can reasonably remove it.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The real issue with first impressions is not only speed. **Speed immediately turns into meaning.** 「最低限」は、その意味づけを止めることではなく、自分で減らせる偶発的な材料を減らすことに置ける。

[Willis & Todorov — First Impressions](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01750.x/) ／ [Hester & Hehman — Dress is a Fundamental Component of Person Perception](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10559650/)

## 3. Visual noise is not ugliness; it is the cost of finding the signal

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
「ノイズ」を、uglyの polite synonym にしてはいけない。

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Ruth Rosenholtz and colleagues studied visual clutter and described how excessive or disorganized display elements can interfere with search, recognition, segmentation, and other perceptual tasks. これは人の外見ではなく、visual displays and scenesを中心にした研究である。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
But the analogy is useful. A single stain on a white shirt can grab attention before the price or design of the shirt matters. 片方だけ大きく折れた襟、残った仕付け糸、意図せず一束だけ跳ねた髪。None says anything reliable about personality. Yet each can become a salient difference.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The problem starts even before somebody concludes「だらしない人だ」. **Attention moved to something that did not need to become a topic.**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That is why the noise model changes the job. You do not need luxury. You do not need trend knowledge. You need a state in which **one accidental detail does not become more legible than the person as a whole.**

[Rosenholtz, Li & Nakano — Measuring Visual Clutter](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18217832/)

## 4. Break down「清潔感」and much of it becomes “remove accident information”

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
「清潔感」is useful in conversation but too vague for defining a floor.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Actual cleanliness can be linked to actions: wash, shower, brush teeth. But perceived cleanliness can blend with hairstyle, skin, color choices, age, body expectations, and gender norms. 「爽やか」「ちゃんとしている」「若々しい」まで一緒にすると、最低限の話がいつの間にかtaste and social preferenceになる。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
For the minimum, I would temporarily drop the word and ask a narrower question: **Is there accident information left over?** 汚れ、ほこり、糸、保管で付いた強いしわ、意図しない寝癖。These are not identity. They are traces of maintenance that stopped halfway.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The floor then becomes modest: **separate intention from accident, and reduce only the accident.** You can do that without becoming stylish, expensive, young-looking, or generic.

## 5. Clothes always communicate, so “neutral clothing” is mostly a fantasy

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Reducing noise does not make you invisible. Dress itself carries information.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Hester and Hehman review how people infer information from dress. DongWon Oh, Eldar Shafir, and Alexander Todorov showed across multiple studies that subtle economic-status cues in clothing affected perceived competence from faces. これは服が実際の能力を示したという話ではない。It shows that observers may let class-coded clothing leak into competence judgments.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Neil Howlett and colleagues also found that small differences between an off-the-peg and bespoke suit changed several first-impression ratings when participants viewed a faceless male model briefly.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the promise cannot be “dress correctly and people will finally see the true you.” People will still read clothing. **The achievable goal is smaller: do not create extra unintended readings when you do not need them.**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
身だしなみ is not deleting individuality. It is separating two kinds of signal: what you chose to express, and what maintenance accidentally expressed for you. 前者はexpression、後者はaccident. That boundary is a plausible floor for「最低限」.

[Oh, Shafir & Todorov — Economic status cues from clothes affect perceived competence from faces](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-019-0782-4) ／ [Howlett et al. — The influence of clothing on first impressions](https://doi.org/10.1108/13612021311305128)

## 6. Four questions make the floor surprisingly visible

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Here is the practical version. This is my framework, not a validated psychological scale.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
鏡を見るとき、do not begin with「似合っているか」「かっこいいか」. Begin with four checks.

1. **事故か / Accident**  
   汚れ、ほこり、糸、強いしわ、意図しない乱れなど、you did not choose this information.

2. **未完了か / Unfinished**  
   Buttons, collar, hem, shoes, hair—does anything look like preparation stopped halfway?

3. **場面から浮いているか / Context mismatch**  
   Not “Is this outfit good?” but “Will the outfit itself become the topic before the role or conversation does?”

4. **注意を横取りしているか / Attention theft**  
   If one detail grabs the eye, is that the intended focal point or an accidental one?

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
These questions differ from “How can I improve the look?” If there is nothing to fix, you are allowed to stop. 時計もいらない。Brand knowledgeもいらない。You do not need to keep studying hair trends.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Minimum grooming needs a stopping rule more than it needs bonus-point knowledge.** The best thing the noise model gives me is not style skill. It is permission to say,「ここまででいい」.

## 7. Processing ease suggests why “nothing caught my eye” can be a successful outcome

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
A neighboring concept is processing fluency.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Rolf Reber, Norbert Schwarz, and Piotr Winkielman proposed that the more fluently an object can be processed, the more positive the aesthetic response tends to be. Their framework integrates factors such as symmetry, prototypicality, repetition, and figure-ground contrast.

<!-- level:3 role:qualification -->
This does **not** directly prove that tidy grooming makes people like you. Different stimuli, different claim. 借りたいのは、「評価は対象そのものだけでなく、その対象を処理した経験にも左右される」という視点である。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Applied cautiously, this suggests a useful priority: before adding impressive signals, remove avoidable friction. 相手が会話中、あなたの服装について一度も考えなかったとしても、それは失敗ではない。Attention remained available for the conversation.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Successful grooming can be invisible. No compliment, no wow. **The design worked because it returned attention to the actual subject.**

[Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman — Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15582859/)

## 8. Do not use “noise” as an excuse to delete the person

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The dangerous version of this idea is obvious: “If other people notice it, remove it.”

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
服装の意味はculture and contextで変わる。Hair, makeup, religious clothing, disability-related devices, ethnic expression, gender expression, and bodily characteristics can all be visually salient. Treating them as noise simply because they differ from a majority norm would turn grooming into conformity pressure.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Person-perception research itself emphasizes the observer's cultural knowledge, stereotypes, attitudes, and preferences. 「相手がそう読んだ」と「その人に問題がある」は同じではない。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So I want a narrow definition: visual noise here means **unintended, reasonably fixable information that is weakly related to the purpose of the interaction.**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Minimum grooming is not making yourself thinner as a person. It is drawing a boundary between chosen expression and accidental residue. **Do not erase individuality. Prevent accidents from impersonating individuality.**

## 9. 身だしなみ can be designed around where the other person's attention remains

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
So what is the floor of「最低限」?

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
I used to imagine a checklist of hair, brows, skin, clothes, shoes, nails, and then wonder how many points each item needed. That approach produces anxiety because beauty knowledge has no ceiling.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The visual-noise model asks less. **Am I handing the other person accidental information they never needed to read?**

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That moves grooming slightly away from self-evaluation. It is not primarily about whether I look beautiful, fashionable, youthful, or expensive. It is about **how much attention I can leave available for the conversation, the work, the greeting, the meal—whatever we actually came here to exchange.**

Maybe grooming is not a technique for saying「look at me better」.

Maybe it is the opposite: **reduce what does not need to be looked at, so the other person can choose what matters.**

Then the floor becomes concrete enough for me:

**Unintended information should not stand in front of the actual subject.**

At least there, I can stop.

>
> Use four criteria:
>
> 1. Accident: 汚れ、ほこり、糸、強いしわ、意図しない乱れなど、本人がそう見せようとしていない情報
> 2. Unfinished: 準備途中、手入れ途中、直し忘れに見える情報
> 3. Context mismatch: その場の目的・役割・関係より先に、服装自体が話題になりそうな不一致

>
> Rules:
>
> - Do not judge beauty, age, body shape, skin texture, or bodily characteristics themselves.
> - Do not treat luxury, trends, brands, or looking younger as the correct answer.
> - Do not label religious, cultural, gender-expression, or disability-related elements as noise merely because they are visually distinctive.
> - Separate「直すべき」「好み」「任意」.
> - Once minimum grooming is satisfied, explicitly say「ここで終了」rather than forcing further optimization.
> - If you suggest improvements beyond the floor, label them「任意の加点」.
>
> Finish with three buckets:「今すぐ30秒」「外出前に3分」「直さなくてよい」.

## Research Note

### What the published research supports

- Very brief exposure can be enough for people to begin forming facial trait impressions; that does not establish that those impressions are accurate.
- Clothing, hairstyle, makeup, and accessories contribute to person perception.
- Inferences from dress are shaped partly by the observer's cultural knowledge, stereotypes, attitudes, and preferences.
- Subtle clothing cues can affect judgments such as perceived competence, without measuring actual competence.
- Visual-clutter research shows that clutter can make search and recognition harder, mainly in displays and scenes.
- Processing-fluency research links ease of processing with more positive aesthetic response under various conditions.

### Interpretation and proposals in this essay

- 「Grooming as visual-noise reduction」is my synthesis, not a standard psychological construct.
- The four-part audit—accident, unfinished, context mismatch, attention theft—is an editorial framework.
- I do not claim that visual-clutter or processing-fluency findings directly transfer to human appearance.
- The proposed floor is intentionally narrow: reduce unintended, reasonably fixable information, not identity or culturally distinctive expression.
