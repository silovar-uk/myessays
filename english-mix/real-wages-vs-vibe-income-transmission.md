# Real Wages +2.4%, Yet the Vibe Is 46.4
## 「給料が増えたのに使わない」日本を追う

Japan’s economic report card on 8 September 2026 looked slightly broken.

July **real wages were up 2.4% year on year**.

Real GDP for April–June grew **0.4% quarter on quarter**, or 1.4% at an annualised rate.

ここまで見ると、quite a respectable answer sheetである。

Then you keep reading.

GDP **private consumption was flat**. The Family Income and Expenditure Survey said real consumption spending by two-or-more-person households was **down 3.6% year on year** in July.

And the August Economy Watchers Survey gave us a current-conditions DI of **46.4**.

Wages: +2.4.
GDP: +0.4.
Household spending: -3.6.
The vibe: 46.4.

同じ国の話をしているように見えない。

And 46.4 has a particularly funny-looking quality.

**Is the economy getting 46.4 out of 100? Is Japan failing the test?**

Five minutes into the research, I had to correct myself.

**46.4 is not a score out of 100.**

The Economy Watchers Survey asks people close to day-to-day economic activity—taxi drivers, retailers, restaurant workers and others—how conditions look, mainly compared with three months earlier. Their responses are converted into a diffusion index, or DI. Fifty is not a “passing grade”; it is closer to a reference point for the balance of judgments.

つまり、最初に赤点扱いしていた私が一番あやしい。

So I changed the investigation.

Instead of asking, “Which number is correct?”, I decided to treat each indicator as a witness.

**Five witnesses. One economy. What did each of them actually see?**

---

## 1. Five witnesses sit at the same table

今回並べるのは5人。

| Witness | Latest headline | What it actually observes | What it cannot tell us alone |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Real wages | July +2.4% YoY | Purchasing power of employees’ wages after price adjustment | Non-employed households, disposable income after tax and social insurance |
| GDP private consumption | Q2 0.0% QoQ | Economy-wide quarterly household consumption | Month-to-month movements and household distribution |
| Household survey | July -3.6% YoY / +0.5% MoM | Spending of a statistical sample of roughly 9,000 households | Economy-wide seller-side revenue, spending by inbound tourists, etc. |
| BOJ Consumption Activity Index | July data released Sept. 7 | Consumption activity estimated from multiple sales/supply-side statistics | Household sentiment and individual household circumstances |
| Economy Watchers | August current DI 46.4 | The economic “feel” reported by people at the front line | Actual yen amount of household consumption |

Already, the mystery becomes less mysterious.

警察ドラマ風に言えば、

- real wages saw the payslip,
- the household survey saw the wallet,
- GDP counted activity across the whole city,
- the BOJ index watched the tills and supply-side data,
- Economy Watchers listened to the room.

Everyone says, “I saw the economy.”

**They just weren’t standing in the same room.**

---

## 2. YoY and MoM are not playing the same match

The next problem was time.

July household spending was **down 3.6% year on year** in real terms.

But seasonally adjusted spending was **up 0.5% from June**.

Same month. Negative 3.6. Positive 0.5.

数字の人格が安定していない。

Except, of course, the denominator is different.

```text
Year on year : July 2026 vs July 2025
Month on month: July 2026 vs June 2026
GDP QoQ       : Apr–Jun 2026 vs Jan–Mar 2026
```

If July 2025 was unusually strong, July 2026 can improve from June and still look weak year on year.

That is elementary arithmetic.

But headlines put the percentages next to one another and our brains quietly make them compete.

**-3.6% and +2.4% looked like they were arguing only because I had put them in the same tournament.**

---

## 3. “Higher wages → more spending” has too few stations

Still, the central question survives.

Real wages were positive for a seventh straight month and rose 2.4% in July. The health and labour ministry also said scheduled cash earnings—the basic-pay component—rose **4.1% year on year**. Slower inflation and firmer basic wages both helped real purchasing power.

So why didn’t spending jump?

I first drew the lazy causal chain:

```text
Wages ↑ → Income ↑ → Spending ↑
```

Three stations. Nice little railway.

The actual line has more stops.

```text
Wages ↑
  ↓
Disposable income after tax and social insurance?
  ↓
Confidence that the income gain will continue?
  ↓
Save, repay debt, or spend?
  ↓
What category receives the spending?
  ↓
Observed consumption
```

Real wages sit fairly high upstream in this system.

実質賃金だけでは、手取りの可処分所得、非雇用世帯への波及、将来所得への安心感までは分からない。

So we also cannot jump straight to the convenient explanation:

**“Japanese households are simply saving the extra money.”**

Maybe. But that requires evidence on household saving, disposable income and distribution.

ここは笑いを止めるところだった。

A national-character story—“Japanese people are anxious savers”—would finish the article very efficiently. It would also outrun the evidence.

---

## 4. One step too far: break income transmission into five stages

Instead of ranking indicators as good or bad, I classified them by **where they sit in the transmission chain**.

### A. Income generation / 稼ぐ力

Real wages: **improving**.

Employment has also remained relatively firm.

This part looks brighter than before.

### B. Spending capacity / 使える力

Disposable income: **needs a separate check**.

Real wages are not the same thing as money freely available after tax, social insurance and fixed obligations.

### C. Confidence / 使う気持ち

Economy Watchers current-conditions DI: **46.4**.

That was up from 45.7 in July. The outlook DI rose to 48.3 from 45.8. Improvement, yes. Full-throated optimism, not quite.

### D. Actual spending / 実際に使った量

GDP private consumption: **flat quarter on quarter**.

Household survey: **-3.6% YoY, +0.5% MoM**.

Not a collapse. Also not the engine of growth.

### E. The whole economy / 経済全体

Real GDP: **+0.4% QoQ**.

External demand contributed positively, while domestic demand contributed little. GDP growth therefore does not mean household demand suddenly became strong.

Put these five stages together and the picture changes.

**The entrance—earning power—is improving. The exit—spending—is not yet accelerating.**

The interesting part is the pipe in between.

---

## 5. Three extreme thought experiments make the disagreement look normal

To stress-test the intuition, I made three deliberately simple scenarios.

### Case 1: Wage gains go mainly to workers

Suppose 60 of 100 households contain workers whose real wages improve.

The other 40 are pensioner or other non-employed households.

Then “employees’ real wages” and “all-household consumption” do not have to move at the same speed.

### Case 2: Income rises, but fixed costs absorb it

Your monthly take-home rises by ¥10,000.

Housing, education, insurance, debt repayment or other fixed commitments absorb ¥9,000.

The wage statistic improves. Your genuinely discretionary spending power rises by only ¥1,000.

### Case 3: Some households splurge while others cut necessities

One group spends more on travel, restaurants and entertainment.

Another cuts food and daily purchases.

The average can print “flat” while lived experience splits sharply.

These are thought experiments, not claims that Japan currently matches them.

でも一つ分かる。

The assumption that **average wages ↑ must produce average spending ↑ by roughly the same amount** was the more aggressive assumption all along.

---

## 6. Why I couldn’t simply celebrate the GDP upgrade

The second estimate released on September 8 revised Q2 real GDP growth to **+0.4% quarter on quarter, +1.4% annualised**.

That is genuinely good news.

But the components matter.

Private consumption was **0.0% QoQ**. Private non-residential investment was **-0.9%**, an improvement from the first estimate of -1.2%. External demand also supported growth.

So:

**The aggregate score improved.**

Households were not the main reason it improved.

ここで、冒頭の「景気46.4点」という誤解が逆向きに効いてきた。

There is no single answer sheet called “the Japanese economy”.

GDP has its sheet.
Wages have theirs.
Households have theirs.
Front-line sentiment has another.

Overlay them carelessly and you mostly get black ink.

---

## 7. Before researching, I thought “people got paid more and still didn’t spend”

My first hypothesis was embarrassingly convenient.

**“Real wages +2.4%, spending weak. Maybe everyone just saved it.”**

After the research, the question is different.

What we know is:

- employees’ real wages have improved,
- real GDP is growing,
- quarterly private consumption is flat,
- the household survey is weak year on year but positive month on month,
- Economy Watchers sentiment improved but remains below 50.

The next investigation therefore needs less psychology and more plumbing.

**Disposable income, propensity to consume, saving rates, and spending by income and age group** matter next.

問いは、

“Why don’t Japanese people spend?”

ではなく、

> **Where, for whom, and through which mechanism does better income fail to transmit into consumption?**

That is a much more useful question.

---

## 8. 46.4 was not a failing grade. It was a clue about the missing arrow

At the beginning, I wanted to give the economy 46.4 points out of 100.

That was wrong.

GDP +0.4% does not mean “Japan became 0.4% better”.

Real wages +2.4% do not mean every household became 2.4% richer and will spend 2.4% more.

Each number is a torch pointing at a different part of the system.

5本のライトを並べてみると、暗い場所の位置が変わった。

Previously, the obvious story was:

**“Prices outran wages, therefore households cannot spend.”**

With July real-wage data, at least for now, the puzzle is shifting downstream.

**What happens after real income improves? How efficiently does that improvement become consumption?**

In other words:

**「稼げへん」から「稼いだ金が動かへん」へ。**

The silly-looking 46.4 led not to an economic scorecard, but to a question about **transmission**.

So the next number I want is not another wage percentage.

**I want to know where the extra ¥10,000 goes.**
