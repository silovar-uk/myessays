# 球場は誰のものか — Whose Stadium Is It, Anyway
## Designated-manager system・self-owned ballparks・acquisitionから、スタジアム所有構造を読む

第13回では、命名権を通じて「球場の所有者」という新しい登場人物が出てきた。

球団が本拠地とする球場は、自治体が所有する場合もあれば、球団自身が所有・建設する場合もある。

第13回の最後に、こう問いを置いた。

**What concrete difference does that ownership structure actually make to a club's business and its relationship with fans?**

今回はここから始める。

> **Information basis: 2026年9月1日**
>
> Construction and acquisition figures used here come from reporting, and since coverage varies in specifics, this article sticks to what's confirmable — share percentages acquired, closing dates, and so on. No value judgment is made about which ownership model is "better."

---

## 1. SHORT ANSWER — ownership and operation split apart under the law

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
球場の所有構造を理解する出発点は、**"who owns the stadium" and "who runs the stadium" are, legally speaking, two different questions**という点である。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
A September 2003 amendment to the Local Autonomy Act introduced the "designated manager system" (指定管理者制度), opening up the management of "public facilities" — previously restricted to public bodies and municipality-funded corporations — to private organizations broadly.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
これにより、自治体が球場の所有権を保ったまま、球団が指定管理者として運営・管理権を持つという構造が生まれた。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
つまり、「球団の本拠地」という一つの言葉の裏には、所有・運営という別々の権限関係が隠れている。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
この分離を知っていると、第13回で見た「命名権は球団だけの契約ではない」という構造も、より一般的な原理の一例として理解できる。

---

## 2. OWNERSHIP MODEL — three types

複数の解説記事を横断すると、NPBの本拠地球場の運営形態は、概ね次の3つに整理できる。これはNPB公式の分類ではなく、シリーズ上の編集上の整理である。

| Type | Owner | Operator |
|---|---|---|
| Publicly-owned, club-operated | Municipality | Club (as designated manager, etc.) |
| Club-owned | The club itself (or an affiliate) | The club itself |
| Acquired | Originally a separate operating company → club buys in | The club (after becoming a subsidiary) |

それぞれ、具体的な事例で見ていく。

---

## 3. Publicly-owned, club-operated — MAZDA Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima

MAZDA Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima is a stadium the City of Hiroshima owns. The Hiroshima Toyo Carp hold operating and management authority as the designated manager, paying a usage fee to the city while securing rights like advertising and merchandise sales.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
この型の特徴は、**the owner bears the long-term capital burden — construction, major renovations — while the club gets the operating rights**という役割分担にある。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
第13回で見た、マツダによる命名権契約が「マツダ・広島市・カープ」の三者契約になっていたのも、この所有と運営の分離構造が背景にある。

---

## 4. Club-owned — ES CON FIELD HOKKAIDO

In 2023, the Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters opened their new home, ES CON FIELD HOKKAIDO, in Kitahiroshima City.

Their previous home, Sapporo Dome, was owned by the City of Sapporo, and the club couldn't directly manage or operate the facility itself. That's said to be part of why the club set out to build its own new ballpark.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
ES CON FIELD became the club's (or an affiliated company's) own home stadium for the first time in **61 years — since Komazawa Stadium, used back in the Toei Flyers era (1953–1961)**.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
報道では建設費は600億円規模とされる。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Owning the stadium outright means taking on the capital burden yourself, in exchange for a wider range of decisions — how to use the facility on non-game days, advertising, naming rights, renovation investments — that can be made without a municipality's consent standing in the way.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
「本拠地球場を持つ」という同じ言葉でも、公設民営型と自前型では、球団が持てる意思決定の範囲がまったく違う。

---

## 5. Acquired — Yokohama Stadium

横浜スタジアムは、もともと球団とは別の運営会社が所有していた。

In November 2015, DeNA announced a tender offer (TOB) aiming to make Yokohama Stadium's operating company a subsidiary. The TOB closed on January 21, 2016, with DeNA acquiring an additional 4.95 million shares — about 71% of outstanding shares — and consolidating it as a subsidiary.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
球団と球場を一体で運営することで、**speeding up decision-making and strengthening the ability to sell advertising signage were reportedly among the goals**と報じられている。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
これは、公設民営型・自前型のどちらとも違う、**もともと別々だった所有と運営を、あとから一体化させる**という第三の経路である。

---

## 6. WHAT CHANGES WITH OWNERSHIP — the range of decisions shifts

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
3つの型を並べて分かるのは、所有構造の違いが変えるのは、主に**how much a club can decide entirely on its own**だということである。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Under public ownership with club operation, a major renovation or a new revenue initiative tends to require coordinating with the municipal owner. Under club ownership or after an acquisition, the club can decide over a wider range on its own.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
第13回で見た命名権交渉のレイヤーの違いも、この所有構造の延長線上にある。誰が球場を所有しているかが、誰が命名権を持つかを規定している。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
つまり、球場の所有構造は、第11回のCONSTRAINT・第13回の交渉レイヤーの、さらに土台にある変数だと言える。

---

## 7. WHAT STADIUM OWNERSHIP DOES NOT MEAN

### A different ownership model ≠ better or worse management

This isn't about ranking publicly-owned/club-operated, club-owned, and acquired models. Each just trades off capital burden against decision-making range differently.

### A club-owned stadium ≠ a guarantee of profitability

Owning the stadium outright widens a club's decision-making range, but it doesn't automatically guarantee the club turns a profit.

### These three types ≠ an official NPB classification

Publicly-owned/club-operated, club-owned, and acquired are this series' own editorial framing, not a category system NPB or a government sports agency has officially adopted.

---

## 8. TAKEAWAYS

- The 2003 designated-manager system lets stadium ownership (a municipality) and operation (a club) legally split apart.
- NPB's home stadiums broadly sort into three types — publicly-owned/club-operated, club-owned, and acquired (this series' own editorial grouping).
- MAZDA Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima represents the first, ES CON FIELD HOKKAIDO the second, and Yokohama Stadium the third.
- The main thing ownership structure changes is how much a club can decide on its own — renovations, naming rights, advertising.
- The naming-rights negotiation layer seen in Episode 13 sits on top of this same ownership structure.
- Whether one ownership model is "better" isn't the point of this article.
