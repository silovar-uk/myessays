# 『Born This Way』was already being sung before Lady Gaga was born
## From 1975's『I Was Born This Way』to the power—and limits—of saying「生まれつき」

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The first strange thing about Lady Gaga's『Born This Way』is not the song. It is the timeline. Gaga was born in 1986. ところが2021年、本人はこの曲と同名アルバムが、Black gay activist and minister Carl Beanからinspiredされたと明言している。Bean recorded『I Was Born This Way』in 1977.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Go back one more step and even Bean was not the first singer. Bunny Jones and Chris Spierer wrote the song, and Valentino released it in 1975. Bean then recorded a Motown version in 1977, making the openly gay disco statement much more widely known. つまり「Born This Way」は、Gagaが生まれる11年前にはalready part of pop vocabularyだった。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This is not a “Gaga copied an old song” story. The interesting part is how the same phrase sounded at different scales. 1970年代には、自分のsexual orientationをshameやerrorとして扱わないための宣言。2011年には、それがone of the world's biggest pop starsのsingleになった。Same phrase, radically different amplifier.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So the real question becomes: why were these three words so powerful? 調べる前は、分かりやすいself-acceptance sloganに見えた。After tracing the history, it looks stranger and stronger. **It explains the self, but it also sends the demand for explanation back to the person asking.**

## 1. 1975年の「Born This Way」は、最初から「違いを直す必要はない」という歌だった

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
What matters about the 1975『I Was Born This Way』is not only that a Gaga-like title existed early. The song already placed direct self-acceptance at its center, in a period when saying it openly carried far more social risk.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Valentino released his version in 1975, and Carl Bean's version followed in 1977. Bean came from gospel, later became a minister, and worked in Los Angeles with Black LGBTQ+ communities and AIDS support. 『I Was Born This Way』was a disco record from the first half of his life, but it connects clearly to the social work of the second half.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Here,「生まれつき」is not merely a statement about the moment of birth. It works as a rebuttal: “this is not a defect that must be corrected.” If the person did not choose a moral failure, then the logic used to shame them gets weaker. A tiny phrase flips the direction of judgment.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That makes the 1970s phrase closer to armor than to a scientific term. **Before explaining why someone is different, it asks why that difference should be treated as a defect at all.** Decades later, that logic would be transplanted into a much larger pop machine.

## 2. 2011年、ガガはその論理を「世界規模のサビ」にした

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Lady Gaga released『Born This Way』on February 11, 2011, and the album of the same name followed on May 23. ここで1970年代のphraseは、club-memoryからglobal mainstream popの中心へ飛び出す。

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The single debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 and became the chart's 1,000th No. 1 song. Billboard JAPAN reported 448,000 first-week digital sales. Gaga's official site still describes the title track as an anthem for the LGBTQ+ community.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The scale mattered, but so did the wording. The song did not hide sexual minorities inside a generic love song; it explicitly named different sexual orientations. それまでにもLGBTQ+ audiencesに愛された曲は多かったが、this record made inclusion itself part of the text.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The difference between 1977 and 2011 is less the message than the amplifier. **A minority declaration—“you do not need to be ashamed of yourself”—was now playing from the No. 1 position of a global pop chart.** Pop cannot make an argument precise, but it can make a phrase common knowledge.

## 3. 「生まれつき」は、三語で議論の順番を変える

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Move away from the song for a moment and dissect the phrase itself. 差別される側はしばしば「why are you like that?」への説明を求められる。“Born This Way” short-circuits that demand.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
In simplified form, the chain looks like this: “I did not choose it” → “it is not a moral failure” → “blaming me for it makes no sense.” 三語しかないのに、cause、responsibility、dignityを一気につないでしまう。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Logically, the chain is not perfect. Something is not automatically good because it is inborn, and a trait is not undeserving of respect because it developed later. それでもsocial argumentとして強いのは、相手が「本人が好きで選んだ」と責任を押し付ける場面で、非常に短いcounterargumentになるからだ。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So “Born This Way” is less a description of the inside of a person than a way to raise the burden of proof for discrimination. **It flips “prove why you are different” into “prove why your difference gives me the right to harm you.”**

## 4. ただし科学は、「本当に全部、生まれつきです」とは言っていない

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
This is where the clean pop ending has to stop. “Born This Way” can be culturally powerful without being a literal scientific summary of how sexual orientation develops.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The American Psychological Association says there is no scientific consensus on the exact reasons a person develops a particular heterosexual, bisexual, gay, or lesbian orientation. 遺伝、hormonal、developmental、social、cultural influences have all been studied, but no single factor or set of factors has settled the question. At the same time, most people report little or no sense of choice about their orientation.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Those two points are compatible. 「本人が自由に選んだわけではない」and「出生時点ですべて固定されている」are not identical claims. A person's experience may involve little choice even when the causal story is complex.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
So grading『Born This Way』as a science paper would be the wrong contest. **Its power came not from solving causation, but from placing dignity before the completion of causation research.** In that sense, taking「生まれつき」less literally can actually preserve the ethical core of the song.

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[American Psychological Association — Understanding sexual orientation and homosexuality](https://www.apa.org/topics/lgbtq/orientation)

## 5. 「生まれつき」を強くしすぎると、逆にこぼれる人もいる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Now the story twists again. “Born This Way” can defend people against prejudice, but a very rigid idea of sexuality can make experiences that do not fit clean boxes harder to see.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Psychology studies beliefs about sexual orientation as innate, immutable, and categorically distinct under the idea of sexual orientation essentialism. 2021年の研究では、lesbian、gay、bisexual participants differed in beliefs about naturalness and discreteness, and those beliefs had complex relationships with LGBTQ+ identification and belonging.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The point is not to reverse into “Born This Way was wrong.” Essentialist language can help defend people against the demand to change. その一方で、fluidity、bisexuality、境界的なexperienceを見えにくくすることもある。A 2016 study likewise argues that naturalness should be separated from beliefs that groups are homogeneous and sharply discrete.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
A broader principle appears: **human dignity does not begin only after someone proves that an identity is inborn and unchangeable.** 「生まれつきだから認めてほしい」はstrong defenseだった。その先には、“even if the story is complex or fluid, you still deserve respect”という地点がある。

<!-- level:1 role:source -->
[2021 study on essentialism and LGBTQ+ belonging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34518957/) ／ [2016 study on sexual-orientation beliefs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26575349/)

## 6. 包摂のアンセムにも、2011年の言葉の古さは残っている

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Important historical works become harder to see when we freeze them as “important.” 『Born This Way』foregrounded inclusion, but some of the words chosen to perform that inclusion were already criticized at release.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
In the section listing racial and ethnic identities, the song uses terms such as “Orient” and “chola.” 当時の批評でも、Asian and Latino peopleを指す語としてoutdated or inappropriateではないかという疑問が出た。Inclusive intent and good word choice are two different questions.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This does not have to disqualify the work. It shows why inclusion is difficult. 名前を出さなければ存在を消すことがある。Name people, and the categories and labels themselves become contestable. Words that felt bold in 2011 can still need revision fifteen years later.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Anthems are not timeless finished products; they carry the language of their moment. **The lasting value of『Born This Way』is not that every word stayed perfect forever, but that it placed the question “who gets named and included?” at the center of mainstream pop.**

[Christianity Today on contemporary criticism of the wording](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2011/04/lady-gagas-inclusion-problems/) ／ [The National's 2011 review](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/music/lady-gaga-s-born-this-way-speaks-to-her-little-monsters-1.376080)

## 7. 15年後にも残ったのは、「原因」より「許可はいらない」という部分だった

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
In 2026, Lady Gaga is still performing『Born This Way』. A Japan tour report described it as a song dedicated to the queer community, and part of the proceeds from Japan-show tickets was donated to the Born This Way Foundation.

<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Gaga and her mother Cynthia Germanotta founded the organization in 2012 to support young people's well-being and a kinder, braver world. 曲名はsingleやalbumを超えて、an institution's nameにまでなった。

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
At this point, the phrase cannot be reduced to a claim about biological causation. If its main value depended on proving exactly what is inborn, the slogan should weaken whenever science complicates that story. But the durable part is different: 「自分の存在をshameとして処理しなくてよい」「他人のpermissionを得てから自分になる必要はない」。

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Before researching it, I heard『Born This Way』mainly as a giant self-love anthem. That was not wrong. でも今は少し違って聞こえる。**A phrase that began as “I was born this way, so accept me” has expanded toward “I do not owe you a causal report explaining why I am me.”** That is why the three words are still alive.
