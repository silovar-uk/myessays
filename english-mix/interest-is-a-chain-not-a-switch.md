# 「興味を持つ」を分解する
## 人はなぜ、あるものだけを「もっと知りたい」と思うのか

「興味を持つ」sounds simple, but the verb is strange when you look at it closely.

Where is interest located?

Do we “hold” it like a wallet, catch it like a cold, or light it like a fire? しかも「この分野には興味がない」と言った五分後に、同じ分野のtiny detailへ急に食いつくことがある。

I do not care about horse racing. But ask, “Do racehorses know what position they are in during a race?” and suddenly there is a hook. 税制に興味はなくても、「同じ一万円なのに、会社から受け取るのとpointsで受け取るのはなぜ扱いが違うのか」と聞かれると話が変わる。

So interest does not look like an “interesting substance” already stored inside the object.

The word itself has a slightly odd history too. 「興味」は古くから「おもむき」「おもしろみ」の意味で使われていたが、1881年の『哲学字彙』ではEnglish “interest”の訳語として現れる。The modern word therefore carries both sides at once: something can have interest, and a person can take interest in it.

This essay will not treat interest as one mood. 心理学、教育研究、neuroscienceを横断し、**attention stops, a discrepancy appears, something feels understandable, a knowledge gap becomes visible, personal relevance grows, and the person returns voluntarily**という複数のprocessへ分ける。

The short version is this: interest is neither a property of the object nor a fixed trait of the person. **It is better understood as a gradient between person and object that makes further exploration feel worth the next step.**

参照：[コトバンク「興味」](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%88%88%E5%91%B3-53054) ／ [田中瑛津子「興味の発達を支える要因の検討」](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sjpr/60/3/60_203/_pdf)

## 1. 興味は「注意」と似ているが、attentionだけでは足りない

First, separate interest from attention.

スマホが床に落ちればattentionは向く。A fire alarm, a flashing red dot, or a loud notification also captures attention. But we do not become interested in everything that captures our eyes or ears.

Interest research often distinguishes **状況的興味（situational interest）**, a temporary psychological state, from **個人的興味（individual interest）**, a more enduring tendency to re-engage with a topic. 日本のreviewでも、前者はmomentary state、後者はよりpersistentなorientationとして整理されている。

Paul J. Silvia argues that interest is not simply attention or pleasure. Interest has an approach function: it pulls us toward exploration and, over time, can help build knowledge and skill. 「見た」は入口であって、interestらしいmotionは「もっと見たい」「分かりたい」から始まる。

This distinction matters in practice. 強い色、派手なanimation、shock footageはattentionを取れる。でもattention captureとinterest formationは同じではない。If the next step has nothing worth exploring, the eyes stop but the relationship does not continue.

**Attention is often a first condition of interest, but it is not a sufficient condition.**

参照：[Silvia, “Interest—The Curious Emotion”](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/current-directions/j.1467-8721.2008.00548.x/) ／ [Silvia, “What is interesting?”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15755222/)

## 2. 最初の火種は“new”より、「予想と少し違う」に近い

What moves attention toward interest?

Classic curiosity research repeatedly examined novelty, complexity, uncertainty, and conflict. Silvia's appraisal model likewise treats a **novelty-complexity appraisal**—new, unfamiliar, complex—as one important condition for interest.

But “new” is not the same as “interesting.”

知らない言語の三百ページ専門書を突然渡されても、interestよりwithdrawalが先に来ることが多い。On the other hand, even in a familiar football match, one tiny discrepancy—“Why is only this player standing differently on goal kicks?”—can pull attention into a narrow tunnel.

A 2025 scoping review synthesized 35 studies from 2006–2022 and extracted six common sources of situational interest: utility/relevance/meaningfulness, novelty, cognitive activation/complexity, social interaction, hands-on activity, and choice. 珍しさは入口の一つであって、全部ではない。

The interesting pattern is that “slightly wrong inside a known world” can be more compelling than total unfamiliarity. 世界そのものが分からないときより、自分のpredictionが一個だけ外れたとき、we can chase the mismatch.

**Interest often begins not with pure unknown, but with a difference we feel we might explain.**

参照：[Guo & Fryer, “What really elicits learners’ situational interest?”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-024-07176-x) ／ [Silvia, “What Is Interesting?”](https://academic.oup.com/book/34093/chapter-abstract/289306638)

## 3. 人は「分からないもの」より、「分かれそうなもの」に食いつく

Novelty alone fails for a deeper reason.

In Silvia's experiments, interest depended not only on judging something as new or complex, but also on **coping potential**—the sense that “I can probably understand this.” 対象がcomplexでも、「少し考えれば読めそう」と感じられるとinterestは上がりやすい。

That gives interest a brutally practical property.

Too difficult, and the object gets filed under “not for me” before it becomes interesting. Too easy, and exploration ends immediately. 必要なのはunknownの量ではなく、**the sense that the unknown is reachable**.

A 2026 study using trivia questions found that the subjective availability of possible answers—how many candidates came to mind—predicted greater curiosity and later memory. Researchers argue that curiosity depends not only on an information gap but also on **knowability**, the feeling that the missing information can be reached.

A totally mysterious box can feel threatening. でも「たぶん昔のgame consoleっぽい。ただ形が違う」と思えば、you want to open it. Interest may require less “courage toward the unknown” than “one visible bridge into the unknown.”

**“I almost know” can be stronger than “I know nothing.”**

参照：[Silvia, “What is interesting?”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15755222/) ／ [Knowability predicts curiosity and learning](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42365778/)

## 4. 好奇心は“information gap”を見つけると、埋めるまで少し気持ち悪い

Now separate interest from curiosity.

George Loewenstein's 1994 **information-gap theory** frames curiosity as arising when attention focuses on the gap between what one knows and what one wants to know. そのgapがdeprivationのように感じられ、missing informationを取りに行くmotivationになる。

The weird part is that simply “not knowing” may not be enough.

「世界には知らないことが無限にある」と考えても、we do not spend all day in epistemic panic. But a question like “What was the first product advertised on television?” draws a border around one tiny missing piece. 穴が見えた瞬間、ignorance becomes local.

In Min Jeong Kang and colleagues' 2009 study, greater curiosity about trivia questions was associated with willingness to spend limited tokens or waiting time to learn the answer, and higher-curiosity answers were remembered better later. 別研究では、高いconfidenceで答えたのにwrongだと分かったとき、curiosityが大きく上がった。

“I was sure it was that. Wait—what?”

That is a small cognitive accident. だから続きを見に行く。

**Curiosity can depend less on how much is unknown than on how sharply the missing piece is outlined.**

参照：[Loewenstein, “The psychology of curiosity”](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75) ／ [Kang et al., “The Wick in the Candle of Learning”](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2009.02402.x/) ／ [Metcalfe et al., “Curiosity: The effects of feedback and confidence”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36048057/)

## 5. 「気になる」と「好き」は別物で、curiosityとinterestも完全には同じではない

An information gap still does not complete interest.

Some information feels impossible to ignore even when we do not enjoy it: a horror-film ending, a flame war, a medical test result, the reason a message was seen but unanswered. 情報探索はalways “I like this”から始まるわけではない。

Later work by Loewenstein and colleagues distinguishes several motives for information seeking: usefulness for decisions, curiosity for its own sake, the pleasure or displeasure of thinking about a topic, and even avoidance. 「知りたい」「役に立つ」「考えていたい」「むしろ知りたくない」が同じ場面で競合しうる。

A neuroscience review likewise argues that curiosity-related behavior is not one mechanism. Novelty seeking and uncertainty-reducing information seeking interact, but they can involve different computations and neural circuits. 「未知が好き」で全部をまとめるには、the system is too heterogeneous.

Interest research adds a longer time scale. Interest can refer not only to a momentary state but also to a tendency to re-engage with the same domain. 一回answerを知って終わるquiz curiosityと、何度も同じtopicへ戻るinterestはoverlapするが同じではない。

“I clicked because I was curious” is weak evidence of deep interest. 「また調べた」「別の場面でも見つけた」「自分でquestionを作った」まで行くと、interest has changed category.

**If you want to detect deeper interest, re-engagement is more informative than a single click.**

参照：[Golman & Loewenstein, “Curiosity, Information Gaps, and the Utility of Knowledge”](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/golman_loewenstein_curiosity.pdf) ／ [Monosov, “Curiosity: primate neural circuits for novelty and information seeking”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41583-023-00784-9)

## 6. 「自分に関係ある」が入ると、面白さはlonger-livedになりやすい

So far, interest sounds too much like a puzzle.

We can approach a topic without an obvious information gap when it suddenly connects to our job, future, values, or identity. 逆にintellectually interestingでも、自分とのbridgeが作れないと続きにくい。

Educational psychology has accumulated work on **personal relevance** and **utility value**. A 2018 review describes how, in early phases of interest, connections to prior experience or existing preferences can trigger situational interest; as interest develops, the topic may become linked more deeply to value and identity.

This gives the popular phrase 「自分ごと化」a more precise meaning.

Saying “this is relevant to you” does not make it relevant. 「今までのexperience」「future goal」「already-liked domain」「自分はこういう人だというself-understanding」とtopicがconnectしたとき、information gets placed on one's own map.

Interest development is therefore not only accumulation of facts about the object. **It is also accumulation of routes between the object and the self.**

参照：[Priniski et al., “Making Learning Personally Meaningful”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6191053/) ／ [Guo & Fryer, situational interest review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-024-07176-x)

## 7. 興味は「その場の面白い」から“voluntary return”へ発達する

Interest has a time axis.

Suzanne Hidi and K. Ann Renninger describe four phases: **triggered situational interest**, **maintained situational interest**, **emerging individual interest**, and **well-developed individual interest**. 日本語で言えば、外部のきっかけから始まり、維持され、自分から再接触する傾向へ深まっていくmodelである。

What makes the model powerful is that it avoids labeling people as permanently “interested” or “not interested.”

At first, someone sent you a video. 次は自分でsearchした。Unknown terms led you to build a glossary. As knowledge grows, differences that were invisible become visible. そのdifferenceからnew questionsが生まれ、interest generates the conditions for more interest.

A 2022 review describes interest as both a psychological state during engagement and motivation to re-engage. A 2026 update by Hidi and Renninger emphasizes that interest is universal as a psychological/physiological state while its triggers vary with individual differences and sociocultural context.

So the causal arrow runs both ways.

**We do not only research because we are interested; research can make us capable of becoming interested.**

参照：[Hidi & Renninger, “The Four-Phase Model of Interest Development”](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_4) ／ [Renninger & Hidi, “Interest: A unique affective and cognitive motivational variable that develops”](https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.adms.2021.12.004) ／ [Hidi & Renninger, 2026 update](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2025.102865)

## 8. 興味を育てるには、「選べる」「できそう」「誰かとつながる」も効く

Looking only at the object misses the person's environment.

Self-Determination Theory highlights three basic psychological needs relevant to high-quality motivation: **自律性（autonomy）**, **能力感（competence）**, and **関係性（relatedness）**—the sense that I choose, I can act effectively, and I am connected to others.

This does not mean “interest has three ingredients.” 興味研究とself-determination theoryは別系譜で、同一視はできない。

But the three needs are useful when designing conditions for sustained engagement. どれだけtopicが面白くても、everything is forced, difficulty stays too high, and nobody can discuss it, exploration becomes expensive. If entry points are chosen, progress feels visible, and knowledge can be exchanged, reasons to return multiply.

The situational-interest review also identifies choice, social interaction, and cognitively activating challenge among commonly studied sources of interest. Interest does not happen only “inside the head”; it shifts with options, difficulty, people, and tools.

So “this person is not interested” can hide a design problem.

**Before treating low interest as personality, inspect the environment that makes returning costly.**

参照：[Self-Determination Theory](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/the-theory/) ／ [Intrinsic Motivation](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/topics/application-intrinsic-motivation/) ／ [Guo & Fryer, situational interest review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-024-07176-x)

## 9. やりすぎて「interest debugger」を作ると、興味がない理由を切り分けられる

Now compress the research into something usable.

本稿では、interestが続くまでを六つの問いで点検する**興味デバッガ（interest debugger）**として整理する。This is not a validated scale; it is a practical hypothesis model synthesized from the literature above.

**①停止（Stop）――何がattentionを止めたか。** Novelty, surprise, a concrete discrepancy, or a question?  
**②可解（Knowability）――分かれそうか。** Is there one bridge from what I already know?  
**③欠落（Gap）――何を知らないかが見えているか。** Is ignorance localized into a specific missing piece?  
**④価値（Relevance）――経験・目標・役割・好みとつながるか。** Why does this matter to me now?  
**⑤接近（Approach）――costを払ってでも次を取りに行くか。** Search, click, ask, test, compare?  
**⑥再帰（Return）――自分からもう一度戻るか。** Will I re-engage without another external trigger?

Take “I am not interested in accounting.” Maybe the failure is not accounting itself. 「減価償却」をdefinition-firstで渡すと②Knowabilityで落ちるが、「三年前に買ったcamera代を、なぜ今年のprofitからも少し引くのか」とown possessionから入ると、①Stop、②Knowability、④Relevanceが同時に立つことがある。

The reverse is also true. Even if you love football, being handed raw event-data CSV for every match can kill engagement. 個人的興味があっても、entry comprehensibilityとaction costはseparate variablesである。

This decomposition turns “no interest” from a cause into an outcome.

**Do not ask only whether interest exists. Ask where exploration stopped. That is the first point you can actually redesign.**

参照：本節の六段階は本稿独自の統合モデル。理論的背景は[Hidi & Renninger](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_4)、[Loewenstein](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75)、[Silvia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15755222/)、[Guo & Fryer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-024-07176-x)を参照。

## 10. 興味を「作る」とは、面白く言うことではなく、next stepを見えるようにすること

The practical question now changes from “How do I make this interesting?” to “How do I make the next exploratory move possible?”

派手にする、驚かせる、笑わせる。These can help ①Stop. But without ②Knowability, ③Gap, or ④Relevance, interest ends like fireworks.

A communicator cannot inject interest directly. 既知との接点を置く。Show one manageable discrepancy. Make the missing piece visible. Give material that lets the person connect it to their own goals. Make a tiny experiment possible. Leave a foothold for return.

This applies to articles, classes, sales, recruiting, sports fandom, and internal communication. 「全部理解させる」before anything else is often the wrong target. A better intermediate goal is to create a state where one more thing feels worth knowing.

Interest design is not a technique for controlling what another person should care about. 何に関心を持つかは本人の側に残る。We can design visibility, accessibility, comprehensibility, and return paths—not the final preference.

At the beginning I asked: where is interest?

Before researching, it looked like an internal switch. 調べた後では、interest seems to sit neither purely inside the person nor inside the object, but **in the relation that makes one more step of exploration feel worth taking**.

So the Japanese phrase 「興味を持つ」is still slightly strange—and surprisingly accurate. Interest is not completed the moment you “have” it. 手に取り、近づき、almost understandし、また戻る。その往復の途中にだけ、interestは生きている。

参照：[Hidi & Renninger, four-phase model](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_4) ／ [Monosov, curiosity and information seeking](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41583-023-00784-9) ／ [Gruber et al.の研究紹介 “How curiosity enhances learning”](https://www.nature.com/articles/514143b)
