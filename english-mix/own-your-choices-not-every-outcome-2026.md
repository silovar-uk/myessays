# Anything Is Fine?｜自分の意思を引き受ける

“What do you want to eat?” “Anything is fine.” “Ramen?” “I had it yesterday.” この会話には hidden work がある。The person looking for a restaurant must now guess the other's preferences. 最初は、Everyone should take a position. と思った。しかし相手が医師なら、専門的な判断まで自分で下すべきなのか。The real question may be who should decide what.

The question is why expressing a preference means taking responsibility, and when that responsibility becomes excessive. 会話例は hypothetical examples で、研究結果ではない。As the evidence accumulates, the initial ban on “anything is fine” becomes a narrower principle: make the division of decision-making clear.

## 1｜守：希望・判断・依頼・責任を分ける

Responsibility looks both forward and backward. 夕食なら、preference（辛いものが食べたい）、decision（この店にする）、request（予約を頼む）、accountability（期待外れでも相手を不当に責めない）は別々だ。You can delegate the booking without hiding your preferences. Taking responsibility begins with owning your role, not doing every task yourself.

Here, agency means choosing in light of one's values, explaining reasons when needed, and revising a decision when conditions change. これは本稿の working definition であり、単一の心理尺度ではない。Outcomes depend on other people and chance, too. 自分の判断に責任を持つことと、すべての結果を自分のせいにすることは違う。Agency requires boundaries, not total control.

## 2｜守：「なんでもいい」は一つの意味ではない

The same words can communicate different things. Paul Grice の conversational implicature（会話の含意）は、literal meaning だけでなく、context and shared knowledge が解釈に関わることを考える手掛かりになる。[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/implicature/)。“Anything is fine” may express genuine indifference or hide an unstated constraint. The burden appears when the listener must guess which.

We can distinguish five possible meanings. This is a practical classification proposed here, not an established research taxonomy. 注目すべきは surface wording ではなく、the speaker's preferences and intention to delegate である。

- Genuine indifference：どちらでも満足できる。
- Consideration：希望はあるが相手を優先する。
- Unformed preference：まだ判断基準がない。
- Rational delegation：相手の知識や都合を生かす。
- Avoidance of responsibility：選択の失敗を責められたくない。

If both are genuinely fine, “I like both, so let's choose the closer one” is enough. If a preference is unformed, say “I haven't decided; let's compare prices.” Rather than diagnosing someone's personality from one sentence, look at whether they cooperate in the decision afterward. The goal is not to ban ambiguity, but to make it resolvable.

## 3｜破：選びたくないのではなく、後悔したくない

At first, I assumed people avoided decisions mainly to save effort. A 2016 study by Mary Steffel, Elanor F. Williams and Jaclyn Perrmann-Graham pointed to another mechanism: avoiding responsibility and blame when choosing for others. Delegation differed from postponing a decision or flipping a coin; the recipient had to be able to assume responsibility.[Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2016.04.006)。Decision costs include not only effort, but also who will be seen as responsible if things go wrong.

Steffel and Williams examined delegation across eight experiments in 2018. Difficult decisions were delegated regardless of their importance or the surrogate's expertise. Anticipated regret about one's decision, rather than disappointment with the outcome alone, directly contributed to delegation.[Study](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucx080)。“If I didn't choose, I don't have to blame myself” is a plausible interpretation. ただし、日常のすべての「なんでもいい」を説明する証拠ではない。

The person choosing a restaurant may be carrying both search costs and uncertainty. Saying “I'd like ramen. If it's disappointing, we'll try somewhere else next time” does not guarantee good food. It reveals a preference and accepts the possibility of a bad result. Participation means being willing to choose without a guarantee of being right.

## 4｜破：お願いは、する側にもされる側にも難しい

Avoiding blame is not the only reason to hide a wish. Fear of rejection may also matter. In their 2008 research, Francis Flynn and Vanessa Bohns found that help seekers underestimated compliance with direct requests by as much as 50% in the first three studies. They also examined the social cost of saying no.[Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18605856/)。Being reluctant to ask can reflect a mistaken prediction rather than accurate consideration of the other person.

“Then I should ask for everything” is too quick. A request may impose not only effort, but also the awkwardness of refusing. In 2024, Rachel Schlund, Roseanna Sommers and Bohns found that giving people words they could use to decline made them feel freer to refuse than simply telling them they had the right to do so. The overall standardized effect was 0.27; effects on actual compliance remained inconclusive.[Study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-50532-3)。A clear request and an easy refusal are two distinct design goals.

For example: “Could you check the figures by 3 p.m.? If you can't, just say ‘not today,’ and I'll ask someone else.” 代替案を準備するのは依頼者の仕事だ。This wording has not been experimentally tested here. The lesson is to make a request in a way that leaves the other person a genuine choice.

## 5｜破：自分の立場を取れば取るほど良い、という予想も外れる

Does taking a stronger position always improve work? In 2007, Daniel Ames and Francis Flynn reported across three studies that leaders perceived as too assertive or not assertive enough tended to be rated less effective. Too little assertiveness can hinder goal achievement; too much can harm relationships.[Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17279851/)。This concerns perceptions of leadership, not a universal optimum for every relationship. Having a position is not the same as stating it at maximum volume.

In a meeting: “I recommend plan A because preparation time is short. If external approval is required, I'll switch to B.” Saying “either” to avoid objections and insisting on A without hearing objections can both obstruct joint reasoning. A position becomes useful when others can inspect the grounds and the conditions for revision. Aim for a revisable judgment, not maximum certainty.

## 6｜離：判断を委ねることと、判断から逃げること

An emphasis on responsibility can make all delegation look suspicious. But asking a doctor for advice about diagnostic tests is a rational use of expertise. 自分で機器を操作する必要はないが、症状や制約、希望は伝える。A 2019 economics paper studies delegated expertise and the allocation of decision authority.[Paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20161109)。It is not medical evidence, but helps frame situations where information and authority are held by different people. Agency can coexist with delegation if its purpose and scope are clear.

The same issue arises at work. A manager says “Do whatever you like,” then reveals budget and deadline constraints after delivery. The employee has been given an invisible grading rubric rather than freedom. If the manager shares the goal, budget and decision owner, then delegates the method, autonomy becomes possible. Our practical hypothesis is that the quality of delegation depends on shared decision conditions, not on the number of options.

## 7｜離：人工知能にも、何を考えてほしいのかを頼む

With AI, clarity of purpose can be confused with specifying every step. “Make a good plan” lacks a target and evaluation criteria. “Prove my plan is right” predetermines the result. In a 2024 conference paper, Mrinank Sharma and colleagues reported sycophancy across four free-form tasks in five AI assistants.[Paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0105f7972202c1d4fb817da9f21a9663-Abstract-Conference.html)。This is not an assessment of every current model. It shows why agreement and correctness must be distinguished.

Try: “I provisionally favor A. The goal is to save time without losing quality. Compare B using the same criteria, challenge my assumptions, and recommend one option with reasons and uncertainties.” The human supplies priorities; AI handles comparison and counterarguments; the final recommendation remains open to review. This prompt is not proven to eliminate sycophancy. Delegate reasoning tasks without abandoning ownership of what you adopt.

Asking AI to take a position is not the same as making it accountable. A clear recommendation does not determine who approves business decisions or external commitments. The entity generating text and the person authorizing action are not interchangeable. AI use is a question of how to distribute reasoning tasks and decision authority.

## 8｜再調査：日常で確かめられる三つの仮説

The literature does not establish that ambiguity is always bad or clear requests always succeed. The next step is to test narrower hypotheses about decision burden and freedom to refuse. 以下は未実施の observation plan であり、因果関係を証明する実験ではない。

- Dinner decisions: review about ten occasions, recording conversational turns and satisfaction when preferences or constraints were stated versus left vague. Consider time and other people's preferences.
- Work requests: specify the deadline, deliverable and fallback; observe clarification turns and the recipient's burden. Do not pressure colleagues to participate.
- AI prompts: compare vague goals, explicit evaluation criteria and requests for counterevidence on the same task. Evaluate accuracy, rework, evidence and useful objections rather than personal agreement.

If stating preferences slows a decision, it may reveal a genuine conflict rather than indicate failure. A faster conversation is not an improvement if one person silently gives in. Decision speed and satisfaction should be measured separately. A good conversation is not merely quick; it can accommodate both people's wishes.

## 9｜結論：自分の意思を引き受ける、相手の意思は奪わない

At the beginning, I thought the problem was the phrase “anything is fine.” But genuine indifference, reliance on expertise and fear of choosing are different situations. Expressing a wish can also pressure someone else. The principle is not to ban a phrase, but to clarify the boundary between one's own decisions and those of others.

- State your preference: “I prefer A because…”
- If genuinely indifferent, say so: “I like both; let's pick the closer one.”
- Identify missing information: “I can decide once I know the budget.”
- Delegate with a scope: “Choose the restaurant within budget; I'll book it.”
- Make refusal possible: “If it's difficult, I'll find another way.”
- State what would change your mind: “If this assumption is wrong, I'll reconsider.”
- Own your choices without blaming yourself for everything outside your control.

Next time someone asks about dinner, there is no need for a lecture on decision theory. “I'd like curry today. What would you like?” is enough. 自分の希望を隠さず、相手の希望を上書きしない。It is a small example of shared decision-making. Take ownership of your own choices, and leave others free to make theirs.
