# The World’s Most Famous “Kiss” Is Not Actually a Kiss

## 金色の絵の人を、一度ばらして組み直す

When you hear Gustav Klimt, 《接吻》 probably appears first.

Gold. A man and a woman. An embrace. 美術館のポスター、magnets、mugs。It is almost aggressively “masterpiece-like.”

ところがBelvedereの解説を読むと、いきなり妙なことが書いてある。

**Klimt deliberately avoids showing the actual kiss itself.**

男は女の顔へ口を寄せている。Her eyes are closed. 題名は《接吻》。Yet their lips never meet.

The event named by the title has not happened yet.

<figure>
  <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c8/Klimt_-_The_Kiss.jpg" alt="グスタフ・クリムト《接吻》1908/09年。金色の装飾に包まれた男女が抱き合う" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>グスタフ・クリムト《接吻（恋人たち）》1908/09年。ベルヴェデーレ所蔵。画像: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Klimt_-_The_Kiss.jpg">Wikimedia Commons</a>（作品はパブリックドメイン）。</figcaption>
</figure>

This is not just a tiny joke about the title.

調べていくと、Klimt keeps creating similar collisions: dimensional skin and flat pattern, living flesh and signs of death, an individual portrait and a golden icon, a lake landscape and something close to abstraction.

Maybe he was less interested in choosing one side than in **holding the boundary where two states collide**.

「クリムトとは？」を、chronologyだけではなく、その境界の作り方から考えてみる。

## 1. まず結論――クリムトは「最初から反逆者」ではない

Gustav Klimt was born near Vienna in 1862 and died in Vienna in 1918. ウィーン世紀末を代表する画家であり、1897年に結成されたVienna Secessionの共同創設者、初代会長でもある。

But he did not begin his career by picking a fight with the art establishment.

1876年からVienna’s School of Applied Artsで学び、弟ErnstやFranz Matschと装飾画の仕事を請け負った。He started with a fairly orthodox career decorating theaters and public buildings.

つまり、first he succeeded as someone who could decorate walls very well. Later, he moved away from the rules governing what those walls were supposed to show.

<figure>
  <img src="https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/klimt/klimt-career-shift.svg" alt="クリムトのキャリアを、公共装飾、分離派と大学天井画、黄金期、晩年の風景へつなぐ年表図" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>クリムトの大きな転回。前半の公共装飾で得た技術が消えたのではなく、分離派以後、装飾そのものが絵の主役へ近づいていく。</figcaption>
</figure>

That background matters when looking at later Klimt.

彼の絵は、the border between painting and craft is strangely thin. 金箔、模様、衣服、画面全体のdecorative designが、人物描写と同じくらい重要になる。

It is easier to understand this not as a sudden discovery of decoration, but as **a professional decorator gradually turning decoration from background into an argument**.

## 2. 《接吻》を3層に分解すると、身体より服のほうが巨大になる

Now return to 《接吻》.

絵の中の肌は、顔や手、足の輪郭、頬の赤みまで比較的naturalisticに描かれている。Then, around the body, the laws of physics suddenly change.

男の衣服にはangular rectangles。女の衣服にはcircles and flower-like patterns。二人の胴体はa single golden massへ溶け、where the man ends and the woman begins becomes difficult to see.

Belvedere describes a sharp contrast between naturalistically rendered skin and two-dimensional ornament in Klimt’s golden works. MoMA makes a similar point about 《Hope, II》: the robe is flat, while the flesh is rounded and dimensional.

So, let us do an unnecessary decomposition.

<figure>
  <img src="https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/klimt/klimt-kiss-layers.svg" alt="クリムトの接吻を肌、装飾、金の場という三つの層に分け、実際のキスは描かれていないことを示す概念図" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>《接吻》を「肌」「装飾」「金の場」に分ける概念図。実作品の複製ではなく、画面の役割を整理したもの。</figcaption>
</figure>

顔、手、足――imagine removing only the exposed skin.

What remains is largely a huge vertical block of gold and pattern.

逆にgold and ornamentを消し、肌だけ残す。The embrace is still legible, but that specifically Klimt-like feeling――二人が一つの形へ融合している感じ――becomes much weaker.

Here ornament is not decoration behind the figures. **It is a device that dissolves the boundary between them.**

And yet the lips never fully connect.

身体の輪郭は溶かすのに、the kiss itself remains unfinished.

Perhaps 《接吻》 holds our attention because it freezes not the completed act of love, but the time just before completion.

## 3. 金は「豪華にする材料」より、奥行きを止める材料だった

So why gold?

Gold looks expensive. そこまでは分かる。

But Belvedere’s explanation is more interesting. 西洋絵画では長く、real goldを貼るより、paintでgoldらしく見せるillusionistic skillが評価されてきた。Around 1900, Klimt reversed that logic and brought actual gold leaf back onto the painted surface.

The important point is that gold is not very good at making believable pictorial depth.

金箔は、遠くの山の色にも、室内の影の色にもならない。It reflects according to light and viewing angle, so part of the image behaves less like “space inside the picture” and more like physical material in front of you.

Belvedere explains that Klimt became less interested in convincing spatial illusion and more interested in the painting itself as a precious, decorative object.

つまり金は、not merely added luxury. **It pushes an eye that wants to travel into depth back toward the surface.**

<figure>
  <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/18/Gustav_Klimt%2C_1907%2C_Adele_Bloch-Bauer_I%2C_Neue_Galerie_New_York.jpg" alt="グスタフ・クリムト《アデーレ・ブロッホ＝バウアーの肖像I》1907年。人物が金と幾何学模様に包まれている" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>《アデーレ・ブロッホ＝バウアーの肖像I》1907年。油彩、金、銀。画像: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gustav_Klimt,_1907,_Adele_Bloch-Bauer_I,_Neue_Galerie_New_York.jpg">Wikimedia Commons</a>（作品はパブリックドメイン）。</figcaption>
</figure>

In 《Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I》, the face and hands come forward as human flesh, while the dress, chair, and background sink into a continuous field of gold and geometry.

肖像画なのに、instead of “a woman located in a room,” Adele approaches “a face emerging from a golden field.”

Klimt visited Ravenna in 1903 and was strongly affected by Byzantine mosaics. 日本美術の影響も研究されており、Belvedere notes that he demonstrably valued Japanese art and repeatedly drew from it.

Rather than treating these influences as quotations, it is useful to see how Klimt re-edited them toward one direction: **making surface stronger than depth**.

## 4. 1902年、展示会を丸ごと「ベートーヴェン体験」にした

If you look at Klimt only painting by painting, you miss another important point.

1897年、彼は保守的な美術制度から距離を取り、新しい芸術の場を作るVienna Secessionの創設に加わった。

The XIV Secession Exhibition of 1902 pushed that idea unusually far.

テーマはBeethoven。Josef Hoffmannのもと21人の芸術家が参加し、architecture, murals, sculptureを一つの空間へ統合した。中央にはMax KlingerのBeethoven像。Klimt created the 《Beethoven Frieze》 across three walls.

It was less “an exhibition” and more **a giant Beethoven immersive event in 1902**.

<figure>
  <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6b/Klimt_-_Beethovenfries.jpg" alt="グスタフ・クリムト《ベートーヴェン・フリーズ》1902年。横長の画面に人物と装飾が連なる" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>《ベートーヴェン・フリーズ》1902年。画像: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Klimt_-_Beethovenfries.jpg">Wikimedia Commons</a>（作品はパブリックドメイン）。</figcaption>
</figure>

Secession records say nearly 60,000 people visited.

しかもthe frieze was not originally meant to be permanent. It was supposed to be removed after the exhibition, but the next year’s Klimt retrospective kept it in place long enough for it to survive.

Again, for Klimt, “the work” did not stop at the frame.

壁の位置、鑑賞者の移動、隣の彫刻、音楽というthemeまで含めて、一つのexperienceを作ろうとしている。

Instead of seeing the gold in 《接吻》 as “lots of decoration,” it makes more sense to remember that **a decorator trained to design whole spaces eventually built a small environment inside a canvas**.

## 5. 大学の天井画では、ついに「こんなの学問じゃない」と拒まれた

Around the same period, Klimt was making three monumental ceiling paintings for the Great Hall of the University of Vienna: 《Philosophy》《Medicine》《Jurisprudence》.

これはprestigious public commissionだった。

But what the university expected――a dignified allegory celebrating the triumph of knowledge――and what Klimt delivered were very different.

Idealized bodiesではなく、naked bodies, illness, death, anxiety。Rather than showing knowledge neatly mastering the world, he brought into the hall what humans cannot fully control.

The works met intense rejection, and Klimt eventually withdrew from the commission.

<figure>
  <img src="https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/klimt/klimt-faculty-paintings-fate.svg" alt="クリムトの大学天井画三作品が制作、拒絶、私有化、1945年焼失へ至った経緯を示す図" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>大学天井画三作品の運命。現存するのは主に白黒写真で、現在見られるカラー再構成は研究とAIによる近似であり、原作の色を確定したものではない。</figcaption>
</figure>

This part should not be treated as a quirky art-world anecdote.

三作品はその後、個人所有となり、第二次世界大戦末期の1945年、オーストリアのSchloss Immendorfで火災により失われた。Belvedere states that the fire was started by withdrawing German troops.

Today, mostly black-and-white photographs survive. 《Medicine》 has one known color detail. Belvedere and Google Arts & Culture used art-historical research and machine learning to estimate the colors, but the museum explicitly says the result is an approximation, not an authentic reconstruction.

つまり、AIで「元通り」にした話ではない。

What this episode reveals is something else.

クリムトは、国家や大学が求める「正しい象徴」を上手に描ける技術を持っていた。Yet in the end, he stopped adjusting himself to that expectation.

初期の優等生的な公共装飾から、an artist arguing over what may be shown in public spaceへ。

That change is larger than the gold leaf.

## 6. 黄金の肖像画は、20世紀の略奪史まで背負っている

《Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I》 cannot be left as a story about the beauty of Klimt’s Golden Period.

After Nazi Germany annexed Austria in 1938, five Klimt paintings belonging to the Bloch-Bauer family were seized.

戦後も長くオーストリアに残ったが、Adeleの姪Maria Altmannらが返還を求め、長い法的闘争の末、2006年に5点が相続人へ返還された。《Adele Bloch-Bauer I》 was later acquired by Neue Galerie New York.

The golden painting was not “golden” in the same way through every period of its history.

ある時代には近代ウィーンの富と文化の肖像であり、その後はlooted propertyになり、戦後にはstate collectionの有名作品となり、さらにrestitutionをめぐる記憶の焦点になった。

The meaning of a painting is not fixed only on the surface of its canvas.

ここでは鑑賞者の側も、decorative beauty and the history the object has passed throughを同時に見る必要がある。

[Neue Galerie「Gustav Klimt: Five Paintings From the Collection of Ferdinand and Adele Bloch-Bauer」](https://www.neuegalerie.org/exhibitions/klimt-paintings-bauer-collection)

## 7. そして金を全部消すと、むしろクリムトがよく見える

So far, we have talked about gold.

では、金を使っていない作品では、does Klimt disappear?

Look at 《Attersee》 from 1900, and the answer is fairly clearly “no.”

<figure>
  <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/86/Klimt_-_Am_Attersee%2C_1900%2C_Inv._4148.jpg" alt="グスタフ・クリムト《アッター湖》1900年。正方形の画面のほとんどを水面の細かな色面が占める" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>《Attersee》1900年。画面のほとんどが水面で、地平線はほぼ消えている。画像: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Klimt_-_Am_Attersee,_1900,_Inv._4148.jpg">Wikimedia Commons</a>（作品はパブリックドメイン）。</figcaption>
</figure>

Leopold Museum describes the work as reaching “the limits of abstraction” in a way highly unusual for its time.

正方形の画面のほとんどがwater。大きな空も、手前の岸もない。Small turquoise strokes spread like a textile pattern.

Then the research gets unexpectedly concrete.

Klimt’s estate contains **a 9.5×5.6cm piece of cardboard with a square hole cut into it――a viewfinder**.

彼は望遠鏡とこの穴を使い、landscape motifsを探したという。In a 1903 letter, he even describes searching with this homemade viewing hole.

It is a smartphone crop tool made of cardboard.

But the tiny object is surprisingly revealing.

Square crop. Reduced depth. Water and trees seen as pattern. 密度を画面いっぱいに作る。

Even without gold, the same operations found in 《接吻》 and the Adele portrait remain.

ここでやっと、「Klimt = gold」という覚え方が逆転する。

**Gold did not create Klimt. Gold simply joined a Klimt method already obsessed with making the surface powerful.**

## 8. では、クリムトとは何者だったのか

If we force everything so far into a short answer:

- 公共建築の装飾で鍛えた技術を、eventually used to break the border between painting and ornament
- 立体的な肌とflat patternを同じ画面で衝突させた人
- 一枚の絵だけでなく、the whole exhibition spaceを一つの作品として考えた人
- 金がなくても、landscapeをcropし、flattenし、patternへ変換した人
- そして、love and non-contact、生と死、portrait and icon、現実と装飾の「あいだ」を繰り返し描いた人

<figure>
  <img src="https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/klimt/klimt-quote.svg" alt="クリムトの、自分を知りたいなら作品を注意深く見てほしいという言葉を示す引用カード" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>クリムト自身も、自分について知りたければ作品を注意深く見て、そこから自分が何者で何を望むか読み取ってほしい、と述べている。出典: <a href="https://www.belvedere.at/en/gustav-klimt-vienna-and-japan-1900-0">Belvedere</a></figcaption>
</figure>

At first, “《接吻》なのにキスしていない” looked like a funny contradiction.

調べた後では、むしろそこがthe most Klimt-like partに見える。

If the lips fully met, the boundary between “two people” would be resolved into one completed event.

クリムトはその直前で止める。The skin stays vividly human, while everything around it dissolves into gold and pattern.

**Maybe what he painted was not gold itself, but the edge just before one thing becomes another.**

だから《接吻》は、without showing the kiss, still remains 《接吻》.

むしろ、because it has not happened yet, it never quite ends.
