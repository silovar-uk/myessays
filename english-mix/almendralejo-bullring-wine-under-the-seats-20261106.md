# 闘牛場の客席の下にワイン75万リットル――アルメンドラレホの奇妙で合理的な経済史

*2026年11月6日｜English Mix：日本語記事と同一の節・段落構造。日本語を理解の足場に英語を混ぜる。*

Under the seats of a bullring, there were tanks with capacity for **750,000 litres of wine**. Above them: space for **6,000 spectators**. People watching bulls upstairs, wine resting downstairs. Why would anyone design—or reuse—a building like that?

It sounds like a strange architecture story. But the deeper I went into official sources, the stranger my own assumptions became. Why did it make economic sense? Why preserve it? And why does a Supreme Court decision appear in the history of a sparkling wine label? Let's follow the clues.

## 0｜要旨――闘牛場の床下から都市の経済史が見える

Almendralejo is a small city in southwestern Spain, in Badajoz province, Extremadura. Its economy is closely linked to grapes and olives. But the surprise is hidden under the seats: beneath a bullring opened in 1843 sits a wine cellar. A winemaker acquired the arena in 1942, and the municipality reports storage capacity of 750,000 litres. **This is not just a quirky building.** [Municipal tourism](https://visita.almendralejo.es/ver_sitio.php?id_sitio=17) / [2014 heritage decree](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2014-10700)

この記事は一つの建物から、geography（地理）、industrial transformation（産業転換）、Cavaの産地認定、heritage preservation（文化財保存）、そして闘牛への批判を追う。The working hypothesis: a city's strength is not just how much it produces, but how much value and memory it can retain. 事実と筆者の分析は明確に分ける。

## 1｜観客席の下に、ワイン75万リットル。そんな改装があるのか

A bullring and a wine cellar seem to belong to different worlds. One is about crowds and heat; the other needs cool, stable conditions. Yet in Almendralejo, one sits literally above the other. The unexpected pairing makes strange economic sense.

![Wine cellar under the bullring seats in Almendralejo](https://geoportal.redex.org/storage/media/cc42acc8ce334185e0193753adb6cb77/6f5fd75912057831e3a5313698c12ba6fd7d3368.jpg)

Photo credit: the rural tourism network REDEX. The image is embedded from its [tourism listing](https://extremadurarural.es/recurso/460/); the source link is available if it fails to load.

In 1942, local winemaker **アルフォンソ・イグレシアス（Alfonso Iglesias）** bought the arena and used the cool space below the stands for wine storage. Reported tank capacity: **750,000 litres**. That equals one million standard 750 ml bottles *as a volume comparison only*. It does **not** tell us actual production, sales, or today's inventory. [City source](https://visita.almendralejo.es/ver_sitio.php?id_sitio=17)

That distinction changes the story. A building's most visible function is not necessarily its only economic function. **Unused space can become productive infrastructure.** The city's history starts to look like a lesson in asset reuse.

## 2｜まず地図から。『泥の土地』に、なぜワインの街ができたのか

To locate Almendralejo, forget the usual Madrid–Barcelona map for a moment. It lies in **エストレマドゥーラ（Extremadura）**, in Badajoz province, relatively close to Portugal. Its official municipal population was **34,587 on 1 January 2025**. An autonomous community（自治州）has broad regional powers; the province and municipality are different administrative levels. [City](https://www.almendralejo.es/contenido.php?id_area=31) / [2025 population](https://relojpublico.es/poblacion/municipios/badajoz/almendralejo/) / [INE statistics](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/INEbase/operacion.htm?c=Estadistica_C&cid=1254736177011&idp=1254734710990&menu=resultados)

The comarca（地域的なまとまり）is **ティエラ・デ・バロス（Tierra de Barros）**, a name associated with clay-rich soils. Climate matters too: the Cava regulatory body describes vineyards at roughly 200–450 metres, with dry conditions and hot summers. [Cava region profile](https://www.cava.wine/en/origin-cava/4-zones/vi%C3%B1edos-de-almendralejo/)

**Terroir（テロワール）** means the combination of local conditions that helps shape agricultural products. But saying “the land made the industry” is too neat. Nature provides conditions; people build processing capacity, distribution channels, institutions, and brands.

## 3｜街の名前はアーモンド、主役はブドウ。歴史は最初からずれている

The name **Almendralejo** comes from land once associated with almond trees. An older name was **アルメンドラル・デ・メリダ（Almendral de Mérida）**. Yet today's economic symbols are grapes and olives. The fruit in the name and the fruit in the economy are different. [Provincial history](https://www.dip-badajoz.es/municipios/municipio_dinamico/historia/index_historia.php?codigo=010)

Its settlement history reaches into the Middle Ages. After the processes commonly called **レコンキスタ（Reconquista）**, the Order of Santiago helped repopulate the region. The precise founding date remains uncertain; the earliest known document mentioning the settlement dates from **1327**. Ancient remains do not establish the founding date of today's town. [Historical source](https://www.dip-badajoz.es/municipios/municipio_dinamico/historia/index_historia.php?codigo=010)

In **1536**, the town obtained a distinct municipal status after disputes with Mérida; later jurisdiction changed again. Further independence was secured in **1665**, and the title of city came in **1851**. These historical rights were not identical to modern municipal autonomy. The recurring question was: who governs, taxes, and decides? [Provincial history](https://www.dip-badajoz.es/municipios/municipio_dinamico/historia/index_historia.php?codigo=010)

Municipal planning records describe an earlier grain economy, with wheat, barley, and grain-storage structures. In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, vineyards expanded. **A place name remembers one landscape while the market creates another.** [Urban planning document](https://agendaurbana.almendralejo.es/contenido.php?id_area=2&id_bloque=4)

## 4｜1843年、1912年、1942年、2014年。建物を四回読む

**1843:** the bullring opened. **1912:** it was remodeled into much of its present appearance. **1942:** a winemaker repurposed space below the seats. **2014:** it received protected cultural heritage status. These are four dates for one property, but also four changes in what the property meant. [Official heritage decree](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2014-10700) / [City tourism](https://visita.almendralejo.es/ver_sitio.php?id_sitio=17)

The official decree gives a capacity of around **6,000 spectators** and a **51-metre** arena diameter. Its **ネオ・ムデハル様式（Neo-Mudéjar architecture）** draws on forms associated with Iberia's Islamic artistic heritage. Arches and vaults support the seats and create the space below. Architecture created an option that later users could exploit. [2014 decree](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2014-10700)

**Adaptive reuse（用途転換）** means putting an existing structure to a new use. It is not the same as demolishing and rebuilding. Here, the useful asset was an existing cool space. But our evidence does not prove today's operating volume. Historical tank capacity is not current production.

Calling it only a 'bullring' hides the wine business. Calling it only a 'tourist landmark' hides the practical cellar. A physical **asset（資産）** can create different kinds of value across time.

## 5｜75万リットルの計算は簡単だ。利益の計算はできない

Let's overdo the comparison a little. **750,000 litres** is equivalent to **one million 750 ml bottles**. Dividing by the arena's **6,000 seats** gives **125 litres of tank capacity per seat**. No, that is not a drinking recommendation. It is a thought experiment about two different capacities in one building. [Seats](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2014-10700) / [Wine tanks](https://visita.almendralejo.es/ver_sitio.php?id_sitio=17)

Now the important correction: capacity is **not sales**. **Utilization（稼働率）** is the fraction of available capacity actually used. **Sales volume（販売量）** counts products sold. **Profit（利益）** is revenue minus costs. A million-bottle equivalent is not evidence that a million bottles were produced or sold.

Winemaking also needs materials, labour, fermentation, storage, bottling, quality control, and working capital（運転資金）. Discovering a strange space creates a **business hypothesis**, not proof of a profitable business.

## 6｜『カバ』と名乗るには、泡だけでなく住所と法律が要る

Not every sparkling wine is **カバ（Cava）**. Cava must satisfy origin, production, and quality rules. Its **traditional method（伝統方式）** creates bubbles through a second fermentation inside the bottle, with a minimum of **nine months** of bottle ageing for the basic category. [Regulator's method guide](https://www.cava.wine/es/origen/el-metodo-tradicional-o-champenoise/)

Cava is often associated with Catalonia. How can a distant town in Extremadura make it? Because the law allows it within approved areas and under its rules. A Spanish legal order in **September 1995**, following a **March 1994 Supreme Court decision**, added Almendralejo to the authorized Cava region. [1995 official gazette](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-1995-21308)

Under the zoning framework approved in 2020 and officially recognized in 2021, **アルメンドラレホのブドウ畑（Viñedos de Almendralejo）** became one of four named Cava zones, alongside the Barcelona area, the Ebro valley, and Requena. The regions are not one contiguous landscape. [Official Cava zoning](https://www.cava.wine/es/origen/zonas/)

**Designation of Origin（原産地呼称）** is a system of protected geographic naming and production rules. Terroir matters, but institutions matter too. A question that sounds like 'Where is this wine from?' can lead straight to legislation and court decisions.

## 7｜ブドウ農家の街ではない。加工と販売を抱えた産業の街である

Growing grapes is not the same as selling wine. Farming, fermentation, storage, testing, bottling, wholesale, restaurants, and tourism form a **value chain（価値連鎖）**. If we count only agricultural output, we miss the businesses that turn crops into products and experiences.

Municipal records describe a shift from grain to vineyards during the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The **2014 heritage decree** reads the bullring cellar as evidence of a later transformation: from small or family wine production toward much larger-scale operations after the mid-twentieth century. A strange building becomes an industrial history document. [City planning](https://agendaurbana.almendralejo.es/contenido.php?id_area=2&id_bloque=4) / [Heritage decree](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2014-10700)

An **industrial cluster（産業集積）** brings related producers, skills, services, and institutions into one region. But proximity is not automatically profitability. Shared logistics, quality control, distribution, learning, and trust must work in practice.

Tourism can add another layer of value: people may pay not just for wine, but also to understand how it was made. Yet regional revenue gains and profit distribution still require evidence. Value creation and value capture are different questions.

## 8｜突然現れる労働者の互助組織。産業史には人間の生活がある

After all those litres and storage tanks, where are the people? Another building offers a clue. The workers' association **エル・オブレロ・エストレメーニョ（El Obrero Extremeño）** was founded in **1895** by local artisans. Members contributed funds to help with medical costs after illness or accidents. [Municipal tourism page](https://visita.almendralejo.es/ver_sitio.php?id_sitio=18)

This is **mutual aid（相互扶助）**: people share risks through contributions and support. It resembles some functions of modern insurance, but it is not identical to today's welfare systems.

An industrial town also needs a social infrastructure. Buildings can preserve memories of labour, illness, leisure, and collective solutions. Productivity without a view of human life is only half a history.

## 9｜文化の保管庫は、観光施設になる。だが『保存』は簡単な正義ではない

Next to the bullring stands a **wine science museum**, housed in a former alcohol factory. Original tanks have become part of the exhibition. A piece of industrial equipment now helps people learn industrial history. [Museum's official page](https://www.museovinoalmendralejo.com/el_museo.php)

Almendralejo also calls itself a city of **ロマン主義（Romanticism）**, linked to writer **ホセ・デ・エスプロンセダ（José de Espronceda）** and poet **カロリーナ・コロナド（Carolina Coronado）**. Here Romanticism is a nineteenth-century cultural movement, not simply romance. The city also celebrates **ラス・カンデラス祭**, a February festival with a ritual burning of figures representing evil spirits. [City](https://www.almendralejo.es/contenido.php?id_area=31) / [Spanish tourism office](https://www.spain.info/es/destino/almendralejo/)

But the city's own urban planning materials acknowledge the destruction of important industrial buildings, including wineries and alcohol-production facilities, during urban development. Saving the bullring and museum does not mean that all industrial heritage survived. [Urban planning](https://agendaurbana.almendralejo.es/contenido.php?id_area=2&id_bloque=4)

**Adaptive conservation（活用と両立する保存）** is not simply freezing every old structure. It requires choices about cost, ownership, safety, residents' needs, and historical value. Preservation and redevelopment are both decisions with trade-offs.

## 10｜批判を止めない。闘牛、農業依存、観光の三つの難問

First, finding the bullring fascinating is not the same as endorsing bullfighting. Spain's **2013 law** gives tauromaquia cultural protection. Meanwhile, animal welfare and children's rights have been raised as criticisms; in **2024**, Spain's Ministry of Equality objected to using certain public funds for bullfighting-related activities. **The building's heritage value, the ethics of bullfighting, and public spending are separate issues.** [Law](https://boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2013-11837) / [Ministry statement](https://www.igualdad.gob.es/comunicacion/sala-de-prensa/el-ministerio-de-igualdad-solicita-retirar-sus-logos-de-las-actividades-taurinas-organizadas-en-andalucia/)

Second, specialization in grapes and olives brings exposure to weather and price changes. But we cannot calculate Almendralejo's exact dependence or financial losses from the evidence used here. **Concentration risk（産業集中リスク）** means that weakness in one activity may affect an entire local economy. Specialization can also create advantages.

Third, a successful local brand does not guarantee that profits remain locally. Farmers, wineries, distributors, hotels, retailers, tourists, and the public sector face different incentives. **Local economic circulation（地域内経済循環）** asks how much income stays in the region through jobs, purchases, spending, and investment.

That is why 'just turn heritage into tourism' is not a complete strategy. We must ask who gains, who pays, and which measurable benefits reach residents.

## 11｜勝手にコンサルを始める。闘牛場を三つの事業として評価してみる

Let's run a **thought experiment**, not present an actual business plan. Imagine three ways to evaluate the same bullring: **① an event venue**, **② a heritage and wine-tourism site**, **③ a showcase for local producers**. These are analytical options, not claims about current revenues.

For the venue, useful metrics include event days, attendance, spending per visitor, security, and setup costs. For tourism, we would track visits, guide costs, and local spillovers. For a producer showcase, we would examine business participation and **conversion rate（購入への転換率）**. A **KPI（重要業績評価指標）** is a measure of progress toward an objective. Different objectives require different metrics.

Next: who funds renovation, what rules protect the property, and how are different views on animal welfare considered? **Stakeholder analysis（利害関係者分析）** identifies the owners, local firms, public authorities, residents, and visitors affected by each choice.

The result is not 'three income streams guarantee growth'. It is more modest and useful: **the definition of a business determines what success means.** A heritage asset may need measures of public value as well as revenue.

## 12｜戦略を組み立てるなら、まず何を測るべきか

For a regional growth strategy, start not with 'How many bottles?' but with 'Where is value created and who retains it?' **Value added（付加価値）** is the new value created after subtracting purchased inputs from output. High production volumes do not prove that profits remain within the town.

Four measurement groups matter. **① Industry:** producer numbers, output, jobs, destinations. **② Earnings:** value added, gross margin, investment, local procurement. **③ City:** vacant historic buildings, conservation costs, pedestrian movement, resident use. **④ Tourism:** visits, time spent, local spending, repeat visits. We do not have a fully verified recent dataset for all these items in this article.

**Resource-Based View（資源基盤観）** asks whether distinctive resources support sustained advantage. Vineyard land, architecture, skills, certified origin, and storytellers are potential resources. But unusual does not automatically mean commercially valuable. Demand, imitation difficulty, and execution matter.

In a field study, I would ask farmers, wineries, shops, museums, and officials a practical question: **Who supplies you, who buys from you, and where do time and money disappear?** Mapping real transactions can reveal more than a slogan about regional growth.

## 13｜調査→追加計画→再調査。どこで思い込みを修正したか

**First research pass:** the municipal tourism site revealed the wine cellar under the bullring. Cross-checking the **2014 official gazette** established its architectural history and industrial significance. A strange tourism claim turned out to have a documentary basis. [Tourism](https://visita.almendralejo.es/ver_sitio.php?id_sitio=17) / [Gazette](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2014-10700)

**Next research plan:** test three claims. ① Is 'the only one in the world' independently proven? ② Does 750,000 litres describe current production? ③ Is the town's Cava status legally documented? The most exciting claims deserve the strictest checking.

**Second pass:** ① The city markets the site as unique, but we did not conduct a worldwide inventory, so this article avoids absolute uniqueness claims. ② The tank volume is historical capacity, not demonstrated current output. ③ The 1995 legal decision and current Cava zoning provide independent documentary grounding. [City](https://visita.almendralejo.es/ver_sitio.php?id_sitio=17) / [1995 gazette](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-1995-21308) / [Cava zoning](https://www.cava.wine/es/origen/zonas/)

**Open questions:** current cellar utilization, annual visitors, tourism spillovers, local profit distribution, and the cost of preserving industrial buildings. These are not hidden assumptions. They form a concrete research agenda for fieldwork.

## 14｜結論――建物の地下ではなく、見方の地下に降りる

At first, wine tanks under bullring seats seem absurd. After looking at the land, local industry, the cool underground space, and a winemaker's decision, the arrangement becomes surprisingly sensible. What looked strange was the overlap of different histories in one building.

But the lesson is not simply 'reuse old buildings and everyone wins'. Other industrial buildings disappeared, Cava's name depends on institutions, and heritage protection raises questions separate from bullfighting itself. The cellar taught me something larger: **a city holds layers of economic life beneath its most visible identity.**
