# Why does a sports bar need a planetarium company?
## Cosmをfull-stackで読む――Shared Realityをprice, history, imitation, criticismから分解する

Imagine the assignment: build a place where people in another city can watch an NBA game and feel almost courtside.

At first glance, the shopping list seems obvious. A huge screen. Great sound. Seats. A bar. Media rights.

ところがCosmを調べると、the story jumps backward to a 1947 planetarium projector, then to Sketchpad in 1963, early VR research in 1968, a digital planetarium in 1981, and finally to unmanned 10.5K sports cameras and a cloud video platform.

That is an absurdly long route if the goal is merely “build a sports bar.”

でも、この遠回りこそCosmの正体に近い。

Cosm is not simply a premium sports bar with a very large screen. It combines planetarium engineering, computer graphics, immersive sports production, ultra-high-resolution capture, content rights, permanent venues, food and beverage, and live operations into one product.

The visible invention is the 87-foot LED dome. The harder part sits behind it.

**Cosm’s real product is the system that turns an event happening somewhere else into a shared physical experience here.**

> **Information date: 2026-10-07**
>
> This essay relies primarily on first-party sources from Cosm, the NBA, DeNA, KDDI, and NTT West, while using trade and news reporting for construction cost and financing. Terms such as “full stack” and “hard to copy” are analytical interpretations, not official Cosm definitions. Cosm does not disclose a standard final development cost per venue, so construction figures, public filings, and reported total-development estimates are kept separate.

## 1. Cosmは「巨大映像施設」ではなく、captureからhospitalityまでを重ねた会社である

As of October 2026, Cosm operates permanent venues in Los Angeles, Dallas, Atlanta, and Detroit. Detroit opened on September 10, 2026, making it the fourth U.S. location, and Cleveland has also been announced.

The visual centerpiece is **The Dome**. Cosm currently describes it as a 12K × 10K LED experience with social seating designed for eating, talking, and sharing. Each venue also includes **The Hall**, a large multi-screen social area, and **The Deck**, an outdoor terrace.

見た目だけなら、premium sports bar + IMAX + planetarium の合体に見える。

But the company stack is much wider.

The first layer is **capture**. C360 cameras record the field of play in ultra-high resolution.

The second is **production and processing**. Software creates virtual camera views from a much larger source image for live and replay use.

The third is **content and rights**. Cosm works with partners such as the NBA, ESPN, and NBC Sports so that premium events can legally and consistently be presented at its venues.

The fourth is **venue and hospitality**. The content becomes a physical product through the dome, reserved seating, GA, food, beverage, staffing, and event operations.

And outside the consumer venues, Cosm also sells technology into planetariums, science centers, and other immersive environments.

So the dome is not the company. **It is the customer-facing interface of a vertically connected media-and-venue system.**

That distinction matters. If you misread Cosm as “a really big LED screen,” the imitation strategy becomes hardware procurement. The actual business starts much further upstream.

Sources: [Cosm](https://www.cosm.com/) ／ [Cosm Companies](https://www.cosm.com/about/cosm-companies) ／ [Content Partnerships](https://www.cosm.com/partnerships)

## 2. 未来っぽいCosmの祖先は、1947年のplanetariumと1960s computer graphicsにいる

Cosm becomes harder to understand if you start its history in 2020.

Its official lineage goes back to 1947, when Armand Spitz built an affordable opto-mechanical star projector. Spitz later became a major planetarium and dome company.

A second lineage runs through Ivan Sutherland. In 1963 he created Sketchpad; in 1968 he worked on the early head-mounted display known as the “Sword of Damocles.” Evans & Sutherland, the company formed by Sutherland and David Evans, developed computer graphics and simulation technology and introduced the Digistar digital planetarium in 1981.

2006年にはEvans & SutherlandとSpitzが一体化する。

Then, in 2020, the immersive sports assets of LiveLikeVR were combined with that planetarium and graphics lineage to form Cosm. In 2023 Cosm acquired C360, adding ultra-high-resolution sports capture and virtual-camera technology.

Seen as a list of acquisitions, this looks messy.

Seen as a sequence of technical questions, it becomes coherent:

**How do you make people feel present in a place where they are not?**

A planetarium recreates the sky inside a dome. Computer graphics render an artificial three-dimensional space. VR puts a different world inside a headset. Cosm captures a real stadium and reconstructs the feeling of that stadium inside another city.

The object changed from stars to sports, but the underlying problem did not change as much as it first appears.

In July 2021, Cosm hosted a live Tokyo Olympics watch party at its Salt Lake City Experience Center using a 20-meter, 180-degree, 8K LED dome. In 2023 the NBA became its first professional sports-league partner, and in 2024 the first permanent venues opened in Los Angeles and Dallas.

So this is not really “a planetarium company pivoted into a sports bar.”

A better reading is: **technology built to make another space appear around you found a large commercial use case in live sports.**

## 3. CosmはTV broadcastを巨大化しているのではなく、最初からDome用に映像を作り直している

A normal sports broadcast is designed for a rectangular television frame. Camera operators follow subjects, a director selects shots, and the viewer receives a finished frame.

C360 starts from a different premise.

Cosm says its compact unmanned cameras can capture the entire field of play at 10.5K with a 180-degree-plus field of view. From that large source image, software-based virtual cameras can create different views after capture. Pan, tilt, and zoom can happen digitally inside the captured image rather than only by moving a physical camera.

This is more important than “better picture quality.”

**The system delays the decision about framing. Capture first, choose the view later.**

That same source can support broadcast, replay, officiating, coaching, social content, OTT, and immersive displays. In 2026, Cosm announced that C360 systems had been installed across all 32 NHL arenas for more than 1,500 games per season.

一方で、The Domeは12K×10K、live sportsは8K級、captureは10.5Kと説明される。

These numbers describe different stages, not one identical resolution. Capture resolution, production/transmission format, and final display resolution are separate parts of the pipeline.

A WWE behind-the-scenes example shows purpose-positioned 8K/60fps cameras, including high-wide and low ringside angles designed specifically for the 87-foot dome. For Wrexham coverage, Cosm has described taking 10.5K cameras to the stadium and designing views for the dome rather than merely stretching a standard television feed.

つまりCosmはbroadcastを拡大しているのではない。

It creates a separate production grammar for the physical display: **What image makes a body sitting under a curved screen feel present at the event?**

At that point Cosm stops looking only like a venue company. It is also a production company.

Sources: [C360](https://www.cosm.com/media/c360) ／ [Broadcasting Solutions](https://www.cosm.com/media/c360/broadcasting-solutions) ／ [NHL deployment](https://www.cosm.com/news/nhl-and-cosm-announce-multi-year-partnership-providing-ultra-high-resolution-camera-technology-installed-in-all-32-nhl-arenas) ／ [WWE production](https://www.cosm.com/news/wwe-in-shared-reality-behind-the-scenes-of-the-immersive-experience-at-cosm)

## 4. “How much is Cosm?” は、ticket price・venue capex・company fundingを分けないと事故る

Search for “Cosm price” and you can find numbers ranging from about $11 to roughly $90 million.

Both can be relevant. They answer completely different questions.

First: **what does a customer pay?**

Cosm uses variable pricing by event and ticket type. For the September 2026 Dallas NFL game Patriots @ Seahawks, GA started at $11, Hall at $17, and Dome at $127. For Celtics @ 76ers in Dallas on November 10, 2026, GA started at $11, Hall at $17, and Dome at $55. For Atlanta’s NBA Opening Night on October 20, 2026, Dome tickets started at $264.

A Shared Reality screening of The Matrix in Dallas started at $39 for the Dome.

So there is no single “Cosm admission price.” Dome, Hall, and GA are meaningfully different products, and event demand changes the price.

Second: **what does a venue cost to build?**

Engineering News-Record described the Dallas project as a **$42 million space**. Other reporting citing Texas filings has put it around the same order of magnitude. Separately, Indianapolis Business Journal reported in 2025 that three sources estimated developing a Cosm venue at about **$80 million to $90 million**.

These figures should not be forced into one number. Construction contract value, building work, AV systems, land, pre-opening expense, and broader development cost can use different scopes. Cosm’s CEO has declined to disclose a standard venue-development figure.

The careful conclusion is therefore: **Dallas has a verified construction-scale figure around $42 million, while broader development estimates of $80–90 million per venue have been reported; Cosm has not published one official all-in standard cost.**

Third: **how much capital has the company raised?**

Cosm announced more than $250 million in funding in July 2024, with reporting placing its valuation above $1 billion. In June 2026, Sony Pictures Entertainment invested $100 million for a minority stake as lead investor in the Series C round.

Ticket price, CAPEX, and corporate financing are three different layers.

混ぜると「Cosmは高いらしい」だけが残る。分けると、business modelの議論ができる。

## 5. 収益モデルを見ると、Cosmはscreening businessよりutilization businessに近い

Cosm does not publicly disclose a detailed revenue mix by tickets, food and beverage, sponsorship, technology, or other lines.

So we should not invent a percentage split.

What is public is the set of monetization surfaces: multiple ticket classes, food and beverage, group sales and private events, sports programming, films, immersive art and entertainment, sponsorship and content partnerships, plus B2B technology through C360 and its dome/planetarium businesses.

ここから先はinterpretationである。

If each permanent venue requires tens of millions of dollars, the enemy is not only bad image quality. It is **dark time**.

Sports schedules create peaks but also leave gaps. Premium games cluster around nights and weekends. Movies, art, private events, other sports, and corporate uses can turn the same physical asset into different products at different times.

Cosm CEO Jeb Terry has told Pollstar that the programming model can support up to roughly three “turns of the house” in a day. That language sounds less like a one-off attraction and more like cinema, hospitality, and live entertainment economics combined.

Food and beverage also matters beyond margin.

A real stadium is valuable because people do things together: shout, eat, drink, talk, arrive early, stay after. Headsets are immersive but isolate the body. Cosm keeps the social layer physical.

So the “Shared” in Shared Reality is not only a technical description.

**It is part of the monetization design.**

## 6. Is it already being copied?――full cloneは確認できないが、component-level convergenceは始まっている

The word “copy” needs discipline.

In this research, I could not verify a publicly documented competitor that combines all of Cosm’s major layers: dedicated immersive capture, premium rights, permanent large-scale dome venues, hospitality, and multi-city rollout.

That is not proof that no such project exists. It is a scoped research conclusion based on public information.

But parts of the model are clearly appearing elsewhere.

The strongest Japanese example is **THE LIVE Supported by 大和地所**, opened by the Yokohama DeNA BayStars on March 19, 2026. The permanent live-viewing arena is about 2,800 square meters. Its first floor combines an approximately 18m × 8m LED screen with nine food outlets; the second floor includes merchandise retail; the third includes a restaurant. Programming extends beyond BayStars games to basketball, soccer, music, and other content.

It does not reproduce Cosm’s wraparound dome or its dedicated 10.5K-to-dome capture stack.

でも、business questionは近い。

How do you turn “watching a remote event” into a permanent destination with food, retail, and year-round programming?

KDDI approached another component in 2024 with **GINZA VIRTUAL STADIUM**. Three walls plus the floor created an immersive soccer environment, including field-level viewpoints and interactive cheering. It was temporary rather than a scaled permanent venue.

NTT West and Mainichi Broadcasting System tested another layer in March 2026: immersive public viewing over **IOWN APN**, focusing on high-capacity, low-latency transmission between remote locations.

Put them side by side:

THE LIVE approaches from **venue + hospitality**.

KDDI approaches from **immersive presentation + interaction**.

NTT West approaches from **network infrastructure**.

There is not enough evidence to say they copied Cosm.

A better description is **convergent evolution**: different companies are discovering similar pieces because they are trying to solve the same problem—how to create a paid experience between ordinary TV viewing and physically attending the original event.

## 7. Cosmを真似しにくい理由は、Domeの価格ではなく、five business conditionsを同時にそろえる必要があるからだ

A giant LED dome is expensive.

But anything that can eventually be bought from a supplier is rarely a complete long-term moat by itself.

Cosm appears harder to reproduce because several conditions have to work at once.

First is **rights**. Cosm has a long-term NBA agreement extending through 2030 and beyond, along with major programming partnerships involving ESPN, NBC Sports, and other rights holders.

Second is **capture access**. The company is not always limited to receiving a finished broadcast feed; dedicated cameras can be installed at the event itself to create dome-specific perspectives.

Third is **real-time production and distribution**. High-resolution sources need to become virtual-camera outputs and reach huge displays reliably and quickly.

Fourth is **real estate and hospitality**. Every city requires a large physical building, seating, kitchens, bars, staffing, crowd flow, safety, and service operations.

Fifth is **programming**. The venue must be filled across a year with sports, movies, art, private events, and other uses.

This is an awkward combination of capabilities.

A camera company does not automatically know restaurant operations. A hospitality developer does not automatically control sports capture. A broadcaster does not necessarily want to develop national entertainment real estate. A league may own valuable rights but not the venue technology.

Cosm stitched these boundaries together through acquisitions and partnerships.

So the working hypothesis here is that Cosm’s advantage sits less in “12K dome hardware” and more in its ability to connect:

**Rights → Capture → Production → Distribution → Venue → Hospitality**

This is not a proven financial moat. Cosm is private, and detailed unit economics are not public.

But as an explanatory model, it answers a difficult question: why do a planetarium business, a VR production business, C360 cameras, NBA rights, and restaurants need to sit inside one ecosystem?

## 8. 弱点も同じ場所にある――great screens do not erase high fixed costs

The strengths reverse cleanly into risks.

First, **CAPEX is heavy**. Dallas alone carries a verified construction-scale figure around $42 million, while broader reporting points to $80–90 million development estimates. Expansion therefore requires serious capital city by city.

Second, **premium content dependency** remains. A world-class display does not fill itself. Rights partnerships are a barrier to entry, but they also create dependency on schedules, contracts, and attractive matchups.

Third, **“going to Cosm” and “sitting in the Dome” are not the same product**.

Cosm’s current FAQ says General Admission does not guarantee a specific view or seat. GA guests may have limited standing areas with partial Dome and Hall views, while Dome reserved tickets guarantee the immersive seating product.

The price examples show why this matters. On some events GA begins around $11 while Dome seats can exceed $100 or even $200.

That is commercially sensible segmentation. It can also create an expectation problem.

If social media teaches people “Cosm = giant dome,” a cheap GA ticket can be misread as a cheaper version of the same experience. It is not necessarily that.

Fourth, there is the **novelty-to-repeat problem**.

The first visit can be sold by scale alone. The third visit needs programming, matchup quality, food, friends, film adaptation, comfort, and habit. Cosm does not publicly disclose venue-level repeat rate, CAC, or mature unit economics, so this remains difficult to test from outside.

Fifth, do not define competition only as “another Cosm.”

The original stadium, cinemas, sports bars, giant home TVs, VR headsets, Sphere, and THE LIVE all compete for some combination of leisure time and entertainment spend.

No direct clone does not mean no competition.

## 9. 用語を雑にすると、Cosmは“fancy VR”にしか見えなくなる

**Shared Reality** is Cosm’s central category language. It describes headset-free immersion experienced together in a physical venue. It is safer to treat it as Cosm’s branded/category framing rather than a universally standardized technical term.

**XR, or Extended Reality**, is the umbrella around VR, AR, and MR. Cosm is not simply headset VR, but it belongs near the broader question of how digital and physical environments are blended.

**LBE, Location-Based Entertainment**, means technology-mediated entertainment that requires going to a physical place. Theme parks, immersive exhibitions, and location-based VR can all sit in this category. For business-model analysis, LBE is one of the most useful labels for Cosm.

**Fulldome** refers to imagery designed for an entire dome surface, historically associated with planetariums. This is the technical-cultural bridge between Cosm’s planetarium ancestry and its current entertainment venues.

**Virtual camera / reframing** means generating a new viewpoint in software from a larger captured image. C360’s value is not merely recording one beautiful shot, but keeping enough visual information to create many shots later.

**Media rights** are the legal/commercial permissions to broadcast, stream, or present content. They are less visible than the dome but essential to sustained premium sports programming.

And **Experience Economy** is the larger business lens.

Cosm does not primarily sell a video file or a television. It sells time spent in a place with other people. Therefore location, seating, food, staff, crowd energy, and programming become part of the media product.

Once the terms are separated, Cosm stops looking like “VR but bigger.”

It starts to look like **a company living on the border between media infrastructure and venue economics**.

## 10. 日本で応用するなら、do not start by buying the Dome

ここからはproposalである。

If a Japanese operator wants to build a Cosm-like business, starting with “How much does a 12K dome cost?” is probably backwards.

Start outside the screen.

First, identify **anchor content** that can fill a calendar across the year. League games, national teams, overseas sports, music, movies, esports—then check the actual rights conditions for commercial public viewing.

Second, decide whether a normal broadcast feed is enough or whether the product requires **dedicated immersive capture**. That choice changes camera systems, network capacity, production staff, archive design, and rights negotiation.

Third, break down the customer offer. Premium immersive seats can coexist with multi-game Hall viewing, standing areas, food, merchandise, private events, and sponsorship inventory.

Fourth, count **dark hours** before counting pixels. What fills weekday afternoons or off-peak periods? If there is no answer, a smaller screen or more flexible venue may create better economics than a full dome.

Fifth, connect the experience to CRM. Can remote viewing become a fan touchpoint tied to membership, food and beverage, merchandise, sponsor activation, and repeat attendance?

This is why THE LIVE is strategically interesting.

It does not begin by recreating Cosm’s full technology stack. It begins with a more locally plausible unit: **permanent big-screen viewing + food + merchandise + team operation**.

If immersive capture, low-latency networking, interactive cheering, or curved display layers are added later, a Japanese version of the model could emerge from a different route.

The practical question is not “How do we copy the look?”

It is **which layer should we own first?**

## 11. 調べる前は“future sports bar”、調べた後は“planetarium-born remote-experience supply chain”に見える

Return to the opening question.

Why does a sports bar need a planetarium company?

Because the company was not really trying to build only a sports bar.

Spitz learned how to surround people with another sky. Evans & Sutherland learned how to render spaces that were not physically there. LiveLikeVR learned how to produce immersive live sports. C360 learned how to capture a field at such high resolution that viewpoints could be created later.

Cosm connected those capabilities to permanent hospitality venues.

Before the research, the 87-foot screen looks like the invention.

After the research, the screen looks like the exit point.

The hard chain is longer:

Get the rights. Enter the stadium. Capture the action. Produce the view. Move the data. Map it to a giant curved display. Fill the building. Feed people. Make them come back.

That is why tracking imitation should not mean only looking for another dome.

A permanent live-viewing venue can be one piece.
A league-wide ultra-high-resolution capture system can be another.
Low-latency immersive public viewing can be another.
A club-owned food-and-retail viewing destination can be another.

Each is a fragment of the Cosm architecture.

The planetarium did not simply become a sports bar.

**Technology for making “somewhere else” feel present found sports as a massive commercial use case.**

That is what makes Cosm worth watching.
