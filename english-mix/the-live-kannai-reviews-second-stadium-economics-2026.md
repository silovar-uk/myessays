# Why watch baseball next to the stadium?

*THE LIVEの評判、seat economics、urban historyを読み解く*

Imagine building a giant-screen baseball venue just a short walk from the real stadium. You leave the ballpark area, sit somewhere else, and watch the game on a screen. Strange enough. しかも、公式にはthe ticket is a **seat reservation for a restaurant**, not a ticket to the game itself. That oddly precise phrase reveals the business model. [公式のseat policy](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2026/08/0814_01.php)

That place is THE LIVE supported by 大和地所, operated by the Yokohama DeNA BayStars. It opened on March 19, 2026, inside BASEGATE横浜関内 near Kannai Station. An approximately 18m × 8m LED screen sits at the center, surrounded by food, merchandise, and viewing seats. [DeNA公式](https://dena.com/jp/news/5296/)

Here is the working conclusion. Reviewers repeatedly praise the giant screen, air-conditioned comfort, and shared fan energy. Yet volume, seat angles, mobile ordering, and reservation rules create friction. これは少数の投稿を読み比べた**qualitative review**であり、a representative satisfaction surveyではない。[肯定的な訪問記](https://note.com/right1020stand/n/n304f0b4e644c)／[不満の記録](https://tabelog.com/kanagawa/A1401/A140104/14103544/dtlrvwlst/B535787550/)

## 1. そもそも「映像観戦施設」と「スポーツバー」は何が違うのか

First, definitions. **Public viewing** means people gathering in one place to watch an event on a large screen. The J.League explicitly defines public screenings of live matches and sets out licensing requirements. **Sports bar**, however, is a commercial dining format, not one universally fixed technical or legal standard. [J.League rules](https://www.jleague.jp/corporate/services/videoutil/)

THE LIVE combines those two formats in a **permanent venue**. Permanent means not a temporary screen installed for a national-team match, but a facility operating on ordinary days too. Floor one is a nine-vendor food hall, floor two the BAYSTORE Flagship YOKOHAMA merchandise shop, and floor three a dining terrace. [2026 facility guide](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2026/03/0312_01.php)

At roughly 18 by 8 meters, the display covers about 144 square meters. But **bigger is not automatically better**: resolution, viewing distance, seat angle, lighting, audio, and production choices matter. The operator says “one of Japan's largest”; that is not proof of being number one by a fixed benchmark. The screen is infrastructure for an experience, not the experience itself. [Original announcement](https://dena.com/jp/news/5296/)

## 2. 評判を調べると、画面より「一緒に見る人」の価値が浮かび上がる

I separated pre-opening coverage from post-visit accounts. Press tours are useful for understanding equipment, but not necessarily busy-game operations. User reviews reveal friction, yet suffer from **self-selection bias**: only some visitors choose to post. Sources include Tabelog, individual accounts, a September 2026 match visit, and official rules. [Tabelog](https://tabelog.com/kanagawa/A1401/A140104/14103544/)／[訪問記](https://note.com/light_slug4480/n/n8dcb61089d3b)

Positive accounts emphasize scale and collective atmosphere. A June Tabelog visitor praised watching Japan's football team on the large screen. Another fan highlighted air-conditioning, proximity to the ballpark, easy access to merchandise, and spontaneous chants and high-fives. The value is not just seeing more pixels; it is **sharing the moment**. [June review](https://tabelog.com/kanagawa/A1401/A140104/14103544/dtlrvwlst/B531972353/)／[Fan account](https://note.com/right1020stand/n/n304f0b4e644c)

By contrast, a September review describes a group organizer struggling with a pick-up-at-each-vendor ordering system. The event volume also made conversation difficult, and the party left early. Yet the reviewer still found the local food vendors interesting. **What feels like electric atmosphere to a fan can feel like noise to someone meeting friends for a chat.** One feature, two opposite experiences. [September review](https://tabelog.com/kanagawa/A1401/A140104/14103544/dtlrvwlst/B535787550/)

Seats are not equally good. One visitor warns that sitting too close can be harder to watch. Official October 4 terms even note that merchandise queues may obstruct views from some upper-level stands, and that those seats must be vacated before post-game events. This is an operator-disclosed condition, not speculation. **With giant screens, front row does not always mean best row.** [Fan account](https://note.com/right1020stand/n/n304f0b4e644c)／[Official terms](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2026/09/0905_05.php)

Tabelog showed 3.09 from 17 reviews when checked on October 11, 2026. That is a platform-specific restaurant rating, not a clean measure of screen quality, atmosphere, and value. With 17 posts, claims about the average visitor or day-of-week patterns would be overreach. **Read the conditions behind a review, not merely the decimal.** [Tabelog](https://tabelog.com/kanagawa/A1401/A140104/14103544/)

## 3. 料金は「入場料」一つでは説明できず、試合ごとに条件が変わる

Pricing is more subtle than a single admission fee. For September 2026 home games, a two-person arena table cost ¥2,000 per group; a two-person bench cost ¥4,000 on weekdays or ¥5,000 on weekends; a five-person box cost ¥10,000 or ¥12,500. These were **per-reservation group prices**, not per-person tickets. [Official seat table](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2026/08/0814_01.php)

For away games in the same period, the **seat reservation itself was free**, but an ¥110 ticket-system fee applied per electronic ticket. So “free” did not mean zero transaction cost. Home/away rules and special events change over time; do not assume these October 2026 examples remain current forever. [August 2026 notice](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2026/08/0814_01.php)

Food is another expense. For a free replay event in late September, the organizer explicitly asked each visitor to order at least one food item and one drink. That rule is documented **for that event**; it should not be generalized to every time slot. Think in three separate wallets: reservation fee, booking-system fee, and food-and-drink requirements. [Replay event rules](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2026/09/0921_02.php)

Reservations also expire after a late arrival: the September notice used 30 minutes after first pitch for home games and 10 minutes for away games, with no refund for delays. If you think you bought a right to watch baseball, that seems harsh. If you see it as a timed restaurant-table reservation, you can understand the operator's **no-show inventory problem**—without having to endorse the policy. [Terms](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2026/08/0814_01.php)

## 4. 映像を見る店の注文導線は、映像より厳しく評価される

Nine food vendors offer choice, but they also create operational complexity: menus, checkout, pickup alerts, walking distance, and splitting bills. The critical review did not say ordering was impossible. It described a **mismatch between a group organizer's workflow and the actual pickup process**. [Visitor report](https://tabelog.com/kanagawa/A1401/A140104/14103544/dtlrvwlst/B535787550/)

An operational hypothesis follows. Proposed improvements: ① explain vendor-by-vendor pickup before ordering, ② generate a multi-vendor pickup checklist for group orders, ③ show expected wait times near kickoff or first pitch. These are **design proposals based on a reported friction point**, not verified claims that current features are absent.

Sound presents the same trade-off. Maximum crowd energy can delight fans and alienate diners who want to talk. A proposed quieter area would require real sound-pressure measurements, careful seating geometry, and safe circulation. A permanent mixed-use venue cannot optimize every square meter only for the loudest game night.

## 5. 1959年の市庁舎跡地から、2026年の観戦施設が生まれた

The story then jumps back to architecture. This is the former Yokohama City Hall district. Architect Togo Murano designed the 1959 city hall, built for the port's centennial. The city moved to its new headquarters in 2020, creating an opportunity to redevelop the old site. [City history](https://www.city.yokohama.lg.jp/city-info/yokohamashi/shichosha/keii/story.html)／[Agency for Cultural Affairs](https://www.bunka.go.jp/kindai/kenzoubutsu/research/kanagawa/009/index.html)

BASEGATE横浜関内 combines a reused heritage building, hotel, offices, retail, learning, innovation facilities, and entertainment. Eight companies joined the development consortium, led by Mitsui Fudosan, including DeNA. **Public-private partnership** means connecting civic goals with private delivery and operations; it does not tell us the subsidy amount or contract allocation by itself. [Opening release](https://www.mitsuifudosan.co.jp/corporate/news/2026/0319_02/)

The shift is bigger than “a baseball club opening a shop.” Former civic land became a place for hospitality, work, tourism, and entertainment. In urban planning, **pedestrian circulation** describes how people move among multiple places, not just to one destination. If fans eat, shop, and explore nearby streets, the district value exceeds the spending captured inside THE LIVE. [City planning overview](https://www.mitsuifudosan.co.jp/corporate/news/2026/0319_02/)

## 6. DeNAの15年間を追うと、球場の外へ出るのは自然な次の手になる

DeNA entered professional baseball in 2011 and brought the Yokohama Stadium operating company under its control in 2016 through a friendly **tender offer (TOB)**—a public offer to purchase shares under announced terms. That enabled more integrated club-and-stadium management. [Team history](https://sports.dena.com/brand/yokohama-dena-baystars)／[TOB announcement](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2016/01/0121_03.php)

In 2017, DeNA articulated a sports-town strategy and later expanded into basketball with Kawasaki Brave Thunders and football with SC Sagamihara. In 2025, BayStars home games drew 2,360,411 cumulative attendances across 71 games, a club record. That indicates demand, but it is **visits, not unique fans**. [Strategy](https://sports.dena.com/report/250207)／[Attendance](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2025/10/1001_06.php)

My interpretation is a sequence of expanding control and reach. First, own the event. Next, integrate the stadium and match-day commerce. Then create an off-stadium touchpoint for away-game fans, non-ticket holders, and ordinary locals. The official emphasis on **every day** can be read as a response to the limits of a home schedule of roughly seventy games. This is analytical interpretation, not a disclosed profit forecast.

## 7. 収益を五つに分けないと「人気施設」が黒字かどうか分からない

Economically, the screen sits inside at least five potential revenue streams: ① reserved seating, ② food and beverage, ③ merchandise, ④ sponsorship and naming rights, and ⑤ event-space rentals. The operator advertises corporate events, presentations, and screenings. But revenue by stream, margin, rent, sponsorship value, and total capex are not publicly verified. [Venue rentals](https://the-live.com/rental)／[Naming-rights release](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2025/09/0924_04.php)

An accounting warning: vendor sales do not necessarily equal revenue booked by the club. For a leased concession, the operator might receive rent or a revenue share, not every yen at the till. Merchandise sales differ from gross profit. **Naming rights** sell an association with the venue's name; the “supported by 大和地所” label comes from such a contract, with no public contract value established here. [Official announcement](https://www.baystars.co.jp/news/2025/09/0924_04.php)

Try a deliberately crude thought experiment: 500 visitors a day × ¥2,000 spending × 300 days equals ¥300 million of gross visitor spending. This is **not an actual result or forecast**, and not necessarily BayStars revenue. It ignores margins, rent, staffing, media rights, and seasonal peaks. Its value is exposing the accounting question: whose sale is it, and who bears the cost?

THE LIVE also advertises venue rentals with large-format video, sound, and lighting. That creates a different question: can corporate events fill otherwise idle weekdays? This is **asset utilization**—getting more productive use from costly fixed space—a familiar problem in cinemas, convention centers, and event venues. [Official rental information](https://the-live.com/rental)

## 8. 画面を持つだけでは足りない。権利処理と運営が本当の難所になる

Other sports and music make the schedule attractive, but rights are not automatic. Under J.League rules, a public screening of a live match can require authorization even when viewers enter for free. **A TV subscription is not automatically a commercial public-screening license.** Those J.League terms do not automatically govern baseball or concerts; rights must be checked by sport, holder, and use. [J.League licensing policy](https://www.jleague.jp/corporate/services/videoutil/)

Rights to show content and the technology to present it are different things. Scaling up a conventional broadcast is possible, but not automatically optimal. Think of scores, player data, alternate feeds, venue lighting, and synchronized in-house events. If the stream is delayed, what happens to the stage program? **Production, rights, and hospitality must run on one timeline** to feel like one seamless experience. [Opening-day report](https://getnews.jp/archives/3702887)

Operations also include peak cheering, alcohol, retail queues, wheelchair positions, flashing visuals, sound levels, and emergency routes. The operator provides wheelchair-priority viewing seats, but their existence alone does not certify every accessibility outcome. The real service test often lies in **handling exceptions**, not showing off hardware specifications.

## 9. 類似施設と比べると「没入型」よりも「街の日常」が独自性になる

Compare Cosm in the US. It uses a large immersive dome and purpose-built capture to make remote sport feel physically present. THE LIVE, by contrast, emphasizes a wide rectangular screen, everyday dining, team merchandise, home-and-away games, and its connection to Kannai. Both sell collective remote viewing, but their center of gravity differs: **immersive production versus everyday urban access**. [Cosm](https://www.cosm.com/)／[THE LIVE](https://the-live.com/)

A game-night fan destination and a neighborhood daytime restaurant share an address but not identical customer jobs. Optimize everything for loud match nights, and weekday diners may leave. Optimize for quiet everyday dining, and the venue may lose what makes a fan travel there. That is a **conflict between customer jobs**, not merely a staff-training issue.

## 10. あえて「誰に向いていないか」を決めると、評判が読みやすくなる

I tried one slightly excessive exercise: classify the venue not by age or fandom, but by the job a visitor wants done. This is an **analytical thought experiment**, not customer-segmentation data from the operator.

- **The cheering fan** values screen scale and shared chants; check the fixture and seat type.
- **The conversation-first diner** values food and talk; check what's on screen and the likely volume.
- **The tactical observer** values sightlines and match information; distance may matter more than front-row status.
- **The first-time organizer** values a predictable group workflow; understand ordering, pickup, and payment first.

A surprising result follows: these complaints are not necessarily signs of a bad concept. Strong match-night design can itself produce friction for nonfans. The same choice generates delight and dissatisfaction. A useful proposed KPI is **fit by visitor purpose**—repeat intent for viewing, dining, and group visits—plus seat-specific sightline ratings, rather than one pooled satisfaction score.

Going further would mean linking outcomes to game/no-game days, wins and losses, opponents, weather, occupancy, actual decibels, and service times. Seventeen public reviews cannot identify those causal effects. The next step is a structured survey joined to operational logs. That turns complaints into **testable service hypotheses**, not internet scorekeeping.

## 11. 結局、球場の隣で映像を見ることは無駄なのか

Back to the puzzle. Why watch on a screen beside a real ballpark? Because stadium seats are finite, away games happen elsewhere, and outdoor viewing comes with weather and movement constraints. A shared screening need not be a cheap imitation of the real thing. It can be **a different product solving different constraints**.

Still, neither promotional photos nor star ratings answer the hardest questions. Can cheering fans, casual diners, and no-game visitors coexist without pushing each other out? Who captures the spending and pays the fixed costs? Will ordinary days work as well as headline games? THE LIVE should ultimately be judged by its **ability to operate different reasons to visit across the year**.

Now the phrase “restaurant seat reservation” no longer sounds silly. It looks as if the venue sells baseball on a screen. More precisely, it sells **a reason for people to gather at the same place at the same time**. If that reason can be renewed every day, the paradox of a second stadium next to the first one disappears.
