# Distance is not measured in meters――デーヴ・グロスマンの「4つの距離」を分解する
## Why do we feel bolder by email? 文化・倫理・社会・機械のdistanceを、別のpsychologyで照合する

「Zoom越しの方が値段交渉を強気にできる気がする」。The conversation that opened the previous essay contains this casual observation. Face to face, the other person feels too human, so asking for millions more can feel awkward. Email feels easier. Then comes the thought: "maybe this is mechanical distance."

戦争心理学で見積書を説明し始めた。The scale of the theory has suddenly become absurdly large.

But the joke is too useful to leave there. In 『戦争における「人殺し」の心理学』, デーヴ・グロスマン（Dave Grossman）organizes distance from a victim not only in meters but through several psychological routes. Among them, he describes cultural, moral, social, and mechanical distance as forms of emotional distance. Does inserting a screen really make another person feel less human? The question is more legitimate than the joke first makes it sound.

ただし、one conclusion must come first. These four distances are not an established four-factor psychological scale. They are Grossman's conceptual organization of battlefield testimony and earlier research, and the book as a whole has major critics. So this essay will not end by memorizing four labels. It will pull them apart using dehumanization research, moral disengagement, Milgram, online disinhibition, and studies of remotely piloted aircraft crews.

> **Information date: 2026-09-30**
>
> This is a sequel to 「『嘘を嘘と見抜け』だけでは、言葉の半分しか見えていない」. Grossman's framework is treated here not as a validated four-factor model but as a conceptual map to test. Connections to email and social media are analogies used to examine where the comparison works and where it breaks, not claims that ordinary communication and combat are psychologically identical.

## 1. The four distances are forms of emotional distance, separate from physical distance

最初に、名前から起きるmisunderstandingを片づけたい。"Four distances" sounds like a universal theory dividing all human remoteness into four types. Grossman actually places physical distance inside the larger issue of total distance from a victim, then separately discusses emotional distance. Cultural, moral, social, and mechanical distance are four forms of that emotional distance. So the structure is not "distance has four kinds," but "besides physical distance, four emotional routes are proposed."

Cultural distance uses racial or ethnic difference to cast the other as fundamentally unlike us, sometimes less than human. Moral distance comes from intense certainty that one's own side is righteous and punishment or revenge is justified. Social distance concerns a stratified environment in which a particular class is repeatedly treated as beneath one's own. Mechanical distance inserts media such as screens, thermal images, or sights, making the target harder to perceive as a directly present human body.

ここでsurprisingなのがsocial distanceである。In ordinary usage it can mean low intimacy or weak familiarity. Grossman's meaning is much more hierarchical: less "I have never met this person" and more "I have been socialized to see people of this rank as not deserving the same standing." The shared English label hides a substantial difference.

Once the four are lined up, a common pattern appears. None of them literally adds centimeters. What changes is **which information required to perceive someone as a particular human being remains available and which information is discarded**. From here, "distance" starts to look less like location and more like resolution.

[筑摩書房『戦争における「人殺し」の心理学』](https://www.chikumashobo.co.jp/product/9784480088598/) ／ [Google Books, On Killing](https://books.google.com/books/about/On_Killing.html?id=BewqAwAAQBAJ) ／ [立命館大学生存学研究所](https://www.ritsumei-arsvi.org/publication/center_report/publication-center17/publication-249/)

## 2. The dangerous part of cultural distance is not difference itself but converting difference into a hierarchy of humanity

Cultural distance is a risky phrase if used carelessly. If we say "people from different cultures are harder to empathize with, therefore violence becomes easier," we make difference itself sound pathological. There is a huge step between speaking another language, practicing another religion, or wearing different clothes and treating another person as less than human.

グロスマンが実際に強調するのも、difference itselfよりdehumanizationに近い。Enemies are assigned derogatory labels and treated not as individuals but as an inferior category. Modern research connects more directly to this process. A large study combined a meta-analysis of 120 studies with 128,022 participants and reported that blatant dehumanization is distinct from simple dislike and uniquely related to support for intergroup violence.

But this is not evidence that "cultural difference causes violence." It is evidence about placing other people below the category of full humanity. If we drop that distinction, a framework meant to analyze prejudice can itself generate a new prejudice: the idea that people from another culture are dangerous because they are distant.

前稿で扱ったRwandan genocideでも同じだった。The existence of Hutu/Tutsi classifications and the spread of language presenting a target group as pests or enemies must be analytically separated. A more precise modern reading of cultural distance is therefore not "how different are they?" but **when does difference get converted into a difference in human worth?**

[Research on dehumanization and support for intergroup violence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40323862/) ／ [前稿](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/speech-acts-truth-is-not-the-whole-story)

## 3. Moral distance is not the disappearance of morality; it is morality becoming a license for harm

"Moral distance" sounds like having less morality. Grossman's account is nearly the reverse. Our side is right; the other side is guilty. This is not revenge but punishment, not aggression but justice. Moral certainty itself can create distance.

A different psychological theory breaks this process down in finer detail: アルバート・バンデューラ（Albert Bandura）and **道徳的離脱（moral disengagement）**. Bandura described ways people can retain moral standards while performing harmful acts: morally justifying the act, giving it sanitized language, comparing it with something worse, displacing or diffusing responsibility, minimizing harm, blaming the victim, or dehumanizing the target.

グロスマンのmoral distanceとBanduraのmoral disengagementは同じ理論ではない。Yet the overlap is clear. People do not always act by thinking, "this is wrong but I will do it anyway." They may perform a second operation: "this is necessary," "they caused it," "this protects everyone." **The act is rewritten until it fits back inside the actor's own moral system.**

At that point the word "distance" itself looks strange. One does not move away from morality. One may illuminate one's own righteousness so intensely that the other person's context and suffering go dark. Distance can be created not only through indifference but through **an excess of one-sided certainty**.

[Albert Bandura, “Moral disengagement in the perpetration of inhumanities”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15661671/)

## 4. Social distance here does not mean "we are not close"; it means rank becoming a difference in human standing

Of the four, social distance is probably the easiest to misapply in everyday conversation. The ordinary Japanese expression suggests intimacy or familiarity. Grossman's social distance is narrower and more hierarchical: a long-standing social structure teaches people to treat members of a particular class as beneath them.

これは"知らない人だから冷たくなる"という話ではない。Birth, occupation, class, or rank can carry an assumption that "this person is not entitled to the same treatment I am." Listening to their view, applying the same rules, or treating their pain as comparable can become weaker because reciprocity itself has been ranked.

The interesting twist is that digital environments can sometimes reduce this kind of distance. One factor in ジョン・スーラー（John Suler）'s online disinhibition theory is minimization of status and authority. Online, clothes, office size, seating order, and other status cues can disappear, making people say things they would not say face to face. A screen may increase something like mechanical distance while decreasing status-based social distance.

この時点で、the four cannot be treated as a simple additive score. One interface can hide a face and hide a title at the same time. Human relations are better imagined not as one distance slider, but as **a mixer whose different channels can move in opposite directions**.

[コトバンク「社会的距離」](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%A4%BE%E4%BC%9A%E7%9A%84%E8%B7%9D%E9%9B%A2-75653) ／ [John Suler, “The Online Disinhibition Effect”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15257832/)

## 5. Mechanical distance becomes more complicated than "screens make people colder" once we look at the evidence

では、the category closest to our original email joke is mechanical distance. No face, no voice, no immediate reaction. It seems plausible that removing sensory cues could lower resistance to harming or pressuring another person.

A classic body of evidence points in a nearby direction: スタンリー・ミルグラム（Stanley Milgram）'s obedience experiments. A synthesis of the Milgram conditions found that greater proximity between the "teacher" and "learner" was associated with lower obedience to the experimenter. In the classic proximity series, the share continuing to the end decreased as the learner moved from another room to the same room and then into required physical contact.

ただし、this is not an experiment showing that screens make people cruel. To focus on digital communication we need another literature. Suler's online disinhibition framework points to anonymity, invisibility, asynchronicity, solipsistic introjection, dissociative imagination, and minimization of status and authority. The screen matters because several social and temporal conditions change together, not simply because glass exists between two people.

A 2026 meta-analysis combined 71 articles, 80 independent studies, and 57,725 participants and reported positive associations between anonymity, online disinhibition, and cyber aggression. But these outcomes concern online aggression, not an ordinary business email asking for a higher fee.

だから"emailで強気 = mechanical distance" is too fast. Email changes more than medium: visibility, response timing, the discomfort of silence, and exposure to the other person's immediate reaction. **What looked like one mechanical distance turns out to be a bundle of social and temporal transformations.**

[Meta-Milgram study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3976349/) ／ [John Suler, “The Online Disinhibition Effect”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15257832/) ／ [2026 meta-analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15248380261476605)

## 6. The fact that drone operators can be harmed breaks the simple idea that remote means emotionally numb

Mechanical distance has an even stronger counterexample: remotely piloted aircraft, commonly called drones. Operators can be physically far from the battlefield and act through screens. If we overread Grossman's vocabulary, this seems like the ideal case of extreme psychological distance.

Empirical research does not support the simple picture that "remote means unharmed." A 2019 study examined 715 U.S. Air Force remotely piloted aircraft personnel who had experienced at least one traumatic combat-related event. 6.15% met DSM-5 symptom criteria for PTSD. The number of events in which civilian bystanders were killed and the operator's sense of shared responsibility for civilian injury or death were significant predictors of symptoms.

Research on remote warfare and **道徳的負傷（moral injury）** has also challenged the picture of drone crews as emotionally detached video-game players. Physical safety and moral distance from the consequences of one's decisions are not the same variable.

ここからmechanical distanceを読み替えた方がよさそうだ。A machine does not simply erase humanity in one direction. It changes what can be seen, what cannot, and how consequences are represented. **Mechanical distance may be better understood as an interface that transforms human information rather than a wall that reduces emotion to zero.**

[Study of PTSD symptoms in USAF remotely piloted aircraft personnel](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30660830/) ／ [Christian Enemark, “Drones, risk, and moral injury”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23337486.2017.1384979)

## 7. The four distances are better used as a map to interrogate than as a psychological formula

After checking each category, we still need to ask how much confidence to place in Grossman's framework itself. As discussed in the previous essay, 『戦争における「人殺し」の心理学』relies heavily on S・L・A・マーシャル（S. L. A. Marshall）'s controversial claims about firing rates in World War II, whose methods and numbers have long been challenged.

The Australian Army professional education site The Cove includes the book on a military reading list while explicitly warning readers to approach it critically. It points to Grossman's reliance on Marshall and to inconsistencies with broader research on human behavior. A recommended military book carrying its own "read this skeptically" sign is, by itself, an interesting object.

本稿で確認できた範囲でも、we did not find a major literature validating cultural, moral, social, and mechanical distance as four independent psychometric factors. Yet separate literatures do exist on dehumanization, moral disengagement, proximity, and online disinhibition. Parts of Grossman's map resemble terrain measured by other methods; that does not guarantee the scale or borders of the whole map.

So the four distances work better as **an index of questions to investigate next** than as a formula calculating danger. Instead of stopping at "this is cultural distance," ask whether dehumanization is present. Instead of "this is moral distance," ask what justification is operating. Ask whether hierarchy is destroying reciprocity, and what an interface is hiding. The theory becomes more useful when used as a search box rather than a verdict.

[Australian Army, The Cove, “On Killing”](https://cove.army.gov.au/read/killing) ／ [筑摩書房『戦争における「人殺し」の心理学』](https://www.chikumashobo.co.jp/product/9784480088598/)

## 8. Once we classify why email feels easier, we discover that four distances are not enough

最後に、the estimate negotiation returns. Imagine making the same price increase face to face, by Zoom, by audio call, by email, and through an automated procurement form. Physical distance after Zoom may barely change, but facial cues, voice, response delay, awkward silence, status signals, and the speed at which you perceive the other person's discomfort all change.

In this thought experiment, what increases most clearly resembles mechanical distance plus Suler's invisibility and asynchronicity. Cultural distance does not necessarily increase. Moral distance may not appear at all. If email weakens corporate status cues, Grossman's social distance may even shrink. The intuition "email makes me bolder" is useful, but reality escapes the four labels when we try to force every mechanism inside them.

そこで、from this point onward comes **the author's proposed reinterpretation**. Read the four distances not as remoteness but as four kinds of compression. Cultural distance compresses "individual → group." Moral distance compresses "complex situation → righteous/guilty." Social distance compresses "person → rank/class." Mechanical distance compresses "body → signal/screen/symbol." Compression makes a situation easier to handle, but it can discard exceptions, histories, expressions, and pain.

調べる前は、I thought the idea was "screens make people stop seeing other people as human." After researching, the question became quieter and more useful: **when an interaction moves to another medium, another label, or another hierarchy, what information about the other person disappears?** Reducing distance may not mean physically approaching. It may mean restoring information that was removed.

This is not a recommendation to open a customer's portrait before sending a quote. The useful proposal is narrower: when a decision has serious consequences, check whether you have reduced someone to a group label, erased doubt through righteousness, changed treatment because of rank, or forgotten consequences outside the screen. What survived the four-distance investigation was less a theory of distance than **an editing theory for checking whether we have rendered another person at dangerously low resolution**.
