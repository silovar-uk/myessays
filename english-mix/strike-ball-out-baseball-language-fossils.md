# ストライクは動作、ボールは物、アウトは状態。野球の3語、統一感がなさすぎる
## The scoreboard looks systematic. The words are historical leftovers.

野球を見ていて、急に気になった。

**Why are “strike,” “ball,” and “out” such different kinds of words?**

Strike comes from striking — an action.

Ball is literally an object.

Out is a state: you are no longer in active play.

なのにスコアボードでは、

```text
BALL    3
STRIKE  2
OUT     2
```

と、ものすごく整然と並ぶ。

The interface says, “These are three items of the same kind.”

Language says, “Not really.”

- **strike** = action
- **ball** = object
- **out** = state

品詞からして、ほぼ別部署である。

At first I assumed there must be an old linguistic reason that ties them together.

調べたら、むしろ反対だった。

**They do not match because baseball itself was not designed all at once.**

このばらばらさは、rule designの失敗というより、歴史が残った跡だった。

---

## 1. They are not even counting the same thing

現在の野球では、balls, strikes and outsが同じ画面に表示される。

But conceptually, they point at different layers of the game.

- **strike**：打者の「打つ」という行為から育った言葉
- **ball**：投手が届けた球を評価する言葉
- **out**：打者や走者がプレーから除かれた状態

Balls and strikes mainly describe what happens inside a plate appearance.

Outs count how many offensive players have been retired across plate appearances.

つまり、この三つは最初から「三択の判定語」ではない。

**The scoreboard grouped them. History did not.**

この一文だけで、違和感のかなりの部分がほどける。

---

## 2. “Strike” did not originally mean “a pitch in the strike zone”

まずstrike。

The ordinary English verb means to hit, beat or make contact.

Online Etymology Dictionary records a baseball use as early as 1841, when “strike” could refer broadly to hitting or making contact with the ball.

今の感覚だと少し変だ。

We hear “strike” and think about the pitcher’s successful pitch.

But the older viewpoint was much closer to the batter: **strike at the ball.**

初期のKnickerbocker Rulesにも、打者が三つの球を“struck at and missed”し、最後の球が捕られた場合にoutになる、という考え方が見える。

The basic logic was simple.

```text
You tried to strike the ball.
You missed.
That failed attempt counts against you.
```

だからstrike。

So far, the word makes perfect sense.

Then baseball changed the rule.

---

## 3. In 1858, baseball invented a strike for not striking

19世紀半ばのpitcherは、現在よりも「打者が打てる球を供給する人」に近かった。

Early rules described the pitcher as delivering the ball “for the striker.”

That created a loophole.

打者が振らなければいい。

A batter could simply wait, delay the game, or give a baserunner time to advance.

これは試合としてかなり困る。

So in 1858, a new rule allowed the umpire to intervene.

SABRが紹介する規則では、打者がgood ballsに繰り返し手を出さず、試合を遅らせるなどしている場合、審判は警告のあと、**one strike, two, three strikes**と宣告できるようになった。

This was an early form of the called strike.

ここで言葉が不思議な拡張をする。

Originally, “strike” was closely tied to trying to strike the ball.

Now baseball could call a **strike when the batter did not strike at all.**

すごい。

A word about an action was reused to penalise the absence of that action.

現在の「見逃しストライク」が当たり前すぎて気づかなかったが、言葉として見るとかなり大胆な仕様変更である。

It feels less like clean terminology design and more like backward-compatible software.

既存の名前は変えない。
意味を拡張して対応する。

---

## 4. “Ball” is even stranger because every pitch is a ball

次はball。

Every pitch is physically a ball.

ストライクも、ボールも、ファウルも、投手が投げた物体としては全部ballである。

And yet, when the pitch is unacceptable, the umpire says:

**Ball!**

つまり、

**「これは球です！」**

知っている。

The ordinary word “ball” is ancient: a round object, a game ball.

野球の判定語としては、その投球自体を指す普通名詞が、しだいに「打者にとって不適切な投球」の分類名になっていったと考えるとわかりやすい。

Online Etymology Dictionary records the modern baseball sense — a pitch outside the strike zone — by 1889, possibly shortened from expressions such as high ball or low ball.

But the rule mechanism for **calling a ball** existed earlier.

ここから歴史がかなり散らかる。

---

## 5. Baseball once needed nine balls for a walk

SABRによると、base on ballsは初期野球のオリジナル機能ではなかった。

The pitcher was expected to give the batter something hittable.

If that assumption worked, there was little need for a formal punishment for bad pitches.

しかし競技が本気になると、当然、投手はルールの限界を使い始める。

Speed increases. Control becomes strategic. “For the striker” stops being enough.

そこで1864年、警告後も投手がunfair ballsを投げ続ければ、審判がballを宣告し、一定数で一塁を与える仕組みが入った。

And then baseball spent years trying to decide what “a certain number” should mean.

SABRの整理では、1875年には**nine balls**で一塁。

Then eight.
Then six.
Then five.
Finally, in 1889, four.

9ボール制。

A modern full count would feel like the conversation had barely started.

さらに時期によってはwideという別判定まで存在した。

This is the important part.

“Ball” was not a perfectly defined category waiting to be named.

**The category itself was being invented while baseball was being played.**

だから言葉も、現在から逆算すると妙に見える。

---

## 6. “Out” is the one word that looks almost suspiciously normal

最後はout。

This one is much easier.

Out is a very old English word meaning outside, away, removed or no longer in a position or state.

スポーツ用語としても、baseball以前の18世紀のcricketですでに使われていた。

Early baseball rules also used “out” to describe conditions under which a batter or runner was removed from active play.

So “out” is not really a pitch label.

**It describes a player’s resulting state.**

この意味ではsafeとかなりきれいに対になる。

```text
OUT  = removed from active play
SAFE = still alive on the basepaths
```

Out and safe feel like a designed pair.

Then we place BALL and STRIKE next to OUT on the scoreboard and suddenly wonder why the vocabulary looks messy.

問題はoutではなく、**違う階層の言葉を一列に見せるUI**だったのかもしれない。

---

## 7. What if we redesigned baseball terminology from scratch?

ここで、少しやりすぎてみる。

Suppose we invented baseball in 2026 and demanded a perfectly consistent terminology system.

### Make everything an action

```text
STRIKE = swing-and-miss
BALL   = miss-the-zone
OUT    = retire
```

説明としては正しい。

Terrible as umpire calls.

### Make everything a state

```text
STRIKE = batter-disadvantage
BALL   = batter-advantage
OUT    = retired
```

急にカードゲームになった。

### Make everything an object

```text
STRIKE BALL
WIDE BALL
OUT PERSON
```

The last one sounds threatening.

この実験は歴史的事実ではない。

It is just a design test.

でも、一つ見えてくる。

**A taxonomy can be consistent and still be worse language.**

競技の言葉は、辞書の分類表だけで生きているわけではない。

They are shouted, remembered, inherited and reused.

既存の語を残したままルールを変えれば、意味は少しずつ伸びる。

Baseball has had more than a century and a half to accumulate exactly that kind of linguistic debt.

言語的technical debtである。

ただし、返済する必要はたぶんない。

---

## 8. The mess is the history

調べる前、BALL / STRIKE / OUTの不統一は単なる命名の雑さに見えた。

After looking at the early rules, it looks different.

Strike kept a trace of the batter’s action, even after the rule expanded to called strikes.

Ball became a formal penalty only after the early assumption — that the pitcher would simply deliver a hittable pitch — stopped working well enough.

Out carried an older sporting idea of being removed from play.

三語は同時に生まれていない。

They do not describe the same object.

They were never designed as one neat family.

だから、そろっていない。

And that is exactly why they are interesting.

いまスコアボードを見ると、BALL、STRIKE、OUTが少し違って見える。

They are not three clean labels created in one naming meeting.

**They are rules from different historical layers compressed into one tiny interface.**

野球のスコアボードは、小さなlanguage museumだった。
