# スマブラでは、背中も技表の一部である
## Why getting behind matters differently in Smash

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
In most fighting games, walking toward someone while facing away from them sounds wrong.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
相手を見る。前を向く。攻撃する。That feels like the normal order.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Smash has a standard technique called **Reverse Aerial Rush, or RAR**: run toward the opponent, turn around, jump, and keep the forward momentum while facing the other way.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
つまり、相手へ近づくために、いったん相手へ背中を向ける。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The point is not style. SmashWiki describes RAR as a practical way to approach with a back aerial, especially for characters whose Bair is stronger or more useful than Fair.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
In Smash, your back is not simply a blind spot. **Sometimes the move you want is stored there.**

[SmashWiki: Reverse aerial rush](https://www.ssbwiki.com/RAR)

That is already a strange design idea.

If a boxing coach said, “Turn your back before closing distance because your stronger punch lives there,” you would have questions.

In Smash, you go to Training Mode.

---

## 1. Cross-ups are not unique to Smash

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
First, we need one correction: “getting behind matters in Smash but not in other fighting games” is too simple.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Street Fighter and many other 2D fighters have **cross-ups** — attacks that switch sides and force the defender to change block direction.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The Fighting Game Glossary defines a cross-up around this left-right switch. CAPCOM's old SFII technical article even describes a rare “reverse hit” that works before the character finishes turning around after a cross-up.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So side-switching is not a Smash invention. 表裏 is classic fighting-game language.
<!-- level:4 role:qualification -->
The more useful question is different: **after the side switch, how long does facing direction remain relevant, and how many actions does it change?**
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Smash stands out because **facing direction often remains a resource in its own right.**

[The Fighting Game Glossary: Cross-up](https://glossary.infil.net/?t=Crossup)  
[CAPCOM: SFII Reverse Hit](https://game.capcom.com/cfn/sfv/column/132437?lang=en)

Not every fighting game uses the same auto-turn rules, and 3D fighters can have explicit back-turned stances.

So this is not “all fighters vs Smash.”

It is mainly a comparison with 2D fighters that tend to restore a face-to-face relationship after the side switch.

---

## 2. In Smash, facing direction changes the move list

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The key is simple: in Smash, your direction can determine **which move exists on the side of the opponent**.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Opponent on your right. If you are facing right, attacking right in the air gives you Fair. If you are still facing left, that same opponent is now on your Bair side.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
SmashWiki defines Back Air as the aerial performed opposite the fighter's facing direction, and notes that Bairs are, on average, among the strongest aerials and often function as finishers.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Fair and Bair are not cosmetic mirrors. Startup, duration, landing lag, hitbox, knockback, combo value and kill power can all differ.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
So distance is not enough. You also need the correct **orientation state** to open the move you want.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Facing direction is not animation. It is an input condition for your toolkit.**

[SmashWiki: Back aerial](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Back_aerial)

This is why RAR is deeper than “jump backward.”

RAR separates two things that normally feel coupled:

**where the body is moving** and **which side of the move list is active**.

You move forward while keeping the back side available.

That is a very Smash sentence.

## 3. “Get behind them” and “use your own back” are the same system

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
There are actually two different backs in Smash.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
One: you cross the opponent and land **behind them**. Two: you turn yourself and place **your own back** toward the opponent so Bair becomes available.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
SmashWiki's Cross-up page explains that landing behind a shielding opponent can remove shield grab as an immediate answer. RAR, meanwhile, exists to reverse your facing direction and access Bair with forward momentum.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The first looks like positioning. The second looks like execution tech.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
But underneath, both are doing the same thing: **editing who faces which way, and therefore which moves occupy front and back.**
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Getting behind in Smash is less “enter the blind spot” and more **rewrite the next move menu.**

[SmashWiki: Cross-up](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Cross-up)  
[SmashWiki: Shield grab](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Shield_grab)

Ultimate also removed ordinary run-throughs between fighters.

You cannot simply sprint through a body for free.

Some aerials and dash attacks can still cross up, so the side switch becomes something you **construct** with attack movement, jump arcs and landing position.

[SmashWiki: Super Smash Bros. Ultimate](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Ultimate)

## 4. Behind is not automatically safe: a three-character audit

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
This is where “always land behind shield” immediately breaks.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The value of a cross-up depends on how the defender's move menu changes by side.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Ultimate Frame Data lists Link's Up B out of shield at 7 frames and marks it **front only**. Behind him, Bair is a 9-frame OOS option.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
So crossing Link's shield can remove one of his fastest front-side punish tools from the immediate menu.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Wii Fit Trainer flips the lesson. Her Bair starts on frame 5 and is 8 frames out of shield. SmashWiki specifically describes it as a strong OOS option when the opponent is behind.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
You cross up, congratulate yourself for avoiding shield grab, and a yoga pose kicks you in the face.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Mario gives another warning. His Up Smash sweeps from back to front, and SmashWiki explicitly mentions it as a reason some cross-ups can be risky.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
Three characters, three different answers.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Being behind is good only when being behind changes the opponent's available responses in your favor.**

[Ultimate Frame Data: Link](https://ultimateframedata.com/link)  
[Ultimate Frame Data: Wii Fit Trainer](https://ultimateframedata.com/wii_fit_trainer)  
[SmashWiki: Wii Fit Trainer Back aerial](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Wii_Fit_Trainer_%28SSBU%29/Back_aerial)  
[Ultimate Frame Data: Mario](https://ultimateframedata.com/mario)  
[SmashWiki: Mario Up smash](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Mario_%28SSBU%29/Up_smash)

This changed the way I think about “taking the back.”

It is not a positional bonus.

It is **UI manipulation**.

You are rearranging the defender's buttons without touching their controller.

## 5. The fighting-game guests auto-face you — and that exception says everything

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Sometimes the exception explains the rule better than the rule.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In Ultimate, Ryu, Ken, Terry and Kazuya have a 1-on-1 facing mechanic that automatically turns them toward the remaining opponent in many grounded situations.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Street Fighter, Fatal Fury and Tekken characters enter Smash and receive a special system whose job is basically: **please keep looking at the other fighter.**
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
SmashWiki notes that this helps command inputs and responses to rolls or cross-ups, but it also makes facing-dependent tools such as Bair more awkward or niche.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
That trade-off is revealing. If facing direction were cosmetic, auto-turn would be cosmetic too. Instead it changes execution, defense and access to back-facing moves.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Facing direction is character design.** The guests need a special rule precisely because normal Smash does not manage it for you.

[SmashWiki: Fighter ability](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Fighter_ability)  
[SmashWiki: Reverse / auto-turnaround](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Reverse)

This is one of those tiny mechanics that feels like a design fossil.

To make traditional fighters feel traditional inside Smash, the game has to restore something Smash normally refuses to guarantee:

face your opponent.

## 6. Duck Hunt: orientation can decide between spacing and killing

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
For Duck Hunt, this is not abstract at all.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Ultimate Frame Data lists both Fair and Bair at frame 7. But SmashWiki describes Fair as a spacing, edgeguarding and combo tool, while Bair's sweetspot is Duck Hunt's strongest aerial and a real KO option.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Same startup. Same opponent standing horizontally beside you. Different facing direction.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
One jump can become “keep them away.” The other can become “take the stock.”
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
Add Can and it gets more interesting. Can can constrain the opponent's route first, while the body prepares the orientation needed for the likely answer.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
For Duck Hunt, projectile control is not only about where the opponent goes. It is also about **which side of your aerial toolkit should be waiting when they get there.**

[Ultimate Frame Data: Duck Hunt](https://ultimateframedata.com/duck_hunt)  
[SmashWiki: Duck Hunt (SSBU)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Duck_Hunt_%28SSBU%29)

Can forces jump.

You already know they like to jump over it.

Instead of waiting for the jump and then choosing an aerial, you can pre-build the state:

**stand or move with Bair already available.**

The read begins one beat earlier.

## 7. The real concept is option denial, not blind spots

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The phrase “take their back” sounds like a vision problem.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
In real combat, the back is dangerous because you cannot see or rotate your body as effectively.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In Smash, both players can still see the whole screen. Crossing behind someone does not hide you from the human being holding the controller.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The advantage comes from system asymmetry: shield grab may disappear, Fair and Bair are different, some Up Bs or Up Smashes cover one side better, and turning itself can require a state change.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
So getting behind is not about becoming invisible. It is about deleting some immediate answers and exposing others.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The better term is **option denial**.

Against Link, behind may remove the 7F front-only Up B.

Against Wii Fit Trainer, behind may activate an 8F Bair.

Against Mario, the back side is not a vacation destination.

The back is not a universal weak point.

It is a different dropdown menu for every character.

## 8. Ten-minute Facing Audit

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Facing direction disappears from your brain if you only learn it as theory.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
So practice the **switch**, not just the technique.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
RAR, cross-ups and out-of-shield differences all depend on the combination of position and orientation.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
A short lab can make that combination visible.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The goal is not to add more tech. It is to notice when the move list quietly changes.

### 2 min — Fair vs Bair

Write one sentence for each:

- Fair is for ______.
- Bair is for ______.

Check startup, landing lag, combo value and KO value.

### 2 min — RAR both directions

Ten reps left, ten reps right.

Do not only ask “did Bair come out?”

Check which direction you are facing **after landing**.

### 2 min — Shield, front vs back

Hit the same opponent's shield and land in front.

Then repeat and land behind.

Test shield grab, Up B, Up Smash, Fair and Bair.

### 2 min — Pick one anti-cross-up character

Choose someone like Wii Fit Trainer or Mario.

Build the habit that **behind is not automatically correct**.

### 2 min — One replay

Pause every time you touch shield.

Ask three questions:

**Where did I land? Which way were they facing? What answer did that orientation make available?**

For Duck Hunt, add one more:

**Can forced a jump. Did I want Fair positioning or Bair kill pressure before the jump even happened?**

## 9. Before the research, I thought this was a positioning article

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
I started with a simple feeling: Smash seems to care about getting behind more than other fighting games.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Research forced a correction. Street Fighter has cross-ups. Side-switching is old fighting-game technology.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
But once RAR, Bair, shield cross-ups, OOS directionality and auto-turning guests sit on the same page, a different pattern appears.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Most Smash fighters do not continuously auto-face the opponent. Fair and Bair are distinct. Landing behind can remove one punish and activate another. Four fighting-game guests literally receive auto-facing as a special mechanic.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the important thing is not the physical location called “behind.”
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
It is the fact that side and orientation change **the set of next actions**.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Smash is a game of distance, yes. It is also a game of **editing facing direction**.

That makes RAR look different.

It is not a funny way to run.

It is orientation management.

A cross-up is not simply “I got behind you.”

It is move-menu manipulation.

So next time you land on the other side of a shield, do not stop at:

**I got behind them.**

Ask:

**What disappeared from their move list? What became faster? And which way should I be facing when the next decision starts?**

In Smash, your back is part of your move list.

## Research Note

### Verified

- RAR is a standard way to retain forward momentum while turning around to access Bair.
- Bair is facing-dependent and is often a high-value aerial.
- Cross-ups can invalidate shield grab, but back-side OOS tools differ by character.
- Ultimate removed ordinary running through fighters, while some attacks can still cross up.
- Ryu, Ken, Terry and Kazuya have 1-on-1 auto-facing.
- Link, Wii Fit Trainer and Mario show clear front/back differences in defensive answers.
- Duck Hunt's Fair and Bair share frame-7 startup but serve different practical roles.

### Analysis proposed here

- “Facing direction = second move list”
- “Cross-up = defender UI rewrite”
- “Getting behind = option denial”
- Ten-minute Facing Audit
- Using projectile-induced movement to pre-select Duck Hunt's orientation
