# IMAGICA GROUPは、結局なに屋なのか
## From film processing to sports video and high-speed cameras

In 2025, a company called Mikaduki was created to take IMAGICA GROUP private.

その資本金は、5,000円だった。

Not 50 million yen. Five thousand yen.

Of course, this does **not** mean IMAGICA GROUP was bought for ¥5,000. The capital of the tender-offer vehicle is different from the funding used for the transaction. 公開資料では、Mikadukiは2025年3月に設立され、IMAGICA GROUP社長の長瀬俊二郎が100％保有する会社として、1株795円の公開買付けを実施した。

Still, the sentence looks strange.

A group with more than ¥100 billion in annual sales, 48 subsidiaries, Pokémon animation, J.League video operations, game production, medical imaging, and high-speed cameras went private through a company whose stated capital was ¥5,000.

この違和感から入ると、IMAGICA GROUPを“video production company”とだけ呼ぶことの弱さが見えてくる。

**A better working hypothesis is this: IMAGICA GROUP has spent 90 years expanding the processes that make images usable.**

現像する。Create. Edit. Localize. Distribute. Broadcast. Archive. Measure. Analyze.

The noun is “video.”  
The more useful unit of analysis is the **transformation process**.

> **Information date: 2026-10-07**
>
> This essay uses primary sources from IMAGICA GROUP, PHOTRON, J.League, and Tokyo Stock Exchange. 「映像を使える状態にする会社」は本稿の分析上の表現であり、会社の公式な自己定義ではない。

## 1. Pokémon and crash-test cameras are relatives inside the same corporate group

IMAGICA GROUP currently reports four business segments: コンテンツ事業, 映像制作技術事業, ゲーム事業, and 計測ソリューション事業. Its Japanese corporate overview lists consolidated sales of ¥104.337 billion for the fiscal year ended March 2026 and 48 subsidiaries.

On the entertainment side, OLM produces animation including Pokémon, while IMAGICA Entertainment Media Services handles finishing processes such as image processing, captions, and formats for broadcasting and streaming.

そのすぐ隣で、フォトロン（PHOTRON）はhigh-speed imaging systemsを自動車、航空宇宙、研究、産業計測へ展開している。Sportsではライブ映像の収録、伝送、cloud delivery, archiveまで扱う。

If we say, “They are all video businesses,” the explanation is technically correct and analytically weak.

What connects them more clearly is a sequence:

**raw material → transformation → usable output**

An animation cut becomes a broadcast-ready program. A film becomes localized content for another market. A live match becomes a distributable and searchable official video asset. A collision becomes measurable information.

映像は共通素材。でも、価値が生まれる場所は「映像そのもの」より、その映像が別の用途で使えるようになるconversion pointにある。

## 2. The company started as a middle process, not as a movie studio

IMAGICA GROUP traces its origin to 1935, when 極東現像所 was founded in Kyoto to process motion-picture film and make release prints.

This matters.

創業時から「作品を企画する会社」が中心だったわけではない。It took already-shot film and made it usable for projection.

That is a middle process.

Over time, the group expanded through acquisitions and integration: PHOTRON joined in 1992, ROBOT became part of the group through integration in 2006, IMAGICA Cosmo Space joined in 2013, OLM in 2016, Pixelogic in 2019, and AOS Technologies in 2025.

At first glance, this is a long M&A list.

But if you redraw the history as a workflow, another pattern appears.

Film processing expanded into editing. Editing expanded into localization and distribution. Distribution expanded into global streaming support. Live production expanded into sports operations and archives. Imaging expanded from entertainment into scientific measurement.

The company did not simply add more “video companies.”

**It repeatedly moved to another place where visual information changes state.**

## 3. Redraw the group by workflow, and 48 subsidiaries become less mysterious

公式セグメントとは別に、the group can be rearranged into five process layers.

**Create.** ROBOT, P.I.C.S., OLM, and IMAGICA GEEQ create films, ads, animation, game assets, and software.

**Finish.** IMAGICA Entertainment Media Services and IMAGICA Lab. edit images and sound, create visual effects, and turn material into finished content.

**Adapt and distribute.** Localization, dubbing, subtitles, format conversion, digital-cinema delivery, and Pixelogic's global media services make one work usable across languages, regions, theaters, and streaming platforms.

**Operate live and preserve.** IMAGICA Cosmo Space handles shooting and live production. PHOTRON's sports business covers transmission, cloud workflows, and archives.

**Measure.** High-speed cameras, medical imaging networks, and image-processing devices use images not mainly for entertainment but for professional decisions.

こうすると、エンタメと産業が別世界ではなくなる。

One side transforms images so people can **experience** them.  
The other transforms images so professionals can **decide** with them.

Different outputs, similar logic.

## 4. Sports is a miniature model of the entire group

J.League is especially useful for understanding the group.

Since 2017, J.League has produced and managed official video centrally and uses it not only for broadcasting and streaming, but also for analysis, data extraction, promotion, and social media.

A match is therefore not only a broadcast event.

It is an asset moving through a pipeline:

capture → production → transmission → monitoring → distribution → archive → retrieval → reuse.

2023年のIMAGICA GROUP記事では、イマジカ・ライヴ、IMAGICAコスモスペース、フォトロンの3社連携が紹介されていた。その後、イマジカ・ライヴは2024年4月にフォトロンへ吸収合併され、事業を承継した。

This matters in competitive research.

If you list “IMAGICA Cosmo Space” and “PHOTRON” as two independent competitors, you miss the group structure and the transfer of capabilities over time.

The better questions are:

Who captures?  
Who owns the operational responsibility?  
Who runs transmission and cloud workflows?  
Who archives?  
Which capabilities are inside the legal entity, and which are available through the group?

**In sports, the unit of competition is often the workflow, not the company name.**

## 5. Then the story breaks: more video work did not automatically mean a better business

A clean corporate-history story would end here: start with film processing, keep expanding the video value chain, become a broad visual-technology group.

Reality became messier.

For the fiscal year ended March 2025, IMAGICA GROUP reported ¥96.91 billion in sales and ¥2.479 billion in operating profit, but a ¥3.717 billion net loss attributable to owners of the parent. 海外の映像制作技術サービスの収益悪化、テレビ向けポストプロダクションの一部撤退、のれん減損などが重なった。

The MBO materials describe an even deeper problem.

The tender-offer party argued that contract production represented a large part of the portfolio and remained labor-intensive. It proposed expanding IP-based businesses and “network models,” considering large M&A and development investment even outside the video field, selling or integrating unprofitable businesses, and using AI to improve operational efficiency.

That changes the meaning of the MBO.

It was not only a delisting event.

**The group was asking whether owning more processes was still enough — or whether it now had to choose which processes could create scalable returns.**

## 6. After the MBO, buying and selling happened at the same time

The post-MBO moves are revealing.

In December 2025, PHOTRON acquired Switzerland-based AOS Technologies, strengthening high-speed imaging in automotive, aerospace, industrial, and defense markets.

In July 2026, PHOTRON transferred its broadcast-oriented video systems business to Hibino. That business had designed, built, installed, and supported systems for broadcasters and production companies.

Buy one imaging business. Sell another imaging business.

If we use “video” as the strategic category, the moves look inconsistent.

If we use **right to own a capability** as the category, they become more legible.

High-speed scientific imaging may gain more value inside PHOTRON because of shared technology, customers, sensors, and global distribution. Broadcast system integration may gain more value inside Hibino, which has broader professional audio-visual equipment capabilities.

これは本稿のinterpretationであり、会社が同じ言葉で説明しているわけではない。

But it suggests a more useful question for the next few years:

**Which process becomes more valuable because IMAGICA GROUP owns it?**

## 7. The core may be “transformation points,” not “all of video”

IMAGICA GROUP officially emphasizes creativity and technology. That is true, but too broad to function as a competitive hypothesis.

“Own the whole video value chain” is also too broad. The group does not own every camera, theater, broadcaster, or streaming platform, and it has already transferred some capabilities outside the group.

A sharper hypothesis is this:

**IMAGICA GROUP has repeatedly built businesses around transformation points.**

Film → projectable print.  
Raw footage → finished content.  
Japanese content → global localized versions.  
Live match → distributable and reusable official video.  
High-speed event → measurable data.

The transformation is where raw material becomes valuable to a different user or a different decision.

1935年の現像所も、まさにtransformation pointだった。

And this makes the MBO plan to invest outside video more interesting, not less.

If the next acquisition is simply “another video company,” the hypothesis is weak. If it adds a new way to transform, route, verify, or reuse complex information, the logic may travel beyond video.

調べる前、IMAGICA GROUPは「映像という言葉が広すぎて何屋か分からない会社」に見えた。

After the research, the mess looks more historical than accidental.

The meaning of “image” itself expanded—from film, to television, to streaming data, live sports infrastructure, and scientific measurement. The group expanded with it.

Now, after the 2025 MBO, the important question is changing again.

Not: **What kind of video company is IMAGICA GROUP?**

But:

**What processes does IMAGICA GROUP believe it must own next?**

The ¥5,000 company was a tiny legal box.

Inside that box sits a much larger experiment: redraw the boundaries of a 90-year-old business while it is still running.
