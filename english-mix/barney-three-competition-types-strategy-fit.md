# Sonyは一社でthree different gamesをしていた
## Wrong game, right strategy――それでも負ける

<!-- level:2 role:hook -->
「FCP」「RB V」「俊平型」。Read an auto-transcribed memo literally and management theory suddenly sounds like a mysterious combat sport. 調べ直すとFCPはSCP、RB VはRBV、俊平型はSchumpeterian competitionだった。
<!-- level:4 role:question -->
But fixing transcription errors leaves a stranger question. Why do theories of competitive strategy look so different? Porterのようにindustry structureを見るべきなのか、Barneyのようにfirm resourcesを見るべきなのか、それともhigh uncertaintyではboth become unreliableなのか。
<!-- level:2 role:example -->
In his 2015 book *ビジネススクールでは学べない世界最先端の経営学*, Akie Iriyama used Sony as an intriguing case. 当時のSonyはsmartphones and games、televisions and other electronics、financial servicesという性質の違う事業を抱え、一社の中でdifferent “types of competition”を同時にmanageする難しさを持っていた。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
If that view is right, asking for “Sony’s competitive strategy” in the singular is already a little too rough. 同じ会社が、one businessではfootball、anotherではshogi、さらに別の場所ではthe rules themselves keep changingという状態に近いからだ。
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
Before choosing a strategy, you need to know what game you are playing. 今回の調査で見えてきたのは、famous SCP-versus-RBV debateより一段手前にある、このdiagnosisの方が重要だということだった。

[入山章栄『ビジネススクールでは学べない世界最先端の経営学』日経BP、2015](https://bookplus.nikkei.com/atcl/catalog/15/244360/)

## 1. “Barney the RBV scholar” classified three competitions before his famous RBV paper

<!-- level:4 role:question -->
Jay BarneyといえばRBV、と覚えるとthe chronology becomes surprisingly interesting.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
His famous paper “Firm Resources and Sustained Competitive Advantage” appeared in 1991. そこではfirms hold heterogeneous resources and those differences can persistという前提から、value, rareness, imitability, and substitutabilityを検討した。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Five years earlier, however, Barney had already published “Types of Competition and the Theory of Strategy” in the *Academy of Management Review*. そこで整理したのがIndustrial Organization competition、Chamberlinian competition、Schumpeterian competitionのthree conceptsだった。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The important point is that these were not presented as mutually exclusive boxes. Barneyの目的は、different microeconomic traditionsから生まれたcompetition conceptsをone frameworkに置き、firmsがover timeどんなcompetitive forcesに直面するかを統合して説明することだった。
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
So before turning strategy into “Porter versus Barney,” Barney himself was saying that competition has different faces with different strategic implications. 対立して見えるtheories may sometimes be describing different games.

[Jay B. Barney, “Types of Competition and the Theory of Strategy: Toward an Integrative Framework,” Academy of Management Review, 1986](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amr.1986.4283938)

[Jay B. Barney, “Firm Resources and Sustained Competitive Advantage,” Journal of Management, 1991](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/014920639101700108)

## 2. IO competition――強いplayerになる前に、profitable fieldをつくる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Under Industrial Organization competition, the main explanatory weight shifts from “what is inside this firm?” toward “what kind of industry structure is this firm in?”
<!-- level:3 role:context -->
SCP in this tradition means Structure-Conduct-Performance: market structure shapes firm conduct, which influences performance. Strategy研究はこのlogicをfirm sideから読み替え、which market to enter and how to build barriers or positions that reduce destructive rivalryを考えてきた。
<!-- level:2 role:example -->
Michael Porter’s Five Forces is an easy practical example. 新規参入、substitutes、buyer power、supplier power、rivalryを通じて、how economic value is divided within an industryを見る。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The idea is less “become the strongest fighter” and more “build or occupy a place where constant fighting does not erase profit.” ただしSCP and Porter are not perfect synonyms; Porter also treats relative positioning and distinctive activities as central to strategy.
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
The IO question comes before capability building: **have you designed a game board where profit is hard to compete away?** One way to win competition is to alter the intensity and structure of competition itself.

[Harvard Business School, “The Five Forces”](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-five-forces.aspx)

## 3. Chamberlinian competition――same marketでもfirms are not the same

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
In Chamberlinian competition, differences among firms inside the same market move to the center.
<!-- level:3 role:context -->
Drawing on Edward Chamberlin’s tradition of monopolistic competition, Barney treated firms as potentially possessing unique skills, knowledge, reputation, and organizational capabilities. 競合が簡単にcopyできないdifferencesがあれば、same industry structureでもperformance can diverge.
<!-- level:2 role:example -->
This is where RBV fits naturally. Brand、technology、people、organizational routines、customer relationshipsのようなresourcesを磨き、“what only we can do”を厚くしていく。
<!-- level:2 role:example -->
Iriyama describes settings such as Japan’s auto industry and the earlier electronics industry as relatively Chamberlinian: many firms differentiated themselves and refined operations in a comparatively stable environment. 競争しながらquality and executionを研ぎ澄ます「切磋琢磨」がstrengthになりやすい。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
But there is a quiet assumption underneath: what you improve today must still be valuable tomorrow. Rulesが大きく変わらないならlearning curves and accumulated resourcesは強い。If the rules collapse, yesterday’s carefully polished strength can become tomorrow’s baggage.
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
The advantage of Chamberlinian competition is not magic profitability amid fierce rivalry. It is the ability to accumulate firm-specific differences while the competitive game stays relatively stable. そして、そのstabilityが崩れる地点からnext problemが始まる。

[入山章栄「日本企業はグローバルで勝ち残るために『シュンペーター型』の競争を学べ」DIAMONDハーバード・ビジネス・レビュー、2024](https://dhbr.diamond.jp/articles/-/10226)

## 4. Schumpeterian competition――rivalryが激しいのではなく、rules move

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If Schumpeterian competition is reduced to “a brutally competitive market,” the most important point disappears.
<!-- level:3 role:context -->
Barney emphasized uncertainty caused by shifts in technology, markets, and demand. Schumpeterian change can reorder not only who is ahead but **which resources are valuable at all**.
<!-- level:2 role:extreme -->
Try an extreme thought experiment. If customer needs, technology, and distribution barely change for ten years, steadily building position and capability makes sense. でもtomorrow the whole product category might become unnecessaryなら、today’s optimizationをten years続けるplan can become dangerous.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Barney’s 1986 paper argues that such revolutions cannot be perfectly anticipated and that firms may partly benefit from luck when resources they already possess suddenly become valuable. これは“great managers accurately predict the future”というcomfortable storyを少し壊す。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So conventional strategy does not become meaningless. Rather, large commitments built on precise forecasts become less trustworthy. Strategyの仕事はone futureを当てることから、multiple futuresに対応できるroomを残すことへずれていく。
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
You are no longer competing only on product quality or efficiency. Learning speed, ease of changing direction, and limited downside per failed experiment also become capabilities. Game skillだけでなく、gameが変わった瞬間に持ち替えられるhandsの多さが問われる。

[Jay B. Barney, “Types of Competition and the Theory of Strategy: Toward an Integrative Framework,” Academy of Management Review, 1986](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amr.1986.4283938)

## 5. Real options――“start small”より広い、the right to wait and abandonまで含む

<!-- level:4 role:question -->
If the future cannot be forecast well, what can a firm do besides freezing and saying “we don’t know”?
<!-- level:3 role:context -->
Iriyama connects Schumpeterian competition with real-options thinking. 実物投資をall-in nowのsingle decisionではなく、future informationを見ながらnext actionを選べるrightとして捉える。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Robert Pindyck highlighted two features of investment: irreversibility and the possibility of delay. 一度使うとrecoverしにくいcapitalがある一方、new informationを待ってcommitmentを遅らせられるなら、the option to wait itself can have value.
<!-- level:2 role:example -->
In management practice, this can become small experiments, staged investment, limited geographic launches, follow-on investment, expansion, or abandonment. Rita McGrath extended real-options logic to technology positioning and argued that uncertainty and boundary conditions affect both option value and exercise timing.
<!-- level:3 role:correction -->
Re-research changed one part of my initial understanding: **real options is not simply “start tiny and move fast.”** 少額でenterするのが合理的な場合もあれば、wait、stop midway、or commit big only when conditions improveが合理的な場合もある。
<!-- level:4 role:development -->
The essence is not smallness but reversibility and preserved choice. 最初のdecisionをfuture selfがchangeできる形にして、informationが増えたときにoptionをexerciseする。
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
In Schumpeterian competition, perhaps the scarce skill is not predicting the future. It is building a system where **being wrong is survivable, while being right can still be scaled aggressively.**

[Robert S. Pindyck, “Irreversibility, Uncertainty, and Investment,” NBER Working Paper, 1990](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3307)

[Rita Gunther McGrath, “A Real Options Logic for Initiating Technology Positioning Investments,” Academy of Management Review, 1997](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/AMR.1997.9711022113)

## 6. Sony in 2015――one company, three clocks

<!-- level:4 role:question -->
Bring the three types into a corporate portfolio and the management problem suddenly becomes concrete.
<!-- level:2 role:example -->
In Iriyama’s 2015 discussion, Sony was described as holding businesses close to Schumpeterian competition such as smartphones and games, electronics such as television whose competitive structure differed by geography, and financial services. これはcurrent Sonyのclassificationではなく、a historical snapshot as of 2015である。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The issue is not simply having many businesses. Different competition types imply different clocks. Stable markets reward capability accumulation and continuous improvement; uncertain businesses need experiments and exit options; IO-like businesses may place more weight on scale, barriers, standards, and bargaining power.
<!-- level:2 role:experiment -->
Now run a deliberately bad experiment: manage every business with the same KPIs, the same payback horizon, and the same approval speed. Management looks beautifully standardized, but one business will find it too slow while another finds it recklessly impatient. 同じclockを全員に配った瞬間に、strategy-environment mismatchが生まれる。
<!-- level:3 role:update -->
Following the story forward also shows that corporate boundaries move. Sony executed a partial spin-off of its financial services business in October 2025, distributing more than 80% of SFGI shares to Sony shareholders. Its 2026 Corporate Strategy explicitly emphasizes entertainment, IP, and creation technology.
<!-- level:3 role:caution -->
That does **not** prove Sony has reduced itself to one type of competition. 現在の各businessにもmultiple competitive forcesはある。What we can say is narrower: treating the 2015 portfolio as present-day Sony would be wrong, and the portfolio itself has been reconfigured over time.
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
“Sony” is too coarse a unit for diagnosing competition. You may need to cut by business, geography, customer, technology generation, and date. そうしないとsame company nameの下にdifferent gamesを混ぜてしまう。

[Sony Group, “The Partial Spin-off,” 2025](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/IR/library/SFG_pso/)

[Sony Group, “Sony Group Corporate Strategy 2026,” 2026](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/News/Press/202605/26-012E/)

## 7. Don’t pick one type――score all three pressures at once

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
After learning the framework, the natural urge is to ask “Which of the three are we?” But forcing the firm back into one box loses Barney’s integrative point.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
The 1986 paper allows a firm to face all three forms of competition at a given time. Schumpeterian change can create a new market or technology, after which industry structure hardens and firm-specific resource differences accumulate. The types can coexist and can also unfold over time.
<!-- level:3 role:method -->
A more useful move is to score each pressure separately from 0 to 5. IO pressure: entry barriers, scale, network effects, buyer and supplier power. Chamberlinian pressure: differences in proprietary resources, brand, knowledge, and organizational capability. Schumpeterian pressure: unpredictable shifts in technology, customer demand, business models, and regulation.
<!-- level:2 role:experiment -->
Then run a “ten-year freeze test.” Would the current strategy still be strong if technology and customer behavior barely moved for a decade? 次に“一夜消失テスト”。If today’s core technology or channel lost half its value overnight, what would you keep, stop, or test with a small option? 並べると、which parts depend on stabilityが見えやすい。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The diagnosis changes which theory deserves more weight. Strong IO pressure points toward industry structure and positioning; strong Chamberlinian pressure toward resources and inimitability; strong Schumpeterian pressure toward learning speed, reversibility, exploration, and dynamic reconfiguration.
<!-- level:4 role:development -->
And do not freeze the score. Update it after major technological changes or at a regular cadence. 競争の型はcorporate personality testではなく、environmentとのrelationshipを測るweather observationに近い。
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
The order of using management theory should be: **diagnose the competitive mechanisms → call the theories that fit → call different theories when the environment changes.** “Pick a favorite theory and force reality into it” is backwards.

[Jay B. Barney, “Types of Competition and the Theory of Strategy: Toward an Integrative Framework,” Academy of Management Review, 1986](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amr.1986.4283938)

## 8. Theory was not a weapon――it was a lens for seeing the rules of the game

<!-- level:4 role:recap -->
At the start, I wanted to line up SCP, RBV, and real options and ask, “Which one should I use?”
<!-- level:3 role:reflection -->
Going back to the original papers reversed the question. Barney’s 1986 article was less interested in declaring a theoretical winner than in integrating different concepts of competition and asking what competitive forces firms face over time.
<!-- level:2 role:recap -->
IO competition asks about the structure of the game board. Chamberlinian competition asks about differences among players. Schumpeterian competition asks whether both the board and the value of players’ strengths can be rewritten. Real options offers a way to preserve flexibility when the last condition becomes important.
<!-- level:3 role:reflection -->
That also changes how the 2015 Sony example looks. It is not merely “diversification is hard.” The harder problem is whether one management system can govern businesses playing under different rules, clocks, and uncertainty levels.
<!-- level:5 role:conclusion -->
Before researching this, competitive strategy looked like the study of **how to win**. Afterward, its first job looks quieter and harder: **do not misidentify the competition you are actually in, and design management on the assumption that the competition itself can change.** A correct strategy in the wrong game is still wrong.

[Harvard Business School, “Strategy Explained”](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/Pages/strategy-explained.aspx)

## Appendix: a prompt for diagnosing your type of competition

<!-- level:4 role:tool -->
Use the following prompt not to force a business into one category, but to observe three competitive pressures separately.
<!-- level:2 role:prompt -->
> 対象事業を、①Industrial Organization pressure、②Chamberlinian pressure、③Schumpeterian pressureの三軸でそれぞれ0～5点評価してください。For each score, show evidence from entry barriers, scale, network effects and bargaining power; firm-specific resources, brand, knowledge and organizational capabilities; and uncertainty in technology, customer demand, business models and regulation. 一つのtypeへ無理に分類せず、business、geography、customer、time periodによる違いを分けてください。そのうえで、current strategy、KPI、payback period、decision speed、exit criteriaがcompetition typeにfitしているかを点検し、SCP／Porter、RBV、real options、必要に応じてdynamic capabilities等のどのtheoryを補助線に使うべきか提案してください。最後に“ten-year freeze test”と“overnight disappearance test”を行い、current strengthsがvalueを失う条件と、そのとき残るoptionsを示してください。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The key is not to make a theory name the answer. 理論はdiagnosisを説明し、next actionを考えるための補助線に置く。
<!-- level:5 role:insight -->
Learning the types of competition is not about choosing one correct book from the management shelf. It is about noticing when the map you should be reading has changed.

[入山章栄「リアル・オプション理論」ダイヤモンド・オンライン、2020](https://diamond.jp/articles/-/256810)
