# ロックマンは、2%で先手を買い続ける
## Win the turn with lemons, then cash out with Bair and Mega Upper

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If you want to make Mega Man feel one-sided, the obvious instinct is to search for a huge combo.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
But his central projectile, the Mega Buster pellet — the famous “lemon” — deals 2%. It looks less like a weapon and more like a vitamin supplement.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Ultimate Frame Data lists the first pellet at frame 7, with up to three shots. SmashWiki notes that Mega Man can walk or jump during the sequence, seamlessly turning the same action into forward tilt or neutral air.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Shoot. Walk. Shoot. Jump. Shoot. The opponent stops; you move. They jump; you watch the landing. They shield; you gain time for another layer.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
The 2% is not the real transaction. **The opponent has already spent an input answering your question.**
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Mega Man's initiative is not “hit first.” It is **make them answer first.**

[Ultimate Frame Data — Mega Man](https://ultimateframedata.com/mega_man)  
[SmashWiki — Mega Man Neutral Attack](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Mega_Man_%28SSBU%29/Neutral_attack)

Two percent.

Cheap.

But whoever sets the agenda usually has more power than whoever speaks louder.

Mega Man is a tiny blue meeting chair.

## 1. The first move is a question, not a hit

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
普通の格闘キャラの感覚だと、攻めの始点は「何を当てるか」になりやすい。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Approach, connect, convert. Straightforward.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
SmashWiki describes Metal Blade and Crash Bomber as tools that provoke predictable reactions: Metal Blade can be thrown in eight directions and creates useful hitstun, while Crash Bomber often makes the opponent shield, approach or otherwise respond to the delayed explosion.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So Mega Man is not merely stopping movement. He is biasing **what the opponent wants to do next**.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
Once your goal changes from “land projectile” to “remove one comfortable answer,” pellets become much more important.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Initiative means placing a problem before the opponent gets to start their preferred game.**

[SmashWiki — Mega Man (SSBU)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Mega_Man_%28SSBU%29)  
[SmashWiki — Crash Bomber](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Crash_Bomber)

A missed pellet can still work.

Jump came out.

Shield came out.

Dash stopped.

Reflector appeared.

You did not get 2%.

You got data.

Very corporate.

Very Mega Man.

## 2. “One-sided” does not mean a ten-hit true combo

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
一方的という言葉は、長いcomboを想像させる。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
But in Smash, the nastier experience is often not “I was comboed.” It is “I never got to restart neutral.”
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Lemon makes them jump. Uair meets the landing lane. They drift away. Metal Blade is already there. They shield. You grab. Now they are at the ledge. They jump from ledge. Bair is waiting.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
None of that is universally guaranteed. DI, air dodge, character size, percent and stage all matter. What is reliable is that Mega Buster can continue through movement, while Metal Blade and Crash Bomber can narrow the opponent's preferred reactions.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the target is not an infinite combo.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The target is **a long stretch where the opponent never gets a clean, comfortable neutral start.**

確定していない。

でも自由でもない。

That middle state is where Mega Man lives.

## 3. Pellets buy movement time

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Calling Mega Man “a projectile character” misses the most useful part.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Mega Buster can fire up to three times, transition into walking forward tilt, and continue into neutral air after a jump. Prima's guide specifically discusses moving through pellet patterns and using the opponent's expectation of another shot to create a dash grab.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
You are not shooting, then moving.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
You are moving **inside the shooting action**.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
The opponent has to process a hitbox while your body changes position. That means the pellet is buying time for your next location.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Mega Man keeps initiative by **advancing attack geometry and body position at the same time.**

[Prima Games — Mega Man Overview](https://primagames.com/eguides/prima-games-8-eguide/mega-man/overview)  
[Prima Games — Mega Man Brawler Arsenal](https://primagames.com/eguides/prima-games-8-eguide/mega-man/brawler-arsenal)

Three simple modes are enough:

- **Stationary lemon** — scout the first response.
- **Walking lemon** — take stage while they retreat.
- **Short-hop lemon** — avoid grounded approach while testing jump / shield.

Do not ask, “Did the pellet hit?”

Ask, **“What did the pellet make them do?”**

## 4. Shield is not the end of your offense; it is a scheduled grab window

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Projectile players often see shield and think: more projectiles.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
But shield also freezes the defender's movement.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
SmashWiki discusses Metal Blade and Crash Bomber as shield-baiting tools. Prima describes the same pattern with pellets: if the opponent waits for another shot behind shield, Mega Man can convert that expectation into a dash grab.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So “they blocked it” is not automatically failure.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
The problem begins when you ask the **same** question again and again. Pellet → pellet → pellet allows one answer forever.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
To keep initiative, **change the type of question as soon as you see the answer.**

Shield → grab.  
Jump → Uair / Bair lane.  
Rush → projectile wall / close-range check.  
Wait → take stage.

This is not mystical mind-reading.

It is a survey with adaptive branching.

Mega Man is Qualtrics with an arm cannon.

## 5. Crash Bomber is a deadline, not just an explosion

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Crash Bomber becomes much stronger when you stop thinking about its damage first.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
SmashWiki explains that a latched bomb forces reactions such as shielding or moving toward Mega Man. Player analysis from TERA4576 similarly divides common responses into waiting in shield, attacking anyway, or approaching to transfer the bomb.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The explosion has not happened yet, but the opponent is already making plans around it.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
For a short period, Mega Man controls two time layers: what he is doing now and what the bomb will do later.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
That means the real value of Crash Bomber is not only damage. It puts a **deadline** into the opponent's decision process.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Mega Man can make pressure feel one-sided by stacking **decision deadlines**, not merely hitboxes.

[SmashWiki — Crash Bomber](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Crash_Bomber)  
[TERA4576 — Crash Bomber analysis](https://note.com/tera4576/n/nb5d005e45927)

相手の頭だけにタイマーが出る。

You do not see it.

They feel it.

Excellent user experience. For you.

## 6. Metal Blade changes jobs near kill percent

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
At some point Mega Man stops being “a zoner with a saw blade.”
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Metal Blade can be thrown in eight directions, stick into surfaces, and be picked up as an item. SmashWiki notes that item-dropped Metal Blade can create multiple hits and followups.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
遠距離ではmovement control。手に持つと、Z-dropやitem tossから近距離のconfirm starterになる。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
SmashWiki's KO combo page lists **thrown Metal Blade → Up Tilt** and **Back Air hits 1–2 → Up Tilt** as Mega Man kill confirms.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Metal Blade is not the kill. It is the hitstun that makes the kill button less reckless.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
The same tiny-hit philosophy has evolved: early game it forces responses; kill percent it guarantees access to a large reward.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Mega Man does not suddenly fish for power moves. He converts his existing initiative into power.**

[SmashWiki — Mega Man (SSBU)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Mega_Man_%28SSBU%29)  
[SmashWiki — KO combo](https://www.ssbwiki.com/KO_combo)

Throw saw.

Confirm.

Uppercut.

Factory safety officer has left the building.

## 7. Bair is the default closer; Up Tilt is the red button

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
撃墜帯で一番危険なのは、「そろそろ倒せるから大技を増やそう」と思うこと。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Mega Man's Bair starts on frame 4 — his fastest aerial — and Ultimate Frame Data lists it as frame 7 out of shield. SmashWiki describes its knockback and edgeguarding KO value.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Fast, horizontal, usable offstage. It keeps the same philosophy as neutral: touch the opponent before they start the preferred action.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Mega Upper, the Up Tilt, starts on frame 6, has full-body intangibility on frames 5–7, and deals 17% on the clean hit. Its reward is enormous, but its ending lag is also enormous.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Bair is “keep the initiative and kill.” Up Tilt is “I have a confirm; now collect everything.”
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
Calling both “kill moves” hides the strategic difference.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Bair is normal business. Up Tilt is the emergency red button. Do not press it because it is red.**

[Ultimate Frame Data — Mega Man](https://ultimateframedata.com/mega_man)  
[SmashWiki — Mega Man (SSBU)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Mega_Man_%28SSBU%29)

Practical order:

1. **Bair** — default horizontal finisher, edgeguard, fast OOS threat.
2. **Up Tilt** — only after Metal Blade, partial Bair, landing / dodge read, or another strong confirmation.
3. **Fair** — solid edge finisher, frame 9 with 11 frames landing lag.
4. **Uair** — punish escape above you.
5. **Fsmash** — long-range line for recovery and hesitation, not the entire kill plan.

The giant glowing shot looks like the main character.

It is marketing.

Bair is operations.

## 8. The funny surprise: Fsmash is not the whole ending

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Mega Man's Forward Smash looks like the obvious final boss of his moveset.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
SmashWiki notes its extreme range — among Forward Smashes, fully charged it reaches unusually far — but also describes its knockback as relatively weak for the category.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Visually: enormous Charge Shot. Emotionally: surely this is the kill button.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Yet consistent stocks often come from a frame-4 Bair, frame-6 Up Tilt, Fair near the ledge, Uair above, or an edgeguard where the opponent's route is already limited.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
Fsmash is better understood as a long deadline line across space, not a universal execution tool.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Mega Man does not chip with big guns and finish with small ones. He controls with tiny pellets, then becomes a close-range knife for one moment.**

[SmashWiki — Mega Man (SSBU)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Mega_Man_%28SSBU%29)

Big projectile: supporting actor.

Two-percent lemon: director.

Poster design has betrayed us.

## 9. At ledge, do not guess everything — remove the easiest answer

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The final destination of repeated initiative is not “more damage.” It is the ledge.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
At ledge the opponent already has fewer comfortable choices. Swinging a huge punish early can give that advantage back.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Use pellets to alter timing. Place Metal Blade. Let Crash Bomber create a deadline. If jump becomes common, meet it with Bair or Uair. If recovery is linear, long-range Fsmash or Dair becomes relevant.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
General ledgetrapping principles also favor keeping stage control, covering multiple common exits from a stable position, then adapting to repeated habits instead of gambling everything on one hard read.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
The purpose is not to “cover all options.”
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
It is to **never return the right to start neutral for free.**

[TrueCombo — Ledgetrapping guide](https://www.truecombo.net/guides/beginner-ledgetrapping)  
[SmashWiki — Edgeguarding](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Edge-guard)

If you Fsmash five times and hit once, memory stores the hit.

The four free escapes are quietly expensed.

Audit your brain.

## 10. The five-step initiative loop

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
技名を全部覚えるより、役割を五つにした方が試合中に使える。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This is not a fixed combo recipe. It is a decision loop.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Remember only this: **when they answer, move to the next question.**

### 1. ASK — Pellet first

Read: shield / jump / rush / wait.

### 2. NARROW — Metal Blade or Crash Bomber

Use angle or timing to remove comfortable choices.

### 3. TAKE SPACE

This is critical.

If the opponent is busy answering a projectile and Mega Man stays in the same place, you bought time and forgot to use it.

### 4. KEEP THEM BUSY

Shield → grab  
Jump → Uair / Bair  
Horizontal retreat → pellet / Metal Blade  
Ledge → preserve ledgetrap

Not universal truths. A starter map.

### 5. CASH OUT

Usually Bair.

Confirmed close range: Metal Blade → Up Tilt or partial Bair → Up Tilt.

Different geometry: Fair / Uair / Fsmash.

**Do not redesign your entire neutral at kill percent. Change the reward.**

## 11. Fifteen-minute practice: train initiative, not combo vanity

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Mega Man practice is hard because “the projectile hit” is not enough as a success metric.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
A pellet can connect while you retreat all the way to the corner. Damage succeeded; position failed.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A pellet can miss, force a jump, let you take center, and create an Uair landing trap. Damage failed; initiative succeeded.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So practice should measure the **next state**.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Train whether your question arrives before their freedom does.

### 3 min — Move during pellets

- one shot → walk
- one shot → short hop
- two shots → jump
- air shot → land and continue

Goal: never freeze the body because the Buster is working.

### 3 min — Four responses

Create one response for:

- shield
- jump
- dash in
- wait

One answer each. Keep it simple.

### 3 min — Metal Blade cash-out

- toss → Bair
- Z-drop → Bair
- Metal Blade → Up Tilt
- Bair hits 1–2 → Up Tilt

Confirm percent / DI / escape conditions in Training Mode.

### 3 min — No early Fsmash at ledge

30 seconds each:

- Bair only
- Fair only
- Uair jump catch
- Fsmash allowed only at the end

Learn what appears when you stop demanding the cinematic ending.

### 3 min — Replay “free-time audit”

Find three moments when the opponent got to start a preferred action with no immediate constraint.

What happened before?

- pellets stopped?
- you failed to take space?
- repeated the same answer?
- kill greed created a whiff?

## 13. I started with “spam more projectiles.” I ended with turn control

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
最初の問いは「どうすればロックマンで相手より先に動き続け、一方的に殴って、最後にバーストできるか」だった。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
At first, the answer looked like projectile volume.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Then the numbers line up differently: 2% pellet. Frame-4 Bair. Frame-6, 17% clean Up Tilt. Metal Blade links into the kill layer.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Mega Buster moves with walking and jumping. Crash Bomber places a future deadline. Metal Blade becomes an item and a confirm starter.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Low-power moves and high-power moves are not competing for the same job. Small hits reduce freedom; big hits enter after freedom is reduced.
<!-- level:4 role:analysis -->
That is why “one-sided pressure” and “clean kill” are not separate topics.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Mega Man does not kill with projectiles. He controls the turn order with small projectiles, then makes exactly one hit matter a lot.**

So start smaller.

Ask one question.

See the answer.

Place the next problem.

Move forward while they solve it.

Bring them to the ledge.

Then finish the meeting with frame-4 Bair or frame-6 Mega Upper.

The chair has recognized no further speakers.

## Research Note

### Verified facts

- Mega Buster pellets deal 2%, begin at frame 7, and can fire up to three times.
- Mega Buster sequences can transition through walking and jumping into Forward Tilt / Neutral Air.
- Metal Blade travels in eight directions and can become a held item.
- Crash Bomber can force defensive or movement responses before its delayed explosion.
- Bair starts on frame 4 and is frame 7 out of shield.
- Up Tilt starts on frame 6, has full-body intangibility on frames 5–7, and deals 17% clean.
- SmashWiki lists Metal Blade → Up Tilt and Bair hits 1–2 → Up Tilt as KO confirms.
- Nintendo's Ver.13.0.1 balance adjustments changed Mega Man Dash Attack, Down Smash and Side Special. Ver.13.0.2 is documented as Sora amiibo compatibility rather than a balance patch.

### Analytical framework proposed here

- Initiative = make the opponent answer first.
- One-sided pressure = reduce clean neutral restart time, not create a fake “infinite combo.”
- Pellet = buy time.
- Crash Bomber = set a decision deadline.
- Metal Blade = bridge pressure into kill confirmation.
- Keep neutral philosophy at kill percent; change only the reward.

### Caution

Kill percentages and true-confirm windows vary by opponent weight, DI, rage, stale moves, stage position and platform geometry. Treat the routes as practice priorities, not a universal percent chart.
