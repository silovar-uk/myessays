# 体重減少は「side effect」なのか。マンジャロが変えた食欲の常識

From diabetes care to obesity treatment：作用、歴史、臨床試験、批判をたどる

Mounjaro's package insert has an odd entry: weight loss is listed as an adverse reaction. 痩せる作用で知られる薬なのに、weight lossがside effectにもなる。It sounds contradictory, but it captures something essential about medicine.

In Japan, Mounjaro is approved for type 2 diabetes, not simply for slimming. Weight loss can be a benefit for one patient and an unwanted effect for another. 薬のvalue depends on the patient and the treatment goal.

Three big questions: Why can a diabetes drug change appetite? What does the famous 20% weight-loss figure actually mean? And why has one medicine triggered debates about beauty standards and access to healthcare?

**Information date: October 8, 2026.** 本稿はclinical studies、PMDA、日本の行政通知、学会声明を照合した解説であり、individual medical adviceではない。

## Mounjaro and Zepbound: same molecule, different indications

The active ingredient is tirzepatide, developed by Eli Lilly. It is given as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection. 日本では2022年9月にtype 2 diabetesの治療薬として承認され、2023年4月に発売された。

Zepbound contains the same molecule but has separate approved indications in Japan: obesity disease, and since May 18, 2026, certain cases of moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea. 適応にはspecific clinical criteriaがあり、ordinary cosmetic weight lossとは異なる。

A molecule and its approved clinical use are different things. Calling Mounjaro a generally approved diet injection in Japan overlooks that distinction.

Sources: [PMDA Mounjaro](https://www.pmda.go.jp/PmdaSearch/rdDetail/iyaku/2499422G1024_1) ／ [Zepbound indication update](https://www.tanabe-pharma.com/ja/news/rel_260601_2.html)

## The gut sends signals to the pancreas and brain

When we eat, blood glucose rises and the pancreas releases insulin. But oral glucose triggers more insulin than intravenous glucose at comparable glucose levels. この発見はincretin effect（インクレチン効果）と呼ばれる。

The gut is not merely a food-processing tube. It acts as a signaling organ. Two important incretins are GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) and GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1). 血糖調節に関わり、GLP-1はgastric emptyingやappetite regulationにも関与する。

Tirzepatide is a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist. One molecule activates two receptors; it is not two medicines mixed together. 体重とblood glucoseの調節に作用するが、each pathway's exact contribution remains under study.

The implication is uncomfortable for simple willpower stories: appetite is biologically regulated. 行動の選択とhunger signalsは関係するが、identicalではない。

Sources: [Incretin history](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2696340/) ／ [PMDA](https://www.pmda.go.jp/PmdaSearch/rdDetail/iyaku/2499422G1024_1)

## Modern drugs emerged from decades of hormone research

The incretin concept dates to the 1930s. Early researchers lacked modern measurement tools. Evidence grew in the 1960s; GIP research advanced in the 1970s and GLP-1 in the 1980s. 科学上のhypothesisが薬になるまで、何十年もの時間があった。

A hormone is not automatically a usable drug. Researchers must extend its action, design delivery, and test efficacy and safety. Discovery and clinical application are separate steps.

Exenatide reached U.S. diabetes treatment in 2005, followed by drugs such as liraglutide and semaglutide. Tirzepatide extended that lineage through dual-receptor action. 米国ではMounjaroが2022年、Zepboundが2023年に承認された。

The breakthrough was not the first-ever drug affecting appetite. It was the way decades of gut-hormone research expanded from diabetes care into obesity treatment.

Sources: [History of incretins](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2696340/) ／ [FDA Zepbound approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-new-medication-chronic-weight-management)

## About 20% weight loss: impressive, but conditional

SURMOUNT-1, published in 2022, randomized 2,539 adults with obesity or overweight but without type 2 diabetes. Mean starting weight was 104.8kg; mean BMI was 38.0. このpopulationを忘れて数字だけ使うと、evidenceを読み違える。

After 72 weeks, mean weight loss was 15.0% (5mg), 19.5% (10mg), 20.9% (15mg), and 3.1% (placebo). But all groups received lifestyle counseling. The placebo-adjusted difference for 15mg was 17.8 percentage points. 20.9%をpure drug effectと呼ぶのは不正確だ。

In the Japanese SURMOUNT-J trial, mean weight changes at week 72 were −17.8% (10mg), −22.7% (15mg), and −1.7% (placebo). These are group averages, not individual promises.

SURMOUNT-5 directly compared tirzepatide with semaglutide: 20.2% versus 13.7% mean weight loss at 72 weeks. But it was an open-label trial in adults with obesity without diabetes. One head-to-head result is not a universal treatment ranking.

Clinical evidence needs context: population, duration, comparator, adherence, and adverse events. A percentage alone is not a complete definition of treatment success.

Sources: [SURMOUNT-1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2206038) ／ [SURMOUNT-J](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/en-latest-detail/jRCT2031210044) ／ [SURMOUNT-5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40353578/)

## Weight loss and weight maintenance are different problems

SURMOUNT-4 studied withdrawal after successful weight loss. Participants first used tirzepatide for 36 weeks and lost an average of 20.9%, then 670 were randomized to continue treatment or switch to placebo for another 52 weeks.

From week 36 to week 88, the continuation group lost another 5.5% on average, while the placebo-switch group gained 14.0%. The 14.0% is measured from week 36, not from original baseline.

The result highlights weight regain after stopping, but does not mean everyone regains the same amount—or that indefinite drug use suits everyone. Japan's optimal-use guidance also sets treatment-duration and lifestyle-care requirements.

The first question is how to lose weight; the next is how to maintain health over time. Cost, side effects, nutrition, activity, and rebound risk belong to the second question.

Sources: [SURMOUNT-4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078870/) ／ [MHLW guidance](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/web/t_doc?dataId=00td0100&dataType=1&pageNo=1)

## Not all lost weight is fat

Ten kilograms of weight loss is not ten kilograms of fat loss. A body-composition substudy of 160 participants found about 75% fat mass and 25% lean mass among weight lost with tirzepatide. The placebo group had broadly similar proportions.

Lean mass is not the same as muscle; it includes organs and water. So '25% muscle loss' is an incorrect interpretation. Especially for older or frail patients, nutrition and physical function matter.

The scale is easy to read, but the number is not the whole outcome. Health includes function, not only mass.

Source: [SURMOUNT-1 body-composition substudy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11965027/)

## Safety needs frequency, severity, and causation

Common gastrointestinal adverse events include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, and abdominal pain. Symptoms can be prominent during dose escalation, and dehydration is a concern if vomiting or diarrhea persists.

Japan's label also lists serious risks such as acute pancreatitis, biliary disease, ileus, and severe hypersensitivity. Hypoglycemia risk matters with insulin or certain diabetes medicines. Delayed gastric emptying can matter before anesthesia or deep sedation.

Animal studies have raised thyroid C-cell tumor concerns, but relevance to humans is uncertain. Japan's September 2026 label also notes NAION, an optic-nerve condition. Observational evidence suggests possible increased risk, not established causation.

Reported, associated, and causally proven are different claims. Good risk communication avoids both dismissing real warnings and overstating uncertain evidence.

Sources: [PMDA labeling](https://www.pmda.go.jp/PmdaSearch/rdDetail/iyaku/2499422G1024_1) ／ [2026 label revisions](https://www.pmda.go.jp/safety/info-services/drugs/calling-attention/revision-based-on-the-consultation/0001.html)

## Obesity, clinical obesity, and insurance are not synonyms

In Japan, BMI 25 or above is generally classified as obesity. But 肥満症 (obesity disease) involves clinical need and obesity-related health problems. Personal preference for weight loss is not enough for approved treatment.

For Zepbound's obesity indication, criteria include BMI 35+ or BMI 27+ with specified obesity-related health conditions. Insurance reimbursement has additional requirements regarding lifestyle treatment, nutrition counseling, and facilities.

Approved indication, off-label prescribing, and reimbursement are three different concepts. Off-label does not automatically mean illegal, but it also does not make the use equivalent to an approved standard indication.

Sources: [MHLW guidance](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/web/t_doc?dataId=00td0109&dataType=1&pageNo=1) ／ [PMDA Mounjaro](https://www.pmda.go.jp/PmdaSearch/rdDetail/iyaku/2499422G1024_1)

## Cosmetic use and clinically indicated treatment are different

The debate contains at least three issues: cosmetic use without clear medical need, inadequate risk counseling, and access for patients who need the drug. The Japan Diabetes Society updated its statement on off-label cosmetic use in November 2023.

It is also wrong to dismiss obesity treatment as 'avoiding effort.' Appetite and weight involve biology, genes, environment, and disease. We can support appropriate medical treatment while criticizing unhealthy social pressure to become thinner.

The real question is not whether weight loss is morally good or bad. It is who benefits medically, who bears risks, and whether access is fair. More powerful treatments require better systems of judgment.

Source: [Japan Diabetes Society position statement](https://www.jds.or.jp/modules/important/index.php?content_id=191)

## A side effect can also be a benefit

Return to the puzzle: why is weight loss listed as a side effect? Because a biological effect is not automatically a health benefit for every patient. The label is describing a real clinical distinction.

Tirzepatide makes the biology of appetite visible through treatment outcomes. But strong effects also expose difficult questions about regain, lean-mass loss, long-term safety, cost, and equitable access.

The story began with a weekly injection and a number on the scale. It ends with a much larger question: how the gut signals to the brain, why hunger feels the way it does, and how far medicine should intervene. The scale was only the entrance.

**The right question is not only how much weight is lost, but who benefits, how, and how that benefit can be maintained safely.**
