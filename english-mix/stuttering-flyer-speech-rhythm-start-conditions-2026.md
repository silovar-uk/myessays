# チラシ配布の一言が出ない――When Speech Has a Starting Line

*English Mix／Research date: October 26, 2026. 日本語正本の論点と出典を引き継いだ派生版。This article is not a diagnosis or individual medical advice.*

## 一番短い「こんにちは」が、一番遠いことがある

Here is a strange contradiction. You can talk with friends for an hour. You can improvise, jump from topic to topic, and even speak more easily when you get into a rhythm. Yet with a flyer in your hand, a tiny scripted “こんにちは” suddenly feels impossible to launch.

The word is simple. The task is simple. Or is it? **The problem may not be whether you know how to speak, but under what conditions speech has to begin.** ただの挨拶に見える瞬間には、the passerby, the timing, the expected response, and the risk of being ignoredが重なっている。

A small question opens a larger one: Is fluency a stable personal ability, or is it also something that changes with the situation?

## 守｜そもそも吃音は「言葉を知らない」状態ではない

Stuttering（吃音）は単なる語彙不足や考えの遅さではない。A person may know exactly what to say, yet experience repetitions（連発）, prolongations（伸発）, or blocks（難発）. 「こ、こ、こんにちは」「こーーんにちは」「……こんにちは」は、その違いを示す例だ。[日本吃音・流暢性障害学会](https://www.jssfd.org/kaisetsu.html)／[国立障害者リハビリテーションセンター](https://www.rehab.go.jp/ri/departj/kankaku/466/2-1/)

Not every hesitation is stuttering. Typical disfluencies happen to everyone. そして、一つの場面で詰まったという自己観察だけでは診断できない。[ASHA clinical guidance](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/fluency-disorders/)

Most persistent stuttering begins in childhood and involves interacting developmental and neurobiological factors. **Nervousness can affect speech without being the sole cause of stuttering.** 成人になって突然始まった、急に悪化した、神経症状を伴う場合は、医療機関で原因を評価することが重要だ。[NIDCD](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/stuttering)

## 守｜一人で話す、歌う、声を合わせる。吃音は場面によって変わる

A remarkable fact: many people who stutter speak more fluently when singing, talking to themselves, or speaking in unison with someone else. Public speaking and phone conversations, meanwhile, may make stuttering more noticeable. These differences are recognized by both Japanese specialists and the US National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders.[日本吃音・流暢性障害学会](https://www.jssfd.org/kaisetsu.html)／[NIDCD](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/stuttering)

Same speaker. Same vocal tract. Different conditions.

So fluent casual talk does not disprove stuttering, and difficulty with a presentation does not automatically mean “lack of confidence.” とはいえ、歌いやすさや斉読時の改善だけで吃音の診断がつくわけでもない。**Situational variability（場面による変動） is a clue, not a diagnosis.**

## 守｜チラシ配布は、「一言の仕事」なのに条件が多すぎる

Think about handing out flyers. Someone approaches. You have only a moment to decide when to speak. You have a fixed opening line. You may not know whether the person will listen, ignore you, or say no. And then you repeat the process dozens of times.

A short line comes with a surprisingly complex interface.

ここからはobservational hypotheses（観察上の仮説）。四つに分けてみる。Who controls the starting time? How fixed are the words? How predictable is the listener? And does “speak without getting stuck” become an invisible performance target?

![発話開始の自由度と定型性から見た場面の違い。英語混じり版でも日本語図を共有](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/stuttering-situations-map-2026.svg "Figure 1｜Original conceptual map. Not a graph of clinical severity or measured data.")

この図は、どの場面が必ず難しいかを示すものではない。It is a way to locate what changes between situations. Once you can describe the conditions, the issue is no longer simply “try harder.”

## 破｜リズムで話しやすくなる。しかし、「なぜ」かは意外と難しい

**Fluency-inducing conditions（流暢性が高まりやすい条件）** include choral speech（斉読）and metronome-paced speech（拍子に合わせた発話）. One hypothesis is that external timing cues help coordinate speech movements. Talking depends on planning sounds, moving the articulators, and monitoring what we hear.[ASHA's review of fluency-enhancing conditions](https://www.asha.org/siteassets/publications/monographs21.pdf)

In 2022, Garnett and colleagues used functional magnetic resonance imaging（機能的磁気共鳴画像法）to study solo and choral reading in adults who stutter and controls. Their findings suggested that coordination between auditory and speech-motor systems may support fluency. **But a brain image does not identify the cause of one individual's stuttering.**[Original study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2022.894676)

![吃音のある成人と対照群における、単独朗読・斉読時の脳活動差](https://www.frontiersin.org/files/Articles/894676/fnhum-16-894676-HTML/image_m/fnhum-16-894676-g001.jpg "Figure 2｜Garnett et al. (2022), Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, Figure 1, CC BY. Group-level imaging; not individual diagnosis.")

Then the neat explanation breaks. A 2025 study compared **20 adults who stutter and 20 controls** while reading alone and with another speaker. Choral reading changed a measure of dominant acoustic rhythm in **opposite directions**: upward for the stuttering group, downward for controls.

That offered only limited support for the idea that people become fluent simply by copying a fluent partner's rhythm.[Meekings et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1044/2024_JSLHR-24-00405)

![2022年の脳機能画像研究と2025年の音声リズム研究の解釈の違い](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/stuttering-rhythm-evidence-2026.svg "Figure 3｜Original research comparison. Rhythm can help without being the only explanatory mechanism.")

So when someone says “rhythm helps me,” that observation deserves respect. But **the effect is not yet a complete explanation of the mechanism**. External timing, auditory feedback, motor coordination, and social context may all matter.

## 破｜「次の一語で詰まるかも」と予期することにも、研究がある

Before the opening line, another event may occur: anticipation（発話予期）. You know which word is coming, and you suspect it might get stuck.

In a study of 30 adults who stutter, all reported anticipating stuttering at least sometimes, and **77%** reported doing so often or always. This is a result from a small research sample, not a statistic about everyone who stutters.[Jackson et al. (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26065618/)

A 2024 magnetoencephalography study（脳磁図研究）also found associations between brain activity involved in inhibitory control before speech and later stuttering. However, an association is not proof that anticipation causes every stuttering event.[Original study (2024)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38911458/)

Repeated experiences may build a loop: difficult moment → expectation → avoidance or effortful self-management → another high-stakes moment. 日本の国立障害者リハビリテーションセンターは、こうした二次的な対処行動と条件づけを説明している。[同センター](https://www.rehab.go.jp/ri/departj/kankaku/466/2-1/)

Yet anticipation is not always harmful. Some people use it constructively. The goal need not be “never anticipate.” It may be to make room for speaking even when the possibility of a block is present.

## 破｜「緊張するとどもる」と「緊張が吃音を生んだ」は違う

**A trigger is not necessarily a root cause.** 人前の発話で症状が強まることと、吃音自体がなぜ生じるのかは別の問いだ。Simply telling someone to relax can accidentally add another demand: “Now I must relax correctly, too.”[NIDCD](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/stuttering)／[慶應義塾大学病院](https://kompas.hosp.keio.ac.jp/disease/000987/)

Treatment can work on speech patterns, but it can also address the burden around speaking. A Japanese systematic review published in 2026 examined 24 studies of psychological interventions for adults who stutter. A synthesis of 13 studies involving 311 people found improvement in psychological symptoms. **That does not mean the same numerical effect on speech fluency, nor does a pre–post comparison automatically establish causality.**[灰谷・青木・飯村（2026）](https://doi.org/10.4992/jjpsy.97.24402)

ASHA also emphasizes communication effectiveness, willingness to speak, and quality of life rather than treating fluency as the only possible goal. What matters is not only whether a sound came out perfectly, but whether the person could participate and be heard.[Clinical guidance](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/fluency-disorders/)

## 離｜通行人への声かけを「声を出す勝負」から「伝える設計」に変える

What if we redesign the task? Instead of “say hello perfectly,” make the goal “deliver the useful information.”

- **Prepare more than one opening line.** 「こんにちは」「本日のご案内です」「よろしければどうぞ」など、自然に選べる候補を持つ。これは万能な治療法ではなく、個別に試す業務上の工夫。
- **Design meaningful pauses.** A short phrase, then a pause, then the next idea. 一律に遅く話すことを強いるのではなく、内容の区切りを明瞭にする。
- **Use another channel.** Let the flyer headline, a sign, or a visual cue carry part of the message. **Communication doesn't have to be audio-only.**
- **Consider optional self-disclosure（自己開示）.** 「言葉が詰まることがあるので、少し待ってください」という説明も選べる。But disclosure is a choice, never an obligation.

A 2026 systematic review covered 18 studies of stuttering self-disclosure. Fifteen reported generally favorable effects; where compared, non-apologetic explanations were often viewed more favorably. Context matters, and no one should be pressured to disclose.[Coalson et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfludis.2026.106200)

A change of design can shift the question from “How can I hide a moment of stuttering?” to “What makes the message accessible and the interaction manageable?”

## 離｜小さな「観察実験」を設計する。結果をでっち上げない

For a more ambitious approach, design a seven-day self-observation. Not a treatment trial, and not proof of causality—just a better map of personal experience. **No observations have been collected for this article.**

Compare manageable situations: spontaneous explanation alone, reading a prepared sentence, lightly using a rhythm cue, and saying a short scripted line at a simulated passerby's timing. Change the order and avoid pushing yourself into distress.

Record four things: context（場面）, the first-word experience（発話開始の感覚）, effort from 0 to 3（負担の自己評価）, and whether the message was communicated（伝達の成否）. Also note fatigue, familiarity, wording, and listener reactions as confounders（交絡要因）.

Success should not mean “zero stuttering.” You might get stuck and still finish a meaningful conversation. That is important data about participation. 観察記録は、必要に応じて言語聴覚士と話すための材料にもなる。[国立障害者リハビリテーションセンターの言語聴覚療法](https://www.rehab.go.jp/hospital/department/rehabilitation/st/)

## 離｜発話の問題から「仕事のインターフェース」へ

A phone lets you type, tap, dictate, or choose a preset response. The goal is communication; the pathway is flexible. Strangely, everyday work often insists on only one interface: say the right words, at the right moment, without interruption.

But a speaking environment can also change. A clearer sign, a shorter prompt, an understanding audience, or more control over when to begin may matter. これは吃音の神経学的原因を証明する話ではなく、**環境と身体を一緒に設計する**という社会的な発想だ。

Return to the flyer. At first, that missing “こんにちは” seemed like a failure to produce one simple word. After this research, it looks more like a crowded intersection of timing, expectation, motor control, and social meaning.

**The deeper question is not only “Can the person speak?” It is “Who gets to shape the conditions in which speech becomes possible?”**

## 調査の往復｜リサーチ→次の計画→再調査→構造化

First pass: check Japanese specialty societies and public-health sources for core symptoms and situational variability. Next question: does rhythm alone explain why choral speech helps? Re-research: compare the 2022 brain-imaging study with the 2025 rhythm study, then add research on anticipation, psychological interventions, and disclosure.

The resulting framework separates **speech characteristics, initiation conditions, anticipation, and communication outcomes**. The remaining uncertainty is personal: individual causes, optimal strategies, and the choice to disclose require personal observation and, if needed, professional assessment.
