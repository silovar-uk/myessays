# ゲームの脇役は、なぜ遊園地の主役になったのか――The NPC Fandom Economy

「推しのNPCに会うため、中国の遊園地へ行く」。At first glance, this sounds strange. NPCはゲームのnon-player character、つまり操作しない側の役柄。Yet a fan crosses borders to meet one. A background character has somehow become the main attraction.

The starting point is a [first-person trip report about Tianjin Happy Valley](https://note.com/kusya_receipt/n/n0dcc71a9d98c). The writer travels to see 胡瀚文（フー・ハンウェン） and describes the challenge of locating his appearances. This is direct evidence of one fan's motivation, not proof that all Chinese visitors behave this way.

This essay asks how a game term became a job in tourism, how interaction connects to fandom and social media, and whether the economics work. Verified facts, interpretation, and proposals are kept separate.

## 1. NPCは「自動で動く人」ではなく、来場者を物語に招き入れる出演者を指す

NPC stands for Non-Player Character, a character not controlled by the player. In Chinese tourism, 景区NPC means a live performer who plays a role and interacts with guests. Both [China Tourism News](https://www.ctnews.com.cn/paper/content/202510/02/content_108660.html) and [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/travel/20260529/920096ca005145ae88ec9d039247c677/c.html) explain this meaning.

There is a reversal here. In games, an NPC is not player-controlled. At a theme park, the performer responds to guests in real time. What seems to transfer is not automation but the idea of a character living inside a fictional world. This is the essay's interpretation.

Useful terms: 景区 (tourist attraction), 沉浸式 (immersive), 互动 (interactive exchange), 情绪价值 (emotional value), 出圈 (breakout beyond a niche), 网红 (internet celebrity), and IP (reusable intellectual property such as characters and stories). Meanings vary by context.

## 2. 1996年の「街を舞台にする演出」が、短動画の時代に別の商売へ変わった

The history predates the label. [China Tourism News](https://www.ctnews.com.cn/paper/content/202510/02/content_108660.html) points to Hangzhou Songcheng, opened in 1996, where actors populated a recreated Song-dynasty town. But this does not prove those performers were called NPCs in 1996. The modern term is partly retrospective.

Another turning point was shareable interaction. In 2019, the 不倒翁小姐姐 performer at Xi'an's Great Tang Mall became widely known through short videos of her reaching toward visitors. [China Newsweek](https://www.chinanews.com/sh/2025/07-28/10454974.shtml) reports the online attention and growing on-site interest. Different reports use different view and visitor-count measures; there is no single comparable number.

Later formats included improvised quizzes with historical figures, martial-arts challenges, and matchmaking performances such as 王婆说媒. As [China Tourism News](https://www.ctnews.com.cn/paper/content/202510/02/content_108660.html) describes, the performer no longer serves only as background. Guest actions can affect the experience.

It is not one inevitable timeline. Atmospheric actors, interactive guides, and celebrity-like performers coexist. The real design difference is what the visitor is invited to do.

## 3. 中国の景区NPCは、少なくとも四つの異なる商品に分けられる

Calling everyone an NPC hides different products and jobs. The following four types are an analytical classification, not an official industry standard.

- **Atmosphere**: officials and merchants who make the world feel inhabited.
- **Participation**: quests, quizzes, and challenges that assign guests a role.
- **Improvisation**: one-off exchanges shaped by the visitor's response.
- **Personal fandom**: a specific performer's appearance becomes the reason to visit.

One performer can combine several types. The point is not how many attractive actors are hired. Staffing, scripts, queues, communication, and KPIs depend on whether the product is atmosphere, participation, or meeting a person.

## 4. 天津歓楽谷の遠征記は、「施設目的の旅行」から「人物目的の旅行」への転換を映す

In the [Tianjin trip report](https://note.com/kusya_receipt/n/n0dcc71a9d98c), the central uncertainty is not the roller-coaster queue. It is whether the fan can find the performer they came to see. The itinerary becomes person-centered rather than ride-centered.

The wider marketing context is visible in a [2025 Tianjin Happy Valley event guide](https://tj.bendibao.com/xiuxian/2025623/158013.shtm), which advertises online celebrities, performers, and high-appeal NPC casts. The guide does not verify the individual artist's 2026 schedule, contract, or contribution to attendance.

[China Tourism News](https://www.ctnews.com.cn/paper/content/202510/02/content_108660.html) also reports parks using influencer-performers to attract audiences. But a famous performer and a durable park-owned experience are different assets. Filling the park today is not the same as creating a reason to return after the performer leaves.

## 5. NPC経済は「入場券だけの商売」ではないが、売上経路を混同してはいけない

Potential revenue paths include admission, food and merchandise during longer stays, repeat visits, special events, character goods, and off-site work or livestreams. Revenue ownership differs among the park, performer, production company, and rights holder. These are possible channels, not proof that every park earns from all of them.

Ten million video views do not automatically become park revenue. The creator may own the audience, while the park bears costs for security, queues, talent, costumes, and operations. Views are a discovery metric, not a profit metric.

A [China Tourism News analysis](https://www.ctnews.com.cn/guandian/content/2026-01/12/content_182983.html) discusses content, audience experience, distribution, training, rights, and incentives as an operating system. The business implication is to evaluate repeatable operations, not just an isolated viral moment. That implication is our interpretation.

## 6. 実際の決算では、観客が増えても利益は減り得る

A useful counterexample comes from the operator of Kaifeng's Qingming Riverside Landscape Garden. According to [Economic Observer](https://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/0616/917768.shtml) reporting on its listing documents, revenue rose from about RMB673m in 2023 to RMB746m in 2025, while annual profit fell from RMB285m to RMB212m. These are park-wide figures, not an NPC P&L.

[Further reporting on the filing](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-06-25/doc-inieqsme2552430.shtml) puts direct labor costs at RMB79.8m in 2023 and RMB141m in 2025, alongside higher third-party performance fees. More performances and NPC interactions contributed to cost growth, but upgrades and other operations also changed. NPCs cannot be isolated as the sole cause of lower profit.

This is not proof that NPCs failed. It shows that better or larger experiences do not automatically improve margins. A park needs incremental contribution per visitor, cost per interaction slot, and repeat-visitor spending to decide how much to scale.

## 7. 出演者を「感情を提供する装置」にすると、労働と安全の問題が表に出る

There is labor behind the smiles. A [2025 China Newsweek report](https://www.chinanews.com/sh/2025/07-28/10454974.shtml) describes student workers, screen actors, and people building social-media careers. Eye-catching high-salary recruitment campaigns should not be mistaken for the industry's typical wage.

[A Legal Daily investigation carried by China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/05-21/10625497.shtml) reports long costume-preparation routines and performers facing unwanted touching, complaints, and harassment after refusing physical interactions. Performing a role never removes personal boundaries.

Criticism also targets experience design. A [2026 People's Daily commentary](https://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0501/c1008-40712588.html) challenges overly intimate or suggestive interactions in public attractions. Attractive performers are not the same thing as interactions built around ambiguous consent.

There is a structural conflict: the park may reward engagement, guests ask for closeness, and performers bear the cost of saying no. Proposed safeguards include explicit consent rules, photography policies, staff intervention, complaint channels, and rest or rotation standards.

Fandom can benefit both the park and the performer, but it also raises questions about image rights, clips, character ownership, schedules, and post-contract use of names. Personal popularity and park-owned IP should not be treated as the same property.

## 8. 日本のキャスト文化と似ているが、「推しに会いに行く」だけでは説明しきれない

Japan also has character greetings, theme-park cast members, historical performers, immersive theater, and idol fan events. China did not uniquely invent performer–guest interaction. A better comparison asks what the service centers on and who owns the relationship with the audience.

A park-owned character and a performer-led fan event need different rules for scheduling, substitute casts, photographs, personal accounts, and rights. Some Chinese NPC attractions combine these functions inside the same venue. That does not make every Chinese park identical.

## 9. 事業として試すなら「会えたか」ではなく「何が繰り返し売れたか」を測る

**Proposal:** For a venue, stadium, or event in Japan, the first question is why guests return. Do they come for the individual, the chance to interact, or an evolving fictional world? Different answers require different business models.

A small pilot could use one trained performer, scheduled sessions, a short non-contact interaction or quest, and consent-based photo rules. Compare it with ordinary operations in a similar time slot. This is an experimental proposal, not a validated formula.

Use four KPI layers: **attention** (participation, queue time, video completion), **behavior** (dwell time, return bookings, purchases), **economics** (incremental contribution after operating costs), and **safety** (complaints, performer burden, boundary incidents, staffing resilience). More views alone are not grounds to scale.

A harder stress test: what remains if the star performer is absent for a month? If the story, participation rules, memories, and anticipation for other performers survive, the venue may have built a durable experience. If not, it may be renting someone else's popularity. Performer independence is not itself a problem.

## 10. 見えていない数字と、これ以上断定できないことを残しておく

Important gaps remain. We have not verified individual NPC contracts, incremental attendance by performer, repeat rates, merchandise sales, or revenue sharing at Tianjin Happy Valley. Park-wide cost data from another venue cannot be converted into an individual performer's profitability.

Even the term 景区NPC has fuzzy boundaries. An attractive performer, a historical interpreter, and a quest-giver are not identical. For analysis, specify the role, guest interaction, and revenue owner.

## 11. NPCの正体は「脇役」ではなく、観客に主役を渡すための仕組みだった

Back to the original puzzle: how did a background character become someone worth flying to meet? The answer is not simply “attractive cast members are popular.” It is the convergence of world-building, live participation, personal fandom, and short-video discovery.

The deeper twist is that visitors can become temporary protagonists too. The performer responds, the guest takes home a story, and the story travels online. But a system that burns out its performers will not last. The real question is whether guests, workers, and the venue can all sustain the next performance.
