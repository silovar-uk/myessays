# スプーンが二本だった、というだけのこと
### To Give Meaning, or Let It Pass

I bought one pudding. There were two spoons in the bag.

「もう一本は、いつか誰かと甘さを分かち合うためのものだ」と言えば、a rather touching opening to an essay. But maybe the cashier simply grabbed two spoons by mistake. **A spoon is a terrible place to store a grand theory of life.**

同じ出来事を、三秒で忘れることも、後の思い出へ結びつけることもできる。Nothing about the spoon itself has changed. What changes is the way we read it. Every day, countless ordinary moments pass without becoming stories. では、どの瞬間にmeaningを与え、どの瞬間をlet passすればいいのか。

## 1. 意味は、出来事の中に初めから入っているのか

To **give something meaning** suggests a strange possibility: perhaps the meaning was not already there. あとから言葉を与え、記憶を結び、自分の人生の中へ置き直す。We are not always discovering a hidden message. Sometimes we are making a new connection.

Imagine getting caught in the rain. “I got wet because I had no umbrella” is a **causal explanation**. 「今日は何もかもついていなかった」はan interpretation of the day. “I'll carry a folding umbrella next time” is a **decision**. These are three different operations.

「意味があった」という短い言葉には、facts, stories, and actionsが混ざりやすい。でも、原因を説明すること、一日の価値を判断すること、次に何をするかは分けてよい。**You can change tomorrow's behavior without turning today's inconvenience into destiny.**

## 2. 心理学の「意味づけ」は、教訓を量産する技術ではない

Psychologist **Crystal L. Park（心理学者クリスタル・パーク）** reviewed research on meaning making in the context of stressful events. 彼女は、人がもともと持つ価値観や世界観と、特定の出来事に与える意味を区別した。また、**trying to make meaning** and **meaning actually made** are not the same thing（[2010 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20192563/)）。

This matters because a neat sentence is not necessarily an insight. 仕事で失敗したとき、「きっと成長のためだった」と言ってみる。その言葉が具体的な改善につながる場合もある。一方、何が起きたのかを検証せず、同じ言葉を繰り返すだけなら、we may have stopped asking questions too soon.

研究が扱うのは主にstressful life eventsであり、プリンのスプーンの科学的説明ではない。The useful distinction is modest but important: **searching for meaning does not guarantee finding it.**

## 3. 忘れることまで、失敗とは限らない

We often treat remembering as a success and forgetting as a loss. でも、今日目にしたすべての看板、信号、通行人のくしゃみを覚えていたらどうなるだろう。Our minds would become storage rooms with no space left to move.

A 2025 review of **responsible remembering（重要な情報を優先する記憶の働き）** discusses how we select what to remember, direct attention, and retrieve useful information. Less important material may be forgotten as part of this prioritization（[research review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39138722/)）。

That research is not a command to ignore everyday life. ただ、「忘れる＝価値を失う」ではないという見方を与えてくれる。Not every moment has to become a permanent exhibit. 何でもないまま流れていくことが、次の出来事の入る場所を作っているのかもしれない。

## 4. 同じ出来事に、四通りの意味を与えてみる

Let's do a slightly ridiculous desk experiment. Four everyday situations, four ways to respond: **build a story / investigate a problem / leave the meaning open / let it pass**. Four times four: sixteen interpretations. これは実際の参加者による実験ではない。An invented thought experiment, not empirical evidence.

**A. Someone replies coldly to your greeting.** 「嫌われた」というstoryを作ることはできる。Or investigate whether it affects work, suspend judgment because you don't know the circumstances, or simply let that brief moment pass. 相手の内心について、証拠なしに断定する必要はない。

**B. You hit three red lights on the way home.** “Everything goes wrong today” is one story. 「この時間帯は混みやすいのかもしれない」はtestable question. “Maybe it's random” keeps judgment open. “The light turned green, so I walked” is letting it pass. Three stops need not become a verdict on the whole day.

**C. An old friend messages you.** 「これは転機だ」と大きな物語にすることもできる。You can ask what they want, answer before interpreting, or decide not to respond. Here, letting it pass is not automatically wise. A small reply might begin something worthwhile. しかし、相手を自分の人生の筋書きの登場人物として決めつけるのも違う。

**D. A meeting is rescheduled at the last minute four times.** 「自分は軽んじられている」はan unverified story. A more useful question might be whether the scheduling process is broken. 理由を聞くために判断を保留することもできるが、「仕方ない」と流し続ければ問題が残るかもしれない。

The surprising result is not that one response always wins. **The right response depends on evidence and what action is needed, not simply on how dramatic the event feels.** 大事なことでも結論は保留できる。小さなことでも、行動は必要になり得る。

## 5. うれしい出来事には、説明しないほうがよい場合さえある

We try to explain pleasant surprises, too. Why was that person so kind? Why did that day feel special? だが、喜びの理由を説明し切れば、もっと幸福になれるのだろうか。

In a 2005 experimental study, **Timothy D. Wilson（心理学者ティモシー・ウィルソン）** and colleagues found that positive moods lasted longer under certain conditions of uncertainty following a positive event. 小さな贈り物に添えた説明の分かりやすさを変えるなど、複数の条件で調べた研究である（[original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15631571/)）。A related 2008 theory describes how explaining an emotional event may help people adapt to it, reducing its emotional intensity（[research article](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/j.1745-6924.2008.00085.x/)）。

This does **not** mean all uncertainty is good. 不安や危険を放置してよいという証拠でもない。But the result challenges our assumption that understanding always adds pleasure. Sometimes, **explaining a beautiful moment is also a way of finishing it.** 花を見たとき、理由を突き止めなくてもよい。

## 6. それでも、流してはいけないものがある

“Just let it go” sounds peaceful. けれども、繰り返される不利益、守られない約束、誰かへの不当な扱いまで流せば、それは配慮ではなくneglectになり得る。Not every problem disappears because we stop narrating it.

At the same time, attaching a life lesson to every painful experience can become exhausting. Research on **rumination（嫌な気分や原因を反復して考え続けること）** describes links with depression and other difficulties, while distinguishing such repetitive thinking from more constructive reflection（[2008 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26158958/)）。これは個々人を診断する根拠ではない。

Meaning making can become overinterpretation. Letting go can become avoidance. だから問いは二つ必要だ。**What evidence am I ignoring? What story am I forcing onto this event?** 両方に目を向けることが、軽率な解釈と無責任な放置の間に道を作る。

## 7. 意味は「確定」ではなく「仮置き」にしておける

There is a third option between “this means everything” and “this means nothing.” **Hold the meaning lightly.** 意味を仮置きするのである。

One simple practice: write **what happened**, then separately write **what you felt it meant**. 最後に、what needs to be checked or doneを考える。If action is needed, take it. If not, let the moment pass. If uncertain, leave it open. これは本稿からの生活上の提案であり、臨床的な効果が検証された方法として紹介するものではない。

「あの人の返事が冷たかった」と「私は嫌われていると感じた」は、似ているが別の文章だ。The first records an impression of an event. The second records your interpretation. 分けて書けば、感じたことを否定せずに、事実との距離を保てる。**A moment can matter without requiring a final verdict tonight.**

## 8. 余った一本をどうするか

Back to the pudding. Why were there two spoons? We don't know. 「誰かと分かち合う運命だった」と書くことはできる。But that would not prove the cashier's intention, let alone the universe's.

Yet you might turn to the person beside you and ask, “Want to share?” その行為によって、余った一本は小さな関係のきっかけになる。The meaning was not hidden inside the spoon, waiting to be discovered. **It began with what you chose to do next.**

Countless moments will pass without becoming anything special. それでいい。Life is not an archive in which every event must be assigned a lesson. それでも、ときどき立ち止まり、何かを手元に残す自由がある。**To give meaning is not to make every moment important. It is to choose what to keep, what to release, and what deserves an answer.**

The sources below are academic research or publisher information. The sixteen cases in Section 4 are **invented thought experiments**, not measured psychological outcomes. 個別研究の結果と、日常に引き寄せた文章上の解釈は区別する。

- [Park (2010), review of meaning making and stressful events](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20192563/) — trying to make meaning vs meaning actually made.
- [Murphy (2025), responsible remembering](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39138722/) — prioritization, attention, and remembering important information.
- [Wilson et al. (2005), the pleasures of uncertainty](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15631571/) — positive mood and uncertainty in specific experimental conditions.
- [Wilson & Gilbert (2008), affective adaptation](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/j.1745-6924.2008.00085.x/) — explaining events and emotional adaptation.
- [Nolen-Hoeksema et al. (2008), rethinking rumination](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26158958/) — repetitive negative thinking and constructive reflection.
- [Eric Hayot, The Elements of Academic Style (2014), Chapter 8](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/the-elements-of-academic-style/9780231168007/) — an approach to writing structure, not psychological evidence.
