# 最短距離では、途中のものを拾えない
## How to enjoy inefficiency without turning suffering into a virtue

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If efficiency were the only measure that mattered, mountain climbing would be a very strange activity.

<!-- level:1 role:description -->
何時間もかけて標高を上げ、景色を見て、写真を撮り、then eventually return to roughly the altitude where you started. In pure transportation terms, the net displacement can be almost comically small.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Yet people buy shoes, wake up before sunrise, sweat for hours, and voluntarily pre-order next day’s muscle pain.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
**Humans do not live only to arrive at destinations with minimum friction.**

僕は効率化が好きだ。Search should be fast. Repetitive work should be automated. 電車の乗り換えは最短がいいし、同じ住所を三回入力させるフォームに「人間らしい余白」は感じない。

But there is another experience. A fully optimized day can feel strangely thin.

最短経路で移動し、動画を倍速で見て、本を要約でつかみ、会議を30分で終え、食事を手早く済ませる。Everything worked. なのに夜になると、“What actually happened today?” となる。

Maybe the problem is not that we have forgotten how to enjoy inefficiency.

**Maybe we have forgotten to ask what efficiency removes together with the waste.**

---

## 1. Effort is costly, but sometimes effort itself becomes part of the value

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
The first complication is psychological: less effort is not always experienced as more value.

<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Michael Inzlicht, Amitai Shenhav, and Christopher Olivola described an “effort paradox.” People generally avoid effort because it is costly and aversive, yet they can also value effort and value outcomes more when effort helped produce them.

<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
つまり “I want the easy option” and “I care more because I worked for it” can live inside the same person.

<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Efficiency can remove cost, but it can also remove **the part that made the result feel participated in**.

[Inzlicht, Shenhav & Olivola (2018) — The Effort Paradox](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477776/)

A 2025 paper pushed this further. Across six studies with 2,883 participants, Aidan Campbell, Yiyi Wang, and Michael Inzlicht manipulated effort and measured how meaningful tasks felt. More effort generally increased experienced meaning, though the final study suggested the relationship did not rise forever.

[Campbell, Wang & Inzlicht (2025)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39854968/)

これは「苦労は買ってでもしろ」という話ではない。Effort really is costly. More pain is not infinitely better.

But zero friction is not automatically ideal either.

**Effort can be both a cost and a trace of participation.**

---

## 2. The IKEA effect is funny because humans can fall in love with a slightly wonky box

Michael Norton, Daniel Mochon, and Dan Ariely studied what happens when people assemble IKEA boxes, fold origami, and build Lego sets. Participants tended to value successfully self-made products more highly. But the effect weakened when they failed to finish or when the creation was destroyed.

[Norton, Mochon & Ariely (2012) — The IKEA Effect](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-ce76-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)

The important part is not “labor makes everything lovable.” It is closer to this:

**Effort becomes valuable when it becomes evidence that I made, solved, or completed something.**

だから、完成品を買えば10秒なのに料理する。Automatic correction exists, but we still sometimes adjust a photo ourselves. 趣味では、便利な道具を持っているのに手を動かしたくなる。

Efficiency loses the stopwatch contest.

Experience may still win.

This changed my framing. 「効率の悪さを楽しむ」は、遅さに耐える能力ではない。

**Good inefficiency contains participation.**

---

## 3. In learning, fast performance can be a terrible proxy for durable learning

Robert Bjork’s research on “desirable difficulties” is useful here. Some learning conditions make practice feel harder or make improvement look slower while improving long-term retention and transfer. Spacing, retrieval practice, and interleaving are classic examples.

[UCLA Bjork Learning and Forgetting Lab](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/)

でも difficulty だけを神格化すると危ない。The Bjork lab itself stresses that many difficulties are simply undesirable. Confusing materials, fatigue, pointless interfaces, or overload can damage learning.

So the useful friction is not suffering.

It is friction that triggers remembering, discriminating, and retrieving.

AI makes this distinction practical. 分からない単語はすぐ調べられる。A summary can appear instantly. 答えも生成できる。

Great.

But we do not need to optimize away the ten seconds in which we first try to remember.

**Outsource answer production if you want. Do not automatically outsource every learning event.**

---

## 4. GPS can optimize arrival while weakening the mental map

Efficiency often separates two goals that used to travel together.

Navigation is a clean example. A 2018 virtual-town experiment found that more attention to a GPS-like aid was associated with less accurate spatial knowledge and longer routes later without guidance. A 2025 study similarly found weaker path and landmark knowledge among GPS users than non-GPS navigators.

[Hejtmánek et al. (2018)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S107158191830171X)

[GPS vs. non-GPS navigation (2025)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S209044792500156X)

GPS does its assigned job: it gets you there.

What it may reduce is another job — choosing turns, noticing landmarks, making mistakes, and stitching the city into a cognitive map.

つまり **arrival and knowing the place are different outcomes**.

もちろん終電前にGPSを閉じて「今日は空間認知を鍛えるぞ」とやる必要はない。それは learning design というより、かなり意識の高い遭難である。

Use the fastest route when speed matters.

On a free afternoon, maybe walk one station. 最後の500メートルだけ案内を閉じる。Take a different street on the way home.

Do not make the entire life inefficient.

**Insert friction only where you want understanding to remain.**

---

## 5. Walking is inefficient transportation but can be useful thinking

Marily Oppezzo and Daniel Schwartz found that walking improved divergent creative thinking compared with sitting, both indoors and outdoors, with some residual benefit afterward. The same pattern did not simply transfer to convergent tasks with one correct answer.

[Oppezzo & Schwartz (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24749966/)

Walking is not a universal intelligence potion.

But something can be inefficient on one metric and productive on another.

A twenty-minute walk is slow transportation. It may be useful ideation.

ここで Shari Tishman の “slow looking” ともつながる。At Harvard Project Zero, her work focuses on careful observation beyond first impressions.

[Harvard Project Zero — Shari Tishman](https://pz.harvard.edu/who-we-are/people/shari-tishman)

Efficiency loves closure.

Read label. Understand. Next.

Slow observation keeps “I don’t know yet” alive a little longer.

**Discovery needs some space before conclusion.**

---

## 6. A good life may need not only happiness and meaning, but psychological richness

Shigehiro Oishi and colleagues have argued for another dimension of a good life: psychological richness — a life filled with diverse, interesting, perspective-changing experiences.

[Oishi & Westgate (2025)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40280834/)

This matters because psychologically rich does not simply mean comfortable.

Unexpectedness, complexity, and changed perspective are not guaranteed by optimized routes.

いつもの店は外しにくい。Recommendation systems fit our taste. 最短経路は迷わない。Known people are easy to talk to.

All sensible.

But if every system is optimized to minimize mismatch, **we may also minimize the lucky mismatch that changes us**.

人間、品質管理だけでは生ききれない。Apparently some defects are plot.

---

## 7. Conversation becomes faster when you delete everything that is not the agenda — and relationships often live there

Nicholas Epley and Juliana Schroeder ran field experiments with commuters. Participants instructed to talk with a stranger reported a more positive commute than those instructed to remain disconnected, without reporting lower productivity. Other participants predicted the opposite beforehand.

[Epley & Schroeder (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25019381/)

From the transport goal, the conversation is unnecessary.

From the experience goal, it may be value.

だから、人との時間では “not required for the task” and “worthless” are dangerous synonyms.

Delete all pre-meeting chat. Make every reply minimal. Never speak to strangers. Keep every conversation on agenda.

Sometimes that is exactly right.

But friendship rarely begins as an agenda item.

**Relational value often appears in the margin of the task.**

---

## 8. Stop here before “inefficiency” becomes a moral virtue

At this point the argument could become dangerous.

Long meetings. Bad forms. Queues. Slow systems. Paperwork. Maybe we start calling them “human.”

No.

Unchosen friction is not automatically meaningful. Meaningless waiting, duplicate work, inaccessible design, and burdens pushed onto people with less power are simply costs.

Even boredom should not be romanticized. A 2024 scoping review of 27 studies found positive, negative, contradictory, and non-significant relationships between boredom and creativity. There is no clean empirical rule saying boredom reliably makes people creative.

[Zeißig et al. (2024)](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rev3.3470)

Carl Honoré’s version of “slow” is helpful here. He is not anti-speed; he argues for using the right speed for the activity. In one interview he joked about speed-reading bedtime stories so aggressively that his version of Snow White ended up with only three dwarfs.

[Interview with Carl Honoré](https://www.slowentrepreneur.com/the-interviews/carl-honor%C3%A9)

Seven dwarfs to three is a 57.1% reduction in dwarf headcount.

Great restructuring.

But bedtime storytelling probably was not trying to maximize story units per minute.

**Before optimizing speed, define what the activity is for.**

---

## 9. Good friction leaves one of five things behind

The literatures above are different. Effort research, learning science, spatial cognition, creativity, psychological richness, and social connection are not one unified theory.

So this section is my synthesis, not a published model.

I would keep friction when it leaves one of five residues.

1. **Skill**  
   一度自分で思い出す。Make one judgment yourself. Assemble something instead of only receiving it.

2. **Memory**  
   GPSを少し閉じる。Look before taking the photo. Read before reading the summary.

3. **Discovery**  
   Walk. Observe longer. Choose one thing outside recommendations. Leave a small part of the route unscripted.

4. **Connection**  
   用件の前後を全部切らない。Do not force small talk, but do not eliminate every margin around the task.

5. **Meaning**  
   Keep the part where you want to feel “I did this” rather than automating the whole chain.

And friction that leaves nothing?

Automate it.

二重入力。意味のない承認。探しにくいファイル。読みにくい資料。Waiting with no learning and no choice.

**Do not protect inefficiency. Protect the residue.**

## 10. Give yourself a twenty-minute “inefficiency budget”

This is a proposal, not an evidence-based intervention.

Instead of slowing your whole life, allocate twenty minutes a day to something that could be done faster — but only when the slower version may leave skill, memory, discovery, connection, or meaning.

条件は五つ。

- chosen, not imposed
- reversible
- bounded to roughly twenty minutes
- connected to one of the five residues
- not used as proof that suffering makes you morally superior

Examples:

手でコーヒーを淹れる。  
Walk one station.  
知らない道を一本だけ曲がる。  
Before searching, write your own hypothesis in one sentence.  
要約の前に本文を10ページ読む。  
Ask one non-agenda question at the end of a conversation.  
写真を撮る前に30秒見る。  
Before asking AI to write, draft the opening yourself.

Then record only three things:

How many extra minutes?  
What appeared because of them?  
Would I choose this again?

If twenty minutes produced nothing, automate it next time.

**Inefficiency should also have to justify its budget.**

---

## 11. Ask AI not only “what can be faster?” but “what should not disappear?”

AI is very good at optimization when we ask optimization questions.

So change the question.

    Analyze this activity not only for efficiency, but for what should remain humanly participatory.

    1. Break the workflow into time, effort, and judgment steps.
    2. Separate removable friction from valuable friction.
    3. Evaluate valuable friction by five residues: skill, memory, discovery, connection, meaning.
    4. Automate effort that leaves no useful residue.
    5. Preserve a minimal amount of effort when doing it personally creates one of the five residues.
    6. Do not romanticize waiting, duplication, confusion, or imposed inconvenience.
    7. State what may be lost if each step is optimized away.
    8. Produce two designs: fastest route and intentionally slightly slower route.
    9. For the slower route, explain exactly what the extra friction is expected to leave behind.

効率化のAIに、効率化の副作用まで監査させる。

That is more interesting than simply asking it to make everything shorter.

---

## 12. Enjoying inefficiency is not about living slowly; it is about refusing to erase every point of contact

I started with a vague question: how do you enjoy inefficiency?

The research changed the question.

Effort can contribute to meaning. Some learning difficulties improve durable learning. Less navigation guidance can increase engagement with spatial information. Walking can support divergent ideation. Varied and perspective-changing experiences relate to psychological richness. Conversations outside the strict task can improve experience.

But none of this proves “slower is better.”

Effort has limits. Many difficulties are bad. Boredom does not reliably generate creativity.

So what do I actually want to keep?

**The parts where I still touch the world on the way to the outcome.**

The shortest route is excellent when the destination is all that matters.

But some activities are partly made of their route: learning, cooking, walking, conversation, writing, hobbies, travel, relationships.

Optimize them completely and you may still arrive.

You simply arrive having picked up less.

だから判断基準を一つだけ残す。

**If I remove this friction, what disappears besides time?**

If the answer is nothing, automate it.

If the answer is skill, memory, discovery, connection, or meaning, do not remove all of it.

“Enjoying inefficiency” is not deliberately becoming slower.

**It is knowing that the shortest path cannot pick up everything — and choosing, sometimes, what you want to pick up on the way.**

## Research Note

### Confirmed from published sources

- Effort is generally costly and avoided, while effort and effort-produced outcomes can also acquire value.
- A 2025 six-study paper with 2,883 participants reported a causal relationship between greater effort and greater task meaning, with evidence that the relationship has limits.
- IKEA-effect studies found higher valuation of successfully self-created products, with boundary conditions around completion and destruction.
- Desirable difficulties can slow apparent acquisition while improving long-term retention or transfer, but many difficulties are undesirable.
- GPS-like navigation has been associated experimentally with weaker spatial learning in some contexts.
- Walking improved divergent ideation in Oppezzo and Schwartz’s experiments, but not all kinds of thinking.
- Psychological richness is studied as a dimension of a good life distinct from happiness and meaning.
- Commuters who talked with strangers in Epley and Schroeder’s experiments reported more positive experiences than those who remained disconnected, contrary to participants’ predictions.
- Research does not support a simple universal claim that boredom causes creativity.

### Interpretation and proposals in this essay

- The five residues — skill, memory, discovery, connection, meaning — are an editorial synthesis across different literatures, not one validated scientific scale.
- The twenty-minute inefficiency budget is a practical experiment, not a proven intervention.
- The AI prompt is a proposed design tool for comparing optimization with intentionally preserved friction.
- This essay does not endorse unsafe, inequitable, or imposed inconvenience.
