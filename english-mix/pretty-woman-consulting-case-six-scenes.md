# 『プリティ・ウーマン』をTurn into a Consulting Case
## M&Aだけじゃない。6 scenesでCX、Negotiation、Organization、PMOまで練習する

If you use *Pretty Woman* to study consulting, the obvious answer is M&A.

Edward is a corporate raider.

He buys companies, breaks them up, and sells the parts.

That is already a business case.

でも、それだけでは近すぎる。

The more interesting exercise is this:

**What if every strange scene were a client problem?**

Vivianが高級店で追い返される。

That can become a Customer Experience case.

Barney, the hotel manager, bends the rules without destroying the hotel standard.

That can become an Operating Model case.

Vivian and Edward negotiate a one-week deal, but the meaning of the deal changes over time.

That can become Negotiation + Scope Management.

People treat Vivian differently after she changes clothes.

That can become a problem of signaling, proxy metrics, and biased screening.

And when Edward changes his business decision at the end, the movie is over.

Consulting work is not.

**The happy ending is basically a kick-off meeting.**

前編の映画エッセイはこちら。

[『プリティ・ウーマン』は誰が誰を救ったのか？](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/pretty-woman-double-transformation-1990)

## 0. The Translation Engine

今回使うのは、難しいframeworkではなく一つの流れだけ。

| Step | Question |
| --- | --- |
| SCENE | What is happening? |
| BUSINESS PROBLEM | 会社なら何の問題？ |
| CLIENT QUESTION | What decision must be made? |
| HYPOTHESIS | What might be true? |
| DATA | What evidence do we need? |
| ANALYSIS | How do we test it? |
| RECOMMENDATION | What should change? |
| DELIVERABLE | What do we show the client? |

This matters because consulting is not mainly about remembering frameworks.

It is about turning messy reality into a decision problem.

映画は、その練習にちょうどいい。

---

## 1. Edward’s acquisition — “Can buy” is not the same as “Should buy”

AFI describes Edward’s business clearly: he buys financially distressed companies, breaks them up, and sells the pieces for profit.

Morse Industries is his next target.

Pause the movie here.

Imagine you are on Edward’s consulting team.

The client question becomes:

**Should we acquire Morse Industries, and where exactly will the value come from?**

McKinsey’s M&A work spans strategy, target assessment, due diligence, integration, and value creation.

So the analysis should not stop at price.

### First hypothesis

Edward’s original thesis seems to be:

**Breakup value > value as a going concern.**

Then we need evidence.

- order backlog
- Navy contract economics
- asset values
- cash flow
- capex
- market outlook
- talent and technical capability
- regulatory constraints
- breakup case vs standalone case vs rebuild case

The useful consulting distinction is this:

**A deal can be cheap and still be a bad deal.**

Attractiveness at entry is not the same as sustainable value creation.

映画終盤でEdwardが変えるのは、まさにinvestment thesisそのもの。

### Three-slide answer

1. Buy / No Buy investment thesis  
2. Breakup / Standalone / Rebuild scenario comparison  
3. Key risks + remaining DD questions

### MINI CASE

You have only 72 hours.

Which three workstreams do you run?

僕なら、Market & Contract、Financial / Asset Value、Execution & Stakeholder Risk。

The third one is easy to miss.

But a deal that cannot be executed is not a valuable deal.

## 2. The $3,000 week — negotiation becomes scope management

The title of the early screenplay, *3000*, came from the amount Vivian receives for the week.

At first, the relationship is explicit and transactional.

Price, time, scope.

This is a neat BATNA case.

Harvard’s Program on Negotiation defines BATNA as the best alternative if no agreement is reached. It gives you a reference point for whether to accept a deal or walk away.

But the more consulting-like problem appears later.

**The contract stays the same while the work changes.**

The relationship expands into social events, emotional support, private expectations, and future possibilities.

案件でもある。

“Please do the market analysis.”

Then:

“Can you also build the business plan?”

“Can you prepare the board deck?”

“Can you stay for implementation?”

From the client side, it feels continuous.

From the team side, it may be a new engagement.

### CLIENT QUESTION

**When assumptions change, when should we re-contract?**

Check:

- scope
- deliverables
- timeline
- roles
- decision rights
- fee / staffing
- escalation

Good negotiation is not only getting a better price.

It is also resetting expectations before ambiguity becomes conflict.

### MINI CASE

The client asks you to move from analysis to implementation.

What do you redefine first?

僕なら、priceより先にsuccess criteria、scope、decision owner、timeline。

A higher fee does not fix unclear ownership.

## 3. Rodeo Drive — don’t jump straight to “staff training”

Vivian enters a luxury store and is treated as someone who does not belong.

As a movie scene, it is satisfying.

As a consulting case, it is dangerous.

Because the CEO will probably say:

**“Train the staff.”**

Maybe.

But that is already a solution.

We still need the problem.

McKinsey defines customer journeys as the full set of interactions a customer has with a brand around a task or decision, and links journey improvement to analytics, frontline transformation, empowerment, and measurement.

So we need multiple hypotheses.

### Three hypotheses

A. Individual service skill problem  
B. An informal customer-screening rule  
C. Incentives and brand culture reward exclusion

Different causes, different fixes.

Training helps A.

It may do almost nothing for B or C.

### DATA

- entrance → service → fitting → purchase conversion
- customer drop-off
- complaints
- mystery shopping
- store / staff variation
- repeat visit and referral
- frontline KPI and incentives

The more interesting question is not simply “Was the staff rude?”

It is:

**Does the current screening logic protect the luxury brand, or does it create costly false negatives?**

Vivian is a false negative.

The system labels a high-value customer as low-value because the proxy is wrong.

### MINI CASE

The CEO insists on service training.

What do you ask before agreeing?

僕なら、研修履歴とoutcomeの差、そしてstaffが何で評価されているかを見る。

People often follow incentives more faithfully than training manuals.

## 4. Barney — frontline empowerment is a design problem

Barney is one of the most interesting characters if you watch the movie like a consultant.

He has standards to protect.

He also has an unusual guest situation.

He does not simply reject Vivian.

He helps solve the problem while preserving the hotel’s dignity.

This is not just kindness.

It is **exception handling**.

Every service organization has exceptions.

If every exception needs head-office approval, service becomes slow.

If every employee can improvise freely, quality and risk control collapse.

### CLIENT QUESTION

**How much decision authority should the frontline have?**

We need to design:

- local decision rights
- financial / risk thresholds
- escalation rules
- service recovery principles
- learning loop from exceptions

The goal is not to hire 100 Barneys.

The goal is to create a system where ordinary people can make **Barney-like decisions**.

That is an Operating Model question.

### MINI CASE

You want to scale Barney’s service style to 100 hotels.

Do you write a 100-page manual?

僕なら、細かいprocedureより、

**Principle / Authority / Escalation**

の3層を設計する。

You cannot pre-write every exception.

You can design how exceptions are judged.

## 5. Clothes as signals — visible proxies can fool the system

Vivian does not suddenly become smarter after changing clothes.

But the world updates its estimate of her.

That is a useful bridge to information asymmetry and signaling.

Michael Spence’s signaling theory examines how better-informed actors can send observable signals to less-informed actors. His work on signaling under asymmetric information was part of the 2001 Nobel Prize in Economics.

もちろん、VivianのdressをそのままSpence modelと同じだと言うのは乱暴。

But the structural analogy is useful:

**When true quality is hard to observe, people use visible proxies.**

Education.

Job title.

Accent.

Clothes.

Response speed.

Slide quality.

Organizations need proxies because not everything can be observed directly.

The risk is not using a proxy.

The risk is forgetting that it is only a proxy.

### CLIENT QUESTION

**What proxy is the organization using as a substitute for real quality?**

### DATA

- actual decision criteria
- relationship between proxy and outcome
- false positives
- false negatives
- exception cases

### DELIVERABLE

A **Decision Criteria Audit**.

What do we observe?

What do we infer?

Where are we wrong?

### MINI CASE

あなたの会社で「仕事ができる人」のproxyは何？

Fast replies?

Speaking often in meetings?

Beautiful slides?

Do those signals predict actual value creation?

## 6. Morse — “irrational stakeholders” are often rational under another objective

Edward looks at Morse Industries as an asset.

James Morse sees a company, history, people, work, and continuity.

So when Edward thinks,

“Why won’t he accept a rational offer?”

we have a stakeholder problem.

Program on Negotiation emphasizes focusing on interests rather than positions, generating options, and using objective criteria.

### POSITION vs INTEREST

Morse’s position: I will not sell.  
Morse’s interests: protect the business, people, continuity, identity.

Once you separate them, the solution space expands.

It is no longer only Sell / Don’t Sell.

You can discuss:

- joint rebuilding
- governance
- capital structure
- employment
- investment commitments
- control rights

Edward’s final change is therefore not only emotional.

It looks like **deal redesign**.

### CLIENT QUESTION

Don’t ask first:

“How do we persuade the resistant stakeholder?”

Ask:

**“What do they believe they will lose?”**

That one question works surprisingly often in transformation projects.

### MINI CASE

A client says frontline staff are irrationally resisting a new system.

What is your first question?

僕なら、

**“What becomes worse for them if this succeeds?”**

Authority?

Evaluation?

Workload?

Customer relationships?

Expert identity?

Resistance may be rational under a different KPI.

## 7. The morning after the happy ending — now build the PMO

The movie ends at exactly the point where a consulting team would get nervous.

Edward changes direction.

He will not break the company apart.

He will work with Morse to rebuild it.

Great.

**What happens Monday at 9:00?**

Strategy is not execution.

McKinsey’s M&A integration work includes master planning, risk and interdependency management, value capture, organization, talent, culture, and communication.

Its work on Transformation Offices also stresses weekly action-oriented meetings, clear owners, value tracking, escalation, and a single source of truth.

Now the film becomes a PMO case.

### WORKSTREAMS

- Strategy / Business Plan
- Finance / Funding
- Operations
- Commercial / Navy Contract
- Organization / Talent
- Stakeholder Communication
- Governance / PMO

### First PMO artifacts

1. success definition  
2. workstream / owner map  
3. milestones  
4. risk & issue log  
5. decision log  
6. weekly cadence  
7. value tracking

A romantic happy ending says:

“They chose a new future.”

A consultant asks:

**“Who owns Tuesday?”**

### MINI CASE

Edward asks you on Monday morning:

“We’re rebuilding Morse Industries. What do we do first?”

僕なら、WBSをすぐ作らない。

First align four things with Morse:

**Purpose / Value / Decision Rights / Critical Risks.**

If those are unclear, a detailed project plan only creates organized confusion.

---

## 8. Consulting practice is the ability to turn scenes into decisions

We started with a romantic comedy.

We ended up with:

M&A DD.

Scope management.

CX.

Operating Model.

Signaling.

Stakeholder management.

PMO.

The point is not that *Pretty Woman* secretly contains every consulting framework.

It doesn’t.

The point is that consulting practice often begins with a translation.

“Staff are rude.”

“People are resisting.”

“The project is late.”

“We want to buy this company.”

These are observations.

Not yet problems.

A consultant keeps translating until the decision becomes clearer.

### MOVIE → CASE DRILL

次に映画を観たら、sceneを一つ止める。

Ask four questions.

1. **What is happening?** 何が起きている？  
2. **What decision?** 誰が何を決める？  
3. **What evidence?** 何が分かれば決められる？  
4. **What deliverable?** 3枚だけなら何を見せる？

That is enough to turn almost any movie into a small consulting case.

『ジョーズ』ならrisk management。

『マネーボール』ならtalent analytics。

『プラダを着た悪魔』ならorganization cultureとretention。

The goal is not to memorize more frameworks.

It is to get better at seeing a messy situation and asking:

**What is the actual problem here?**

And somehow, after all that, the character I want to rewatch most in *Pretty Woman* is Barney.

Not because he is charming.

Because I now want to know his escalation policy.
