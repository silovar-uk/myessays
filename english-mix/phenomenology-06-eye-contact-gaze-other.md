# 目が合った瞬間、自分が「見られる人」に変わる
## Another person creates another centre of the world.

電車で向かいの人をなんとなく見る。

Clothes. Shoes. Phone. Sleepy face.

こちらは観察する側。

Then they look up.

目が合う。

Suddenly you notice your posture, your face, and the possibility that you were staring.

**The one who was looking becomes the one being looked at.**

Nothing physical changed. 座席も距離も照明も同じ。

But the structure of the situation changes.

Sartre pushes this ordinary awkwardness surprisingly far.

---

## 1. Another person is not an object like a chair

第1回のコップにはhidden sidesがあった。でもit never looks back.

A person can.

I see the other, and the other can see me.

つまり他者は、私のworldの中のobjectでありながら、**a subject with another point of view**でもある。

A second centre appears.

From here, I am “here” and they are “there.” From their side, that relation reverses.

当たり前。でも重大。

**I am not the only centre of a world.**

---

## 2. Sartre's famous keyhole scene

In *Being and Nothingness*, Sartre analyzes someone peeping through a keyhole.

その人は見ることへ没頭している。At that moment, they may not be explicitly thinking of themselves as “a person spying.”

Then footsteps sound in the hallway.

**Someone may be watching me.**

Shame appears.

重要なのは、他人の眼球というobjectを見たことではない。

The possibility of being seen changes what I am to myself: not simply a viewer, but **a voyeur as seen by another**.

---

## 3. Over-serious experiment: classify elevator gaze directions

4人のエレベーター。

Look at the door: normal.
Look at the floor indicator: normal.
Look at the ceiling: slightly odd.
Stare continuously at a stranger's face: suddenly intense.

眼球の向きは数十度しか違わない。

The social experience changes dramatically.

With others present, seeing is entangled with **how I may be seen**.

A gaze is not only information gathering. It can become a relation.

---

## 4. “Ignore what people think” is harder than it sounds

「人の目なんて気にしなくていい」とよく言う。

Sartre makes complete indifference look structurally difficult.

The other's look is not merely an optional rating added to my private self.

Through another person, I can experience myself as something visible from outside.

髪型、話し方、仕事ぶり。Of course, social psychology has other theories and empirical explanations; not everything reduces to Sartre.

The phenomenological point is narrower:

**Adding another subject to the world can change how I myself appear.**

---

## 5. Other people are not character objects placed in my scene

目が合う前、相手を観察していた。

After eye contact, the person is no longer merely “something I see.” Actually, they never were. I had simply forgotten the other direction.

The other has a first-person world I cannot fully occupy.

そしてそのworldの中に、**I appear too**.

自分が他人にどう見えているかは完全には見られない。

**The other is in my world, and I am in theirs.**

A few seconds of eye contact makes that fact embarrassingly vivid.
