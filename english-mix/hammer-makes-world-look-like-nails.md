# 金づちを持つと、世界に釘が増える
## 得意な道具は、解決策だけでなく「何を問題と呼ぶか」まで変える

“If all you have is a hammer, everything looks like a nail.”

有名な比喩で、the basic lesson feels obvious. Excelが得意なら表を作る。会議が好きな組織はmeetingを開く。広告を扱う人はadvertisingで解こうとする。If you are excited about artificial intelligence, the reflex becomes, “Can we automate this with AI?”

At first, this sounds like a warning against overusing your favorite technique. でも、その来歴とcognitive psychologyを追うと話は深くなる。People do not merely reuse the same tool. **They can reshape the problem until it becomes solvable with the tool they already have.**

The hammer is dangerous not because we swing it too often. 金づちを握った手が、quietly redraws the map of the world.

> **Information date: 2026-10-02**
>
> This essay distinguishes documented findings on fixation from business interpretations and practical proposals. 実務例は心理実験そのものの結果ではなく、researchを仕事へ接続した解釈である。

## 1. 「金づち」の話は、もともと工具の話ではなく研究方法の話だった

この比喩は一般に「マズローの金づち」と呼ばれる。But by 1964, philosopher エイブラハム・カプラン（Abraham Kaplan）had already discussed the **道具の法則（law of the instrument）** in 『行動科学のための探究方法論（The Conduct of Inquiry）』. His target was not DIY. It was research methodology.

Kaplan's point was that a researcher can formulate problems so that they require the techniques the researcher already knows well. つまり、“this method fits the problem”だけではない。The method can come first, and the problem gets cut into a shape that the method can handle. 道具と問題の順番が逆転する。

Two years later, psychologist アブラハム・マズロー（Abraham H. Maslow）used the famous hammer-and-nail formulation in 『科学の心理学（The Psychology of Science）』. 同書では、available techniquesに問いを合わせる**手法中心（method-centering）**より、important problemsに合わせて方法を探す**問題中心（problem-centering）**を重視している。

Seen this way, the lesson changes. 「工具箱を増やそう」だけではない。**Look at the problem first. Open the toolbox later.**

参照：[Kaplan, The Conduct of Inquiry, Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL5911740M/The_conduct_of_inquiry) ／ [Maslow, The Psychology of Science, Open Library](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL21519482M/The_psychology_of_science)

## 2. 人を縛るのは、失敗した方法より「成功した方法」である

A failed method is relatively easy to question. 本当に厄介なのは、ちゃんとworksする方法である。

Psychologist アブラハム・ルーチンス（Abraham S. Luchins）の1940年代のwater-jar problemsは、その典型として知られる。Participants first solve several problems with the same slightly complicated procedure. そのあと、もっとsimpleな解法がある問題が出ても、many people keep using the learned procedure.

This is the **構え効果（Einstellung effect）**. 一度成功したsolutionが、“this is how this kind of problem is solved”というmental setを作り、better alternativesの探索を妨げる。The old solution does not have to be wrong. It can be good enough. だからこそ捨てにくい。

Work has the same temptation. 「前回これで伸びた」「この分析で原因が見えた」「この会議体で事故を防げた」。Success builds capability, but it also pre-selects what we notice next time.

**We question the hammer that failed. 成功した金づちは、疑う理由そのものを減らす。**

参照：[Wang, Okada & Takagi, 2023, fixation/defixation systematic review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2023.1183025/full)

## 3. 金づちは、手だけでなく「目」まで固定する

The Einstellung effect is not only an abstract story about thinking. It can show up in attention.

2013年、ヘザー・シェリダン（Heather Sheridan）とエヤル・ラインゴールド（Eyal M. Reingold）は、expert and novice chess playersに、a familiar advantageous moveと、better optimal moveが共存する局面を解かせ、eye movementsを測った。Participants who chose the familiar move kept looking at board regions related to that move, while experts who found the optimal move gradually disengaged their attention from it.

ここが妙である。A player may feel, “I am looking for another move,” while the eyes keep returning to the first solution. 探索しているというself-perceptionと、実際に探索している場所がずれる。

So “everything looks like a nail” may still be too gentle. **Sometimes non-nails stop entering the visual search at all.**

A tool does not only guide the hand. 何をinformationとして拾い、どこへattentionを向けるかという、problem solvingより手前の段階にも入り込む。

参照：[Sheridan & Reingold, 2013, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0075796)

## 4. 専門性は、世界をよく見る能力であり、同時に切り捨てる能力でもある

ここで“experts are rigid”という雑な結論へ行くと、everything collapses.

The value of expertise is compression. チェスの熟練者は盤面をbrute-forceで一個ずつ読まない。A physician does not reinvent medicine for every patient. 経理担当者もinvoiceごとにaccountingを再発明しない。Experience narrows the search space so complex work becomes possible in finite time.

The chess evidence also shows a boundary condition: when the familiar move was clearly a blunder, experts were much better at avoiding it. 熟練はfixationを生むだけでなく、固定化から抜けるためのricher knowledgeも与える。

So the problem is not having patterns. 型は必要である。The question is whether, while using a pattern, you can still notice **what the pattern is filtering out**.

Expertise increases what you can see, and also makes you fast at not seeing irrelevant things. そのcompressionが強力だからこそ、ときどきuncompressed worldへ戻る操作がいる。

参照：[Sheridan & Reingold, 2013, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0075796)

## 5. Excelを開いた瞬間、問題は「表にできるもの」へ寄っていく

From here, this is an application to work rather than a direct experimental finding.

売上が落ちた。If you are good at Excel, you decompose the numbers. 広告運用者ならdelivery settingsを見る。SNS担当者ならcontent ideasを考える。A project manager checks process and roles. コンサルタントなら、たぶん四角を描く。Two axes feel even safer.

None of these is bad. むしろprofessional instinctとして自然である。But if you define declining sales as an “awareness problem,” advertising becomes an obvious candidate. 「購入途中の離脱」と定義すればUXや接客へ行く。“Product value has weakened” sends you toward the offer. “The market itself is shrinking” changes the whole field of analysis.

The same symptom can require different tools depending on problem framing. 本来はproblem → toolの順番である。But in practice, available tools can generate the framing: agencies see awareness problems, system vendors see efficiency problems, training providers see capability problems.

This does not require bad faith. 得意だから見える。You can sell it because you can deliver it. だからこそ強い。

**The hammer is an excellent salesperson.**

## 6. 生成AIは「何でもできる」からこそ、巨大な金づちになりうる

Artificial intelligence makes this problem unusually visible.

2026年の『オーガニゼーショナル・ダイナミクス（Organizational Dynamics）』で、ヘンリー・アドボー（Henry Adobor）は、complex organizational problemsを“solvable mainly through algorithms and data”としてframeしてしまう傾向を、**AIによる万能解決主義の罠（AI solutionism trap）**として論じた。Closing the problem too early around AI adoption can itself become a management failure.

For example, once the sentence becomes “AIで問い合わせ対応を効率化する,” AI is no longer a candidate solution; it is baked into the problem statement. でも、本当のproblemは問い合わせ量かもしれない。The product explanation may be confusing. 入力画面が壊れているかもしれない。Staff may lack authority, creating repeated escalations.

A CHI 2025 study collected 936 first-hand GenAI use cases from 319 knowledge workers. 生成AIへのconfidenceが高いほど、participants reported less critical-thinking effort, while the study also found that critical thinking shifts toward verification, integration, and stewardship rather than simply disappearing.

This does not prove that “AI makes people stupid.” 大事なのは、as AI gets stronger, the critical question moves one step upstream. **Before asking how to use AI, ask whether this is a problem that should be solved with AI at all.**

参照：[Adobor, 2026, Organizational Dynamics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0090261626000136) ／ [Lee et al., 2025, Microsoft Research / CHI](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers/)

## 7. 「柔軟に考えよう」では固定化から抜けにくい

So how do we loosen the grip of the hammer?

「思い込みを捨てよう」「柔軟に考えよう」だけではweak instructionである。A 2023 systematic review by 王詩軍、岡田猛、高木紀久子 examined 53 experimental studies on fixation and defixation. 一つの中心的な論点は、the need to expand search beyond the existing frame.

The effective intervention depends on the source of fixation and the problem type. 誤ったcueの影響を弱める、reflectionを促す、search rangeを直接広げる、といったapproachesが役立つ場合がある。一方、open-ended problemsでは、less fixation does not automatically mean more creativity; deeper search within the frame can also help.

So the rule is not “always think the opposite.” 大事なのは、自分がいま**which frame**の中を探しているかを見えるようにすること。

Fixation is easier to address as process design than as personality. **Instead of trying to become a flexible person, build a step that forces flexibility into the work.**

参照：[Wang, Okada & Takagi, 2023, Frontiers in Education](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2023.1183025/full)

## 8. 一度「道具名禁止」で問題を書くと、見えていなかった選択肢が戻ってくる

ここからはresearchを踏まえたpractical proposalである。

The simplest move is to define the problem **without naming the tool**. 「AIで問い合わせ対応を効率化する」ではなく、“reduce human handling time per inquiry without lowering first-contact resolution.” 「SNSで集客したい」ではなく、“初回来場者を月500人増やす”。「ダッシュボードを作りたい」ではなく、“make abnormal values noticeable within ten minutes each morning.”

Once you rewrite the sentence, options come back. 問い合わせならFAQ、product explanation、input flow、authority design、そして問い合わせ自体の発生原因まで候補になる。Acquisition can include referrals, search, partnerships, product changes, and pricing. 異常検知もdashboardではなくnotificationやmeeting removalが最適かもしれない。

If you want to overdo the experiment slightly, ban your first solution for five minutes. 「Excel禁止」「会議禁止」「広告禁止」「AI禁止」。You do not need to ban it forever. The point is to test whether attention has already collapsed around the first answer.

The goal is not wild creativity. **It is simply to restore the order: problem first, tool second.**

## 9. 「金づちを捨てろ」ではなく、金づちを持ったまま問題を見直す

Return to the original line.

「金づちしか持っていない人には、すべてのものが釘に見える」。

Before researching it, I read it as “do not overuse your favorite technique.” でもKaplan、Maslow、Einstellung research、chess eye-trackingまでたどると、it becomes a more uncomfortable claim.

The deepest risk is not repeating one tool. **The tool can begin deciding what counts as the problem, where attention goes, and where search stops.**

So the answer is not to throw away the hammer or collect a hundred more tools. 道具を選ぶ前に、“what is happening here?” and “what would have to change for us to call this solved?”を言葉にする。Then, occasionally, put the first tool down and look again.

A strong expert is not someone without a hammer.

金づちを使いこなしたうえで、**still able to ask whether the thing in front of them is actually a nail**――そこまで含めて専門性なのだと思う。
