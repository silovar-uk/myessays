# 浦和レッズは、6日後に同じ問題を解き直す。東京V戦は岡山戦の「追試」になる

## Seven league games, zero draws｜最初の5試合は11-15、直近2試合は2-2。Where Urawa stand before Tokyo Verdy

Urawa Reds have not drawn a league match this season. Not once.

Three losses, then three wins, then another loss. Written as a sequence: **LLLWWWL**. Seven matches, 13 goals scored and 17 conceded. Across Urawa's league games, the scoreboard has moved 30 times.

Tomorrow's opponent, Tokyo Verdy, are 0-4-3 with three scored and nine conceded. Four of seven matches were draws, and their games contain only 12 total goals. By simple arithmetic, Urawa matches have produced 2.5 times as many goals as Verdy matches.

[J.League official: Urawa Reds](https://www.jleague.jp/club/urawa/)
[J.League official: Tokyo Verdy](https://www.jleague.jp/club/tokyov/)

Very roughly, this is **a team whose matches keep moving against a team whose matches keep stopping.**

Kwi Jae Cho does not reduce that contrast to 'Urawa attack, Verdy defend.' In his September 17 press conference, he was careful not to define either team only by goals for and against. The deeper question is whether Urawa can choose the speed of the match for themselves.

[Urawa Reds official: Kwi Jae Cho press conference, Sep. 17](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/topteamtopics/245682/)

---

## 1. FACT｜Urawa are slowly shrinking games that used to explode

Across seven matches, Urawa simply look high-scoring and high-conceding. Split the sample into five games and two, and another pattern appears.

The first five were 3-4 against Gamba Osaka, 1-4 against Hiroshima, 1-3 against Machida, 3-2 against Yokohama F. Marinos and 3-2 against Fukuoka. Total: 11 scored, 15 conceded. Those matches averaged 5.2 total goals.

The last two were 1-0 against Kashima and 1-2 against Okayama. Total: two scored, two conceded. The average match total fell to 2.0 goals.

[J.League official: Urawa Reds fixtures and results](https://www.jleague.jp/club/urawa/)

Two matches are nowhere near enough to declare the defence fixed. But Kashima was Urawa's first clean sheet of the league season. Cho described winning 1-0 while still trying to play proactively as a new kind of experience for the team.

[Urawa Reds official: Kwi Jae Cho after Kashima](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/topteamtopics/245452/)

That changes how progress should be measured. Early Urawa could move matches dramatically, but they were also moved dramatically by opponents. The next phase looks less like 'score even more' and more like learning how to win while reducing that swing.

---

## 2. RETEST｜The problem Urawa failed to solve against Okayama returns six days later

What makes the Verdy match interesting is that the opponent changes, but the problem presented to Urawa may look remarkably familiar.

Against Okayama, Urawa had 60% possession but only 10 shots. Okayama had 14. Harumi Minamino equalised in the 27th minute, but Urawa conceded in 90+11 and lost 1-2.

[J.League official: Urawa vs Okayama](https://www.jleague.jp/match/j1/2026/091303/)

Cho said after the match that Urawa could not break through Okayama's strength in duels. Before the Verdy match, he added a more specific diagnosis: Okayama become more numerous near their own penalty area, and Urawa neither pulled that block out enough nor finished enough attacks before it could settle.

In the same press conference, Cho noted that Verdy may use a shape similar to Okayama's.

[Urawa Reds official: Kwi Jae Cho press conference, Sep. 17](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/topteamtopics/245682/)

It would be crude to say Verdy simply equal Okayama. Their defending, personnel and possession ideas differ. But Urawa's homework is similar: **can they finish before the opponent rebuilds numbers, and if the block is already set, can they move it enough to create a real gap?**

That makes this more than Matchweek 8. It is a retest six days after Okayama. The main subject is not only opponent analysis; it is Urawa's ability to correct themselves.

---

## 3. TEMPO｜The answer is not simply more possession, but less wasted time after winning the ball

Urawa's 60% possession against Okayama can look like control. But time on the ball and time that hurts an opponent are not the same thing.

Verdy average 43.4% possession and 0.4 goals per league match. They are not a high-possession team by the numbers. Yet both away games, Okayama and Cerezo Osaka, finished 0-0: 180 away minutes without conceding.

[J.League official: Tokyo Verdy](https://www.jleague.jp/club/tokyov/)

Cho describes Verdy as compact defensively and well positioned after regaining the ball so they do not immediately give it back, while looking for gaps. If Urawa attack too slowly, Verdy can recover numbers. If Urawa force vertical passes carelessly, they may hand Verdy exactly the transition they want.

So the choice is not 'counterattack or possession.' The mature version is faster before the block forms, patient after it forms, then fast again when movement creates a new gap. Urawa's attacking level can be measured by how often they make that switch correctly.

---

## 4. SYSTEM｜What Kashima showed was not 'three at the back,' but a concept that survives shape changes

Urawa used a back three at Kashima and won 1-0. From outside, it looked like a major system switch. Cho later explained that the larger concept had not changed; positions with and without the ball were adjusted to the opponent, schedule and available players.

[Urawa Reds official: Kwi Jae Cho press conference, Sep. 11](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/topteamtopics/245611/)

That matters against Verdy too. Similar opponent shapes do not mean copying the previous lineup. The important questions are functional: who jumps to the wing-back, who runs forward immediately after a regain, and whether Urawa keep enough players high instead of becoming heavy behind the ball.

Formation is not the answer; it is the map used to reach an answer. Kashima showed defensive adaptability. Verdy asks whether Urawa can use the same adaptability to solve an attacking problem.

---

## 5. AVAILABILITY｜Before tactics, the available lineup may keep changing until matchday

During the Okayama week, multiple Urawa players and staff became unwell. Cho says some are recovering before Verdy, but being healthy enough for daily life is not the same as being ready for a match, so decisions still depend on medical and physical assessments.

Danilo Boza also went off injured after 15 minutes against Okayama. The J.League preview likewise describes Urawa's final selection as difficult to predict.

[Urawa Reds official: Kwi Jae Cho press conference, Sep. 17](https://www.urawa-reds.co.jp/topteamtopics/245682/)
[J.League official: Urawa vs Tokyo Verdy preview](https://www.jleague.jp/match/j1/2026/091904/)

This cannot be separated as mere bad luck. Cho has also said that, through league and Emperor's Cup matches, more players have gained real match experience, and that this needs to become squad depth rather than just emergency usage.

So the test is not only whether Urawa can field their best eleven. **Can whoever plays share the same triggers for acceleration and control?** Over a long season, a concept that survives personnel changes matters beyond one result.

---

## 6. PLAYERS｜Urawa's 13 goals come through several doors, not one single scorer

Urawa have scored 13 league goals. Harumi Minamino leads with three, Matheus Savio has two, while Kenta Nemoto, Kotaro Hayashi, Takuro Kaneko, Ryoma Watanabe, Hiiro Komori and Ado Onaiwu have also scored.

[J.League official: Urawa season scoring data](https://www.jleague.jp/match/j1/2026/082905/stats/player/score/?source=season)

Chance creation is also distributed: Kaneko leads Urawa with 17, Savio has 11 and Watanabe 10. At least by the available numbers, the final pass is not concentrated in one player either.

[J.League official: Urawa vs Tokyo Verdy](https://www.jleague.jp/match/j1/2026/091904/)

Against a compact opponent such as Verdy, the task is less 'wait for one player to break everything' and more about connecting multiple entrances: pull the block wide then return inside, attack depth immediately after a regain, or use set plays. Urawa have routes. The question is whether they choose the right one before and after Verdy settle.

---

## 7. WATCH｜Tomorrow, watch the seconds before an attack starts, not only the final shot count

The most revealing moment against Verdy may be the instant Urawa win the ball.

Can the first forward pass come quickly? Are runners threatening behind the last line? If that route is closed, can Urawa resist forcing it and stretch the opponent again?

The second moment comes after the first goal. Urawa have won 3-2 twice and also 1-0. A shootout is no longer their only route to three points. Against Verdy, the next question is whether they keep opening the game after scoring or use the opponent's urgency to control it.

Compressed to one viewing cue: **look for the moments when Urawa change the tempo on purpose.** That is where we can see whether the team is moving from momentum toward control.

---

## 8. DISCOVERY｜The next step is not winning chaos, but choosing whether the match becomes chaotic at all

Before looking closely, the Verdy match looked simple: can Urawa properly beat a winless 19th-place opponent?

After looking across Urawa's seven matches, that question feels too small.

In the opening three games, the more matches moved, the more Urawa themselves broke. Then they survived two 3-2 wins against Yokohama FM and Fukuoka, and won 1-0 at Kashima. Okayama exposed another problem: attacking after an opponent is set. The results zigzag, but the types of match Urawa have experienced are expanding.

Verdy are a useful opponent for measuring the next step. They defend compactly and have not conceded away. If Urawa only attack with energy, they may simply extend the kind of 0-0 game Verdy already know how to play.

So tomorrow, the most interesting version is not merely 'attacking Urawa.' It is **Urawa who accelerate when the moment is there, stop when it is not, and can accelerate again.**

Six days after Okayama, a similar problem appears again. Do Urawa write the same answer, or has the method itself changed? Beyond three points, the Verdy match can show how quickly Cho's Urawa are learning.
