# One performer on screen, 740 people behind it――カバー株式会社の会社・組織を知る

*Understanding COVER through people, workflow, revenue, studios, and governance*

*Information cutoff: October 14, 2026. Verified disclosures, illustrative workflows, and interpretation are kept distinct.*


*First essay：[ホロライブ入門――Why do virtual idols stream games?](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/hololive-idol-gaming-streaming-relationship-system)。*

## 一年に2万9,000本以上。画面に一人、会社に740人

A VTuber stream usually shows one animated performer. Behind that seemingly simple screen sits a company with around 740 employees. COVER reported more than 29,000 livestreams from affiliated creators in the fiscal year ended March 2026. Averaged over 365 days, that is roughly 79 streams a day—one every 18 minutes in a purely arithmetic sense. Streams are not actually scheduled at equal intervals. Still, the contrast between one visible avatar and the production scale behind it is striking.

The previous essay examined hololive as a system that builds shared history between performers and fans through everyday streaming. This sequel turns the camera around. Who does what inside COVER so that one person can keep streaming, record music, hold concerts, sell merchandise, and reach viewers abroad? The point is not to memorize departments. It is to trace how human creativity becomes an organization capable of operating at scale.

Information cutoff: October 14, 2026. Employee figures are identified by reporting date and definition. Official job categories are distinct from the analytical workflow map developed in this article. We do not invent an internal organization chart or undisclosed departmental headcounts.

## そもそもカバーとホロライブは、どのような関係にあるのか

The legal company is カバー株式会社（COVER Corporation）. 「ホロライブプロダクション」is a talent and content brand operated by that company. Affiliated performers appear under the brand; the corporation signs contracts, hires staff, purchases equipment, and bears business responsibilities. A brand name and a legal entity are not interchangeable. The distinction matters whenever we discuss revenue, employment, intellectual property, or accountability.

COVER was founded in 2016. It experimented with VR table tennis and streaming software before ときのそら began activities in 2017, the female group hololive launched in 2018, and HOLOSTARS followed in 2019. The company listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth Market in 2023. This is not an entertainment agency that merely added software later: technology is part of its origin. That history explains why talent management and capital-intensive studio engineering live under the same roof.

Sources: [COVER company profile](https://cover-corp.com/company) and [FY2026 securities report](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026).

## 740人と735人と169人――同じ「社員数」でも意味が違う

A quarterly investor presentation put employee headcount at 740 at the end of June 2026. The statutory securities report recorded 735 regular employees as of March 31, with average age 34.8, average tenure 2.8 years, and average annual compensation of 6.7 million yen. Those numbers differ by five, but the difference cannot automatically be treated as five new hires. The reporting dates—and possibly the counting conventions—must first be aligned.

The 735 figure means active regular employees. A further 169 appears in parentheses, but that is an annual average of temporary workers, including contract and part-time staff and excluding dispatched workers. Therefore, simply adding 735 and 169 to claim that exactly 904 people worked on March 31 would combine a point-in-time count with an annual average. In corporate research, definitions matter more than a satisfying total.

The recruitment site also displays 741 total employees. It is an official recruiting metric, not necessarily the same measure as the statutory count of regular employees. Rather than forcing a false reconciliation, this essay keeps the figures separately labeled: 735 regular employees at the fiscal year-end, and 740 on the June 2026 quarterly presentation.

Sources: [FY2026 report](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026), [official recruitment facts](https://cover-corp.com/recruit/newgraduate/business), and [June 2026 headcount report](https://gamebiz.jp/news/430942).

## 部署を覚える前に、四つの職種群を知る

COVER's recruitment materials divide jobs into four groups: creative and technology development (30%), production operations (25%), live events and media mix (32%), and back office (12%). The displayed shares add up to 99%, consistent with rounding. Importantly, these are job categories, not a disclosed hierarchy of divisions, departments, and teams.

Creative and engineering staff build avatars, content, broadcast systems, and software. Production operations support performers and coordinate activities. Live and media-mix functions turn that activity into shows, products, and partnerships. Back-office functions include people operations, finance, legal work, and corporate planning. Twelve percent may seem surprisingly large until we remember the company handles intellectual property, global transactions, and listed-company disclosure. Corporate control is part of production quality, not separate from it.

A useful mental model is four functions: create expression; support the performer; expand expression into business; and make the company sustainable. They overlap within projects rather than forming a single assembly line. The key discovery is that talent management is only one part of a much larger operating system.

Sources: [Official job categories](https://cover-corp.com/recruit/newgraduate/business) and [career roles](https://cover-corp.com/recruit/career ).

## 「部署」と「売上分野」は別物――四つの稼ぎ方を重ねて読む

The financial report describes four revenue activities: streaming/content, concerts/events, merchandising, and licensing/collaborations. Yet the company reports a single accounting segment: the VTuber business. Four revenue categories do not imply four independent businesses, four subsidiaries, or four neatly separated departments. Management reporting and organization charts answer different questions.

For the year ended March 2026, revenue was 49.33 billion yen: streaming/content 9.137 billion, concerts/events 9.247 billion, merchandising 23.747 billion, and licensing/collaborations 7.198 billion. Minor rounding differences occur. Merchandise represented roughly 48% of revenue. This is the first real surprise: a company famous for people appearing on screens earned its largest revenue category from physical and digital products sold beyond the screen.

Revenue is not profit. Merchandise involves manufacturing, inventory, warehousing, shipping, and sales fees. A licensing deal may generate revenue without COVER producing every unit itself. But which is more profitable in a given project depends on production costs, royalty splits, and other expenses. Revenue by category is public; profit by each category is not disclosed in the same way. We should not invent departmental profitability.

Source: [FY2026 securities report: activities and revenue categories](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026).

## 社員136人のいるスタジオ――仮想の身体は物理的な設備で動く

The securities report locates 599 regular employees at head-office and other facilities and 136 at studio facilities, as of March 2026. This is a workplace breakdown, not a department chart. It does not mean that every studio employee is filming at all times, or that office-based employees do no production. But it establishes that virtual entertainment depends on a substantial physical workforce.

The studio facilities carried a book value of roughly 3.246 billion yen, compared with 1.568 billion yen for head-office and other facilities. Book value is an accounting figure, not total construction cost or market valuation. A large studio opened in 2023 uses motion-capture equipment, video switching, audio, and recording systems to produce multi-person three-dimensional concerts. The invisible cameras and cabling are very real parts of a supposedly virtual business.

A motion-capture studio is not merely a room to rent. It coordinates tracking, character models, backgrounds, lighting, sound, and streaming in real time. Panasonic Connect's case study documents the use of its video-production technology inside COVER's studio. Some capabilities are built internally; others are purchased from specialist suppliers and integrated. That hybrid is characteristic of a company sitting between entertainment production and engineering.

Sources: [Facilities in the FY2026 report](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026), [Panasonic Connect case study](https://connect.panasonic.com/jp-ja/case-studies/cover-corp), [Official studio feature](https://note.cover-corp.com/n/nefc047ddc6ee).

## 試しに「新衣装のお披露目」を一件の仕事として解剖する

Follow a hypothetical new-outfit reveal rather than memorizing department names. This is an analytical example based on public job descriptions, not a verified account of any particular broadcast. First, the performer's ideas and the creative concept must be reconciled with scope, budget, and launch date. What looks like an exciting announcement to fans initially exists as a specification and a schedule.

The work then moves through illustration, rigging or model integration, facial expressions, movement testing, and technical checks. External illustrators and model makers require clear contracts, specifications, and acceptance criteria. Marketing coordinates the announcement; streaming staff test the system; the talent manager connects the performer to other teams. A single fan reaction—“That outfit is adorable”—sits atop design, contracts, project management, engineering, and promotion.

The hardest question is who decides that the work is finished. An illustration can look complete but fail in motion; a functioning model may still miss the performer's intention. Without clear review stages and decision rights, changes can continue indefinitely. Creative quality and fair treatment of external makers turn out to depend on the same project-design discipline.

Sources: [Production project-manager role](https://hrmos.co/pages/cover-corp/jobs/0000494), [Official job descriptions](https://cover-corp.com/recruit/newgraduate/business), [JFTC order](https://www.jftc.go.jp/houdou/pressrelease/2024/oct/241025_cover.html).

## グッズ一つにも、企画から在庫まで別の組織が動く

Consider selling a commemorative acrylic stand. The artwork may be simple, but the product workflow is not: concept development, rights checks, manufacturing, quality control, demand planning, warehousing, e-commerce listing, customer support, and returns. COVER advertises jobs in merchandising, supply-chain management (SCM), and e-commerce (EC). The organization must move physical objects, not only digital emotions.

COVER describes its direct-to-consumer shop, retail distribution, international delivery, and warehouse consolidation in its annual report. Coming up with a desirable product and delivering it reliably are separate competencies. Demand may spike after a stream or announcement; too much inventory is costly, but too little causes lost sales and disappointed customers. The logistics spreadsheet is part of the fan experience.

The April–June 2026 quarter exposed this issue. Management said product availability and operational transitions had affected merchandise sales. To a fan, the problem is simply “I wanted it, but it was not available.” To management, it raises questions about forecasting, procurement, fulfillment, and storefront operations. Increasing popularity is insufficient if the supply chain cannot convert interest into delivered orders.

Sources: [COVER career roles](https://cover-corp.com/recruit/career), [FY2026 merchandise operations](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026), and [Q1 financial briefing](https://finance.yahoo.co.jp/quote/5253.T/financials?styl=presentation).

## 493億円を稼いでも、493億円が自由になるわけではない

In FY2026, COVER recorded 49.33 billion yen in revenue, 25.823 billion yen in cost of sales, 16.45 billion yen in selling, general and administrative expenses, and 7.056 billion yen in operating profit. Product production, concerts, creator revenue sharing, people, and outsourced services all cost money. The gap between revenue and profit is another view of the machinery required to support a global entertainment business.

Revenue increased from the previous fiscal year, yet operating profit fell from 8.001 billion yen to 7.056 billion yen. “Higher revenue, lower profit” does not by itself prove that fandom weakened. Product costs, the timing of concerts, staffing, and technology spending all affect margins. Reading the business properly requires comparing cost growth and sales mix, not just counting viewers or headlines.

On September 24, 2026, the company raised its April–September operating-profit forecast from 1.93 to 3.5 billion yen, citing stronger licensing related to a new game and a different merchandise-driven cost mix. But this was an earnings forecast, not a finalized half-year result as of this article's cutoff date. Forecasts and reported results must be kept in separate columns.

Sources: [FY2026 financial statement](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026) and [September 24 earnings-guidance revision](https://japanir.jp/company/company-5253/ir/5253-20260924-01_wp_earnings_guidance/).

## 社員数は増え続けるのか――急成長から配置の見直しへ

Regular headcount rose from 537 in March 2024 to 679 in March 2025 and 735 in March 2026. The annual increase slowed from 142 to 56. The quarterly investor metric stood at 740 in June 2026, one fewer than in the prior quarter on that same series. The data suggest a shift in managerial attention: less emphasis on endless hiring and more on how existing people and teams are deployed.

A February 2026 investor presentation explicitly discussed the risk of functional silos and plans to improve internal cost allocation and cross-department project operations. This is not primarily about whether individuals are talented. Sales, creative, engineering, and legal staff can each perform well while the overall project slows because priorities, budgets, and decision rights are misaligned. At scale, coordination becomes a core production technology.

Adding people does not automatically make projects faster. More specialists also create more coordination interfaces. Clear ownership, decision rules, shared production infrastructure, and transparent budget allocation start to matter. The next productivity question is not only “Can we make more?” but “Can we make reliably, with the same workforce, without shifting hidden costs onto performers and external creators?”

Sources: [FY2025 report](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2025), [FY2026 report](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026), [February 2026 investor presentation](https://www.kabutec.jp/pdf/202602/140120260212557046.pdf), and [headcount trend](https://gamebiz.jp/news/430942).

## 年収、国籍、定着率――仕事の中身は社員の数字にも出る

Regular employees averaged 34.8 years of age, 2.8 years of tenure, and 6.7 million yen in annual compensation. That salary figure includes bonuses and additional pay; it is an average, not a distribution of individual earnings. It cannot tell us what a junior manager, engineer, lawyer, or new graduate earns. Short average tenure can also arise from rapid hiring, not only high turnover.

The annual filing reported one-year employee retention of 91.3%, an employee engagement score of 64, and a foreign-national employee share of 14.3%. Stated goals were 90% retention, 72 engagement points, and 20% foreign-national representation. The recruitment site separately lists 133 foreign-national staff. Without harmonized dates and definitions, we should not recompute or claim a contradiction. Even “people metrics” are not immune to measurement problems.

COVER also describes distinct career paths for specialist professionals and people managers. That is a recognizably technology-company approach. An excellent 3D artist is not necessarily best deployed as a personnel manager. Rewarding specialist depth separately from supervisory authority helps preserve creative capability while allowing teams to scale.

Sources: [FY2026 employees and human capital](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026) and [recruitment data](https://cover-corp.com/recruit/newgraduate/business).

## タレントは社員なのか――会社との契約を取り違えない

Affiliated performers should not be counted as ordinary full-time employees. The securities report describes a “Virtual YouTuber Basic Agreement” under which COVER commissions creator activities and grants access to company-owned character assets and software. The company pays creators a contractual share of relevant revenues. Actual percentages are not disclosed; we cannot assume all performers have identical terms.

Nor do all talents perform the same work. Some emphasize everyday streaming, others music, events, or collaborations. Managers support those activities, but the corporation does not own a person's life. This business combines company-managed character intellectual property with a real performer's skills, time, and choices. Contract renewal, workload, changing creative direction, and graduation therefore become central organizational questions.

Two simplistic views fail: that a performer is easily replaceable because a company manages the character, or that the performer alone produces all value. Long-term collaboration among talent, technical production, operations, and commercial teams is what sustains the enterprise. Seen this way, the 740-person figure describes a support environment, not a machine for replacing people.

Sources: [FY2026 material contracts](https://edinetdb.jp/company/E38478/text?fy=2026) and [the first essay](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/hololive-idol-gaming-streaming-relationship-system).

## 上場企業の「守り」は、炎上対策という一語では終わらない

COVER became a listed company in 2023. Listing brings financial reporting, internal controls, audits, disclosures, and supplier obligations. Legal and finance teams are not merely people who file paperwork after a creative project. They help determine whether the project can operate responsibly. The company's governance page discusses external oversight and auditing.

The most concrete failure was the October 2024 recommendation and guidance from Japan's Fair Trade Commission. It found that COVER required 23 subcontractors producing illustrations and 2D/3D models to perform 243 rounds of unpaid rework after delivery, in violation of subcontracting rules. It also addressed payment delays. This was not merely an online controversy; it was an officially documented compliance failure.

Translated into operations, the issue concerns initial specifications, change requests, acceptance criteria, payment deadlines, and ownership of decisions. These procedures can feel slow, especially under creative pressure. But skipping them may simply export costs to smaller suppliers. Good concerts require good contracting practices. The lesson extends well beyond entertainment.

Sources: [Japan Fair Trade Commission](https://www.jftc.go.jp/houdou/pressrelease/2024/oct/241025_cover.html) and [COVER governance information](https://cover-corp.com/ir/corporateGovernance).

## 2026年の経営課題は「もっと社員を採る」だけではない

In 2026, COVER ended its standalone virtual-world service Holoearth while looking for ways to reuse its technology in existing entertainment activities. Brand renewal, talent projects, music, and games continued. From outside, this can look like endless expansion. Internally, it requires selection: what to continue, what to close, what to integrate, and where scarce specialist time should be spent.

The September 2026 forecast revision made the trade-offs visible. COVER maintained its April–September revenue forecast at 22.27 billion yen while raising expected operating profit to 3.5 billion yen. Games and licensing exceeded expectations; merchandising mix and costs evolved differently. The same revenue total can produce a different profit result depending on what is sold. Management means allocating scarce people and money, not simply finding more popular ideas.

Three questions matter next: how much production can be standardized; how much talent support must remain individually tailored; and how can fair supplier treatment be guaranteed? Standardization and individuality are in tension. Supplier protection prevents that tension from being resolved by shifting costs outward. Organizational maturity will be visible in how these three objectives coexist.

Sources: [September 2026 guidance](https://japanir.jp/company/company-5253/ir/5253-20260924-01_wp_earnings_guidance/), [quarterly briefing](https://finance.yahoo.co.jp/quote/5253.T/financials?styl=presentation), and [part one](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/hololive-idol-gaming-streaming-relationship-system).

## 少しやりすぎた分析――仮の業務地図を作ると、何が測れるか

A slightly over-engineered exercise helps here. Imagine a matrix with seven outputs on one axis: talent projects, regular streams, 3D concerts, music, merchandise, brand collaborations, and international distribution. On the other axis, place six capabilities: talent support, creative, technology, sales/marketing, legal/finance, and fulfillment/customer care. At each intersection, record tasks, decision owners, approvals, outside vendors, budgets, and deadlines. This is not COVER's actual reporting chart; it is an analytical map that generates useful questions.

For a 3D concert, measure elapsed time from concept approval to release, separating production hours from waiting time at handoffs. For merchandise, analyze unmet demand, forecast accuracy, stockouts, and replenishment lead time—not just sold units. For talent support, streaming volume alone is a weak proxy; workload sustainability and individual goals also matter. Detailed internal KPIs for these questions are not publicly available.

We cannot allocate 740 employees mechanically across those 42 cells. Staff work across projects, external contractors contribute, and workloads change over time. Understanding an organization is not the act of putting every person in a box. It is identifying where work connects—and where accountability can fall through the gaps.

## 結局、画面に映らない人たちは何を作っているのか

Return to the opening question: why does a seemingly solitary virtual performer exist within a company of hundreds? Not because 740 employees gather around each livestream. They support a continuous business of daily streams, milestone concerts, products, contracts, and global distribution for a large community of fans.

The company builds technology, supports people, turns creative property into products, and must repair its procedures when they fail. If everyday streaming creates memories for fans, organizational work preserves the conditions under which those memories can continue. The screen still shows one performer. But once you know what sits behind the screen, the invisible work is difficult to unsee.
