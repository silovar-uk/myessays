# Don’t Chase the Zone. Build the Rhythm First
## 集中の入口は「次が読める時間」にある

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
To study how people enter “the zone,” imagine wearing an EEG cap, listening to music, and brisk-walking on a treadmill for ten minutes. 字面だけ見ると、かなり特殊な朝のルーティンである。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A 2024 study had 20 college students walk under three conditions: fast-tempo music, slow-tempo music, and no music. Self-reported flow scores were higher with both fast and slow music than with no music, while the difference between fast and slow music was not significant.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So the clean story—“faster song, deeper zone”—does not survive the data. むしろ妙なのは、速さの勝負にならなかったことだ。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That raises a better possibility: what matters near the entrance to flow may be less “exciting music” than **a time structure in which the next moment becomes slightly predictable**.

本稿では日常語の「ゾーン」を、心理学で研究されるフロー状態（flow state）に寄せて扱う。ただし、they are not treated as perfectly identical terms. Nor is there an established general law saying that rhythm directly causes flow. The narrower question is this: **instead of trying to summon flow itself, can rhythm help arrange some of the conditions that come before it?**

> **Information checked through: 30 September 2026**
>
> This essay separates flow research, temporal expectation, sensorimotor synchronization, and background-music research. 「研究で観察されたこと」「本稿の解釈」「実践上の提案」を混同しない。In particular, results from motor tasks are not treated as direct proof for writing or knowledge work.

[Zhang et al., “The effect of music tempo on movement flow”](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1292516/full)

## 1. Flow is not “more concentration”; it is a state supported by conditions / フローは条件から立ち上がる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
When we want to enter the zone, the instinct is to command the inside of the mind: “focus harder,” “remove distractions.” しかしフロー研究の古典的整理は、その気合いより先に活動側の条件を見る。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The best-known antecedents are **challenge-skill balance, clear goals, and immediate, unambiguous feedback**. More recent frameworks stress that these are not the whole story; autonomy, self-efficacy, skill, interest, and intrinsic motivation also matter.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A 2024 perspective in Communications Psychology argues that reliably inducing flow in the laboratory is still methodologically difficult and warns against reducing the phenomenon to challenge-skill balance alone. A systematic review and meta-analysis of flow and performance found a cross-study correlation of about 0.31. That is an association, not proof that flow unidirectionally causes high performance—or vice versa.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
つまり、flow is not a mental volume knob. It emerges when task difficulty, the next target, feedback, skill, and motivation fit together well enough for absorption to develop.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Once we see it that way, the practical question changes from “How do I empty my mind?” to **“How do I keep the next move and its feedback from breaking apart?”**

[A framework for neurophysiological experiments on flow states](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-024-00115-3)  
[Harris et al., “A systematic review and meta-analysis of the relationship between flow states and performance”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1929402)

## 2. Before the zone, there is a period that is not yet the zone / 手前には準備時間がある

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If flow depends on a configuration of conditions, entry may be better understood as a process in which those conditions come together—not as a single switch.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In 2022, Roberta Antonini Philippe and colleagues interviewed 10 athletes and 12 musicians in detail about remembered flow episodes. Their qualitative analysis described three phases: preparation, entry into flow, and exit from flow. Preparation included warm-up, attentional focus, bodily feelings, and emotional state; entry included goal setting, high involvement, control over performance, and positive emotions and sensations.
<!-- level:3 role:qualification -->
Of course, a retrospective qualitative study of 22 performers cannot establish a universal sequence. 研究者自身も、flow entryの時間的な動態はまだ十分に分かっていないとする。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Still, the useful shift is that the study gives flow a **pre-flow period**. People do not teleport into maximum absorption; they warm up, narrow attention, set a goal, and move deeper into the activity.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Instead of trying to reproduce “the zone,” it may be more practical to reproduce **the runway that leads toward it**.

[Antonini Philippe et al., “Achieving Flow: An Exploratory Investigation of Elite College Athletes and Musicians”](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.831508/full)

## 3. Rhythm’s special power is not “making you focus,” but pre-deciding “when” / リズムは「いつ」を先に決める

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
What might rhythm add to that runway? To answer, it helps to step away from flow research and look at temporal perception.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
When regular events repeat, attention can be oriented toward the moment when the next event is expected. This is called **temporal expectation**. リズムは「何が起こるか」を教えなくても、「いつ起きそうか」を狭められる。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
In a 2021 dual-task experiment, people responded faster to a target after regular than irregular visual rhythms. Under a simultaneous working-memory load, the benefit remained in the fast 500-ms condition but weakened in the slow 3,500-ms condition. The authors interpreted fast-rhythm temporal expectation as relying more on automatic processing, while slower timing appeared to require more controlled processing.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This is not a recommendation to run office work at 500-ms intervals. 課題も刺激も限定された一研究で、数字を理想BPMへ変換する根拠はない。The useful idea is narrower: regularity can reduce the need to decide “when is next?” from scratch every time.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Rhythm is not a spell that deepens attention. It is better understood as **a mechanism that prepares attention for a moment before that moment arrives**.

In an earlier essay, I used sensorimotor-synchronization research to examine how people do not merely react to a metronome but anticipate the next beat. 今回はその先へ進む。The value of predictable beats is not only timing accuracy; **some of the work of deciding “next” can be delegated to temporal structure**.

[Xu et al., “Effect of Tempo on Temporal Expectation Driven by Rhythms in Dual-Task Performance”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8692373/)  
[Bruno Repp, “Sensorimotor synchronization: a review of the tapping literature”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16615317/)  
[前稿「リズムに頼るのは、ズルではない――乗れるまでは、まずゆっくり」](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/rhythm-scaffold-start-slow)

## 4. Flow and rhythm meet in the action-feedback loop, not necessarily in music / 接点は「一手→反応→次の一手」

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Now we can connect the two literatures. ただし、ここからはdirect causal evidenceではなく、複数研究をつないだ**本稿の解釈**である。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Flow has antecedents such as clear goals, immediate feedback, and challenge-skill balance. Rhythm can help predict timing and bind repeated actions into a temporal pattern. Put them together and rhythm becomes broader than music: **one action → one response → the next action**, turning over without long gaps.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
For writing, that might be “read one paragraph → write one sentence of meaning → read the next.” For analysis: “inspect one chart → note one finding → move to the next.” For implementation: “change one thing → test → observe.” 拍子記号はないが、行動と返事が短い間隔で反復する。
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
In such loops, the goal shrinks to the next move, feedback returns after each move, and difficulty can be adjusted before the chain breaks. Rhythm does not replace flow’s antecedents; it may help keep them **temporally continuous**.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The rhythm that matters at the entrance to the zone may be less an audible beat than **the cycle by which one action calls the next**.

This also connects to the earlier essay about counting and micro-rules. 数に集中効果があるという話ではない。A small rule can create a stretch of time in which “next” is already known. Flow, rhythm, and micro-rules are different concepts, but they meet at one design principle: **make the undecided future one move smaller**.

[「数えているだけなのに、少し落ち着く――小さなルールは『次』を決めてくれる」](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/#/essay/counting-simple-rules-next-step-rhythm)

## 5. The fast-music experiment actually weakens the “faster is better” story / 速ければよい、ではなかった

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
Return to the treadmill experiment and a reason appears to separate rhythm from raw musical speed.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
For self-reported flow, both fast music versus no music and slow music versus no music showed statistically significant differences. But fast versus slow did not. The paper also measured EEG; here, we avoid building a strong neurophysiological story from those signals and use only the subjective-flow comparison.
<!-- level:3 role:qualification -->
The sample was small and the task was ten minutes of brisk walking. 運動の結果を読書、執筆、企画、会議へそのまま一般化できない。Preferences, exercise intensity, and familiarity add further complications.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
Still, the absence of a clear fast-over-slow advantage is useful. It pushes us away from a simple arousal story—“more stimulation means more flow”—and toward a question about whether movement and sound form a sustainable temporal pattern.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
At minimum, the experiment leaves room to think of the entrance to flow not as **strong stimulation**, but as **a cycle that can keep going**.

## 6. Re-research makes “play music to focus” look dangerously simple / 音楽は逆に邪魔にもなる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If we stop here and say “then play music whenever you work,” the argument becomes sloppy. Background-music research contains clear cases where silence performs better.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
A 2023 experiment found that music with lyrics impaired verbal memory, visual memory, and reading comprehension relative to silence, with roughly small effect sizes. Instrumental music in that study did not credibly improve or impair performance. Another short-term-memory study found poorer serial recall with irrelevant music than silence, with tempo variation adding further disruption.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
On the other hand, a study of preferred background music during a low-demand sustained-attention task found more self-reported task-focused states and less mind-wandering. Yet reaction time and reaction-time variability did not significantly improve over silence.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So “feeling focused” and “performing better” are not identical, and music’s effect depends on the task. 歌詞を言語処理へ重ねれば、rhythmを得る代わりに別の言語情報を同じ作業空間へ流し込むことになる。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
That is why music and rhythm must be separated. **For knowledge work, the best rhythm can even be silent.** What matters is a predictable cycle of action and feedback, not the existence of a song.

[“Should We Turn off the Music? Music with Lyrics Interferes with Cognitive Tasks”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162369/)  
[“Irrelevant music: How suprasegmental changes of a melody’s tempo and mode affect the disruptive potential of music on serial recall”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7381464/)  
[“The effect of preferred background music on task-focus in sustained attention”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8357712/)

## 7. In practice, don’t “create flow”; externalize the first three cycles / 最初の三周だけリズムを外に置く

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
From here on, this is a **practical proposal**, not an experimentally validated flow intervention. The aim is not to guarantee flow. It is to reduce early “what next?” friction and start an action-feedback cycle.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
僕なら、最初の数分だけ使う入口を“three-cycle runway”として設計する。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
1. **Define one cycle** — Pair an action with its feedback, such as “read one paragraph → write one sentence.”  
2. **Repeat the same order three times** — Do not chase speed; make the next move predictable.  
3. **Fade the external cue** — If you used a timer, count, or music, remove it after three cycles.  
4. **See whether cycle four starts naturally** — If it does, continue. If not, revise the cycle size instead of blaming concentration.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
The point is not “achieve three cycles.” It is to test whether an externally supplied order becomes an internal sense of next. 合図を消した瞬間に止まるなら、まだrhythmを借りている。If the loop survives, the work cycle itself has at least begun to carry momentum.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Designing an entrance is less about exciting yourself and more about **making work move one step ahead without requiring a fresh decision every time**.

For writing: “one heading → one piece of evidence → one implication.” For research: “one source → one verified fact → one new question.” For implementation: “one change → one check → one line of notes.” 大きな目標を小さくするだけでなく、**同じ形で戻ってこられる一周**にするのがrhythmとしての要点になる。

## 8. Test the entrance friction, not whether you “got into the zone” / 自分に効くかは入口を測る

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
If you test this idea on yourself, avoid a single verdict like “I was in flow today.” Flow is multidimensional, and an everyday rating need not pretend to be a validated scientific scale.
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Instead, try the same kind of low-risk task for 12 minutes under two conditions. A: work as usual. B: fix the “action → feedback” loop for the first three cycles, then continue freely. 別の日には順番を入れ替え、数回繰り返す。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Record only four things: (1) time until the first uninterrupted five-minute stretch, (2) number of self-initiated diversions, (3) number of stalls caused by “what next?”, and (4) a 0–10 immersion rating after 12 minutes. This is not a validated flow measure; it is an observation of entrance friction.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
If B raises immersion but not progress, rhythm may be affecting experience more than performance. 逆に没入感が同じでも「次」で止まる回数が減るなら、effectはflowより前のdecision frictionに現れている可能性がある。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
Separating arousal, attention maintenance, and next-action clarity lets us measure **the entrance problem hidden inside the big word “zone.”**

## 9. The zone may begin when “next” no longer needs a verbal explanation / 無心より、次を説明しなくてよくなる

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
I began with a simple thought: maybe rhythm matters for entering the zone. Before the research, “rhythm” in that sentence was close to music—play something energetic, build momentum, push concentration upward.
<!-- level:2 role:analysis -->
The evidence complicated that picture. Flow is not one switch; goals, feedback, challenge, skill, motivation, and other conditions matter. There can be a preparation phase before entry. Rhythm can make “when” predictable. Music, meanwhile, can help or hinder attention depending on the task.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
So “getting into rhythm” changes meaning. 必要なのは必ずしも音を足すことではない。One action produces feedback, that feedback calls the next action, and the cycle becomes short enough that you stop narrating every next move to yourself.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
The conclusion, then, is not “rhythm causes flow.” It is this: **rhythm can make the immediate future one beat more predictable before flow has any chance to appear.**

Fast music on a treadmill does not deliver mastery on demand. むしろfastとslowに明瞭な差がなかったことの方が、今回の問いには都合がよい。

You do not have to begin at full concentration.

One move. See the response. The next move becomes visible.

もう一周する。

When the cycle begins to outrun self-instruction and your hand moves toward the next step before you have to negotiate with yourself, that may be the kind of period we later describe as “I was in the zone.”

**Don’t get focused first and then find the rhythm. Build a rhythm you can enter first.**

## Evidence boundary / 調査上の線引き

- **Fact / 事実**: major flow antecedents; the observed flow-performance association; the preparation phase reported in qualitative interviews; temporal-expectation findings; and the results of the cited background-music studies.
- **Interpretation / 解釈**: treating “action → response → next action” as a rhythm that may help keep flow antecedents temporally continuous.
- **Proposal / 提案**: the three-cycle runway and 12-minute self-comparison. Neither is an established psychological intervention.
- **Not established / 言えないこと**: that one tempo induces flow for everyone, that music generally improves knowledge work, or that flow is a one-way cause of better performance.
