# 新規事業は「アイデアを作る仕事」ではない
## 不確実性を安い順に壊す、七つの関門

New-business meetings often look strangely good with sticky notes.

百枚の付箋、fifty customer interviews, a huge market-size slide, and a beautifully detailed spreadsheet. By the end of the meeting, everyone has a strong feeling that work happened. ただし、売れるかどうかだけは、まだよく分からない。

Sticky notes are innocent. But sticky notes do not pay.

Strategyzer’s 2026 Innovation Evidence Scale makes this gap almost cruelly visible. In its framework, interviews belong to evidence about what people say. Five interviews and a million interviews are still the same category; more data can raise confidence within that method, but it does not magically become evidence of what people actually do.

That is uncomfortable because activity is easy to mistake for progress. 百人に聞いた。Sixty slides were made. A prototype was revised three times. All of that can be real work. But the key question in a new business is not “How much did we do?” It is “Which unknown became known, and by how much?”

Steve Blank defines a startup as a temporary organization searching for a repeatable and scalable business model. 既存事業がknown modelをexecuteするのに対し、新規事業の初期はmodelそのものをsearchする。When we confuse the two, we start building precise budgets around unknown assumptions and demanding delivery against repeatability that has not been discovered yet.

The thesis of this essay is simple. **New-business building is not mainly the work of producing more ideas. It is the work of listing the assumptions required for the business to exist, attacking the most fatal and least-tested assumptions cheaply, and increasing the size of the bet only as evidence becomes stronger.** Ideas are the entrance. Uncertainty management is the core.

## 1. 新規事業は「小さな会社」ではなく、事業モデルを探す組織である

The first mistake is treating a new business as a small version of an existing business. 人数が少ないだけで、planning, budgeting, development, and sales should follow the same logic. That assumption imports execution tools into a search problem.

Blank’s Customer Development makes a sharper distinction. An established company executes a known business model; a startup searches for a repeatable and scalable one. 探索中はcustomer, problem, price, channel, usage frequency, and acquisition methodまでhypothesisになりうる。A detailed number in a plan is not the same thing as a validated premise behind that number.

You can type “10,000 customers × ¥3,000 per month” into a spreadsheet in three seconds. でも、who pays, why they abandon the substitute, how you reach them, and whether you can serve them at 10,000 customers are not revealed by adding more cells. A formula calculates assumptions; it does not validate them.

So an early-stage deliverable is better judged by the gap between **what is known and what is still unknown** than by the beauty of a finished plan. 計画を捨てるのではない。We change its job: from a promise about the future into an inventory of assumptions that must be tested.

Sources: [Steve Blank, “Why Companies Are Not Startups”](https://steveblank.com/2014/03/04/why-companies-are-not-startups/) ／ [Steve Blank, “The Customer Development Manifesto”](https://steveblank.com/2012/03/29/nail-the-customer-development-manifesto/)

## 2. 最初に作るべき成果物は、企画書より「成立条件の一覧」である

So where should search begin? Not with one hundred clever ideas. Start by decomposing the idea into **what must be true for it to work**.

Strategyzer recommends turning an idea into testable assumptions and prioritizing the riskiest ones across desirability, feasibility, and viability. 発見駆動型計画法（Discovery-Driven Planning）のRita Gunther McGrathとIan C. MacMillanも、new venturesではdangerous implicit assumptionsを表へ出し、checkpointsでlearningに合わせてplanを更新することを重視する。

Suppose the idea is a video-analysis service for local sports facilities. 成立には、painful unmet problem, a decision-maker with budget, higher value than current alternatives, lawful and stable access to footage, acceptable analysis cost, and customer economics that survive acquisition costsなどが要る。If one fatal assumption collapses, a beautiful interface will not rescue the business.

This creates a practical priority rule. Do not research every assumption equally. Start with **assumptions that would kill the business if false × assumptions with the weakest evidence**. 新規事業のspeedは、tasksを速く回すことより、致命的なwrong assumptionへ早く到達することにある。

Sources: [Strategyzer, “How to Test Your Idea: Start With the Most Critical Hypotheses”](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/how-to-test-your-idea-start-with-the-most-critical-hypotheses) ／ [Columbia Business School, “Discovery-Driven Planning”](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/discovery-driven-planning)

## 3. 顧客に千人聞いても、「買った」という証拠にはならない

After assumptions comes a harder question: what counts as knowing? 調査量とevidence strengthを混同すると、a team can stay extremely busy without becoming much more certain.

Strategyzer’s 2026 scale moves roughly from plans and desk research, to what people say, to reactions to an artifact, to low-stakes action, meaningful-stakes action, and finally real-market behavior. The crucial line is between “say” and “do.” 同じinterview methodの中で件数を増やすことはconfidenceを高めうるが、speech evidence自体をbehavior evidenceへ変えるわけではない。

Saying “Yes, I want this” is cheap. メール登録、time spent in a trial, internal approval, a Letter of Intent（LOI）, a reservation deposit—each adds some cost or commitment and makes the distance between words and behavior more visible. None of these proves final success. Evidence is not a verdict; it is material for making the next bet less irrational.

Another trap is **whose evidence** you have. 利用者が喜んでもdecision-makerがbudgetを動かさなければ止まる。A buyer can sign while users never adopt. So do not compress everything into “the voice of the customer.” Record whether the evidence tests the user, payer, decision-maker, or operator hypothesis.

**New-business research is not collecting information. It is collecting evidence strong enough to change the next decision.** 件数は重要。ただし、evidence categoryを一段強くすることも同じくらい重要である。

## 4. MVPは「小さい製品」ではなく、一つの仮説を壊す装置として使う

Once a team wants stronger evidence, the next sentence is often, “Let’s build an MVP.” ここにもtrapがある。If Minimum Viable Product（MVP）means only “a smaller finished product,” a mini development project begins before the test question is clear.

Eric Ries’s Lean Startup centers on Build-Measure-Learn and validated learning. MVP is used to gain important learning with minimal effort. Strategyzer similarly argues that an experiment artifact should be designed for learning; it does not have to be a miniature of the final product.

If the unknown is demand, you may not need six months of back-end automation. 人が手でserviceを代行するconcierge test、a landing page, or a limited manual pilot can sometimes reveal whether customers act. If technical performance is the biggest unknown, then a technical prototype may come first. The shape of the MVP should follow the most dangerous assumption, not the product category.

This is not permission to build badly. **Do not build features that do not buy learning.** MVPのminimumはproduct sizeではなく、次のcritical decisionを変えるために必要なexperiment sizeと捉えるほうが、searchの筋がよい。

Sources: [Knowledge at Wharton, “Eric Ries on the Lean Startup”](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/eric-ries-on-the-lean-startup/) ／ [MIT Orbit, “What is the Lean Startup framework?”](https://orbit-kb.mit.edu/hc/en-us/articles/204857066-What-is-the-Lean-Startup-framework/) ／ [Strategyzer, “Designing Strong Experiments”](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/designing-strong-experiments)

## 5. 収支計画は最後の飾りではなく、最初から逆算して無理筋を露出させる

Customers wanting something does not automatically make it a business. ここで新規事業は、fun customer discoveryから急にarithmeticへ戻される。

Discovery-Driven Planning uses a reverse-income-statement logic: start from the required outcome and work backward into revenue, price, customers, costs, and capabilities. The purpose is not precise forecasting of a distant future. **It is to expose the assumptions that must be true for the economics to work.**

Imagine a business aiming for ¥100 million in operating profit with only a 20% gross margin. 必要売上は一気に膨らむ。If annual revenue per customer is ¥100,000, required customer count jumps; then sales capacity and acquisition cost push the required economics again. “The market is huge” is dragged down into concrete acquisition and delivery requirements.

The value of the spreadsheet is less about predicting the future than about **finding impossible combinations early**. Price up, cost down, retention up, customer changed—数字がassumptionの接続部を露出させると、the next research question becomes visible. Only then does the spreadsheet become a search tool.

## 6. 七つの関門に分けると、「次に何を調べるか」が見える

Put the pieces together, and a new business is easier to manage as evidence gates than as “plan→develop→launch.” 本稿では、the literature aboveをintegrateしたpractical scaffoldとして七つに分ける。

Gate 1 is **strategic hypothesis**: why now, why us, and what growth opportunity? Gate 2 is **problem existence**: who struggles, when, how painfully, and what do they use today? Gate 3 is **solution reaction**: when shown something concrete, do they understand it as a solution? Gate 4 is **action and commitment**: registration, trial, internal approval, reservation, contract—does behavior get heavier than words?

Gate 5 is **business economics**: do price, margin, acquisition cost, retention, and delivery capacity connect? Gate 6 is **repeatability**: can acquisition, delivery, and retention recur without heroic work by the founder or internal superstar? Gate 7 is **scale and organization**: can authority, talent, technology, legal constraints, capital, and links to the core support growth without breaking the model?

The point is not to pass each gate exactly once. Gate 4 fails? Go back to the problem. Gate 5 fails? Rework customer, price, or delivery. Gate 6 fails? Convert personal heroics into a process. **The gates are not a progress meter; they are a map for locating where to go back.**

The weekly questions therefore change. Not “What percent is complete?” but “Which gate is weakest now?”, “What is the lightest experiment that can test it?”, and “Would that result change a continue/pivot/stop decision?” この三つが答えられると、weekly reviewはstatus reportからlearning designへ変わる。

Sources: [Strategyzer, “The Innovation Evidence Scale”](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/business-project-succcess) ／ [Rita McGrath, “Discovery Driven Growth”](https://www.ritamcgrath.com/book/discovery-driven-growth/) ／ [Stage-Gate International, “Discovery-to-Launch Process”](https://www.stage-gate.com/about/stage-gate-innovation-performance-framework/discovery-to-launch-process/)

## 7. 社内新規事業では、会社の強みが同時に「摩擦」にもなる

Corporate ventures start with assets a startup may not have: customers, brand, technology, people, channels, and capital. これだけ見るとeasy modeに見える。The problem is that the organization owning those assets often runs on a different logic.

Charles A. O’Reilly III and Michael L. Tushman’s work on the ambidextrous organization argues that exploitation of existing businesses and exploration of new ones may need different structures, processes, and cultures. ただしexploration unitを完全に孤立させると、core assetsを使えない。Separation and senior-level integration have to coexist.

McKinsey’s 2026 work on corporate business building similarly describes conflicts between core-business incentives, governance, culture, and the speed or autonomy a venture needs. 初期ventureへmature P&L responsibilityをそのまま当てれば、learning spendが“loss”に見える。But if “innovation freedom” means economics are never tested, weak learning can survive forever.

The implication is not simply “put the new-business team in another room.” **Change the ratio of freedom and discipline by stage.** Early on, protect customer contact and experiment speed. As evidence grows, connect legal, quality, finance, channels, and core assets more tightly. 既存企業の強みは最初から全部載せるequipmentではなく、appropriate stageで接続するamplifierとして扱う。

Sources: [O’Reilly & Tushman, “The Ambidextrous Organization”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15077368/) ／ [McKinsey, “The CEO’s critical role in building new businesses”](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-ceos-critical-role-in-building-new-businesses)

## 8. 段階審査では、売上より「この段階で増えるべき証拠」を見る

Once you introduce gates, governance itself can become heavy. 資料のための資料、approvalのためのapprovalが増えると、a system built to protect exploration starts slowing it down.

The real value of stage-gate logic is staged investment decisions. McKinsey also describes short review cycles, measurable hypotheses, stopping weak ideas, and reallocating resources. 重要なのは、every stageをsame metricで測らないこと。A team still exploring the problem cannot be judged with the same revenue expectations as a mature operation.

In this essay’s proposal, early gates ask whether evidence for the problem is stronger; then whether customers accept meaningful commitment; then whether unit economics work; then whether acquisition and delivery repeat. 売上はもちろんimportantだが、discount, personal connections, and bespoke work can create one-off revenue. The question is whether a repeatable causal engine sits underneath the number.

Set stop conditions in advance as well. Not a universal “sell to ten companies in three weeks,” but hypothesis-linked conditions: if budget-holding buyers repeatedly refuse to fund the problem, revisit the customer; if acceptable pricing cannot produce gross margin, change the delivery model. **A stop is not automatically a defeat. It can be knowledge bought early to avoid a larger loss.**

Sources: [Stage-Gate International, “Discovery-to-Launch Process”](https://www.stage-gate.com/about/stage-gate-innovation-performance-framework/discovery-to-launch-process/) ／ [McKinsey, “The three building blocks of a successful venture factory”](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/business-building/our-insights/the-three-building-blocks-of-a-successful-venture-factory)

## 9. 実務では「六週間で完成」ではなく、「六週間で次の不確実性を特定する」

Knowing frameworks is weak if Monday morning still has no next move. そこで本稿のproposalとして、a six-week exploration cycleへ落とす。This is not a universal standard. It is a starting template for relatively lightweight digital-service experiments; hardware, regulated industries, medical contexts, and safety-critical domains need technical, legal, ethical, or clinical work earlier and often in parallel.

Week 0: compress the business hypothesis onto one page—customer, problem, substitute, value, price, channel, delivery, major costs—and mark roughly three “if false, we die” assumptions. Week 1: desk research plus exploratory interviews on problem and current behavior. Week 2: show the lightest artifact. Week 3: seek a relevant action signal such as registration, trial, approval, reservation, or payment.

Week 4: reverse the economics—price, margin, required customers, acquisition cost, delivery capacity—and look for impossible combinations. Week 5: run a tiny real operation, even if part of it is manual, from acquisition to value delivery. Week 6: decide continue, pivot, or stop, then pick the most dangerous assumption for the next cycle. 毎週のtaskを固定するのではなく、**evidenceを一段強くする**ことだけを固定する。

The operating sheet should therefore include more than task, owner, and due date. Add hypothesis, current evidence, next experiment, decision threshold, and the decision that would change. 工程表がlearning ledgerへ変わる。The comforting “80% complete” number disappears, but unknowns become harder to hide.

MIT’s Lean Startup explainer also notes that lightweight iteration fits software more easily, while hardware and heavily regulated contexts require adaptation. 六週間というnumberに忠実である必要はない。Protect the sequence instead: **before making a larger bet, ask whether a cheaper test can attack the dangerous assumption.**

Sources: [MIT Orbit, “What is the Lean Startup framework?”](https://orbit-kb.mit.edu/hc/en-us/articles/204857066-What-is-the-Lean-Startup-framework/) ／ [McKinsey, “How to launch a new business: Three approaches that work”](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/how-to-launch-a-new-business-three-approaches-that-work)

## 10. 新規事業の正体は、アイデアを増やすことより、未知を減らすことである

Back to the sticky notes.

A wall full of them is not bad. アイデアを広げるphaseでは必要なこともある。Interviews, market sizing, and financial models are also necessary. The problem begins when we line them up only by activity volume and mistake motion for reduced uncertainty.

Before researching this, I expected “how to build a new business” to be mainly a craft of finding good problems, designing good solutions, and testing quickly. 複数のframeworkを重ねると、a drier picture appeared. Customer Development says go outside the building; Lean Startup loops learning; Discovery-Driven Planning exposes assumptions; stage gates slice investment; ambidextrous organization separates search from execution.

Different vocabularies point in one direction: **build a system that does not pretend to know what it does not know.**

In a new business, the strongest person is not the one who predicts the future correctly. 仮説が外れる前提でcheaply returnでき、当たったときだけbetを大きくできる人とorganizationが強い。Creativity matters, but discipline not to fall in love with the idea matters too.

So when the next meeting produces one hundred sticky notes, ask one question before adding note 101.

**“Which assumption would hurt us most if it were false, and has almost no one tested yet?”**

Peel that note off the wall, and the new business finally begins.
