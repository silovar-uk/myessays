# 「誰が詳しいか」に詳しい人は、何者なのか

## 何にも一番詳しくないのに、質問の行き先だけ全部知っている人

Some workplaces contain a strange kind of expert. 「この契約は誰？」「昔の経緯は？」「この数字の定義は？」と聞くと、本人は答えを知らないのに、almost instantly a name comes back. 「契約はAさん。ただし海外案件ならBさん」「その数字はCさんだけど、2024年以前の定義変更はDさん」。A human transfer guide.

Trying to place this person on an expertise chart is awkward. 法務のexpertではない。データのexpertでもない。They do not know every historical case either. Yet when they are absent, questions begin stopping in odd places. All the specialists still exist, but **the team cannot reach them**.

In the earlier essays, I treated transactive memory as a map of “who knows what.” 今回は、そのmapの持ち主を調べたい。Does everyone need the same map, or can a team work better when one person is unusually good at knowing where knowledge lives?

Before researching this, I thought “well-connected coordinator” was probably enough of a label. ところが研究には、ほとんどそのままの役割が**catalyst――触媒**として出てくる。Not the person who creates every answer, but the person who makes answers meet each other.

## 1. 「誰が何を知るか」を、一人だけ知っている方が強いことがある

A study by Julija Mell, Daan van Knippenberg, and Wendy van Ginkel removed one quiet assumption from TMS research. 「誰が何を知っているか」というmetaknowledgeは、team membersにだいたい均等に分布していると考えられがちだった。What if metaknowledge itself is unevenly distributed? Source: <a href="https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2012.0589">Mell, van Knippenberg & van Ginkel</a>.

The researchers compared teams in which metaknowledge was concentrated in one central member with teams in which it was distributed across members. 112チームのlaboratory decision-making experimentで見つかったのは、単純な「centralized is better」ではない。When task information created **high coordination demands**, centralized metaknowledge gave teams a performance advantage. The central member could act as a catalyst for information retrieval and integration.

That finding is slightly uncomfortable. 組織では「誰でも分かる」「属人化しない」「information should be democratized」が善に見えやすい。But keeping a complete expertise map inside every member also carries an updating cost. When many fragments have to be connected quickly, one person who knows “A to B, then C and D” may reduce coordination friction.

So the person from the opening is no longer merely helpful. **Knowing who knows what can itself become a specialization in knowledge integration.** Holding knowledge and routing knowledge are different jobs.

## 2. それは「知識」ではなく「配車」の専門性だった

Imagine organizational knowledge as a delivery network. 専門家はwarehousesで、legal knowledge、customer history、system specifications、field experienceが別々の場所にある。A question is a pickup request. Adding more warehouses does not help if nobody knows where to send the truck.

The catalyst in Mell's model is not simply the biggest warehouse. どのwarehouseへ行き、どのknowledge fragmentsを組み合わせるべきかを知る**routing layer**に近い。The person may not possess the final answer, but can start the right retrieval process and bring several specialties onto the same problem.

This contribution is easy to lose in performance evaluation. 成果物だけを見ると、配車係は「何も作っていない」ように見える。They send the question to A, connect A's answer to B, B points to C, and the final document is delivered under C's name. The routing work disappears inside the finished output.

From a TMS perspective, however, that invisible work is part of the memory system. **Organizational knowledge has not only content and addresses, but route selection: where should this question go now?** Having a map and operating traffic on that map are separate capabilities.

## 3. 全員一致していても、住所録が間違っていたら意味がない

Two concepts that sound similar need to be separated. チーム全員が「法務ならAさん」と一致していることと、本当にAさんがその領域で最適なexpertであることは同じではない。The first is **consensus**; the second is **accuracy**.

John Austin studied mature, continuing organizational groups in 2003 and measured TMS through knowledge stock, specialization, consensus about who knows what, and the accuracy of those perceptions. これらを組み合わせたTMSは、group goal performanceやinternal/external evaluationsと正に関連していた。The paper also highlights accuracy as an important predictor. Source: <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14516250/">Austin (2003)</a>.

A healthcare study makes the distinction even sharper. Xi Zhu and Douglas Wholey followed 26 interdisciplinary mental-health teams and separated TM accuracy――who correctly recognizes the experts――from TM consensus――how much members agree about who the experts are. Accuracy was related to team outcomes, while consensus did not show the same significant relationship. Source: <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6232407/">Zhu & Wholey (2018)</a>.

Everyone can agree on the same wrong directory. むしろ一致しているほど、nobody thinks to check it. **A good TMS is not merely a shared map; it is a map that continues to track real expertise.** A knowledge dispatcher needs an updated capability map, not a popularity-based phone book.

## 4. 住所録は、能力だけでなく「それっぽさ」からも作られる

How do people learn that someone is “the expert”? 実績を見る、話を聞く、一緒に仕事をする。But when those signals are missing, humans fill gaps with other cues. One of those cues is stereotype.

Andrea Hollingshead and Samuel Fraidin tested how gender stereotypes could shape expertise allocation in transactive memory. 参加者は実際には会わないassumed partnerとcollective memory taskに取り組み、the study used categories that reflected then-current stereotypes, such as cosmetics and soap operas versus sports and cars. Participants imagining an opposite-sex partner were more likely to allocate categories according to gender stereotypes and consequently learned more stereotype-consistent information themselves. Source: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-1031(02)00549-8">Hollingshead & Fraidin (2003)</a>.

The categories are from a 2003 laboratory study and should not be mechanically generalized to today's workplace. 重要なのはcategoryの中身ではない。It is the cycle: **an assumption about who should know something changes task allocation, and task allocation can later create the expertise the assumption predicted.** A rough map can begin making reality resemble itself.

That is the dangerous side of knowledge routing. 配車係がwrong mapを持てば、質問を誤配するだけではない。Work and learning opportunities repeatedly flow to the same people, while others never receive the experience required to become visible experts. **An expertise map does not only record expertise; it helps produce future expertise.** Therefore the map itself needs auditing.

## 5. リモートワークでは、専門性そのものより「専門性の見え方」が変わる

Many cues for expertise never appear in a formal profile. 「あの会議でこの論点を即答していた」「何度も助けてもらった」「隣で作業していて異様に詳しいと知った」。These tiny observations accumulate almost accidentally when people share physical and social space.

Chunke Su studied 208 people across 17 organizational groups in 2012. 他者の専門性をどれだけ正確にrecognizeできるかは、communication network上のdegree centralityと正に関連し、work remotenessとは負に関連した。一方、digital knowledge repositoriesを多く使う場合、the negative association with remoteness was weaker. Source: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650211433825">Su (2012)</a>.

This is not a result saying “remote work is bad.” 2012年の特定サンプルであり、today's tool environment is different. The more useful interpretation is that **the channels through which expertise becomes visible change**. If hallway and desk-side cues disappear, repositories, search, answer histories, and task records may need to carry more of that signaling work.

That changes the meaning of knowledge documentation. 答えそのものだけでなく、「誰がこのanswerを作ったか」「誰がrecently solved this class of problem」「誰が更新できるか」を残す。Then a repository stops being only a warehouse and becomes **a sensor for discovering expertise**.

## 6. せっかくなので「知識の配車センター」を三方式で比較する

Now push the research into three deliberately extreme organizations. Aでは全員が全員のexpertise mapを持つ。Bではone catalystが詳しいmapを持ち、questionsをrouteする。Cでは誰もreal expertise mapを持たず、org chartとjob titleを検索エンジン代わりにする。Real organizations are hybrids, but extremes expose failure modes.

A is resilient but expensive because everybody must keep learning the map. B handles complex routing well but magnifies the “stale map” problem if the catalyst leaves. C is easy to explain but risks confusing formal position with actual expertise. Across the research, the deeper difference is not simply information volume but **who is responsible for updating the routing function**.

My design bet would therefore be: **centralize the routing function when useful, but make its holders redundant.** 複数人でquestion triageを回し、searchable expertise directoryには肩書より最近解いた課題やprimary-information proximityを残す。When a question gets forwarded from one person to another, treat the forwarding history as data for improving the map. This is an evidence-informed design hypothesis, not a direct result of Mell's experiment.

The goal is not to eliminate all person-dependence. むしろ「誰が何を知るかを知っている」というrare capabilityだけは、一人の頭に閉じない。**Routing is expertise; therefore routing expertise also needs handover and audit.**

## 7. 組織の記憶には、内容・住所・配車係の三層がある

The first essay in this series argued that memory includes not only content but an address: “who should I ask?” 次に、人が入れ替わればdirectoryが古くなると分かった。AI adds conditional trust to each address. Hidden-profile research showed that even a correct address does not guarantee that information reaches discussion. This essay adds another layer: sometimes someone must decide **which address the current question should use**.

Organizational memory now looks at least three-layered. 第一はknowledge content。第二はwho holds itというaddress。第三は、current questionをどこへ送り、必要なら複数のaddressesを接続するrouting functionである。

Before this research, a person who “only knows who to ask” seemed secondary to a real domain expert. 今は逆に、その人が消えた瞬間に**experts may stop being discoverable as experts**と見える。The warehouses remain, but logistics can stop when the signs and dispatch desk disappear.

So what is the person who is expert at knowing who is expert? その人の本当の専門分野は「人」ではないのかもしれない。It is **the topology of knowledge**. And the next governance problem is not only maintaining a knowledge list, but asking: Who updates the map? What evidence supports it? Can the routes be reconstructed when the dispatcher disappears?
