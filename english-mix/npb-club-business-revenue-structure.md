# 球団は何によって成り立っているのか — What a Club's Business Actually Rests On
## 非上場のparent-company model・broadcast rights・Japan Series分配から、球団経営の土台を読む

第10回では、球団がドラフト・育成・契約更改・FA・トレードという5つのmechanismを、限られた支配下枠・時間・契約条件の中で組み合わせる様子を読んだ。

そこでCONSTRAINTとして扱った「契約」「budget」は、どこからやってくるのか。

第10回の最後に、こう問いを置いた。

**What does that club earn revenue from, and what actually makes running the club as a business possible in the first place?**

今回はここから始める。

> **Information basis: 2026年9月1日**
>
> The club ownership structure and the relevant articles of the baseball agreement are corroborated across multiple independent sources. Individual clubs' specific financial figures — revenue, profit, and so on — are not guessed at here, since most NPB clubs are unlisted subsidiaries that don't publicly disclose detailed financials.

---

## 1. SHORT ANSWER — Most clubs aren't built to stand on their own as profitable companies

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
NPBの球団経営を理解する出発点は、**most clubs are not designed around standalone profitability as an absolute requirement**という点である。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
The baseball agreement defines a club as "a stock company under Japanese law with capital of at least 100 million yen," and caps foreign ownership at under 49%. All 12 current clubs are unlisted, with a parent company holding most or all of the shares.
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
For example, the Yomiuri Giants are 100% owned by Yomiuri Shimbun Group Holdings, and the Tohoku Rakuten Golden Eagles are 100% owned by Rakuten Group. The Tokyo Yakult Swallows are split between Yakult Honsha and Fuji Media Holdings; the Yokohama DeNA BayStars between DeNA and TBS Holdings.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
つまり球団は、独立した投資家が利益を求めて所有する会社というより、**a subsidiary positioned as one business line of its parent**である。
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
この所有構造を知らないまま「球団経営」を読むと、黒字か赤字かだけで球団の良し悪しを判断してしまいやすい。

---

## 2. WHY A DEFICIT CAN BE FINE — the 1954 tax office notice

ここに、NPB球団経営を長く支えてきた税務上の仕組みがある。

An August 10, 1954 notice from Japan's National Tax Agency (Chokuho 1-147), "On the Treatment of Advertising Expenses Paid to Professional Baseball Teams," held that when a parent company in an industry like film, newspapers, or railways covers the deficit of its subsidiary baseball team, that spending — up to the amount of the deficit — can be treated as advertising expense and deducted in the fiscal year it's paid.

This notice is over 70 years old, yet it still exists on the National Tax Agency's own site today.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
この仕組みが意味するのは、**a club's deficit can, as a matter of tax treatment, function as its parent's advertising cost**という構造である。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
When a parent company covers its club's deficit, that spending can be booked as advertising expense within the limit of the deficit.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
That club names so often carry the parent's name or brand isn't unrelated to this structure. Getting covered in the news day after day is itself a form of advertising value for the parent.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
したがって、「球団は赤字でも構わない」という規範があるわけではないが、**not treating standalone profitability as the single overriding goal**が、他の産業の子会社と比べて成立しやすい。

ここで避けたい誤解が一つある。

"Parent-company model, so no effort needed" doesn't follow. The tax treatment and the actual work of building a competitive, popular, newsworthy team are two different things.

## 3. WHO EARNS WHAT — revenue is basically club-by-club

では、レギュラーシーズンの収益はどう発生するのか。

まず放送権について、Article 44 of the baseball agreement (broadcast permission rights) gives each club the right to freely authorize radio, television, cable, and internet broadcasting of its own **home games** during the regular season.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
つまりNPBの放送権は、**not a league-pooled-and-redistributed system, but a club-by-club one**である。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
This differs from a league structure where broadcast rights are centrally managed and the revenue is redistributed across clubs.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
As of 2026, the six Pacific League clubs share common streaming outlets — DAZN, Rakuten TV, Persol Pa League TV, Baseball LIVE — while Central League clubs each negotiate their own streaming deals, with no single shared service visible.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
このセ・パの違いの背景には、親会社の業種差がある。The Central League has historically included several clubs — the Yomiuri Giants, Tokyo Yakult Swallows, Yokohama DeNA BayStars, Chunichi Dragons — whose parent companies are media-related, and these clubs are said to have managed terrestrial broadcast rights relatively tightly. Pacific League parents tend not to be media companies, and the constraints on streaming are said to be comparatively looser.

入場料収入・スポンサー収入についても、基本的には各球団が個別に得る収益である。

第1回で見た「NPBの地図」も、ここでつながる。Home markets and attendance are literally the ground this club-by-club revenue stands on.

## 4. THE ONE EXCEPTION — only the Japan Series pools revenue at the league level

ここまでは「収益は球団単位」という話だったが、一つだけ例外がある。

**The Japan Series** is organized not by a club, but by NPB itself.

Revenue is pooled entirely into NPB first, then distributed according to a defined set of rules. Per expert commentary, the structure works roughly like this:

- Ticket revenue from the first four games feeds a player fund — about 28% goes to the players.
- From game five onward, players get no share, and NPB's own portion grows larger.
- There's no flat "championship prize" — player compensation centers on this ticket-revenue-based fund.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
つまりNPBの収益構造は、**a two-layer structure: club-by-club as the default, with the Japan Series as the one event pooled and redistributed at the league level**として読むことができる。
<!-- level:1 role:evidence -->
Regular-season broadcast rights, ticket revenue, and sponsorship all accrue club-by-club, while Japan Series revenue is pooled into NPB and allocated by a rule that shifts with the game count.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
This two-layer structure differs from a league that centrally manages broadcast revenue for the whole season and distributes it to every club. In NPB, outside of this one event, there's little visible mechanism that evens out revenue gaps between clubs.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
したがって、人気・地域規模・親会社の業種によって、球団ごとの収益基盤には構造的な差が生まれやすい。この差は、後述するように編成のCONSTRAINTへつながる。

これはNPB公式が「二層構造である」と定義しているわけではなく、シリーズ上の編集上の整理である。

## 5. CONNECTING TO EPISODE 10 — revenue structure sits behind the roster CONSTRAINT

第10回のRoster Construction Auditでは、CONSTRAINTの一つとして契約・budgetを扱った。

第7回では、

**PERFORMANCE ≠ SALARY**

という区別を扱った。Evaluation doesn't turn directly into contract terms — it passes through negotiation, rules, and information asymmetry on the way to becoming a contract outcome.

<!-- level:4 role:claim -->
ここまでの話をつなげると、契約更改やFA・トレードで実際にどれだけの条件を提示できるかという**the budget-shaped CONSTRAINT**は、突き詰めれば球団単位の収益構造（broadcast rights・ticket revenue・sponsorship、and support from the parent company）の上に乗っている。
<!-- level:2 role:description -->
Different clubs sitting on different revenue foundations means that even the same NEED — "we want to sign a good player" — can differ in which MECHANISMs are realistically available, and how often, and at what scale.
<!-- level:3 role:analysis -->
ただし、これは特定球団の予算規模を数値で比較する話ではない。Most clubs are unlisted subsidiaries that don't publicly disclose detailed financials, so specific figures aren't part of this article.
<!-- level:5 role:implication -->
重要なのは、**the "constraint on roster construction" doesn't fall from the sky — it sits on top of the club's business as a going concern**と理解することである。

## 6. WHAT CLUB BUSINESS DOES NOT MEAN

### Club business ≠ a simple profit-or-loss ranking

Since a parent's deficit-covering can be booked as advertising expense for tax purposes, judging a club's management purely by whether it posted a profit or a loss in a given year is a rough read.

### Club business ≠ working within a salary cap

As confirmed in Episode 10, NPB has no general salary cap of the North American kind. Different revenue foundations can still shape different starting conditions for roster construction, but there's no uniform institutional ceiling.

### Club business ≠ a league that splits revenue evenly

Outside the Japan Series exception, NPB doesn't appear to have a clear, even revenue-sharing system of the kind where the league centrally pools broadcast revenue and redistributes it to every club.

---

## 7. TAKEAWAYS

- Most NPB clubs are unlisted stock companies, subsidiaries with a parent company holding most or all of the shares (Articles 27 and 28 of the baseball agreement).
- A parent's spending to cover its club's deficit can be booked as advertising expense within the limit of that deficit (the 1954 National Tax Agency notice). This doesn't mean club management takes no effort.
- Home-game broadcast permission rights sit with the club, not a league-wide pool (Article 44). Central and Pacific League parent-company profiles differ, and so does how unified their streaming deals are.
- Only the Japan Series is organized by NPB itself, pooling revenue and distributing it under a rule tied to the game count.
- NPB's revenue structure reads as a two-layer system: club-by-club by default, league-level only for the Japan Series.
- The roster-construction CONSTRAINT (contract terms, budget) sits on top of this club-by-club revenue structure.
- Specific club-level revenue or profit figures aren't guessed at here — public disclosure is thin.
