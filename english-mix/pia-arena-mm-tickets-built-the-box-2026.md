# チケット会社がthe venueを建てた――ぴあアリーナMMの裏側

*Research date: October 24, 2026. 公的資料、会社の開示、設計会社・座席メーカーの記録を使ったdesk research。No site visit, original interviews or on-site measurements.*

**A ticketing company built the place where tickets get used.** チケット会社の「ぴあ」が、横浜のぴあアリーナMMを約100億円の初期投資で造った。That is a serious move from selling access to owning the infrastructure of live entertainment. [ぴあ開発資料](https://corporate.pia.jp/news/detail_mmnews_vol4.html)

Here is the stranger part. 2026年の有価証券報告書には、土地等の年間賃借料が**6億7,200万円**と書かれている。A building designed for moments of excitement carries costs every day, even when no one is singing. [ぴあ・有価証券報告書21頁](https://corporate.pia.jp/news/files/2025fy_yukasyoken.pdf)

![Pia Arena MM exterior](https://pia-arena-mm.jp/assets/images/about/top_main_image.jpg)

*Photo reference: [official Pia Arena MM site](https://pia-arena-mm.jp/about/index.html). Image remains hosted by its publisher.*

Is it just naming rights? いや。これは「ぴあ」という名前を他社の会場につけただけではない。ぴあ自身が建設・運営を単独主導した国内でも珍しい大型の民設民営施設である。[公式会場紹介](https://pia-arena-mm.jp/about/index.html)

## 守｜そもそもアリーナとは、音楽を聴くための「席」ではなく興行を成立させる「器」である

What is an arena? 多数の観客を収容する大きな屋内イベント空間。Some arenas are multipurpose sports venues; others are built mainly for music. 「ホール」は固定舞台と客席を持つことが多いが、names alone never define the actual building.

A concert is not simply a singer plus an audience. 主催者（promoter/organizer）が公演を企画し、制作側が照明・映像・音響などを手配し、会場運営者（venue operator）が施設と安全な利用環境を提供する。Ticket sellers are another part of this chain. Different roles, different revenue streams.

Pia Arena MM stands in **みなとみらい3-2-2、38街区**。It has one basement and four above-ground floors, a 12,000-square-meter site, and 23,139.81 square meters of total floor area. The maximum capacity is **12,141**, while a seated concert is roughly **10,000** and standing configurations can reach roughly **12,000**. ここでのcapacityは施設の上限。It is **not** the number of tickets available for every concert. Stage layout and production equipment change what can actually be sold. [公式概要](https://pia-arena-mm.jp/about/index.html)／[みなとみらいの建物データ](https://ymm21.jp/database/pia-arena-mm/)

Official travel times include about seven minutes from Sakuragicho or Minatomirai stations, eight from Shintakashima, and eighteen from Yokohama Station. Accessibility sounds great — but thousands leaving together also create pressure on nearby sidewalks. Access is a whole journey, not only the approach to the entrance. [ぴあの施設事業紹介](https://corporate.pia.jp/business/hall/)

## 守｜「ぴあ」の文字は命名権の広告ではなく、興行インフラを自分で造った足跡だった

Naming rights and private development are different concepts. **Naming rights** buy the right to place a name on a venue. **民設民営** means a private entity leads construction and operations. It does not automatically mean the company owns the land, organizes every concert, or takes every ticket payment. The distinction matters here. [ぴあ・ホール劇場ビジネス](https://corporate.pia.jp/business/hall/)

Pia began with the information magazine 『ぴあ』 in 1972 and moved into computerized ticket selling in 1984. First: discover an event. Then: buy the ticket. Finally: help provide the place. こう考えると、会社の事業拡張に一定の連続性が見える。ただし「創業当初から建設を計画していた」という証拠ではない。[2017年の関係者取材](https://hamarepo.com/story.php?story_id=6347)

The immediate reason was the **2016 venue shortage**. Major arenas faced renovations while Olympic preparations increased pressure on available dates. 横浜アリーナとさいたまスーパーアリーナの改修時期も重なった。Demand for concerts could exist, yet the number of available nights remained limited. In business terms, this was a **supply bottleneck**: demand was not enough because capacity was missing. [2017年のぴあ総研インタビュー](https://www.videor.co.jp/digestplus/article/3611.html)

Instead of merely waiting for other venue owners, Pia chose to build. The problem changed from "How do we sell more tickets?" to "How do we make more performances possible?" これは広告塔を持つ話ではなく、自分の事業が依存する供給条件へ踏み込む話だった。

## 守｜100億円の工事は、2020年4月に完成しても予定どおり開業できなかった

Timeline first. The plan was announced in July 2017, with construction starting that December. Early documents estimated about **¥10 billion** in initial investment. The planned opening was April 25, 2020, with Yokohama duo **ゆず** scheduled to perform. [2019年の開発発表](https://corporate.pia.jp/news/detail_mmnews_vol4.html)／[当初の公演案内](https://corporate.pia.jp/news/detail_mmnews_vol6.html)

Then COVID-19 arrived. The planned shows were canceled or postponed. The actual opening date became **July 10, 2020**, the anniversary of Pia's founding magazine. Yuzu performed "栄光の架橋" for an **advance-recorded video release**, not a packed public concert. Don't confuse the date of opening with the first normal public concert. [公式発表](https://pia-arena-mm.jp/news/626.html)／[ゆず公式](https://yuzu-official.com/contents/information/988b6c80-50f9-4af8-a9c1-71c657a83826)

Japanese has a useful word here: **こけら落とし**, an inaugural performance at a new or renewed venue. Here, its expected in-person form became a video. A building created to gather people opened when gathering people was restricted. The contradiction is more than irony: venue investors spend years upfront, while future revenue depends on events actually happening.

## 守｜音楽専用なのに固定舞台がない。客席の近さも、音の外への漏れ方も設計の仕事になる

A music-specific arena without a fixed stage? Yes. 音響設計を担った永田音響設計の記録によれば、この施設には通常のホールのような固定舞台や恒久的な舞台装置がない。Instead, the ceiling offers **132 rigging points** for temporary lighting, sound and stage equipment. Each point includes a way to check load. **Rigging（吊り込み）** means hanging production equipment safely from designated supports. [永田音響設計・技術記録](https://www.nagata.co.jp/news/news2012.html)

That reverses the ordinary intuition. Specialization need not mean a fixed layout. A concert-focused arena may specialize in the freedom to build a new stage for each artist.

![Seating and ceiling in Pia Arena MM](https://www.kotobuki-seating.co.jp/Portals/0/%E7%B4%8D%E5%85%A5%E4%BA%8B%E4%BE%8B%E8%A8%98%E4%BA%8B/%E3%81%B4%E3%81%82%E3%82%A2%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8A/04612_pia-arena-mm_0002.jpg)

*Photo reference: [Kotobuki Seating project documentation](https://www.kotobuki-seating.co.jp/projects/list/detail.html?pdid1=00264). Hosted by the original source.*

The flat-floor arena is surrounded on three sides by tiered stands. Ground-floor chairs can be moved in linked groups using wheels. That helps crews make room for equipment or another seating setup. ただし「客席が近い設計」と「全席から実際に見やすい」は違う。Sightlines depend on the artist's stage and lighting rig. [座席メーカーの解説](https://www.kotobuki-seating.co.jp/projects/list/detail.html?pdid1=00264)

Acoustics have a second challenge: **keeping sound inside**. A heavy roof tends to block sound better, yet adds weight and expense. Designers had to balance acoustic isolation, structural loads, and cost. 三重の防音シャッターや試験測定も記録されている。The measurements document a specific test, not a guarantee of zero noise during every future show. [永田音響設計](https://www.nagata.co.jp/news/news2012.html)

## 破｜「完売なら1億円」は正しくても、会場の売上が1億円とは限らない

Try this simple calculation: 10,000 tickets × ¥10,000 each = **¥100 million in face-value ticket sales**. Correct arithmetic; wrong venue accounting if you call all of that revenue for the arena. The promoter may pay artists and production teams, while venue operators receive contracted venue fees and possibly related food, facilities or premium-service income. Each actual agreement can differ. We have not inspected Pia's event-by-event contracts.

![Venue, organizer and audience: simplified money flow](https://silovar-uk.github.io/myessays/assets/pia-arena-mm-business-flow-2026.svg)

*Original explanatory diagram, **not** Pia's actual contracting structure. Annual rent figure: [2026 securities report, p.21](https://corporate.pia.jp/news/files/2025fy_yukasyoken.pdf).*

Now the fixed cost. The official 2026 filing lists **¥672 million per year** in rent for land and related leased property at Pia Arena MM. The same equipment table separately lists the building and facilities as corporate assets and no owned land value for this site. 賃借料は収益ではなく費用である。Owning the building and owning the land are different propositions.

A deliberately simplified thought experiment: **150 events/year → ¥4.48 million of annual land rent per event**, if allocated evenly; **200 events/year → ¥3.36 million**. These are not measured event counts or actual event costs. They deliberately exclude staffing, repairs, equipment, electricity, depreciation and financing. The lesson is about scale: even the land rent alone makes occupied dates meaningful. [有価証券報告書](https://corporate.pia.jp/news/files/2025fy_yukasyoken.pdf)

Pia said in 2021 that it expected to recover the investment over roughly **30 years**, despite pandemic cancellations. In 2026, its president wrote that weekend reservations were booked **two years ahead**. Those statements are useful evidence of management's outlook, but **not** a disclosed standalone profit figure for the arena, nor a measurement of weekday occupancy. [ぴあIR Q&A](https://corporate.pia.jp/ir/finance/qa/)／[社長メッセージ](https://corporate.pia.jp/corp/message/index.html)

## 破｜会場が足りなかった街に、会場が増えすぎる未来はないのか

There is a real counterargument: **what if building new venues creates oversupply?** Interestingly, Pia's own research representative raised that concern in 2017, noting that more arenas could become available after the Olympics. In other words, the developer acknowledged that today's bottleneck might become tomorrow's excess capacity. [2017年インタビュー](https://www.videor.co.jp/digestplus/article/3611.html)

The nearby K Arena Yokohama, opened in 2023, officially lists **20,033 seats** — significantly larger than Pia Arena MM. More seats are not automatically better. A show must match the artist's demand, production budget, preferred dates and desired audience experience. [Kアリーナ公式](https://k-arena.com/floor/)

My working hypothesis: the competitive unit is not simply venue capacity; it is **a workable concert on the right date with the right cost and audience experience**. That is a hypothesis, not a confirmed market finding. Testing it would require comparable rental costs, booked dates, setup days, actual sold tickets and audience feedback across venues.

## 破｜アリーナの成功と、近隣の暮らしの成功は同じではない

A show ends inside the building. The audience does not disappear there. Waiting, merchandise exchanges, toilets, and trips to the station spill into nearby streets and workplaces. In August 2023, Pia Arena MM published a warning that neighbors had raised many concerns about people lingering on sidewalks and office decks, obstructing passage, or using toilets in other buildings without using those facilities. [施設公式・2023年の注意喚起](https://pia-arena-mm.jp/news/3307.html)

This is not a joke and not proof of conditions in October 2026. It is documented evidence of a specific issue in 2023. Economists call impacts on people outside a transaction **externalities（外部性）**. Nearby stores may benefit from visitors; neighbors may bear congestion or noise. The size of those effects has not been measured in this article.

So a successful venue and a successful neighborhood are not necessarily identical outcomes. Venue operations must be evaluated beyond what happens in the audience seats.

## 離｜なぜここに1996年の「空飛ぶトマト」がいるのか

After reading all those financial and technical documents, I found an unexpected tomato.

The arena houses **『フライング・トマト（Flying Tomato）』**, a tile mural by illustrator **安西水丸**. It originated in 1996 at the Flying Tomato Cafe inside Osaka's Imperial Hotel. After renovation and restoration, it moved to the Yokohama arena. An actual artwork migrated from a hotel cafe to a concert venue. [施設公式・アート紹介](https://pia-arena-mm.jp/about/index.html)

Why mention it? Because a show lasts hours, but its venue exists across decades, just as a mural can move across buildings and eras. The arena accommodates momentary performances while accumulating physical investment and cultural history. **The event is temporary; the infrastructure has a memory.**

## 離｜アリーナを理解するには、集客装置でなく「都市の共同運営者」として見る

Back to the opening puzzle. Pia was not simply buying advertising space for its name. It was trying to solve a supply bottleneck in the live-entertainment business. But becoming a venue developer also meant accepting a different set of duties: ongoing rent, crowd safety, acoustics, neighborhood relations, and long investment horizons.

That is the shift: **from distributing information and tickets to operating part of the city**. The most revealing performance measures might not be concert ticket sales alone. They may include booked and usable days, profitability of the venue itself, load-in efficiency, the whole visitor journey, and the impact on neighbors.

From outside, the building looks like a large rectangular box. Inside are 132 rigging points, acoustic trade-offs and flexible seats. On the balance-sheet side is a ¥672-million annual land rent. And somewhere within it, a tomato that traveled from Osaka. The interesting part of this live venue may be everything that happens before and after the live show.

## 用語の整理｜この記事を読んだあとに使える言葉

- **Venue operator（会場運営者）**: the party managing the place and its facilities.
- **Promoter / organizer（主催者）**: the party responsible for making the event happen.
- **Naming rights（命名権）**: paid rights to put a name on a facility; distinct from building it.
- **Privately built and operated（民設民営）**: private-sector-led construction and operations, not necessarily land ownership.
- **Capacity（収容人数）**: the venue maximum, not tickets actually sold for an event.
- **Inaugural performance（こけら落とし）**: the first ceremonial show associated with a new venue.
- **Rigging（吊り込み）**: safely suspending sound, lighting or stage elements.
- **Sound insulation（遮音）**: limiting sound transmission, different from sound quality inside.
- **Utilization rate（稼働率）**: how frequently available facilities are used; denominator definitions matter.
- **Fixed cost（固定費）**: cost incurred even with few performances, such as land rent.
- **Externality（外部性）**: effects on outsiders, positive or negative.

## 調査手順と再検証で残った論点

The evidence path was: venue and city records → 2017 decision context → specialist acoustic and seating documentation → 2020 opening events → 2023 neighborhood notice → 2026 financial disclosure → clearly labeled scenario calculation.

Three corrections matter most: never treat maximum capacity as tickets sold, never describe the opening video as an ordinary audience concert, and never call annual rent either construction cost or revenue.

Still missing: **venue-only sales and operating profit, real booked and setup days, rental contracts, comparable audience outcomes, and neighborhood complaints over time**. Without them, strong claims about venue profitability or community benefit would be premature.
