# セルフリッジズで「15歳以上向けのちいかわ」が売られている
## ロンドンはなぜ日本の「かわいい」を受け入れられるのか

At London department store Selfridges, you can buy a Chiikawa sushi figure for £10. しかもproduct page says “suitable for years 15+.” A tiny character dressed as sushi, sold as a mystery item to teens and adults inside a premium department store. Written out plainly, it is a slightly strange scene.

Look around the shelf, though, and Chiikawa is not a random Japanese visitor. Selfridges’ collectables section already places Sonny Angel, Smiski, Sanrio, and Chiikawa in the same shopping grammar. Nearby, British plush brand Jellycat turns fish and chips into stuffed characters, while Squishmallows collaborates with Pokémon. It is a toy floor, but the audience is obviously larger than children.

At first I thought the story was simple: MINISO had followed its 2024 North American Chiikawa pop-ups with a London activation. 調べると、そこが違った。The 2026 Selfridges experience is led by Spiralcute and Jazwares, not MINISO. That correction makes the story better, because Chiikawa’s overseas expansion is not one retailer exporting goods. It is several companies and retail formats assembling a place where “cute” can be bought.

> **Information date: 2026-10-05**
>
> This essay checks public information from Jazwares, MINISO, Selfridges, Jellycat, and market-research company Circana. Selfridges inventory changes frequently, so the “shelf audit” here is a snapshot of public product pages viewed on October 5, 2026.

## 1. ロンドンのちいかわは、ミニソウではなく別ルートで上陸していた

The first thing to fix is the route itself. In autumn 2026, the immersive Chiikawa area at Selfridges Oxford Street arrived as part of the UK expansion by Spiralcute and Jazwares. Total Licensing describes a character-discovery wall, sushi-conveyor-style product display, photo opportunity, and a screen showing new video content.

Behind the display is a clear division of labor. Spiralcute manages artist-driven character brands including Nagano’s Chiikawa, and in 2026 it signed a multi-year agreement with Jazwares. Jazwares became master toy partner and global distributor outside Asia for Chiikawa, Mofusand, and Koupen Chan. The first Chiikawa toy lines outside Asia were scheduled for autumn 2026.

MINISO still matters; it is simply another route. In August 2024, MINISO and Chiikawa opened a U.S. IP-themed short-term pop-up at Tangram Mall in New York with nearly 100 products. By late 2025, MINISO’s Warsaw flagship also had a dedicated Chiikawa zone alongside Disney and Sanrio. So the pattern is not “MINISO takes Chiikawa everywhere.” It is multiple entry points: MINISO in some markets, Spiralcute + Jazwares + Selfridges in this UK activation.

The useful insight is not that the original assumption was wrong, but why the mistake was easy to make. Consumers see the store and the merchandise; behind them sit rights management, product development, manufacturing, wholesale, department stores, retail chains, and video distribution. Chiikawa’s Western expansion is not one export pipeline. **It is the same character world being inserted into several different retail infrastructures.**

## 2. セルフリッジズの棚は、すでに「大人が玩具を買う」前提でできている

Why Selfridges? To answer that, I did a slightly excessive shelf audit using its online catalogue. On October 5, 2026, the Chiikawa brand page listed ten products. It was not only large plush: there were decorative figures, mystery plush, and sushi mystery figures designed around collecting itself.

The age labels are even more revealing. The Selfridges-exclusive 15cm Kiramekko plush costs £25 and is marked 15+. The £10 sushi mystery figure is also 15+. Of course, the label alone does not prove that the products are “for adults”; safety classifications such as small-parts rules also matter. それでもKids / Toysというcategoryの中に15+の商品が複数あることは、“toy department”と“children’s product”の境界が一致しなくなっている一つのsignalになる。

The neighboring shelf confirms that this is not an exception. Selfridges’ collectables page includes Sonny Angel, Smiski, Sanrio, and Mofusand—small, displayable, photographable products that work well with mystery-box buying. 玩具売り場が、playing equipmentだけでなく、hobby, collecting, and self-expressionを買う場所へ広がっている。

That changes how we read Chiikawa’s arrival. It is not only that British consumers “understand” Chiikawa. **The buying behavior is already understood before the story is.** Small, affordable, multiple variants, surprise opening, desk display: consumers can participate before learning every narrative detail. Sometimes overseas expansion begins with translating the way of buying before translating the culture.

Sources: [Selfridges, “CHIIKAWA”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/cat/chiikawa/) ／ [Selfridges, “Kiramekko Vinyl Soft Toy Assortment”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/product/chiikawa-kiramekko-vinyl-soft-toy-assortment-15cm_R04692043/) ／ [Selfridges, “Sushi Figure Blind-Box Toy Assortment”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/product/chiikawa-sushi-figure-blind-box-toy-assortment-4-6cm_R04692038/) ／ [Selfridges, “Collectables and Blind boxes”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/cat/kids/toy-shop/collectables/)

## 3. ジェリーキャットは、ぬいぐるみを商品から「体験」へ変えた

Jellycat is important because it shows what London’s plush market has already learned. The British brand does not stop at animals; it turns bread, drinks, vegetables, and meals into soft characters. At Selfridges, it has run a Fish & Chips experience since 2024, using a familiar British food ritual as retail theatre.

This is more than putting products on a shelf. On the fourth-floor Toy Shop, a retro van and counter turn purchase into a small performance, with staff packaging characters like food. Exclusive items include a fish pie, a can of pop, a London pigeon, and a seagull charm. ぬいぐるみを買う行為が、order, receive, photographというmini-eventになる。

Calling this simply “popular with adults” misses the mechanism. Jellycat did not merely make adults feel less embarrassed about buying plush. **It gave adults a reason to travel, experience, photograph, and collect.** The toy is connected to food culture, gifting, tourism, social posting, and collecting rather than remaining only a child-play object.

That is why Chiikawa’s sushi-conveyor-style display at Selfridges matters. It does not explain Japanese lore in a long paragraph; it makes the act of touching the product into a tiny story. 海外展開ではcharacter translationだけでなく、**a ritual you can join in one minute at the store** can carry the world too.

Sources: [Jellycat, “Jellycat Fish & Chips Experience at Selfridges, London”](https://jellycat.com/jellycat-fish-chips-experience-london/) ／ [Selfridges, “Events & Experiences at Selfridges London”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/stores/events/london-events/) ／ [Total Licensing, “Spiralcute at Harrods and Selfridges”](https://www.totallicensing.com/spiralcute-at-harrods-and-selfridges/)

## 4. スクイッシュマロは、キャラクター同士を接続する「器」になった

Squishmallows provides a second comparison. At Selfridges, its original round plush characters sit next to collaborations with Pokémon and Marvel. ここではthe Squishmallows form itself becomes a container that can accept another intellectual property.

Pokémon began as games and animation, but as a Squishmallow it shifts slightly from “character to play in a story” toward “object to hug, display, and collect.” Selfridges sells 25cm Pikachu and Gengar versions for £18. That small fact shows how very different franchises can plug into the same plush-buying culture.

This matters for Chiikawa because overseas consumers do not need the full manga context on day one. まずexisting behaviorsに入ればよい: collect soft things, open mystery boxes, place small objects on a desk. The deeper story, videos, and character relationships can come later.

So global character expansion is not only building awareness from zero. **It is also designing an entry into hobbies that already exist.** Squishmallows is an extreme case of that logic, while Chiikawa can enter through plush, collectables, and mystery products that use the same familiar behaviors.

Sources: [Selfridges, “Squishmallows”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/cat/squishmallows/) ／ [Selfridges, “Pokémon Gengar Soft Toy 25cm”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/product/squishmallows-pokmon-gengar-soft-toy-25cm_R04587329/) ／ [Selfridges, “Pokémon Pikachu Soft Toy 25cm”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/product/squishmallows-pokmon-pikachu-soft-toy-25cm_R04587330/)

## 5. 欧州の玩具市場は、子どもの人口減を大人の購買が埋め始めている

If we stop at “trendy London department store,” we miss the scale. Circana says the UK toy market for ages 12+ grew 5% over the 12 months to June 2025, reaching £1.2 billion and 31% of total toy spending. In 2025, 43% of adults had bought a toy for themselves or another adult; among ages 18–34, the figure was 76%.

The same direction appears across Europe. In the five largest European toy markets, the 12+ segment was worth €4.5 billion in 2023 and represented 28.5% of toy sales, with adults the fastest-growing age group. Circana’s 2026 global report says recipients aged 15+ now represent nearly 20% of global toy sales, with spending more than doubling since 2020.

The common label is “kidult,” combining kid and adult. ただし“childish adult”と読むと違う。What changed is the job toys perform: nostalgia, collecting, fandom, stress relief, room decoration, and social connection now sit beside traditional play. Adult spending became visible because the category itself expanded.

That makes Chiikawa’s UK expansion part of a larger structural story. It is not only a Japanese character searching for overseas children. **It is also a Japanese character entering a mature European market where adult toy demand has become a meaningful growth engine.** The exporter changed, but so did the receiving market.

Sources: [Circana, “UK toy market grows to £3.9bn”](https://www.circana.com/post/building-up-to-christmas-uk-toy-market-grows-to-3-9bn-as-creativity-and-collectibles-lead-festive) ／ [Circana, “Fandom and nostalgia fuel growth of teens and adults’ €4.5bn toy market in Europe”](https://www.circana.com/post/playful-profits-fandom-and-nostalgia-fuel-growth-of-teens-and-adults-4-5bn-toy-market-in-europe) ／ [Circana, “Global Toy Market Hits $123B”](https://www.circana.com/post/global-toy-market-hits-123b-as-play-expands-across-ages-and-markets)

## 6. ミニソウは、ちいかわを売る会社というより「キャラクターを運ぶ小売インフラ」である

Now return to MINISO, the company from the original mistaken assumption. MINISO increasingly describes itself not merely as a variety retailer but as an IP operation platform. The company says it works with more than 150 well-known IPs and launches over 10,000 new IP-related products each year. By June 2025, it had 319 stores in Europe.

The store format has changed with that strategy. Its Warsaw flagship opened at more than 1,000 square metres, with over 60% of products related to IP and dedicated zones for Disney, Sanrio, Chiikawa, and others. MINISO is shifting the store from “many cheap goods” toward a place where customers choose which character world to encounter.

The financial direction supports the point. In 2025, overseas markets generated 44.2% of MINISO-brand revenue, and by the first half of 2026 the group had reached 113 countries and regions. The company continues to name IP and large-format stores as its two growth drivers. キャラクターはproduct decorationではなく、a reason to visit the store.

From this angle, the 2024 U.S. Chiikawa pop-up looks less like a one-off collaboration and more like a piece of MINISO’s own transformation. **To move characters globally, a retailer with prime locations and repeatable “discovery shelves” can matter as much as a publisher or media company.** MINISO is both a sales channel for Chiikawa and a retail medium connecting character consumption across markets.

Sources: [MINISO, “MINISO Wins Radar Award at The Licensing Awards 2025”](https://miniso.com/brand/news_173.html) ／ [MINISO, “MINISO LAND Wins Best New Store Concept at MAPIC Awards 2025”](https://miniso.com/brand/news_174.html) ／ [MINISO, “First Polish Flagship in Warsaw”](https://www.miniso.com/brand/news_181.html) ／ [MINISO Group, “Full Year 2025 Results”](https://ir.miniso.com/2026-03-31-MINISO-Group-Announces-December-Quarter-and-Full-Year-of-2025-Unaudited-Financial-Results) ／ [MINISO Group, “2026 June Quarter Results”](https://ir.miniso.com/2026-08-28-MINISO-Group-Announces-2026-June-Quarter-and-Interim-Unaudited-Financial-Results)

## 7. ちいかわの海外展開は、「翻訳」より「受け皿」の問題として見ると分かりやすい

When a Japanese character goes overseas, language is the obvious first problem. How should dialogue be translated? Will cultural jokes travel? How should names be explained? Those questions matter, but Selfridges shows another translation happening before language.

The Chiikawa activation combines products with a discovery wall, photo spot, sushi-conveyor-style display, and video screen. It mixes Jazwares plush with authentic Japanese merchandise and spans small collectables to larger plush. 初見の人には入口、existing fansにはauthenticity and collectabilityを残す設計になっている。

Break the system apart and “work → translation → sales” looks too simple. You need a company managing the creator’s world, a company making local toys, a global distributor, a trusted retailer, an easy physical experience, short-form content, and product formats people already collect. Without that stack, the character can remain only an unfamiliar face on a shelf.

So when researching whether a character can travel globally, search volume and social reactions are not enough. **Ask who carries the rights, who makes the product, which shelf receives it, which buying ritual lowers the entry barrier, and what sends the consumer back into the story.** Popularity is emotion, but diffusion is also infrastructure.

Sources: [Jazwares, “Master Toy Partnership With Spiralcute”](https://jazwares.com/newsroom/spiralcute-international-announces-master-toy-partnership-with-jazwares/) ／ [Total Licensing, “Spiralcute at Harrods and Selfridges”](https://www.totallicensing.com/spiralcute-at-harrods-and-selfridges/) ／ [Selfridges, “CHIIKAWA”](https://www.selfridges.com/GB/en/cat/chiikawa/)

## 8. ロンドンで売れているのは、日本のかわいさではなく「大人がかわいさを買ってよい仕組み」かもしれない

Before the research, “Chiikawa is now in London” looked like a simple sign that Japanese characters were getting more popular in the West. That is not wrong. But put the 15+ labels, Jellycat’s ordering experience, Squishmallows × Pokémon, and the fact that 12+ consumers account for 31% of UK toy spending together, and cause and effect begin to look different.

Adults did not start buying plush because Chiikawa arrived. Adult collecting and self-purchase grew first; department stores turned that behavior into events and curated shelves; distributors learned how to plug overseas characters into those formats. その受け皿へChiikawaが入った。

This is less romantic than saying “Japanese kawaii conquers the world,” but more useful for business thinking. Instead of looking only at successful characters, **look for the existing behavior, shelf, distribution system, price band, and collecting culture they managed to ride.** That makes the conditions for the next exportable IP easier to see.

The odd starting image was an adult in a premium London department store buying a mystery box containing a tiny Chiikawa dressed as sushi. After the research, it no longer looks that odd. It looks almost perfectly fitted to the current market. **Global expansion is not only carrying yourself to a distant market. It is also discovering the shelf where you already make sense.**
