# Worry May Be the Sound of Unresolved Values
## 悩みのある人のほうが好き

I think I am drawn to people who have something unresolved.

「これでいいのか分からない」

“I want this, but I do not want to lose that.”

「どちらを選んでも何かが残る」

That kind of person can suddenly feel three-dimensional.

でも、suffering itself is not attractive or noble.

反復思考は不安や抑うつを維持することもある。

So what exactly am I responding to?

A useful clue comes from research on unresolved personal goals.

2020年のexperimentでは、未解決の個人的goalを思い出させると、そのgoalに関する自発的なruminationが増えた。

Unfinished goals come back.

Maybe a worry is sometimes the mind saying:

**this still matters, and it is not settled yet.**

[Roberts et al., 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005791619300461)

---

## 1. “Worry” is not one thing / 悩みを一枚岩にしない

Everyday Japanese packs many processes into the word「悩む」.

Future-oriented worry.

過去を繰り返すrumination.

Decision-making.

Problem solving.

同じ場面を延々再生すること。

Research often distinguishes worry and rumination, but they also share features such as repetition, negativity and difficulty disengaging.

2009年のreviewでは、それらを**repetitive negative thinking**という横断的processとして見る考え方が整理されている。

So the important question is not only:

“Do you think about it repeatedly?”

むしろ、

**Does the repetition change anything?**

[Ehring & Watkins, 2009](https://guilfordjournals.com/doi/10.1521/ijct.2008.1.3.192)

---

## 2. Repetition can be a hole or a staircase / 反復は穴にも階段にもなる

Edward Watkins’ 2008 review is useful because it refuses a simple answer.

Repetitive thought can be unconstructive.

不安、抑うつ、身体的な負担につながることがある。

But it can also support preparation, recovery, planning and behavior change.

One important difference is **construal level**.

「なぜ自分はいつもこうなんだ」

is abstract.

「明日、最初に誰へ何を確認するか」

is concrete.

Same topic, different cognitive movement.

悩みがあること自体がdepthではない。

The way a person holds the worry matters.

[Watkins, “Constructive and Unconstructive Repetitive Thought,” 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2672052/)

---

## 3. Behind a worry, there is often a desired state / 悩みの下に「こうありたい」がある

Goal Progress Theory offers a simple lens.

人は、望む状態へのprogressが足りないと感じると、そのdiscrepancyについて考え続けやすい。

Not every rumination can be reduced to one goal gap.

でも日常の悩みを見るにはかなり使える。

If work worries you, some aspect of work matters.

人間関係で悩むなら、relationや自分のあり方を雑に捨てたくない。

If expression bothers you, you care about how something lands.

So beneath many worries sits a sentence like:

**“I want this to be different.”**

[A roadmap to rumination, 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2832862/)

---

## 4. Maybe I do not like “worried people.” I like unresolved complexity.

ここで最初の言い方を直せる。

I do not like suffering.

I like people who have not flattened every contradiction too quickly.

成果もほしい。

But I also want integrity.

相手を大事にしたい。

But I need to protect myself.

自由でいたい。

But I also accept responsibility.

When two valued things collide, the person becomes visible.

悩みを聞くと、その人が何を簡単には捨てられないかが見える。

That is probably what I find interesting.

---

## 5. Translate the worry into a gap / 悩みを差分にする

Instead of writing:

「仕事を辞めるか悩んでいる」

break it into four parts.

1. **Current state / 現在地**
2. **Desired state / 望む状態**
3. **What must not be lost / 守りたいもの**
4. **Next concrete move / 次の具体**

Then “quit or stay” may become:

“I want more growth, but I do not want to destroy financial stability.”

悩みがfogからmapに変わる。

The 2020 unresolved-goal study found that less specific goal-focused rumination was associated with less helpful affective recovery.

Specificity is not a universal cure, but it changes the form of thinking.

[Roberts et al., 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0005791619300461)

---

## 6. Some worries are solved. Others are carried differently.

Not every dilemma has a clean solution.

転職か残留か。

Relationship or distance.

Commercial value or expression.

Two real values can conflict without one being wrong.

だから“solve the problem”だけを要求すると、悩みの中にある大事なものまで削ることがある。

Progress can also mean naming the conflict correctly.

---

## 7. Worry may be friction between things we still refuse to abandon

I began with:

「悩みのあるやつのほうが好き。」

Now I would say it differently.

Suffering is not a badge.

悩みが多いほど深いわけでもない。

But worries contain information.

Unresolved goals.

Slow progress.

Competing values.

A desire that has not yet found language.

だから惹かれるのは、悩みそのものではない。

It is the moment when I can see **what this person still refuses to give up on**.

Maybe worry is not a flaw or a medal.

その人の中で複数の大事なものが同時に存在しているときに鳴る、friction soundなのかもしれない。
