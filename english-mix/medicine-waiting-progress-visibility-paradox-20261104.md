# 薬を飲んだあと、なぜ時計を見てしまうのか — The Cost of Checking

My throat hurts. I take a pill. Five minutes later, I check the clock. まだ痛い。Of course it still hurts—it's been five minutes. Yet why did I look? A clock cannot reduce inflammation. What I seem to want is not merely relief, but evidence that relief is on its way. **A process can be working without being perceptible.** そのずれが、時計を見るという奇妙な習慣を生む。

## Two different clocks

ペラックT錠に含まれるトラネキサム酸は、炎症に関わるプラスミンの働きを抑える。The manufacturer's explanation describes a mechanism, not a universal minute-by-minute timetable for feeling better. “I still feel pain” and “the medicine is doing nothing” are not equivalent claims. 痛みは重要な本人の感覚だが、薬理作用の進捗計ではない。Maybe I am not checking elapsed time. Maybe I am looking for a moment at which uncertainty feels acceptable.

There is an important medical boundary here. Monitoring worsening symptoms or difficulty breathing or swallowing can matter for care decisions. だから観察そのものを否定する話ではない。The question is narrower: **what decision changes because I checked again?** Without an answer, more frequent checking may add little information. Observation becomes valuable not simply through repetition, but through its ability to support a decision.

## The tiny moving dot

Now open a delivery app. A small dot approaches your home, passes it, and stops around the corner. Why? We don't know the delivery sequence or road conditions. それなのに、点だけは細かく見える。Waiting has acquired a new task: watching, predicting, and correcting our predictions. If I can change the delivery window, location data may be actionable. If I cannot, more precision does not necessarily mean more control.

Progress visibility has real benefits. It can provide an estimate and sometimes reduce uncertainty. But consider a hypothesis, not an established result: **high-frequency updates may also increase checking behavior and the mental effort of interpretation.** 問題は情報量そのものではない。The better question is what the recipient can do with it. Once a process becomes visible, watching the process can become work.

## When a dashboard changes the work

職場の進捗管理画面でも似たことが起こる。A manager gains visibility, while employees may spend time updating numbers, explaining delays, and keeping the display consistent. Some work is easy to count; exploration and trial-and-error often are not. The concern is not that dashboards are inherently bad. It is that **measurement can alter the behavior being measured.** 管理のための情報が、管理される仕事の形まで変える。

Imagine three versions of the same delivery experience. One offers a live map. Another sends only milestone notifications. A third alerts you when you can actually change the delivery arrangements. これは実施済みの実験ではなく、比較実験の提案だ。We would measure not only satisfaction, but checking frequency, interruptions, successful changes, and missed deliveries. If the third design preserves delivery success while reducing interruptions, less information becomes a feature rather than a defect.

## The freedom not to look

Return to the pill and the clock. What mattered was never the passing of five minutes alone. It was whether anything required a new decision. だから情報設計の目的を「すべて見せる」から「判断が必要な瞬間を知らせる」へ変えてみる。Alert me to meaningful exceptions. Tell me when I have a choice. Otherwise, let me get on with my day. **Transparency is not the same as constant surveillance.** A good system may not only show us more of the world; it may give us back the freedom to look away.

## Research notes and next test

- **Verified**: The drug's described mechanism does not establish a universal time when relief becomes perceptible.
- **Interpretation**: Visible progress can create work for the person who monitors it.
- **Hypothesis**: Frequent, non-actionable updates may increase interruptions without improving decisions.
- **Next test**: Compare live tracking, milestone notifications, and actionable alerts using checking frequency, interruptions, decisions, and outcomes.
