# 人生は速すぎるのではなく、通り過ぎすぎている
## Slow down not the world, but the rate at which experience disappears

最近、「もっとゆっくり生きたい」と思う。

But I do not actually want everything to be slow. 電車は速い方がいい。Search should be fast. 会計で前の人がずっと財布を探していたら、普通に焦る。

それでも、I finish a coffee and barely remember its taste. 駅まで歩いたのに空の色を見ていない。I read something interesting, then open the next thing ten seconds later.

So maybe the problem is not the speed of life itself.

**I want fewer moments that I merely pass through.**

---

## 1. 「ゆっくり」は、動作速度ではなく attentional dwell time かもしれない

「速い／遅い」を一つのscaleにすると話が雑になる。There are at least three speeds in daily life: physical movement, input density, and how long attention stays with one thing.

快速電車で移動しながら一曲を最後まで聴く人は、physically fast but attentively slow. ソファで寝転びながらshort videosを次々送る人は、身体は静止していてもexperiential switchingは速い。

That distinction matters. 「ゆっくり生きる」は、everythingを低速化することではない。

What I seem to want is **attentional dwell time**――一つの対象にattentionが少し長く滞在すること。

This is good news because efficiency does not have to become the enemy. 速い電車も検索もAIも使えばいい。ただ、そこで浮いた時間までautomatic nextnessで埋める必要はない。

Maybe the place to slow down is not the whole day. **It is the few seconds before “next.”**

---

## 2. Psychology has a surprisingly literal word for 「味わう」

調べていて一番うれしかった概念が **savoring** だった。It means attending to a positive experience in ways that enhance its value or emotional quality.

2025年に公表されたstudyでは、25〜85歳の成人285人が10日間、1日6回のexperience samplingに回答した。Across all sampling occasions, people reported savoring about 42% of the time. 親しい相手と一緒にいた場面ほどsavoringが報告されやすく、more frequent savoring was also associated with psychological well-being.

This is association, not a magic causal law. 「味わえば幸福になる」と断定する研究ではない。

Still, the idea is powerful: **savoring is not only a poetic personality trait. It is something that happens—or does not happen—inside ordinary moments.**

[Momentary Savoring in Daily Life in an Adult Life-Span Sample, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11922089/)

Mindfulness and savoring are also related but distinct. 2017年の9週間のlongitudinal studyでは、present-moment awarenessとしてのmindfulnessと、positive experienceを味わう能力が別のconstructとして扱われ、その組み合わせがdaily positive emotionと関連していた。

A rough distinction helps me: mindfulness asks, **“What is here?”** Savoring adds, **“Can I stay with what is good here for a little longer?”**

気づくことと、味わうことは同じではない。There may be two steps.

[Being present and enjoying it: Dispositional mindfulness and savoring the moment, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5755604/)

---

## 3. Having time is not the same as feeling that time is yours

「もっと時間があれば、もっとゆっくりできる」。True, but incomplete.

Research on **time affluence** does not define richness of time only by the number of free hours. It often includes a subjective sense of having enough time and some autonomy over how to use it.

A 2026 scoping review整理した12研究では、higher perceived time affluence was generally associated with better life satisfaction and well-being and lower stress. ただし、多くはcross-sectionalで、definitions and measures also vary. Strong causal claims would be too much.

[Time affluence and health: a scoping review, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13414762/)

Still, this helps explain something familiar. 同じ10分でも、電車遅延で奪われた10分と、「この10分は何もしなくていい」と自分で決めた時間は違う。

The clock is identical. The relationship to the clock is not.

So slowness may begin before we secure a whole empty afternoon. **Sometimes margin begins with ownership, not duration.**

---

## 4. Slow Looking is less about looking longer than refusing to close the object too early

「味わう」をmoodの話だけにしないために、見る行為まで降りる。

Harvard Project ZeroのShari Tishmanが扱う **Slow Looking** は、時間をかけて対象を観察し、first impressionを越えていくpracticeである。

One of Project Zero's routines, “Looking: Ten Times Two,” is almost comically literal. 対象を少なくとも30秒見る。気づいたことを10個挙げる。Then look again and find ten more.

[Slow Looking: The Art and Practice of Learning Through Observation](https://pz.harvard.edu/resources/slow-looking-the-art-and-practice-of-learning-through-observation)

[Project Zero: Looking: Ten Times Two](https://pz.harvard.edu/resources/looking-ten-times-two)

The interesting instruction is not “interpret better.” It is “assume there is still more to notice.”

That changes what slowness means. ゆっくり見るとは、目をゆっくり動かすことではない。**It means delaying closure.**

Coffee can stop at “bitter,” or you can notice temperature, aroma, aftertaste, the feel of the cup. いつもの道は「いつもの道」で閉じられるし、看板、風、工事中の場所、知らない音まで開き直すこともできる。

Maybe the world is not always thin. **Sometimes we close it too quickly.**

---

## 5. There is no magic trick for making life feel longer—but event boundaries matter to remembered time

ここで「ゆっくり生きれば人生が長く感じる」というtoo-good-to-be-trueな話にはブレーキをかけたい。

Time perception is complicated. 時間を体験している最中のjudgmentと、あとから「長かった」と振り返るjudgmentでもmechanismは同じではない。So “do novel things and life becomes longer” is too neat.

But a 2026 study gives one useful clue. Participants watched 80-second everyday-event videos containing two to four subevents, then recalled them immediately and again seven days later. For judgments of the whole event's duration, remembered event structure—especially the number of subevents—was related to perceived duration more strongly than fine-grained content details.

[Retrospective duration judgments of naturalistic events depend on memories of event boundaries, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12769706/)

This does **not** prove that adding events makes life subjectively longer.

I take a smaller lesson from it. 一日を刺激で満杯にしなくても、**「ここは一つの時間だった」と残るboundaryをつくることには意味があるかもしれない。**

Morning coffee. 一曲だけ立ち止まって聴く。夕方の空を一分だけ見る。

These are too small for a calendar. But they may keep a day from becoming one continuous blur.

Not more time. **More contour.**

---

## 6. In an information-rich world, the scarce resource may be the right to stay

世界が速く感じる理由を、「自分の集中力が弱い」で全部説明したくない。

Herbert Simon famously argued in 1971 that information abundance creates scarcity elsewhere—especially in attention. Contemporary discussions of the **attention economy** inherit that basic problem.

[Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Attention](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/attention/)

今はarticleを読み終える前からrelated linksが出る。A song ends and another begins. 動画を閉じてもnext recommendationが待っている。

Most of this is convenient. I use it happily.

But when every experience has a prebuilt exit into the next one, **not moving on becomes the action that requires choice.**

So modern slowness may not be rebellion against speed. It may be a few seconds of distance from **nextness**――常に次へ送られる力。

The opposite of fast is not always slow.

**Sometimes it is simply: not automatic.**

---

## 7. Try one moment a day where you do not move on

ここまでをlife experimentにする。ただし、30分の瞑想、digital detox、週末を完全に空ける、みたいな大改革にはしない。

If the practice itself becomes another productivity task, the point collapses.

So:

**Once a day, do not move on.**

対象は何でもいい。Coffee, one song, dinner, the walk home, a conversation, a photograph, the sky, a stadium before kickoff.

About 90 seconds is enough for an experiment.

1. First 30 seconds: no recording. 見る・聞く・味わう。Do not rush to name everything.
2. Next 30 seconds: find three things you did not notice at first.
3. Final 30 seconds: ask, **“What was this?”** 一文だけ置く。No sentence is also allowed.

This is my own rough translation of Slow Looking into everyday life. 90秒という効果がscientifically validatedされているわけではない。

The aim is smaller: **touch the experience once more before it passes through you.**

映画を見終えたあと10秒だけscreenを閉じたままにする。After a conversation, recall one phrase that remains. 「美味しかった」で閉じる前に、what exactly was good? と一個だけ考える。

Do not savor in order to produce a record.

**Let a record exist only if savoring leaves one behind.**

---

## 8. Do not turn slowness into another moral obligation

ここまで来ると、a necessary counterpoint appears: 「それ、余裕のある人の話では？」

Partly, yes.

仕事、育児、介護、移動、money constraints。When autonomy over time is structurally limited, “just slow down” can become insulting. Time affluence includes perceived control precisely because time is not distributed only as equal 24-hour blocks in any meaningful social sense.

There are also moments we do not need to savor. Pain, anxiety, boredom, emergencies. 「全部を丁寧に味わおう」は、新しいperformance standardになりうる。

So I do not want slowness to become a character test.

**Savoring can be one freedom available when something good appears—not a duty applied to every minute.**

味わえなかった日は、そのままでいい。忙しく通過した日もある。Nothing remains from some films, meals, or conversations. That is not failure.

The only thing I want to protect is smaller: **when there is room to stay, do not surrender that room automatically to the next stimulus.**

---

## 9. Do not slow the world. Slow how quickly the world disappears inside you

最初は「もっとゆっくり生きたい」というvague wishだった。

After the research, I can say something more precise.

Savoring is about attending to positive experience. Time affluence is partly about enoughness and control, not only empty hours. Slow Looking teaches us not to close an object at first impression. Research on retrospective duration suggests remembered event structure matters to how past time is judged.

日常語に戻せば、全部かなり小さい。

Taste the coffee once more after swallowing. 帰り道で一回だけ空を見る。Keep the tab closed for ten seconds after finishing an essay. 会話のあと、一つだけ残った言葉を思い出す。

None of these will dramatically transform a life.

But they insert a tiny stay between **experience → next experience**.

That tiny stay may be the closest thing I have found to “slow.”

この先も、たくさん見たい。I want books, matches, new technologies, new places, new people. 速いものは速いまま使いたい。

But there is a difference between **seeing many things** and **becoming someone in whom a few things remain.**

I want to lean toward the second.

世界を遅くすることはできない。We cannot add hours to the life already given to us.

But we can sometimes stay for a few seconds before moving on.

**Maybe we can make the world disappear a little more slowly inside us.**

この後は、そういうふうに生きていきたい。
