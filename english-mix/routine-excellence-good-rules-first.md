# Routine Excellence Only Works When the Routine Is Good
## 凡事徹底の前に、凡事設計

「凡事徹底」は強い言葉だ。

Do ordinary things, consistently and thoroughly.

派手な一発より、日々の基本を崩さない。

Hard to disagree with.

But there is one uncomfortable question.

**What if the ordinary thing is wrong?**

間違ったprocedureを、全員が毎日100％守る。

No omissions. No exceptions. Perfect discipline.

That may be worse than inconsistency.

A bad rule with high compliance reproduces error reliably.

To test this idea, look at a place where consistency really matters: the operating room.

WHO's Surgical Safety Checklist is designed to support critical checks around identity, surgical site, anesthesia, infection prevention and team communication.

But WHO's own implementation guidance does not simply say “follow the list.”

It emphasizes early staff involvement, leadership, education, multidisciplinary participation, feedback, and **local adaptation**.

Even a safety standard needs to be designed, tested and owned by the people using it.

[WHO, Implementation Manual: WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, 2009](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241598590)

---

## 1. Consistency has no direction / 徹底そのものには方向がない

A rough model:

**quality of the rule × execution rate**

良いruleが80点でも、executionが30％なら弱い。

So consistency matters.

But if the rule itself is poor, 100% execution does not rescue it.

目的と関係のない確認。

A procedure that made sense five years ago but no longer does.

例外を想定していないmanual.

A prohibition nobody can explain.

Doing these perfectly still wastes time or creates risk.

**Consistency is not a steering wheel. It is an amplifier.**

だから「もっと徹底しよう」の前に、「これは徹底する価値があるか」を見る必要がある。

---

## 2. A good checklist is not a longer checklist / 項目を増やせば安全になるわけではない

WHO's implementation manual gives practical principles for modifying a checklist.

**Focused. Brief. Actionable. Collaborative. Tested.**

本当に重要な項目に絞る。

Keep it short enough to fit the workflow.

具体的なactionに結びつける。

Include the people who will use it.

小さく試して、feedbackで直す。

This is almost the opposite of “add everything to be safe.”

項目が多すぎれば、形式だけになる。

If an item is vague, people can “check” it without changing behavior.

A good rule is not the strictest rule.

**It is a rule that connects purpose to observable action in the real workflow.**

[WHO Surgical Safety Checklist Implementation Manual](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/patient-safety/9789241598590-eng.pdf)

---

## 3. “Installed” is not the same as “working” / 導入と機能は違う

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis looked at 13 observational studies covering 17,867 participants.

WHO checklistのoverall complianceは73％。

But complete implementation was only **51%**.

制度はある。

The form exists.

記録も残る。

Still, the full process may not actually happen.

Factors associated with better implementation included training, managerial support, a supportive work environment, and regular audit and feedback.

A 2011 multi-hospital qualitative study found something similar: explaining **why** and **how** the checklist should be used supported adoption, while top-down mandates without ownership could produce resistance or abandonment.

“Follow the rule” is not an implementation strategy.

[“Beyond compliance: examining the completeness and determinants of WHO surgical safety checklist,” 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11971763/)

[“Effective Surgical Safety Checklist Implementation,” 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1072751511000858)

## 4. High-reliability organizations do not worship rules / 高信頼性組織は盲従しない

Organizations that cannot tolerate failure still do not rely on rigid compliance alone.

High Reliability Organization research often highlights principles such as:

- **preoccupation with failure** / 小さな失敗を軽視しない
- **reluctance to simplify** / 単純化しすぎない
- **sensitivity to operations** / 現場で起きていることを見る
- **commitment to resilience** / 想定外から回復する
- **deference to expertise** / 階級より、その状況を知る人の専門性を優先する

This is not anti-standardization.

むしろ、標準を持ちながら、現実が標準から外れた瞬間を見逃さない。

Reliable systems need both routine and judgment.

[“High reliability organizations and healthcare safety outcomes,” 2026](https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pgph.0006181)

---

## 5. Before routine excellence comes routine design / 凡事徹底の前に凡事設計

I want to add one invisible step before「凡事徹底」.

**凡事設計。**

Decide what deserves to become ordinary.

Audit the rule with five questions.

### 1. Purpose / 目的

What should this rule improve?

一文で言えるか。

### 2. Causality / 因果

Why should this behavior move the purpose forward?

「昔からやっている」で止まっていないか。

### 3. Action / 行動

Can two people independently tell whether it was done?

「意識する」「丁寧に」だけになっていないか。

### 4. Exceptions / 例外

When reality differs from the standard, who decides and how much flexibility is allowed?

### 5. Update / 更新

Do failures and near misses flow back into the rule?

この五つを通ったあとで、徹底する。

Then “routine excellence” becomes operational design rather than a slogan.

---

## 6. Before blaming the person, inspect the rule / 守らない人の前に、守れる設計かを見る

When operations fail, it is tempting to blame execution.

確認不足。

Low awareness.

ルールを守っていない。

Sometimes that is true.

But implementation research points to other possibilities too.

Too long.

意味が見えない。

Does not fit the actual workflow.

例外が多すぎる。

No feedback when people follow it.

The more strictly an organization demands compliance, the more responsibility falls on the designers of the standard.

---

## 7. Consistency is the second half of the idea / 凡事徹底は後半だった

My original suspicion survives.

凡事徹底は大事。

Good standards, repeatedly executed, are powerful.

But consistency is only the second half.

What should become routine?

本当に目的につながる？

Does it work in reality?

例外を拾えている？

Does failure update the rule?

**Consistency is a multiplier, not a direction.**

だから強い組織は、同じことを守り続ける組織ではない。

It consistently protects what matters while continuously questioning what deserves protection.

「凡事徹底」の前に、見えない一語がある。

**良き凡事を、徹底する。**
